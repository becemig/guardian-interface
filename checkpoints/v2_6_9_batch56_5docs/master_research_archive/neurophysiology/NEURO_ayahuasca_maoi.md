# Ayahuasca and MAOI Pharmacology

## Abstract

Ayahuasca is a psychoactive brew prepared from the combination of Banisteriopsis caapi vine containing beta-carboline MAOIs and Psychotria viridis leaves containing DMT — a pharmacological combination that represents one of the most sophisticated empirical discoveries in the history of ethnopharmacology, developed by Amazonian shamans over centuries without knowledge of the underlying biochemistry. The beta-carbolines harmine, harmaline, and tetrahydroharmine in the Banisteriopsis caapi vine are potent reversible inhibitors of monoamine oxidase A RIMA-A — the enzyme responsible for breaking down DMT, serotonin, and tyramine in the gut and liver before they reach systemic circulation. Without MAOI activity, orally ingested DMT is completely destroyed by gut MAO before reaching the brain — it is only active when smoked or injected, or when gut MAO is inhibited. The MAOI activity of the beta-carbolines enables oral DMT bioavailability, allowing the ayahuasca brew to produce 4-6 hours of DMT-mediated psychedelic experience through the 5-HT2A mechanism. The tyramine interaction is the critical safety consideration — MAOIs block the enzyme that prevents tyramine absorption from food. Tyramine-rich foods aged cheese, cured meats, fermented foods, red wine, beer, soy sauce, overripe fruit contain tyramine that is normally destroyed by gut MAO. With MAO inhibited, tyramine floods the bloodstream triggering massive norepinephrine release from sympathetic terminals producing hypertensive crisis that can be fatal. Traditional ayahuasca dietary protocols — no aged cheese, fermented foods, alcohol, caffeine, red meat, pork for several days before and after ceremony — are empirically derived MAOI dietary restrictions that exactly match modern pharmacological safety guidelines. TCM herb MAOI overlap: several TCM herbs contain beta-carboline alkaloids or otherwise inhibit MAO, requiring similar dietary and herb-herb interaction caution.

## Beta-Carboline MAOIs

- Harmine and harmaline are potent reversible MAO-A inhibitors from Banisteriopsis caapi.
- Tetrahydroharmine is a weaker MAO inhibitor but also a serotonin reuptake inhibitor.
- Together they produce the MAOI effect enabling oral DMT bioavailability.
- Beta-carbolines also have 5-HT2A partial agonist activity contributing independently to the experience.

## Tyramine Interaction

- MAO-A normally breaks down tyramine in the gut preventing its systemic absorption.
- With MAO inhibited, tyramine from aged cheese, cured meats, fermented foods, red wine enters circulation.
- Systemic tyramine triggers massive norepinephrine release from sympathetic nerve terminals.
- Resulting hypertensive crisis can produce stroke, myocardial infarction, and death.

## TCM Herb MAOI Overlap

- Huang Lian berberine inhibits MAO-A and MAO-B producing antidepressant and interaction risk.
- Shi Chang Pu beta-asarone has MAOI-adjacent activity amplifying psychoactive herb combinations.
- Peganum harmala Syrian rue used in TCM-adjacent traditions contains harmine and harmaline directly.
- Ma Huang ephedra is a sympathomimetic — combining with any MAOI produces severe hypertensive risk.
- The classical eighteen incompatibilities and nineteen antagonisms in TCM encode empirical interaction warnings that partially overlap with enzyme inhibition and receptor competition mechanisms.
