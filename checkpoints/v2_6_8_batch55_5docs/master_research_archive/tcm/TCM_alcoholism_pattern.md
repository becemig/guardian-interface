# Alcoholism in TCM

## Abstract

Alcohol in TCM energetics is hot, pungent, and sweet in nature — initially moving Liver Qi, warming the center, and temporarily lifting the Shen through its hot pungent dispersing quality. This explains alcohol's initial attractive effects: the warmth in the chest, the loosening of social inhibition, the temporary relief of Liver Qi stagnation. However with chronic use alcohol produces its opposite effects through the classic TCM principle of excess producing its contrary — the hot damp nature of alcohol generates damp-heat in the Liver and Gallbladder, the sweet quality injures the Spleen, and the dispersing quality eventually depletes rather than moves Qi. Damp-heat in the Liver and Gallbladder is the defining pathological pattern of alcoholism — producing the irritability, red face, bitter taste, hypochondriac pain, and yellow sclera of alcoholic liver disease. Spleen collapse from the sweet dampening nature of alcohol produces the progressive digestive weakness, bloating, diarrhea, and nutritional deficiency characteristic of alcoholism. Shen disturbance progresses from initial stimulation through the Heart Fire pattern of agitation and insomnia to the Phlegm-Heat misting the Heart pattern of cognitive impairment and alcoholic psychosis. Gu Syndrome — a classical TCM concept for complex chronic illness involving pathogenic factors that have penetrated deeply and transformed — has been applied to the complex dysbiotic, neuroinflammatory, and metabolic disorder of alcoholism. Jing exhaustion from prolonged alcohol use corresponds to the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis burnout and testosterone suppression of chronic alcoholism. TCM detox combines acupuncture, herbs that clear damp-heat from the Liver, tonify the Spleen, and calm the Shen.

## Alcohol Energetics

- Initial: hot pungent quality moves Liver Qi, warms center, temporarily lifts Shen — the attractive phase.
- Short term excess: hot nature generates Liver heat and irritability, sweet nature dampens Spleen.
- Chronic use: damp-heat accumulates in Liver-Gallbladder, Spleen collapses, Phlegm-Heat mists Heart.
- Constitutional depletion: Kidney Jing exhausts, Liver Blood depletes, Shen loses its anchor.

## Damp-Heat Liver Pattern

- Damp-heat in Liver-Gallbladder produces irritability, hypochondriac fullness, bitter taste, yellow sclera.
- This corresponds to alcoholic hepatitis — elevated liver enzymes, inflammatory cytokines, hepatocyte damage.
- Herbs: Long Dan Cao, Yin Chen Hao, Zhi Zi — bitter cold herbs clearing Liver damp-heat.
- Acupuncture: LR-2 Xingjian, GB-34 Yanglingquan, SP-9 Yinlingquan draining damp-heat.

## Spleen Collapse

- Chronic alcohol sweet-damp nature injures Spleen Qi producing progressive digestive weakness.
- Spleen collapse corresponds to alcoholic malnutrition, thiamine deficiency, and dysbiosis.
- This is the TCM basis for Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome — Spleen failing to transform and transport nutrients to the brain.
- Tonifying Spleen Qi with Dang Shen, Bai Zhu, Fu Ling is essential in alcoholism recovery.

## TCM Correspondence

- Damp-heat Liver pattern corresponds to alcoholic hepatitis and liver inflammation.
- Spleen collapse corresponds to alcoholic malnutrition, dysbiosis, and nutrient malabsorption.
- Phlegm-Heat misting Heart corresponds to alcoholic encephalopathy and cognitive impairment.
- Kidney Jing exhaustion corresponds to HPA axis burnout, testosterone suppression, and premature aging.
