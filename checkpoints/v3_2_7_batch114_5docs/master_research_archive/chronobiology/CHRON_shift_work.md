# Shift Work Pathophysiology

## Abstract

Shift work pathophysiology — the comprehensive health consequences of chronic circadian disruption produced by rotating and permanent night shift work, affecting approximately 20 percent of the workforce in industrialized countries and producing a constellation of metabolic, cardiovascular, oncological, psychiatric, and reproductive harms through the multiple mechanisms of chronic sleep deprivation, circadian misalignment between the SCN and peripheral clocks, suppression of nocturnal melatonin by light at night, and the metabolic consequences of eating during the biological night — represents one of the largest occupational health problems of industrial modernity. Circadian disruption mechanisms in shift work — the combination of light exposure at night suppressing melatonin and delaying the SCN clock, sleep during the day being shorter and of lower quality due to social noise and light, eating during the biological night disrupting the liver and gut peripheral clocks, and the chronic desynchrony between the light-entrained SCN and the feeding-entrained peripheral clocks — produces a state of internal circadian misalignment analogous to permanent chronic jet lag that impairs virtually every organ system. Shift work cancer risk — the International Agency for Research on Cancer IARC 2007 classification of shift work involving circadian disruption as a probable Group 2A carcinogen based on sufficient animal evidence and limited human evidence, with subsequent epidemiological studies consistently finding 20-50 percent elevated breast cancer risk in long-term night shift nurses, elevated colorectal and prostate cancer risk, and biological mechanisms including melatonin suppression reducing its anti-proliferative effects, circadian disruption of tumor suppressor gene expression, and immune circadian gating disruption impairing cancer surveillance. Metabolic syndrome and night work — the consistent epidemiological finding that night shift workers have significantly elevated rates of obesity, type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome, and cardiovascular disease, with experimental circadian misalignment studies demonstrating rapid deterioration of glucose tolerance, insulin sensitivity, lipid profiles, and blood pressure within days of simulated night shift schedules. The reversed day-night Qi inversion correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that working and being active during the Yin night hours and sleeping during the Yang day hours inverts the natural Yin Yang cycle, depleting Kidney Yang and disturbing the Shen, producing the exhaustion, metabolic disorders, and increased disease vulnerability characteristic of chronic night shift work.

## Circadian Disruption Mechanisms

- Light at night: suppresses melatonin delays SCN phase.
- Daytime sleep: shorter less restorative social noise light interference.
- Nocturnal eating: entrains peripheral clocks opposite to SCN — internal misalignment.
- Chronic sleep deprivation: cumulative deficit compounds circadian disruption harms.

## Cancer Risk

- IARC 2A classification: probable human carcinogen — circadian disrupting shift work.
- Breast cancer: 20-50 percent elevated risk in long-term night shift nurses.
- Mechanisms: melatonin suppression immune disruption clock gene tumor suppressor dysregulation.
- Dose response: risk increases with years of night shift work.

## Metabolic Consequences

- Glucose tolerance: impaired within days of simulated night shift.
- Insulin resistance: elevated fasting insulin reduced insulin sensitivity.
- Lipid profile: elevated triglycerides reduced HDL.
- Cardiovascular: 40 percent elevated coronary heart disease risk in rotating shift workers.
