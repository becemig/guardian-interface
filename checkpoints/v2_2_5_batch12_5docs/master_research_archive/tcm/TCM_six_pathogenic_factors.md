# Six Pathogenic Factors

## Abstract

The six pathogenic factors Liu Yin are the six climatic influences that can invade the body and produce disease when Wei Qi is insufficient to resist them or when exposure exceeds the body's adaptive capacity. Each pathogenic factor has characteristic clinical presentations reflecting the nature of the climate it mimics. Wind is the chief pathogen carrying other factors into the body and producing moving changeable symptoms. Cold contracts, slows, and obstructs Qi and Blood producing severe pain and restriction. Heat agitates, consumes fluids, and rises upward producing inflammatory presentations. Dampness accumulates, obstructs, and weighs down producing heaviness and fluid retention. Dryness consumes fluids and parches tissues. Summer Heat combines the qualities of Heat and Dampness in the hottest season.

## Wind

- Wind is the chief pathogen of spring and the primary vehicle carrying other factors into the body.
- Wind produces moving changeable symptoms corresponding to its nature — pain that moves, itching, dizziness.
- Wind invades primarily through the nape of the neck at BL-12 Fengmen and the upper back.
- Internal Wind arises from Liver Yang rising or Blood deficiency failing to nourish sinews.

## Cold

- Cold is the chief pathogen of winter contracting, slowing, and obstructing Qi and Blood.
- Cold invasion produces severe sharp fixed pain that improves with warmth.
- External Cold invasion produces exterior Wind Cold with aversion to cold, fever, no sweating, and tight pulse.
- Internal Cold from Yang deficiency produces cold limbs, pale complexion, and slow deep weak pulse.

## Heat

- Heat agitates, consumes fluids, and rises upward producing fever, thirst, red face, and rapid pulse.
- External Wind Heat produces fever greater than chills, sore throat, and floating rapid pulse.
- Interior Heat from excess or Yin deficiency produces deeper inflammatory or deficiency-heat presentations.
- Heat easily injures fluids requiring cooling and fluid-generating treatment.

## Dampness and Dryness

- Dampness is heavy, sticky, and turbid producing heaviness, bloating, and fluid retention.
- External Dampness invades in humid environments while internal Damp arises from Spleen dysfunction.
- Dryness is the pathogen of autumn consuming fluids and parching the Lung and skin.
- External Dryness produces dry cough, dry skin, and dry nose while internal Dryness reflects fluid depletion.

## TCM Correspondence

- Modern respiratory viral infections correspond to Wind Cold or Wind Heat invasions depending on presentation.
- Inflammatory arthritis corresponds to Bi syndrome from Wind Cold Damp invasion in the joints.
- Seasonal affective patterns correspond to the organ vulnerabilities of each season and its pathogenic factor.
