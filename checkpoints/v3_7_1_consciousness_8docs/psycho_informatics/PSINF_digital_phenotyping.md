# Digital Phenotyping

## Abstract

Digital phenotyping — the moment-by-moment quantification of the individual-level human phenotype using data from personal digital devices — represents a paradigm shift in psychiatric assessment from the periodic clinic visit to continuous passive monitoring of behavioral biomarkers in the natural environment. The smartphone has become the most powerful psychiatric monitoring device ever created, capturing through its sensors a continuous stream of behavioral data that reflects the underlying mental state: GPS location patterns reveal social withdrawal and reduced mobility in depression; accelerometer data reflects motor retardation and sleep-wake cycle disruption; screen time and app usage patterns reflect engagement, motivation, and cognitive function; communication patterns including call frequency, duration, and social network size reflect social functioning and relational health. Ecological momentary assessment EMA — the real-time sampling of current mental state, symptoms, and context through brief questionnaires delivered to the smartphone multiple times per day — provides the subjective complement to the objective passive sensing data, capturing moment-to-moment fluctuations in mood, energy, cognition, and symptom severity that are invisible to weekly or monthly clinical assessment. The combination of passive sensing and EMA creates a continuous multimodal data stream that reveals the temporal dynamics of mental state — how mood fluctuates through the day and week, how behavioral patterns precede symptom changes, and how environmental and social factors modulate mental state in real time. The Guardian Interface telemetry system — capturing HRV, respiration rate, and confidence through wearable sensors — represents the somatic complement to digital phenotyping, extending the monitoring framework into the body's physiological state and providing the interoceptive dimension that smartphone behavioral data alone cannot capture.

## Passive Sensing Biomarkers

- GPS mobility: reduced range and increased time at home predicts depression severity.
- Accelerometer: reduced physical activity, disrupted sleep-wake cycle, motor retardation.
- Screen time: increased passive consumption and reduced active engagement reflects withdrawal.
- Communication: reduced call frequency, shorter calls, and smaller active social network reflects isolation.

## Ecological Momentary Assessment

- Brief questionnaires delivered 4-8 times per day capture moment-to-moment symptom fluctuation.
- EMA reveals within-day and within-week mood dynamics invisible to clinical assessment.
- EMA-passive sensing integration identifies the behavioral correlates of subjective state changes.
- Real-time EMA enables just-in-time adaptive interventions triggered by detected state changes.

## Guardian Interface Integration

- HRV telemetry provides the physiological correlate of digital phenotyping behavioral data.
- Respiration rate captures the somatic dimension of mental state change.
- Confidence telemetry reflects the cognitive-emotional engagement dimension.
- The somatic bridge between digital phenotyping and physiological monitoring is the Guardian Interface architecture.
