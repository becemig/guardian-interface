# Gallbladder Meridian: Wood Element, Decision, Lateral Body, and Shao Yang

## Abstract

The Gallbladder is the Yang organ paired with the Liver in the Wood element system of Traditional Chinese Medicine. While the Liver governs the smooth flow of Qi and stores Blood, the Gallbladder governs decision-making, courage, and the execution of the Liver strategic vision. The Gallbladder meridian is the longest meridian pathway traversing the lateral aspect of the entire body from the lateral eye to the fourth toe, corresponding closely to the Lateral Line myofascial train of Anatomy Trains. As a Shao Yang meridian the Gallbladder governs the pivot between interior and exterior and is the primary entry point for pathogenic influences moving inward from the surface.

## Organ Functions

### Decision and Courage

- The Gallbladder governs decision-making and the capacity to act on the Liver strategic vision.
- Gallbladder Qi deficiency produces timidity, indecision, and inability to initiate action despite clear intention.
- Gallbladder excess produces recklessness and impulsive decision-making without adequate deliberation.
- Courage Hu in Chinese medicine is a function of Gallbladder Qi strength supporting decisive action in the face of difficulty.

### Sinew and Lateral Body

- The Gallbladder governs the lateral sinew channels running along the sides of the body.
- Lateral hip, knee, and ankle stability are Gallbladder meridian functions corresponding to IT band and peroneal fascial trains.
- Lateral neck tension and temporal headache are common Gallbladder Qi constraint presentations.
- GB-34 Yanglingquan is the influential point for sinews governing the entire musculotendinous system.

### Bile Secretion

- The Gallbladder stores and excretes bile produced by the Liver supporting digestion of fats.
- Liver Qi stagnation disrupts Gallbladder bile secretion producing digestive symptoms and hypochondriac discomfort.
- Damp Heat in the Gallbladder produces acute cholecystitis patterns with bitter taste, jaundice, and right hypochondriac pain.

## Shao Yang Pivot

- The Gallbladder is a Shao Yang channel governing the half-interior half-exterior pivot of the six channel system.
- Pathogenic influences caught at the Shao Yang level produce alternating fever and chills, bitter taste, and hypochondriac fullness.
- The Shao Yang pivot function makes the Gallbladder a key regulator of immune boundary and adaptive response.
- Triple Burner as the other Shao Yang channel pairs with Gallbladder governing Yang fluid metabolism and defensive surfaces.

## Meridian Pathway

### Head and Neck

- Originates at GB-1 Tongziliao lateral to the outer canthus of the eye.
- Zigzags across the temporal region and occiput in a complex pattern unique among meridians.
- GB-20 Fengchi at the occiput governs wind invasion, headache, neck tension, and visual disorders.
- Descends along the lateral neck to the shoulder at GB-21 Jianjing governing shoulder tension and lactation.

### Trunk

- Descends along the lateral ribcage through the hypochondriac region.
- GB-24 Riyue is the Front Mu alarm point of the Gallbladder on the lateral chest.
- Continues along the lateral abdomen to the hip region.
- GB-25 Jingmen is the Front Mu alarm point of the Kidney on the lateral lumbar region.

### Leg and Foot

- Descends along the lateral thigh and knee to the lateral lower leg.
- GB-34 Yanglingquan below the fibular head is the He Sea point and influential point for sinews.
- GB-39 Xuanzhong above the lateral malleolus is the influential point for marrow and a key point for stroke and wind disorders.
- Terminates at GB-44 Zuqiaoyin on the lateral fourth toe.

### Key Acupoints

- GB-20 Fengchi: Wind pool, occiput, headache, neck pain, wind invasion, visual disorders, hypertension.
- GB-21 Jianjing: Shoulder well, shoulder tension, difficult labor, mastitis, descends Qi.
- GB-34 Yanglingquan: He Sea and sinew influential point, lateral knee pain, sinew disorders, hypochondriac pain, bitter taste.
- GB-39 Xuanzhong: Marrow influential point, lateral ankle, stroke rehabilitation, bone marrow disorders, leg weakness.
- GB-40 Qiuxu: Source point, lateral ankle, ankle sprain, hypochondriac pain, Gallbladder disorders.

## Anatomy Trains Correspondence

- The Gallbladder meridian lateral pathway corresponds closely to the Lateral Line myofascial train.
- Peroneus longus and brevis along the lateral lower leg are both Gallbladder meridian territory and Lateral Line components.
- The IT band iliotibial band traversing the lateral thigh is a primary Lateral Line structure within Gallbladder meridian territory.
- Lateral neck scalene muscles correspond to the upper Lateral Line and upper Gallbladder meridian pathway.
