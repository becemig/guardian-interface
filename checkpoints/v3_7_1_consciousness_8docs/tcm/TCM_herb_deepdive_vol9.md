# TCM Herb Deep Dives Vol 9

## Abstract

The hemostatic category of TCM herbs stops pathological bleeding through multiple convergent mechanisms — platelet activation, coagulation cascade enhancement, local vasoconstriction, and direct tissue-sealing effects — while simultaneously preventing the Blood stasis that can result from incomplete hemostasis. This dual action of stopping bleeding while preventing stasis is the defining characteristic of the superior hemostatic herbs and corresponds to the TCM principle of stopping bleeding without retaining Blood. San Qi Panax notoginseng contains notoginsenosides and dencichine that simultaneously activate platelets to stop acute bleeding and inhibit platelet aggregation to prevent excessive clotting — producing the remarkable dual hemostatic and anti-thrombotic action that makes it perhaps the most sophisticated hemostatic herb in the materia medica. Notoginsenoside R1 reduces myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury making San Qi the primary TCM herb for both trauma bleeding and cardiovascular protection. Bai Ji Bletilla striata contains militarine and bletillins that form a highly viscous mucilage on contact with blood directly sealing bleeding surfaces — corresponding to the TCM action of astringing and stopping bleeding through physical tissue-sealing. Ce Bai Ye Platycladus orientalis contains hinokiflavone and amentoflavone that produce vasoconstriction, enhance platelet activation, and produce the cooling astringent hemostatic effect that makes it particularly indicated for Heat-type bleeding. Di Yu Sanguisorba officinalis contains sanguiin tannins with potent astringent hemostatic effects particularly in the lower gastrointestinal tract — corresponding to its TCM indication for bloody dysentery and hemorrhoidal bleeding. Ai Ye Artemisia argyi mugwort contains cineole and thujone with hemostatic, uterotonic, and warming properties — it is the primary moxa herb and simultaneously a warming hemostatic for Cold-type uterine bleeding.

## San Qi Dual Action

- Dencichine activates platelets and enhances coagulation cascade stopping acute hemorrhage.
- Notoginsenosides simultaneously inhibit excessive platelet aggregation preventing thrombosis.
- This dual mechanism stops bleeding without creating dangerous clots — the ideal hemostatic profile.
- Notoginsenoside R1 cardioprotection makes San Qi essential in TCM cardiovascular and trauma protocols.

## Bai Ji Physical Sealing

- Militarine mucilage forms a physical seal over bleeding surfaces directly stopping hemorrhage.
- The viscous gel-forming property explains the classical TCM use for lung hemorrhage and gastric bleeding.
- Bletillin antimicrobial activity prevents infection at the bleeding site corresponding to clearing Toxic Heat.
- Bai Ji is incompatible with Wu Tou aconite in the eighteen incompatibilities — competing alkaloid profiles.

## Ai Ye and Moxa

- Cineole in Ai Ye produces the warming penetrating heat of moxibustion when burned.
- Uterotonic effect stops Cold-type uterine bleeding while simultaneously warming the uterus.
- Thujone content requires careful preparation — prolonged high-dose internal use is hepatotoxic.
- As the primary moxa herb Ai Ye connects the hemostatic category to the broader Qi and Yang warming system.

## TCM Correspondence

- San Qi dual hemostatic and anti-thrombotic action corresponds to the TCM principle of stopping bleeding without retaining stasis.
- Bai Ji physical sealing corresponds to the TCM astringent action consolidating and containing Blood.
- Ce Bai Ye cooling hemostatic corresponds to the TCM principle of using cold herbs for Heat-type bleeding.
- Ai Ye warming hemostatic corresponds to the TCM principle of using warm herbs for Cold-type bleeding.
