# Liver Blood and Yin

## Abstract

Liver Blood and Liver Yin are two closely related yet distinct aspects of the Liver organ network's Yin dimension — Liver Blood being the more material and nutritive aspect while Liver Yin is the more subtle cooling and moistening aspect. The Liver stores Blood — during rest and sleep Blood returns to the Liver for storage, replenishment, and purification, and during activity Blood is released from the Liver to nourish the muscles, sinews, and sense organs. This dynamic storage and release function makes the Liver Blood the primary nutritive reservoir of the body analogous to hepatic blood storage and gluconeogenic regulation. Liver Blood nourishes the sinews tendons and ligaments maintaining their elasticity and resilience — Liver Blood deficiency produces tendon stiffness, cramping, and poor recovery from physical exertion. Liver Blood governs the eyes — the Liver opens to the eyes through its meridian connection — Liver Blood deficiency produces dry eyes, blurred vision, and night blindness. In women Liver Blood is the primary substrate of the menstrual cycle — Liver Blood deficiency is the most common cause of scanty or absent menstruation, and the Liver's governing of the free flow of Qi determines the regularity and ease of menstrual flow. Liver Blood must be adequate to anchor the Hun soul during sleep — Liver Blood deficiency allows the Hun to wander producing disturbed dream-filled sleep, somnambulism, and disoriented waking. The close relationship between Liver Blood and Heart Blood means that deficiency of either often affects the other producing the combined pattern of Heart and Liver Blood deficiency with anxiety, insomnia, palpitations, and poor memory alongside the Liver Blood symptoms.

## Blood Storage Function

- During rest the Blood returns to the Liver for storage corresponding to hepatic blood sequestration at rest.
- During activity the Liver releases Blood to the muscles and sinews enabling sustained physical performance.
- The Liver Blood volume determines the body's capacity for sustained physical and mental activity.
- Liver Blood deficiency produces fatigue, poor exercise tolerance, and slow recovery from exertion.

## Sinew Nourishment

- Liver Blood nourishes all sinews tendons and ligaments maintaining elasticity and resilience.
- Liver Blood deficiency produces tendon stiffness, cramping, spasm, and poor recovery from physical stress.
- LV-8 Ququan is the primary point for tonifying Liver Blood and nourishing the sinews.
- The Liver governing the sinews corresponds to the hepatic role in protein synthesis for connective tissue repair.

## Menstrual Regulation

- Liver Blood provides the primary substrate for menstrual blood — its quantity determines menstrual volume.
- Liver Qi governs the free flow and timing of menstruation — stagnation produces painful or irregular cycles.
- Liver Blood deficiency produces scanty, pale, delayed menstruation or amenorrhea.
- SP-6 Sanyinjiao and LV-8 Ququan are the primary points for tonifying Liver Blood for menstrual regulation.

## TCM Correspondence

- Hun anchoring by Liver Blood during sleep corresponds to REM sleep cholinergic regulation of dream activity.
- Liver Blood nourishing the eyes corresponds to hepatic vitamin A metabolism essential for retinal function.
- Liver Blood deficiency producing night blindness corresponds to vitamin A deficiency impairing rhodopsin synthesis.
- Liver governing sinews corresponds to the liver's role in protein synthesis for tendon collagen maintenance.
