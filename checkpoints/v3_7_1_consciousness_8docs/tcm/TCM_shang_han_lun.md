# Shang Han Lun

## Abstract

The Shang Han Lun — Treatise on Cold Damage and Miscellaneous Diseases — written by Zhang Zhong Jing during the Eastern Han dynasty around 220 CE, is the foundational clinical text of Chinese medicine and the origin of the Jing Fang classical formula tradition. Zhang Zhong Jing wrote the Shang Han Lun in response to a devastating epidemic that killed the majority of his clan, driven by the imperative to systematize clinical medicine into a reliable and teachable diagnostic and therapeutic framework. The Shang Han Lun's six channel Bing framework — organizing febrile disease progression through the six channel stages of Tai Yang, Yang Ming, Shao Yang, Tai Yin, Shao Yin, and Jue Yin — provides a complete system for understanding the dynamic progression of pathogenic invasion from the exterior surface through progressively deeper levels of the body to the constitutional core. Each channel stage has characteristic pulse, symptom, and sign patterns, and specific classical formulas precisely matched to those patterns. The Jing Fang classical formulas developed in the Shang Han Lun — Gui Zhi Tang, Ma Huang Tang, Xiao Chai Hu Tang, Si Ni Tang, and over 100 others — represent the most rigorously tested and refined herbal prescriptions in the Chinese medical tradition, each formula a precisely calibrated combination of herbs whose synergistic action produces clinical effects consistently reproducible across two millennia of clinical practice. The Shang Han Lun's pattern-based diagnostic approach — identifying the pattern Zheng rather than the disease entity — is the foundational methodology of all classical Chinese medicine and the principle that makes Chinese medicine's individualized treatment possible.

## Six Channel Framework

- Tai Yang: exterior surface — chills, fever, stiff neck, floating pulse. Gui Zhi Tang or Ma Huang Tang.
- Yang Ming: interior Heat excess — high fever, sweating, thirst, constipation, flooding pulse. Bai Hu Tang.
- Shao Yang: half-exterior half-interior pivot — alternating chills and fever, bitter taste, wiry pulse. Xiao Chai Hu Tang.
- Tai Yin: interior Cold deficiency — diarrhea, abdominal pain, vomiting, deep weak pulse. Li Zhong Wan.
- Shao Yin: constitutional crisis — extreme cold or heat, exhaustion, minute pulse. Si Ni Tang or Huang Lian E Jiao Tang.
- Jue Yin: terminal stage — alternating heat and cold, Wumei Wan.

## Jing Fang Formulas

- Each formula is a precise prescription matched to a specific pattern rather than a disease entity.
- Formula modifications are systematic — adding or subtracting specific herbs for specific symptom variations.
- The Jing Fang tradition treats the formula as a crystallized clinical intelligence reproducible across practitioners.
- Modern Kampo Japanese herbal medicine preserves the Shang Han Lun formula tradition in its most intact form.

## Pattern-Based Medicine

- Zheng pattern identification rather than disease diagnosis is the foundational diagnostic methodology.
- Two patients with the same disease entity may receive different formulas if their patterns differ.
- Two patients with different disease entities may receive the same formula if their patterns are identical.
- This pattern-based individualization is what modern evidence-based medicine struggles to capture in clinical trials.
