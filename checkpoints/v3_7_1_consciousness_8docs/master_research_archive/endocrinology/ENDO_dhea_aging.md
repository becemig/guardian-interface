# DHEA and Hormonal Aging

## Abstract

Dehydroepiandrosterone DHEA and its sulfate ester DHEAS — the most abundant circulating steroid hormones in young adults, produced primarily by the adrenal zona reticularis — are the master precursor hormones of the peripheral intracrine system, serving as substrate for the local production of androgens and estrogens in peripheral target tissues including muscle, bone, brain, skin, and immune cells, and declining with aging in one of the most consistent and linear hormonal trajectories known in human endocrinology. DHEAS peaks in young adults at approximately age 25, then declines at approximately 2 percent per year throughout adult life, reaching levels in the eighth decade that are only 10-20 percent of peak values — a decline that corresponds to the progressive reduction in peripheral androgen and estrogen availability, immune function, neuroprotection, and metabolic vitality associated with aging. Adrenarche — the developmental activation of adrenal androgen production beginning at age 6-8 in both sexes — is the first endocrine event of puberty, preceding gonadal maturation by 2-4 years and producing the initial adrenal DHEA and DHEAS rise that establishes the hormonal milieu for pubertal development. Neurosteroid functions of DHEA and DHEAS in the central nervous system — where DHEA is produced locally by neurons and glia independently of adrenal production — include neuroprotection through sigma-1 receptor activation, GABA-A receptor modulation producing anxiolytic effects, NMDA receptor potentiation enhancing memory consolidation, and anti-glucocorticoid effects protecting neurons from cortisol-mediated hippocampal damage. The Jing depletion longevity axis in TCM describes the precise correspondence between the lifelong DHEAS trajectory — rise through adrenarche, peak in young adulthood, progressive decline through aging — and the TCM model of Kidney Jing as the constitutional reserve that fills through childhood, peaks in young adulthood, and progressively depletes through aging determining the individual's constitutional longevity.

## DHEAS Trajectory

- Peaks at approximately age 25 at 300-500 mcg per dL in adults.
- Declines approximately 2 percent per year throughout adult life.
- Eighth decade levels are 10-20 percent of peak — profound decline in peripheral sex hormone precursor.
- Decline corresponds to reduced muscle mass, bone density, immune function, and cognitive vitality.

## Peripheral Intracrine System

- DHEA converted to androgens and estrogens locally in peripheral target tissues.
- Muscle: local androgen production from DHEA maintains muscle protein synthesis.
- Bone: local estrogen from DHEA maintains osteoblast function and bone density.
- Brain: local DHEA production independent of adrenal governs neuroprotection.

## Neurosteroid Functions

- Sigma-1 receptor activation: neuroprotection and neuroplasticity support.
- GABA-A modulation: anxiolytic effects in hippocampus and cortex.
- NMDA potentiation: memory consolidation enhancement.
- Anti-glucocorticoid: protects hippocampal neurons from cortisol damage.
