# Immunosenescence and Aging Immunity

## Abstract

Immunosenescence and aging immunity — the progressive deterioration of immune function with advancing age producing increased susceptibility to infection, reduced vaccine efficacy, increased cancer incidence, and the paradox of simultaneously reduced adaptive immunity and increased innate inflammatory activity — the latter termed inflammaging — with the aging immune system characterized by thymic involution reducing naive T cell output, clonal expansion of late-differentiated memory T cells filling the peripheral T cell space, reduced B cell diversity and antibody affinity maturation in germinal centers, and chronic low-grade systemic inflammation from senescent cells, persistent CMV infection, and dysregulated innate immune activation. Thymic involution and T cell repertoire contraction — the progressive replacement of the functional thymic cortex and medulla by adipose tissue beginning at puberty and accelerating after age 40, reducing naive T cell output by 95-99 percent by age 70, with the peripheral T cell pool maintained by homeostatic proliferation of existing memory cells that progressively fills the available immunological space, contracting the T cell receptor repertoire diversity and reducing the capacity to mount effective responses to novel antigens including new pathogens and vaccines. Inflammaging chronic low-grade inflammation — Claudio Franceschi's concept of the chronic, sterile, low-grade systemic inflammation that accumulates with aging, characterized by elevated CRP, IL-6, TNF-alpha, and IL-1beta, driven by the senescence-associated secretory phenotype of accumulating senescent cells releasing pro-inflammatory cytokines, the activation of innate immune cells by endogenous damage-associated molecular patterns from mitochondrial DNA and oxidized lipids, gut dysbiosis-driven LPS translocation, and persistent viral infections particularly cytomegalovirus that drives massive clonal expansion of CMV-specific late-differentiated CD8 T cells. Vaccine response aging — the clinically important reduction in vaccine immunogenicity in older adults, with influenza vaccine producing only 17-53 percent efficacy in adults over 65 compared to 70-90 percent in young adults, requiring high-dose and adjuvanted formulations to partially compensate, with the reduced germinal center responses, reduced naive T cell help, and increased regulatory T cell suppression all contributing to impaired vaccine-induced immunity in aging. The Kidney Jing depletion immune aging correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that aging involves the progressive depletion of the Kidney Jing essence stored from birth and gradually consumed over life, with Jing depletion manifesting as reduced vital capacity, reproductive decline, bone and marrow weakness, and reduced resilience — a classical framework corresponding to the immunosenescence of aging in which the bone marrow and thymus fail to maintain the immune cell renewal that depends on constitutional Jing-rooted vitality.

## Thymic Involution

- Timeline: functional thymus begins declining at puberty — 95 percent lost by age 70.
- Naive T cells: output reduced — reduced capacity for novel antigen responses.
- Repertoire: memory T cell clonal expansion fills space — diversity contracts.
- Rejuvenation: IL-7 growth hormone approaches — partial thymic restoration.

## Inflammaging

- Drivers: SASP senescent cells CMV gut dysbiosis mtDNA damage.
- Markers: elevated CRP IL-6 TNF-alpha IL-1b in healthy older adults.
- Consequences: cardiovascular disease neurodegeneration cancer frailty.
- Centenarians: inflammaging without excessive inflammation — controlled balance.

## CMV Acceleration

- Seroprevalence: 50-90 percent adults — lifelong latent infection.
- Immune space: CMV-specific CD8 T cells up to 30 percent of peripheral T cells.
- Repertoire: CMV inflation crowds out naive and other memory T cells.
- Immune risk: CMV seropositivity associated with accelerated immune aging.
