# Adipokines and Adipose Endocrinology

## Abstract

Adipose tissue — previously understood as a passive energy storage depot — is now recognized as the body's largest endocrine organ by mass, secreting dozens of adipokines that regulate appetite, insulin sensitivity, inflammation, reproduction, bone metabolism, and immune function, with the pathological expansion of visceral adipose tissue producing the pro-inflammatory, insulin-resistant adipokine profile that underlies the metabolic syndrome and corresponding in TCM to the accumulation of Dampness and Phlegm that obstructs the Spleen's transformative function. Leptin — the adipokine discovered by Jeffrey Friedman through positional cloning of the mouse obese gene — is the primary satiety signal communicating the body's fat stores to the hypothalamus: secreted proportionally to fat mass, leptin acts on hypothalamic neurons to suppress appetite and increase energy expenditure, with leptin deficiency producing severe obesity and leptin replacement producing dramatic weight loss in leptin-deficient individuals. Leptin resistance — the failure of the hypothalamus to respond to elevated leptin despite high circulating levels, analogous to insulin resistance — is the primary mechanism maintaining obesity once established: high-fat diet consumption, inflammation, and elevated triglycerides impair leptin transport across the blood-brain barrier and leptin receptor signaling in hypothalamic neurons, creating a vicious cycle in which obesity impairs the leptin signaling that would otherwise correct it. Adiponectin — the most abundant adipokine, paradoxically reduced in obesity despite being produced by adipocytes — is the primary anti-inflammatory and insulin-sensitizing adipokine: adiponectin activates AMPK in muscle and liver to promote fatty acid oxidation and glucose uptake, suppresses hepatic glucose production, reduces vascular inflammation, and its reduction in obesity directly contributes to insulin resistance and cardiovascular disease. Brown adipose tissue thermogenesis — the heat production by UCP1-expressing brown adipocytes that dissipates chemical energy as heat rather than ATP — is a major regulator of energy balance, with brown fat activation by cold exposure, sympathetic stimulation, and irisin producing meaningful metabolic effects and representing a therapeutic target for obesity. The Dampness Phlegm accumulation correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding of excessive fat accumulation as the pathological product of Spleen Qi deficiency failing to transform and transport, producing Dampness that congeals into Phlegm — the TCM pathological substance corresponding to the metabolically active visceral adipose tissue that drives systemic inflammation and insulin resistance.

## Leptin System

- Secreted proportionally to fat mass — communicates energy stores to hypothalamus.
- Hypothalamic POMC neurons: activated by leptin to suppress appetite.
- Hypothalamic NPY/AgRP neurons: inhibited by leptin to reduce hunger signaling.
- Leptin resistance: high leptin, impaired signaling — obesity maintenance mechanism.

## Adiponectin

- Most abundant adipokine — paradoxically low in obesity.
- Activates AMPK: promotes fat oxidation and glucose uptake in muscle and liver.
- Anti-inflammatory: suppresses NF-kB and TNF-alpha in macrophages and endothelium.
- Low adiponectin: independent predictor of type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease.

## Brown Adipose Thermogenesis

- UCP1 uncoupling protein: dissipates proton gradient as heat rather than ATP.
- Activated by cold, sympathetic stimulation, irisin, bile acids.
- Adult humans have functional brown fat — measurable by FDG-PET during cold exposure.
- Beige adipocytes: white fat cells that acquire brown fat characteristics through browning.
