# Cannabis and Endocannabinoid System

## Abstract

The endocannabinoid system ECS — discovered through the investigation of cannabis pharmacology — is one of the most widespread and functionally important neuromodulatory systems in the body, present in virtually every tissue and governing fundamental physiological processes including pain modulation, anxiety regulation, appetite, immune function, memory, and the maintenance of physiological homeostasis through retrograde synaptic inhibition. The ECS consists of two primary receptors — CB1 expressed predominantly in the central nervous system and CB2 expressed predominantly in immune cells and peripheral tissues — two primary endogenous ligands — anandamide and 2-arachidonoylglycerol 2-AG — and the enzymatic machinery for their synthesis on demand and rapid degradation. Retrograde inhibition is the fundamental mechanism of ECS function: postsynaptic neurons synthesize endocannabinoids on demand in response to depolarization and release them retrograde across the synapse to activate CB1 receptors on the presynaptic terminal, reducing neurotransmitter release and providing the primary homeostatic feedback mechanism in neural circuits throughout the brain. THC tetrahydrocannabinol — the primary psychoactive phytocannabinoid — is a partial CB1 agonist that mimics anandamide but with greater potency and longer duration, producing analgesia, anxiolysis, appetite stimulation, and altered sensory perception through CB1 activation. CBD cannabidiol — the primary non-psychoactive phytocannabinoid — modulates the ECS through multiple indirect mechanisms including inhibition of anandamide breakdown, antagonism of GPR55 receptors, and 5-HT1A partial agonism, producing anxiolytic, anti-inflammatory, anticonvulsant, and neuroprotective effects without CB1-mediated psychoactivity. Ethan Russo's entourage effect research demonstrated that the full spectrum of phytocannabinoids and terpenes in whole cannabis produces therapeutic effects that exceed those of isolated THC or CBD — the terpenes modulate cannabinoid receptor binding, modify blood-brain barrier permeability, and produce their own therapeutic effects synergistically. Wei Qi tonification through cannabis corresponds to the historical TCM use of cannabis Huo Ma Ren in formulas for pain, spasm, and constitutional deficiency.

## Retrograde Inhibition

- Postsynaptic endocannabinoid synthesis on demand provides feedback inhibition to presynaptic terminals.
- This retrograde signaling is the primary homeostatic mechanism in neural circuits throughout the brain.
- ECS tone governs the excitation-inhibition balance in neural networks.
- Endocannabinoid deficiency syndrome corresponds to loss of this homeostatic regulation.

## CB1 and Pain

- CB1 activation in the periaqueductal gray and spinal dorsal horn inhibits pain signal transmission.
- THC analgesia through CB1 corresponds to the gate control mechanism operating at multiple levels.
- Cannabis pain relief for neuropathic pain is among the most robust findings in clinical cannabis research.
- This corresponds to the TCM analgesic actions of Huo Ma Ren in classical pain formulas.

## Entourage Effect

- Full-spectrum cannabis produces therapeutic effects exceeding isolated THC or CBD.
- Terpenes modulate CB1 binding affinity and modify blood-brain barrier permeability.
- Beta-caryophyllene is a dietary CB2 agonist — an anti-inflammatory terpene activating immune cannabinoid receptors.
- The entourage effect corresponds to the TCM principle that whole herbs produce effects exceeding isolated constituents.
