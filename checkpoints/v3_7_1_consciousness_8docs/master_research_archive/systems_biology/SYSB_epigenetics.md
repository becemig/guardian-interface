# Epigenetics and Systems Regulation

## Abstract

Epigenetics describes heritable changes in gene expression that do not alter the underlying DNA sequence, operating through DNA methylation, histone post-translational modifications, chromatin remodeling, and non-coding RNA regulation. These mechanisms constitute a systems-level regulatory layer above the genome that integrates environmental signals into lasting changes in cellular gene expression programs. The Horvath epigenetic clock uses DNA methylation patterns to measure biological age independently of chronological age. Transgenerational epigenetic inheritance demonstrates that acquired epigenetic marks can be transmitted across generations, providing a mechanistic basis for ancestral trauma and constitutional predisposition concepts in both Western medicine and TCM.

## DNA Methylation

- Mechanism: DNMT enzymes add methyl group to cytosine at CpG dinucleotides
- Effect: methylation at gene promoters generally silences transcription
- Stable: DNA methylation is mitotically heritable - maintained through cell division
- Reversible: TET enzymes oxidize methylcytosine initiating demethylation
- CpG islands: promoter regions enriched in CpG - methylation state controls gene access
- Cancer: global hypomethylation with local hypermethylation of tumor suppressor promoters
- Aging: methylation patterns drift with age in tissue-specific patterns

## Histone Modification

- Histones: octameric protein spools around which DNA is wound
- Modifications: acetylation, methylation, phosphorylation, ubiquitination of histone tails
- H3K27ac: active enhancer mark - open chromatin and active transcription
- H3K27me3: Polycomb repression mark - silenced developmental genes
- HAT enzymes: add acetyl groups - open chromatin
- HDAC enzymes: remove acetyl groups - close chromatin - stress and trauma upregulate HDACs

## Non-Coding RNA Regulation

- miRNA: 22-nucleotide RNAs that bind mRNA 3-prime UTR - degrade or repress translation
- lncRNA: long non-coding RNAs scaffold chromatin complexes and regulate gene expression
- Stress-responsive: miRNA profiles change rapidly with stress, exercise, diet
- Exosomal: miRNAs packaged in exosomes enable cell-to-cell epigenetic communication

## Transgenerational Epigenetic Inheritance

- Meaney rat studies: maternal licking and grooming methylates glucocorticoid receptor gene in pups
- Low-grooming mothers: hypermethylated GR - stress-reactive offspring
- High-grooming mothers: hypomethylated GR - stress-resilient offspring
- Human Holocaust studies: FKBP5 methylation differences in Holocaust survivor offspring
- Dutch Hunger Winter: famine epigenetic marks transmissible to grandchildren
- TCM parallel: Jing as the constitutional substrate carrying ancestral epigenetic inheritance

## Epigenetic Clocks

- Horvath clock: 353 CpG sites predict biological age across all tissue types
- GrimAge: mortality-predictive clock based on DNAm surrogates of plasma proteins
- Acceleration: biological age can exceed chronological age - predicts disease and mortality
- Deceleration: caloric restriction, exercise, sleep reverse clock acceleration
- Reprogramming: Yamanaka factors reset epigenetic clock - partial reprogramming extends lifespan in mice

## Environmental Epigenetics

- Stress: chronic cortisol alters methylation of stress-response genes
- Exercise: aerobic exercise demethylates BDNF promoter in hippocampus
- Nutrition: folate, SAM, choline provide methyl donors for DNA methylation
- Meditation: mindfulness practice alters HDAC2 and inflammatory gene methylation
- TCM: acupuncture and herbal medicine shown to alter methylation of target gene promoters
