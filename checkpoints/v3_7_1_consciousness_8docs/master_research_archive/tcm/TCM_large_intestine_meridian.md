# Large Intestine Meridian: Metal Element, Release, Defensive Qi, and Elimination

## Abstract

The Large Intestine is the Yang organ paired with the Lung in the Metal element system of Traditional Chinese Medicine. While the Lung governs Qi descending and the intake of pure Qi from heaven, the Large Intestine governs the elimination of waste and the release of what is no longer needed. The Metal element at the psychological level governs the capacity for letting go, accepting loss, and finding value and purity. The Large Intestine meridian ascends the arm to the face and head making it a key pathway for treating head and face disorders. LI-4 Hegu is one of the most clinically important and widely researched acupoints governing pain, immune function, and facial disorders.

## Organ Functions

### Elimination and Release

- The Large Intestine receives the dregs of digestion from the Small Intestine and absorbs remaining fluids before eliminating waste.
- Healthy Large Intestine function requires sufficient Lung Qi descending to drive peristalsis.
- Large Intestine Qi stagnation produces constipation corresponding to difficulty releasing and letting go at the psychological level.
- Large Intestine Damp Heat produces diarrhea, urgency, and burning corresponding to inability to complete elimination cleanly.

### Metal Element Psychology

- The Metal element governs the capacity to recognize value, let go of what is spent, and grieve loss appropriately.
- Po the corporeal soul housed in the Lung is the somatic dimension of Metal element consciousness.
- Grief and sadness are the emotions of the Metal element processed through Lung and Large Intestine.
- Unresolved grief congests the Metal element producing both respiratory and eliminatory dysfunction.

### Defensive Qi Support

- The Lung and Large Intestine together govern Wei Qi defensive Qi at the body surface.
- Large Intestine function supports immune barrier integrity in the gut mucosa corresponding to gut-associated lymphoid tissue.
- LI-11 Quchi is the primary Large Intestine point for clearing heat and supporting immune response.
- Seasonal transitions affecting Metal element function produce susceptibility to respiratory and digestive infection.

## Meridian Pathway

### Hand and Arm

- Originates at LI-1 Shangyang on the radial side of the index finger tip.
- Ascends along the radial aspect of the forearm and lateral upper arm.
- LI-4 Hegu in the dorsal hand webspace between thumb and index finger is the Source point.
- LI-5 Yangxi at the wrist radial snuffbox is the Fire point.
- LI-11 Quchi at the lateral elbow crease is the He Sea point.

### Shoulder and Neck

- Crosses the shoulder at LI-15 Jianyu governing shoulder disorders and frozen shoulder.
- Ascends the lateral neck to LI-17 and LI-18 governing throat and thyroid disorders.

### Face and Head

- Crosses to the contralateral side at the philtrum.
- Terminates at LI-20 Yingxiang beside the nostril governing nasal disorders and facial paralysis.

### Key Acupoints

- LI-4 Hegu: Source point, dorsal hand, headache, toothache, facial pain, labor induction, immune support, analgesic master point.
- LI-11 Quchi: He Sea point, lateral elbow, fever, skin disorders, hypertension, immune heat clearing.
- LI-15 Jianyu: Shoulder, frozen shoulder, shoulder pain, upper limb paralysis.
- LI-20 Yingxiang: Beside nostril, nasal congestion, loss of smell, facial paralysis, rhinitis.

## LI-4 Clinical Research

- LI-4 Hegu is among the most researched acupoints in the world with hundreds of clinical trials.
- Needling LI-4 produces measurable analgesia through endorphin release and descending pain modulation.
- LI-4 stimulation activates the default mode network and limbic structures on fMRI imaging.
- LI-4 combined with SP-6 San Yin Jiao is the classical formula for labor induction through uterine stimulation.
- LI-4 is contraindicated in pregnancy due to its strong descending and moving effect on Qi and Blood.

## Four Gates Protocol

- LI-4 bilateral combined with LV-3 Tai Chong bilateral forms the Four Gates protocol.
- Four Gates moves Qi and Blood throughout the entire body releasing stagnation in all organ systems.
- This combination addresses chronic pain, emotional stagnation, stress, and whole-body Qi regulation.
- Four Gates is one of the most commonly used protocols in general acupuncture practice worldwide.
