# Psychoactive TCM Herbs

## Abstract

Several classical TCM herbs in the categories of Shen-calming, orifice-opening, and Qi-moving contain phytochemicals with significant psychoactive properties — producing their traditional effects through direct modulation of neurotransmitter receptors, enzyme inhibition, and neuroactive compound synthesis. Understanding the psychoactive pharmacology of these herbs provides both a scientific validation of their traditional uses and essential safety information for their clinical application. Shi Chang Pu Acorus calamus is the primary TCM orifice-opening herb — used to clear Phlegm misting the Heart and open the sensory orifices. Its active constituent beta-asarone is a phenylpropanoid with MAOI-adjacent activity, serotonin and dopamine receptor modulation, and significant psychoactive effects at high doses including hallucinations. The traditional Chinese preparation uses very small doses corresponding to sub-psychoactive threshold activity. Yuan Zhi Polygala tenuifolia is among the most neurochemically rich TCM herbs — containing tenuifolin, polygalasaponins, and 3,4,5-trimethoxycinnamic acid that collectively inhibit acetylcholinesterase, activate sigma-1 receptors, upregulate BDNF, and modulate serotonin and dopamine systems. Suan Zao Ren Ziziphus jujuba contains jujubosides and saponins that enhance GABA-A receptor sensitivity producing anxiolytic and hypnotic effects comparable to benzodiazepines at therapeutic doses. Da Ma Cannabis sativa was used in classical TCM for pain, insomnia, and altered states — its cannabinoids act on CB1 and CB2 receptors throughout the brain and immune system. Ma Huang Ephedra sinica contains ephedrine and pseudoephedrine — powerful sympathomimetics that stimulate alpha and beta adrenergic receptors producing the TCM Ma Huang effect of dispersing Lung Qi, opening bronchi, and strongly activating Yang.

## Acorus and Beta-Asarone

- Beta-asarone is the primary active constituent of Acorus calamus with MAOI-adjacent and serotonergic activity.
- Sub-threshold doses produce the orifice-opening alerting effects without psychoactive hallucination.
- Combining Acorus with other MAO-inhibiting herbs or tyramine-rich foods creates interaction risk.
- Classical TCM dose is 3-9g dried rhizome — modern preparations use standardized low-beta-asarone varieties.

## Polygala Sigma-1

- Yuan Zhi sigma-1 receptor activation by 3,4,5-trimethoxycinnamic acid corresponds to endogenous DMT's sigma-1 mechanism.
- Acetylcholinesterase inhibition increases synaptic acetylcholine improving memory and cognitive clarity.
- BDNF upregulation through TrkB activation promotes neuroplasticity and neuroprotection.
- Yuan Zhi is the closest TCM herb pharmacologically to the therapeutic mechanism of psychedelic neuroplasticity.

## Cannabis in TCM

- Da Ma seeds Huo Ma Ren are classified as a moistening laxative — low cannabinoid content, safe.
- Historical texts reference cannabis flower and leaf for pain, insomnia, and ritual purposes.
- CB1 receptor distribution in basal ganglia, hippocampus, and cerebellum explains the TCM indications.
- Cannabis Liver Qi moving and Heart Shen calming effects correspond to CB1-mediated anxiolytic action.

## TCM Correspondence

- Orifice-opening herbs acting through cholinergic and serotonergic mechanisms corresponds to clearing Phlegm from the Heart.
- Sigma-1 activation by Yuan Zhi corresponds to the deepest level of Shen cultivation — opening the original spirit.
- Ephedra strongly dispersing Lung Qi corresponds to massive sympathoadrenal activation through adrenergic agonism.
- Cannabis calming Liver Qi and Shen corresponds to CB1-mediated reduction of amygdala and limbic hyperactivation.
