# Jing Essence

## Abstract

Jing essence is the densest and most fundamental of the three treasures governing constitutional vitality, growth, development, reproduction, and the aging process. Pre-Heaven Jing inherited from parents at conception determines the constitutional strength and potential of the individual. Post-Heaven Jing refined from food, breath, and practice replenishes and supports the Pre-Heaven Jing through life. Kidney Jing is the storehouse of both forms providing the material substrate for bone marrow, brain, reproductive essence, and hormonal function. The gradual depletion of Jing through life is the TCM explanation for aging while cultivation practices aim to conserve and replenish Jing to extend healthy vitality.

## Pre-Heaven Jing

- Pre-Heaven Jing is inherited from both parents at conception determining constitutional strength and potential.
- It corresponds to genetic endowment, telomere length, mitochondrial inheritance, and epigenetic programming.
- Pre-Heaven Jing is finite and cannot be increased — only conserved and supported by Post-Heaven Jing.
- Excessive sexual activity, overwork, chronic stress, and insufficient sleep deplete Pre-Heaven Jing prematurely.

## Post-Heaven Jing

- Post-Heaven Jing is refined from food essence, breath, and the products of practice throughout life.
- Strong Spleen and Stomach function producing good Post-Heaven Jing supports the Pre-Heaven Jing reserve.
- Internal arts practice refines Post-Heaven Jing into Qi and Shen through the alchemical transformation sequence.
- Adequate sleep, nourishing food, and moderate activity preserve and replenish Post-Heaven Jing.

## Jing and Aging

- The natural aging process in TCM reflects the gradual depletion of Kidney Jing through the life cycle.
- The Neijing describes Jing depletion in 7-year cycles for women and 8-year cycles for men.
- Kidney Jing decline produces greying hair, hearing loss, dental weakness, reduced fertility, and cognitive decline.
- These correspond to telomere shortening, hormonal decline, reduced neurogenesis, and stem cell exhaustion.

## Jing Cultivation

- Conservation is the primary Jing cultivation strategy — avoiding unnecessary depletion.
- Kidney tonic herbs including Rehmannia, He Shou Wu, and Eucommia support Jing replenishment.
- Regular practice sleep and seasonal eating aligned with Water element principles support Jing conservation.
- Microcosmic orbit meditation recirculates Jing essence as refined Qi preventing its dissipation.
