# TCM Dietary Therapy

## Abstract

TCM dietary therapy Shi Liao applies the same theoretical framework as acupuncture and herbal medicine to food — classifying foods by their five flavors Wu Wei, thermal nature Han Re, and organ affinities to support health and treat pattern imbalances through everyday eating. The five flavors correspond to the five organ networks: sour Suan enters the Liver, bitter Ku enters the Heart, sweet Gan enters the Spleen, pungent Xin enters the Lung, and salty Xian enters the Kidney — each flavor having specific physiological actions in its corresponding organ. Thermal nature ranges from cold through cool, neutral, warm, to hot — foods warm or cool the body independently of their caloric content or preparation temperature. Seasonal eating aligns dietary choices with the energetic qualities of each season — light spring foods supporting Liver ascent, cooling summer foods supporting Heart, late summer foods nourishing Spleen, pungent autumn foods supporting Lung, and warming winter foods supporting Kidney Yang. Paul Pitchford's synthesis of TCM dietary principles with modern nutritional science provides a comprehensive evidence-informed framework for food-based healing. Spleen nourishment is the central concern of TCM dietary therapy — the Spleen as the primary organ of digestive transformation and transportation produces Post-Heaven Qi from food and is easily damaged by cold foods, raw foods, excessive sweet, and irregular eating, making its protection the foundation of all dietary advice.

## Five Flavors

- Sour flavor enters the Liver — astringes, consolidates, prevents dispersion, supports Liver Yin and Blood.
- Bitter flavor enters the Heart — descends, dries Dampness, clears Heat, supports Heart Yin and calms Shen.
- Sweet flavor enters the Spleen — tonifies, moistens, moderates, supports Spleen Qi and nourishes deficiency.
- Pungent flavor enters the Lung — disperses, moves Qi and Blood, opens the surface, supports Lung diffusion.
- Salty flavor enters the Kidney — softens hardness, purges, moistens, supports Kidney Yin and Essence.

## Thermal Nature

- Cold and cool foods clear Heat and are appropriate for Heat patterns, excess Fire, and summer conditions.
- Neutral foods support all constitutions without adding warmth or cold and are the foundation of balanced eating.
- Warm and hot foods dispel Cold, tonify Yang, and are appropriate for Cold patterns, Yang deficiency, and winter.
- Food preparation method affects thermal nature — raw foods are cooling, cooking adds warmth.

## Seasonal Eating

- Spring eating emphasizes light, ascending, slightly sour foods supporting Liver Qi ascent and wood energy.
- Summer eating emphasizes cooling, bitter, and light foods supporting Heart and managing excess Heat.
- Late summer eating emphasizes warm, cooked, sweet foods supporting Spleen transformation and Earth energy.
- Autumn eating emphasizes pungent, moistening foods supporting Lung and preparing for winter.
- Winter eating emphasizes warming, nourishing, salty foods supporting Kidney Yang and conserving essence.

## TCM Correspondence

- Spleen nourishment as the foundation of dietary therapy corresponds to the digestive microbiome as the primary health substrate.
- Five flavor organ affinities correspond to the targeting of specific phytochemicals to specific organ systems.
- Thermal nature of foods corresponds to their metabolic effects on inflammation, circulation, and thermogenesis.
- Seasonal eating aligns with modern chronobiological research on seasonal variation in metabolism and immune function.
