# Environmental Illness and Chemical Sensitivity

## Abstract

Environmental illness and chemical sensitivity — a spectrum of conditions in which individuals develop multisystem symptoms in response to low-level chemical exposures that are tolerated without apparent effect by the general population, with multiple chemical sensitivity representing the most characterized form, encompassing the toxicant-induced loss of tolerance hypothesis proposing a two-stage mechanism of initial high-dose sensitization followed by low-dose triggering, the neural sensitization and limbic kindling models proposing that repeated low-level exposures progressively lower the threshold for symptom generation through sensitization of olfactory-limbic circuits, and the emerging role of mast cell activation as a peripheral mediator of chemical sensitivity reactions. Multiple chemical sensitivity mechanisms — the clinical syndrome characterized by recurrent multisystem symptoms triggered by chemically unrelated substances at low concentrations tolerated by others, with symptoms spanning neurological, respiratory, musculoskeletal, gastrointestinal, and cardiovascular domains, lacking consistent objective biomarkers, and associated with impaired quality of life, chemical avoidance behaviors, and high comorbidity with fibromyalgia, chronic fatigue syndrome, and post-traumatic stress disorder. Toxicant-Induced Loss of Tolerance — Claudia Miller's two-stage mechanistic hypothesis proposing that environmental illness begins with an initiating high-dose or prolonged exposure that disrupts normal tolerance mechanisms through as yet incompletely characterized mechanisms involving the nervous system and immune system, followed by a triggering stage in which previously tolerated low-level exposures to chemically unrelated substances and other stressors elicit multisystem symptoms through the now sensitized biological systems. Limbic kindling in MCS — the neurological model proposing that repeated sub-threshold chemical exposures progressively sensitize olfactory-amygdala-limbic circuits through kindling-like mechanisms analogous to the progressive lowering of seizure threshold with repeated sub-threshold electrical stimulation, with the olfactory system providing direct access to limbic structures and the amygdala amplifying threat responses to chemical cues through associative learning. The Wei Qi boundary failure environmental intrusion correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding of Wei Qi as the defensive boundary that protects the body from external pathogenic influences, with Wei Qi deficiency allowing external pathogens including Wind, Cold, Heat, and Dampness to penetrate and disturb internal organ function — a correspondence with the compromised environmental barrier function and heightened chemical sensitivity of environmental illness.

## TILT Mechanism

- Stage one initiation: high-dose or prolonged exposure disrupts tolerance.
- Stage two triggering: low-dose unrelated exposures trigger multisystem symptoms.
- Tolerance loss: previously tolerated exposures become triggers after initiation.
- Spreading: sensitivity spreads to chemically unrelated substances over time.

## Neural Sensitization

- Olfactory pathway: direct access to amygdala without thalamic gating.
- Limbic sensitization: repeated sub-threshold exposures lower response threshold.
- Kindling analogy: progressive sensitization of olfactory-limbic circuits.
- Amygdala: threat amplification and associative chemical fear conditioning.

## Mast Cell Activation

- Mast cells: distributed at body-environment interfaces lung gut skin brain.
- Chemical triggers: non-IgE activation by fragrances solvents pesticides.
- Mediator release: histamine tryptase prostaglandins cytokines.
- Overlap: MCS fibromyalgia CFS MCAS share mast cell activation features.
