# Pain-Body Schema Integration

## Abstract

Pain-body schema integration — the clinical science of how chronic pain distorts the brain's body schema and how restoring accurate body representation reduces pain — represents one of the most innovative developments in pain medicine, demonstrating that chronic pain is not merely a symptom of tissue damage but a distortion of the brain's body model that can be directly addressed through body schema rehabilitation techniques including graded motor imagery, mirror therapy, and somatic experiencing. Chronic pain body image distortion — documented by Lorimer Moseley and colleagues in complex regional pain syndrome CRPS, phantom limb pain, and chronic low back pain — demonstrates that chronic pain produces measurable distortions in body perception: CRPS patients perceive the affected limb as larger than it is, hotter than it is, and more foreign than the unaffected limb; chronic low back pain patients have difficulty accurately identifying the location of tactile stimuli on their back; and these perceptual distortions correlate with pain severity and respond to treatment. Graded motor imagery GMI — the three-stage rehabilitation approach developed by Moseley progressing from implicit motor imagery through explicit motor imagery to mirror therapy — specifically rehabilitates the cortical motor and body schema representations underlying chronic pain, producing significant pain reduction in CRPS and phantom limb pain through the progressive restoration of accurate cortical body representation without triggering pain-guarded movement. Mirror therapy — the visual feedback technique using a mirror to create the illusion of normal movement in a painful or amputated limb — reduces phantom limb pain, CRPS pain, and post-stroke pain through the visual correction of the incongruence between motor intention and sensory feedback that maintains central sensitization. Somatic experiencing — Peter Levine's body-centered trauma therapy based on the observation that animals in the wild complete incomplete defensive responses through spontaneous shaking and trembling, and that humans develop chronic somatic tension patterns through the suppression of these incomplete responses — addresses the somatic dimension of trauma and chronic pain through guided awareness of body sensations, titrated approach to overwhelming sensations, and facilitation of the spontaneous discharge and completion of incomplete defensive responses. The Qi stagnation somatic pattern resolution correspondence in TCM describes the clinical principle that chronic pain corresponds to Qi and Blood stagnation held in the body as somatic tension patterns, and that resolution requires the direct engagement and movement of these stagnation patterns through needling, bodywork, movement, and breath.

## Chronic Pain Body Distortion

- CRPS: affected limb perceived as larger hotter and more foreign than actual.
- Chronic low back pain: reduced tactile acuity and poor location discrimination on painful back.
- Distortion correlates with pain severity — addressing distortion reduces pain.
- Two-point discrimination training on painful area reduces chronic pain through cortical normalization.

## Graded Motor Imagery

- Stage 1 implicit motor imagery: left-right judgment of body parts — laterality recognition.
- Stage 2 explicit motor imagery: imagining movement without moving.
- Stage 3 mirror therapy: visual feedback of normal movement in affected limb.
- Progressive stages avoid triggering pain-guarded responses while restoring cortical representation.
