# Gait Analysis and Pathology

## Abstract

Human bipedal gait is a complex neuromuscular achievement requiring precise timing of muscle activation, joint stabilization, and center of mass control across a repeating cycle of stance and swing phases. Normal gait minimizes energy expenditure through elastic energy storage and release in tendons and fascia, optimized pelvic rotation, and passive pendulum dynamics. Pathological gait patterns arise from pain, weakness, spasticity, proprioceptive loss, or structural deformity and produce characteristic compensatory deviations that can be identified through observational or instrumented gait analysis. The Trendelenburg sign indicates gluteus medius weakness — one of the most common gait pathologies. TCM meridian pathways map onto the functional muscle chains active during gait, providing a complementary diagnostic framework for movement dysfunction.

## Normal Gait Cycle

- Cycle definition: one complete stride - heel strike to ipsilateral heel strike
- Stance phase: 60 percent of cycle - foot in contact with ground
- Swing phase: 40 percent of cycle - foot in the air
- Double support: both feet on ground - 10 percent at beginning and end of stance
- Cadence: normal 100-120 steps per minute
- Stride length: normal 1.4-1.6 meters
- Walking speed: normal 1.2-1.4 meters per second

## Stance Phase Breakdown

- Initial contact: heel strike - tibialis anterior eccentric control - shock absorption begins
- Loading response: weight acceptance - hip abductors stabilize pelvis - knee flexes 15-20 degrees
- Midstance: single limb support - center of mass rises - hip abductors critical
- Terminal stance: heel rise - gastroc-soleus propulsion - hip extends
- Pre-swing: toe-off preparation - hip flexors initiate swing

## Swing Phase Breakdown

- Initial swing: hip flexion accelerates limb - iliopsoas primary
- Midswing: foot clearance - dorsiflexion essential - tibialis anterior
- Terminal swing: limb deceleration - hamstrings eccentric - preparation for heel strike

## Pelvic Rotation and Energy Conservation

- Pelvic rotation: 4-6 degrees each direction in transverse plane - lengthens stride
- Pelvic tilt: 4-5 degrees lateral drop on swing side - lowers center of mass arc
- Knee flexion: at midstance lowers center of mass - reduces vertical oscillation
- Determinants of gait: six determinants minimize center of mass displacement - energy efficiency
- Elastic storage: Achilles tendon stores and returns 35 percent of energy per stride

## Common Gait Pathologies

- Trendelenburg gait: gluteus medius weakness - pelvis drops to contralateral side at midstance
- Antalgic gait: shortened stance on painful limb - reduces load time
- Steppage gait: foot drop - excessive hip and knee flexion to clear foot during swing
- Scissor gait: hip adductor spasticity - legs cross midline during swing
- Spastic hemiplegia: circumduction during swing - hip hikes to clear stiff leg
- Parkinsonian: shuffling - reduced stride length, cadence, and arm swing - festination

## Hip-Knee-Ankle Coupling

- Ankle dorsiflexion: minimum 10 degrees required for normal midstance progression
- Ankle restriction: forces knee hyperextension or early heel rise - chain compensation
- Knee valgus: tibial internal rotation couples with foot pronation and hip internal rotation
- Hip extension: terminal stance hip extension requires adequate iliopsoas flexibility
- Lumbar compensation: limited hip extension drives lumbar extension and anterior pelvic tilt

## TCM Meridian Gait Correspondences

- Bladder meridian: posterior leg chain - hamstrings, gastroc - swing deceleration and stance propulsion
- Stomach-Spleen: anterior leg chain - tibialis anterior, quadriceps - swing clearance and loading response
- Gallbladder: lateral line - IT band, peroneals - frontal plane stability and lateral weight shift
- Liver-Kidney: medial line - adductors, tibialis posterior - medial arch and knee medial stability
- Yinqiao-Yangqiao: medial and lateral ankle balance - weight-bearing and non-weight-bearing regulation
