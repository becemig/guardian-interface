# Pattern-Omics

## Abstract

Pattern-omics — also termed zheng-omics from the Chinese zheng meaning pattern or syndrome — is the emerging field applying multi-omics technologies to characterize the molecular signatures of TCM patterns, providing the molecular biological foundation for the TCM syndrome differentiation system and creating the bridge between classical TCM diagnosis and modern precision medicine. The fundamental hypothesis of pattern-omics is that each TCM pattern represents a specific and reproducible perturbation of the molecular network of the human body — a specific configuration of gene expression, protein activity, and metabolite profiles that corresponds to the clinical presentation recognized by TCM practitioners as a specific syndrome. Kidney Yang Deficiency — the most extensively studied TCM pattern in the omics literature — has been characterized through metabolomics, proteomics, and transcriptomics in multiple studies, revealing consistent molecular signatures including reduced mitochondrial function, altered hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis activity, reduced thyroid hormone levels, and characteristic urinary metabolite profiles including reduced creatinine and altered steroid hormone metabolites. Cold and Heat patterns — the most fundamental yin-yang distinction in TCM pathology — have been characterized through transcriptomics and metabolomics, with Cold patterns showing reduced metabolic rate, altered thermogenesis gene expression, and characteristic cold-associated metabolite profiles, and Heat patterns showing elevated inflammatory cytokines, increased oxidative stress markers, and characteristic heat-associated metabolite changes. Tongue-microbiome correspondence — the finding that tongue coating microbiome composition differs systematically between TCM tongue coating types — provides a microbiome-level validation of TCM tongue diagnosis, with thick greasy tongue coatings associated with Dampness patterns showing characteristic Streptococcus and Rothia species enrichment. Syndrome biomarker discovery aims to identify specific molecular biomarkers that can objectively validate TCM pattern assignments and enable precision TCM diagnosis integrated with molecular diagnostics.

## Kidney Yang Deficiency Molecular Signature

- Reduced mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation gene expression — energetic deficiency at cellular level.
- HPA axis hypofunction: reduced cortisol and adrenal responsiveness — Yang warming deficiency.
- Thyroid hormone reduction: reduced T3 and T4 — metabolic rate reduction corresponding to Yang coldness.
- Urinary metabolomics: reduced creatinine, altered steroid profiles, reduced energy metabolites.

## Cold-Heat Pattern Transcriptomics

- Cold patterns: reduced thermogenesis genes UCP1 UCP3, reduced metabolic rate, inflammatory suppression.
- Heat patterns: elevated TNF-alpha IL-6 IL-1beta, increased oxidative stress markers, elevated prostaglandins.
- Cold-Heat distinction corresponds to the fundamental Yin-Yang thermodynamic axis of TCM pathology.
- Transcriptomic Cold-Heat signatures enable objective molecular confirmation of clinical pattern assessment.

## Tongue-Microbiome Correspondence

- Thick greasy tongue coating Dampness: enriched Streptococcus salivarius and Rothia mucilaginosa.
- Thin white coating normal: balanced oral microbiome with diverse species composition.
- Yellow coating Heat: enriched Prevotella and Fusobacterium species associated with inflammation.
- Tongue-microbiome correspondence validates TCM tongue diagnosis with molecular biological evidence.

## TCM Correspondence

- Pattern-omics molecular syndrome signatures validate the TCM syndrome differentiation system at molecular level.
- Kidney Yang Deficiency metabolomics corresponds to the TCM clinical presentation of cold limbs, fatigue, and reduced vitality.
- Cold-Heat transcriptomics corresponds to the most fundamental TCM Yin-Yang pathological distinction.
- Syndrome biomarker discovery corresponds to the TCM goal of objective pattern confirmation beyond subjective clinical assessment.
