# Avian Magnetoreception and Quantum Sensing

## Abstract

Avian magnetoreception — the ability of migratory birds and many other animals to sense the Earth's magnetic field and use it for navigation — is the best-studied example of a biological quantum sensing mechanism, with compelling evidence that the magnetic compass of the European robin and other migratory birds is implemented through a quantum mechanical radical pair mechanism in the cryptochrome proteins of the retina. The radical pair mechanism RPM — proposed by Klaus Schulten in 1978 and developed into a specific magnetoreception model by Thorsten Ritz in 2000 — proposes that light absorption by cryptochrome generates a pair of radicals — molecules with unpaired electrons — whose quantum mechanical spin states are entangled and evolve differently in the presence of a magnetic field, producing different chemical products depending on the direction and inclination of the magnetic field. The quantum mechanical spin of the electron — a purely quantum property with no classical analogue — determines whether the radical pair undergoes singlet or triplet recombination, and the Earth's magnetic field shifts the probability distribution between these two outcomes, producing a chemical signal that depends on the orientation of the molecule relative to the magnetic field. The light-dependent and inclination-sensitive properties of avian magnetic compass behavior — it requires light of the right wavelength and responds to magnetic field inclination rather than polarity — correspond precisely to the predictions of the cryptochrome radical pair model. Cryptochrome proteins are found throughout biology including in human retinal cells, raising the question of whether humans retain a vestigial or functional quantum magnetic sense. The broader implication of quantum magnetoreception is that evolution has discovered how to use quantum mechanical properties — spin entanglement, coherent superposition — as functional biological sensors, demonstrating that quantum biology extends beyond energy transfer into the domain of information sensing and navigation.

## Radical Pair Mechanism

- Light absorption by cryptochrome generates electron transfer creating a radical pair.
- The two radicals have entangled quantum spin states — singlet or triplet.
- Singlet and triplet states interconvert at a rate influenced by the local magnetic field.
- The ratio of singlet to triplet recombination products encodes magnetic field direction and inclination.
