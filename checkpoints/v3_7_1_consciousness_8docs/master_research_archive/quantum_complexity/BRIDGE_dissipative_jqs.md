# Dissipative Jing-Qi-Shen Thermodynamics

## Abstract

The dissipative Jing-Qi-Shen thermodynamic model — integrating Prigogine's dissipative structure theory, Schrodinger's negentropy framework, and Mae-Wan Ho's quantum coherence biology with the classical TCM three treasure system — proposes a physically grounded thermodynamic interpretation of the Jing-Qi-Shen framework that preserves its clinical and cultivational utility while situating it within the modern understanding of biological thermodynamics. In this model, Jing — the constitutional essence stored in the Kidney — corresponds to the thermodynamic free energy reserve of the organism: the stored negentropy that represents the organism's capacity to maintain its far-from-equilibrium dissipative structure through time, analogous to the energy stored in the chemical bonds of the organism's most stable macromolecules and to the organizational information encoded in the genome and epigenome. Qi — the dynamic vital force that flows through the meridian system and drives all physiological processes — corresponds to the dissipative flow of energy through the organism's open thermodynamic system: the continuous throughput of negentropy from food, breath, and environmental input that maintains the organism's ordered structure and drives its functional processes. The rate and quality of Qi flow determines the efficiency of the organism's dissipative thermodynamic processes — smooth, abundant Qi flow corresponds to efficient energy dissipation with maximum work output and minimum entropy production, while Qi stagnation corresponds to inefficient dissipation with increased entropy production and reduced functional output. Shen — the luminous awareness and coordinating intelligence of the organism — corresponds to the quantum coherent field that integrates and coordinates the organism's dissipative processes: the macroscopic quantum coherence that Mae-Wan Ho proposes as the organizing principle of the living system, coordinating the activities of trillions of cells through quantum electromagnetic fields rather than through classical chemical signaling alone. Cultivation as entropy management describes the TCM cultivation practices as thermodynamic optimization strategies: practices that increase the efficiency of Qi dissipation, preserve Jing by reducing wasteful entropy production, and enhance the coherence of the Shen field.

## Jing as Negentropy Reserve

- Constitutional Jing corresponds to the organism's stored thermodynamic free energy reserve.
- Pre-heaven Jing from parents corresponds to the inherited organizational information of the genome.
- Post-heaven Jing accumulated through cultivation corresponds to increased negentropy stored in the epigenome.
- Jing depletion through overwork and excess corresponds to accelerated entropy production exhausting the reserve.

## Qi as Dissipative Flow

- Qi flow through meridians corresponds to the dissipative energy throughput maintaining the organism far from equilibrium.
- Smooth abundant Qi flow corresponds to efficient energy dissipation with maximum work and minimum entropy.
- Qi stagnation corresponds to blockage of the dissipative flow increasing local entropy production.
- Qi cultivation practices optimize the efficiency and abundance of the organism's dissipative energy flow.

## Shen as Coherent Field

- Shen corresponds to the macroscopic quantum coherent field coordinating the organism's dissipative processes.
- Quantum coherence of Shen integrates trillions of cells into a unified coordinated living system.
- Shen clarity corresponds to high quantum coherence of the biological field — maximum coordination efficiency.
- Shen disturbance corresponds to decoherence of the biological field — reduced coordination and integration.
