# Gut Microbiome Composition and Diversity

## Abstract

Gut microbiome composition and diversity — the characterization of the complex community of trillions of microorganisms inhabiting the human gastrointestinal tract comprising approximately 38 trillion bacteria plus archaea, fungi, viruses, and protozoa with a collective genome encoding over 150 times more genes than the human genome itself, with the four dominant bacterial phyla being Firmicutes, Bacteroidetes, Actinobacteria, and Proteobacteria, and with the gut microbiome representing a metabolically active organ capable of synthesizing vitamins, short chain fatty acids, neurotransmitter precursors, and secondary bile acids that profoundly influence host physiology. Core microbiome phyla — the four dominant bacterial phyla of the human gut, with Firmicutes including the important genera Lactobacillus, Clostridium, Ruminococcus, and Faecalibacterium constituting 30-50 percent of gut bacteria, Bacteroidetes including Bacteroides and Prevotella constituting 20-40 percent, Actinobacteria including the crucial genus Bifidobacterium constituting 1-10 percent and dominant in infants, and Proteobacteria constituting a minor but clinically significant fraction whose expansion is associated with inflammation and dysbiosis. Firmicutes to Bacteroidetes ratio — the most widely studied compositional metric of the gut microbiome, with a high F/B ratio associated with obesity and metabolic syndrome and proposed as a biomarker of metabolic health, though subsequent research has complicated this simple ratio with the recognition that within-phylum diversity and functional capacity are more clinically relevant than phylum-level ratios alone. Microbiome development from birth — the dramatic colonization of the previously sterile infant gut beginning at birth, with vaginal delivery exposing the infant to maternal Lactobacillus-dominated vaginal microbiome that seeds the infant gut, versus Cesarean delivery exposing the infant to skin and environmental microbiome including Staphylococcus and Clostridium with demonstrated differences in immune development and allergy risk, with breastfeeding providing prebiotic human milk oligosaccharides that selectively feed infant Bifidobacterium and introducing maternal immune factors that guide immune calibration. The Spleen Earth Post-Heaven Qi microbiome correspondence in TCM describes the classical teaching that the Spleen and Stomach govern the transformation and transportation of food Qi — the Post-Heaven Qi extracted from food and drink that sustains postnatal life — a classical framework corresponding to the gut microbiome's central role in the digestion and transformation of dietary substrates into the short chain fatty acids, vitamins, and signaling molecules that constitute the biochemical basis of nutritional Qi.

## Core Microbiome

- Firmicutes: 30-50 percent — Lactobacillus Clostridium Ruminococcus Faecalibacterium.
- Bacteroidetes: 20-40 percent — Bacteroides Prevotella — polysaccharide degradation.
- Actinobacteria: 1-10 percent — Bifidobacterium dominant in infants.
- Proteobacteria: minor fraction — expansion indicates dysbiosis and inflammation.

## Diversity Metrics

- Alpha diversity: species richness and evenness within an individual.
- Beta diversity: compositional difference between individuals.
- Higher alpha diversity: generally associated with better health outcomes.
- Low diversity: associated with IBD obesity antibiotic use C. diff risk.
