# Prefrontal Cortex and Working Memory

## Abstract

Working memory is the capacity to actively maintain and manipulate information over short time periods in the service of ongoing cognitive tasks — the mental workspace of conscious thought. Patricia Goldman-Rakic established the cellular basis of working memory in the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex DLPFC through recording studies in monkeys performing delayed-response tasks — DLPFC neurons fire persistently throughout the delay period between stimulus and response, actively maintaining the representation in the absence of sensory input. This delay-period activity is the neural signature of working memory — information held in mind through sustained neural firing rather than passive storage. Alan Baddeley's influential model proposes that working memory comprises multiple components: the phonological loop for verbal information, the visuospatial sketchpad for spatial and visual information, the episodic buffer integrating information across modalities, and the central executive directing attention and coordinating the slave systems. The central executive maps onto the prefrontal cortex and anterior cingulate system. Working memory capacity is one of the best predictors of fluid intelligence and academic achievement. Dopamine in the DLPFC modulates working memory through an inverted-U relationship — optimal D1 receptor stimulation enhances delay-period activity while too little or too much impairs it. Alpha oscillations 8-12 Hz in posterior cortex during working memory tasks reflect active inhibition of task-irrelevant processing. ADHD involves impaired prefrontal working memory function responding to stimulant medication that increases dopamine and norepinephrine. Shen deliberation in TCM — the Heart-Mind's capacity for sustained focused consideration that holds multiple factors in awareness simultaneously — corresponds to DLPFC working memory as its neurobiological substrate.

## Delay-Period Activity

- DLPFC neurons fire persistently during the delay between stimulus and response maintaining the representation.
- This sustained firing requires recurrent excitation within prefrontal circuits to maintain without sensory input.
- Delay-period activity is disrupted by distractors corresponding to the fragility of working memory under interference.
- The strength of delay-period activity predicts behavioral accuracy at the end of the delay period.

## Baddeley Model

- The phonological loop maintains verbal information through subvocal rehearsal in Broca and Wernicke areas.
- The visuospatial sketchpad maintains visual and spatial information in posterior parietal and occipital cortex.
- The episodic buffer integrates information across modalities and connects working memory to long-term memory.
- The central executive directs attention, switches tasks, and coordinates the slave systems from prefrontal cortex.

## Dopamine Modulation

- Optimal D1 receptor stimulation in DLPFC enhances signal-to-noise ratio improving delay-period activity quality.
- Too little dopamine reduces signal strength producing working memory failures as in depression and fatigue.
- Too much dopamine reduces selectivity producing noise and distractibility as in mania and psychosis.
- Methylphenidate and amphetamine enhance working memory by increasing dopamine availability in DLPFC.
