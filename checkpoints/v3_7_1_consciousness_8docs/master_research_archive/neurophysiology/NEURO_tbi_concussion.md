# TBI and Concussion Neurochemistry

## Abstract

Traumatic brain injury TBI encompasses a spectrum from mild concussion through severe penetrating injury, unified by a two-phase pathophysiology: the primary mechanical injury occurring at the moment of impact and the secondary neurochemical cascade that unfolds over hours to weeks and determines the ultimate extent of damage and recovery. Primary injury involves the direct mechanical disruption of neural tissue through axonal shearing, contusion, laceration, and the immediate depolarization and ionic flux produced by the impact force. The secondary cascade begins within minutes of the primary injury: massive glutamate release produces NMDA receptor-mediated excitotoxicity, potassium efflux triggers cortical spreading depression, calcium influx disrupts mitochondrial function, and the neuroinflammatory cascade activates microglia producing cytokine storm, blood-brain barrier disruption, and ongoing neuronal death over days to weeks. Axonal shearing — the mechanical tearing of white matter axons by rotational forces — is the primary mechanism of diffuse axonal injury DAI in concussion and moderate TBI. Sheared axons develop axonal swellings that accumulate beta-amyloid precursor protein and tau protein, initiating the pathological tau aggregation that leads to chronic traumatic encephalopathy CTE with repeated exposure. Ann McKee's neuropathological research established CTE as a distinct tau pathology — neurofibrillary tangles accumulating at the depths of cortical sulci in a pattern distinct from Alzheimer's disease, caused by repetitive subconcussive impacts over years. The glymphatic system critically clears tau and other proteins during sleep — chronic sleep disruption from TBI directly impairs this clearance, accelerating CTE progression. ## Primary Injury

- Axonal shearing from rotational forces produces diffuse axonal injury — the primary concussion mechanism.
- Contusion produces focal necrosis with immediate neuronal and glial death at the impact site.
- Immediate ionic flux — potassium efflux, sodium and calcium influx — produces the acute concussion syndrome.
- Post-concussion metabolic crisis: increased glucose demand with reduced blood flow produces energy failure.

## Secondary Cascade

- Glutamate excitotoxicity: NMDA overactivation produces calcium influx and neuronal death cascade.
- Neuroinflammation: microglial activation produces IL-1b, TNF-alpha, IL-6 causing ongoing neuronal damage.
- Blood-brain barrier disruption allows peripheral immune cells to enter the brain amplifying inflammation.
- Mitochondrial dysfunction reduces ATP production impairing ion pump function and extending depolarization.

## CTE Pathology

- Repetitive subconcussive impacts trigger tau hyperphosphorylation and neurofibrillary tangle formation.
- CTE tau accumulates at cortical sulci depths in a perivascular pattern distinct from Alzheimer's.
- Progressive CTE produces cognitive decline, mood disorders, impulsivity, and dementia.
- Glymphatic tau clearance during sleep is essential — TBI-disrupted sleep accelerates CTE progression.
