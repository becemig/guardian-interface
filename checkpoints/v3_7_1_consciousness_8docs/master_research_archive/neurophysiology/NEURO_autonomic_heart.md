# Autonomic Cardiac Regulation

## Abstract

Cardiac function is regulated by the autonomic nervous system through the opposing actions of sympathetic and parasympathetic innervation of the sinoatrial node and myocardium. The baroreflex is the primary moment-to-moment blood pressure regulator — arterial baroreceptors detecting pressure changes trigger reciprocal changes in sympathetic and parasympathetic tone to restore setpoint. Respiratory sinus arrhythmia RSA is the normal variation in heart rate with respiration — acceleration during inhalation and deceleration during exhalation — reflecting phasic vagal modulation. Heart rate variability HRV measures the variation in inter-beat intervals and serves as the primary non-invasive index of cardiac autonomic tone and baroreflex sensitivity. HRV coherence describes the state of rhythmic orderliness in heart rate oscillations associated with paced breathing, positive emotion, and contemplative practice. The Heart-Kidney axis in TCM governing the balance between Yang activation and Yin restoration corresponds to the sympatho-vagal balance reflected in HRV coherence.

## Cardiac Autonomic Innervation

- The sinoatrial node pacemaker receives both sympathetic noradrenergic and parasympathetic cholinergic innervation.
- Sympathetic activation increases heart rate, contractility, and conduction velocity through beta-1 receptors.
- Parasympathetic activation decreases heart rate and AV node conduction through M2 muscarinic receptors.
- The balance between these opposing inputs determines heart rate, rhythm, and cardiac output moment-to-moment.

## Baroreflex

- Baroreceptors in the carotid sinus and aortic arch detect arterial pressure and signal the nucleus tractus solitarius.
- Pressure increase triggers parasympathetic activation and sympathetic withdrawal reducing heart rate and vasomotor tone.
- Baroreflex sensitivity measures the change in RR interval per mmHg change in blood pressure.
- High baroreflex sensitivity reflects strong vagal cardiac control and predicts better cardiovascular outcomes.

## HRV Coherence

- HRV coherence describes rhythmic orderliness in heart rate oscillations centered around 0.1Hz.
- Paced breathing at 5 to 6 breaths per minute maximizes RSA amplitude and coherence through baroreflex resonance.
- Coherence states are associated with positive emotion, calm alertness, and improved prefrontal function.
- Regular coherence training improves resting HRV, baroreflex sensitivity, and emotional regulation capacity.
