# Spinal Cord Organization

## Abstract

The spinal cord is the primary conduit for sensory information ascending to the brain and motor commands descending from the brain, as well as the site of local reflex circuits enabling rapid adaptive responses without brain involvement. The gray matter of the spinal cord is organized into dorsal horns receiving sensory input, intermediate zones containing autonomic preganglionic neurons, and ventral horns containing motor neurons. Rexed's laminae provide a cytoarchitectural map of spinal gray matter corresponding to specific sensory and motor functions. Central pattern generators in the spinal cord can produce rhythmic locomotor activity independently of supraspinal drive. The autonomic outflow from thoracolumbar sympathetic and sacral parasympathetic preganglionic neurons governs visceral function throughout the body. Facilitated spinal segments show reduced threshold for neural activation producing referred pain, autonomic changes, and muscle hypertonicity. The Governing Vessel traversing the posterior spinal midline from sacrum to head corresponds to the central integrating axis of spinal cord function.

## Dorsal Horn Organization

- Rexed laminae I and II receive nociceptive input from A-delta and C fibers.
- Laminae III and IV receive mechanoreceptive input from A-beta fibers.
- Lamina V wide dynamic range neurons integrate nociceptive and mechanoreceptive input.
- Laminae VI through VIII contain interneurons mediating local reflex circuits and propriospinal pathways.

## Motor Circuits

- Ventral horn motor neurons are organized into medial and lateral columns corresponding to axial and limb muscles.
- Alpha motor neurons directly innervate extrafusal muscle fibers producing muscle contraction.
- Gamma motor neurons innervate intrafusal spindle fibers adjusting proprioceptive sensitivity.
- Central pattern generators for locomotion are distributed throughout the lumbar spinal cord.

## Autonomic Outflow

- Thoracolumbar sympathetic preganglionic neurons in the intermediolateral cell column govern sympathetic outflow.
- Sacral parasympathetic preganglionic neurons govern pelvic organ parasympathetic innervation.
- Spinal autonomic organization corresponds to the TCM concept of Back Shu points reflecting organ Qi status.
- Facilitated segments with reduced activation threshold correspond to chronic organ dysfunction patterns.
