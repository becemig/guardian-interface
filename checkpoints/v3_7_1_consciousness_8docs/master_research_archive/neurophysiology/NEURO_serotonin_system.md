# Serotonin System

## Abstract

Serotonin 5-hydroxytryptamine is a monoamine neurotransmitter synthesized from tryptophan by tryptophan hydroxylase with approximately 95 percent of the body's serotonin located in the gut rather than the brain. Central serotonin is produced exclusively in the raphe nuclei of the brainstem — a cluster of nuclei along the midline of the pons and medulla projecting to virtually every brain region including the cortex, limbic system, basal ganglia, and spinal cord. Fourteen distinct 5-HT receptor subtypes mediate diverse serotonin effects on mood, cognition, appetite, sleep, pain, and motor function. SSRIs block the serotonin reuptake transporter SERT increasing synaptic serotonin availability and are the first-line pharmacological treatment for depression and anxiety. Gut serotonin — produced by enterochromaffin cells in response to luminal stimuli — governs intestinal motility, secretion, and the gut-brain serotonergic axis. Inflammatory cytokines shift tryptophan metabolism from serotonin toward kynurenine reducing serotonin availability and contributing to the depression-inflammation link. Heart Fire cooling in TCM — the clearing of excess Heart Fire to restore Shen clarity and emotional equanimity — corresponds to the serotonergic modulation of arousal, mood, and emotional regulation.

## Raphe Projections

- Dorsal raphe nucleus projects extensively to the cortex, striatum, and limbic system governing mood and cognition.
- Median raphe projects primarily to the hippocampus and septum governing spatial memory and anxiety.
- Descending raphe-spinal projections provide serotonergic pain modulation at the dorsal horn.
- Raphe neurons fire tonically during waking, slow during NREM sleep, and virtually cease during REM sleep.

## 5-HT Receptor Diversity

- 5-HT1A autoreceptors in the raphe provide negative feedback regulating serotonin neuron firing rate.
- 5-HT2A receptors in the cortex mediate the psychedelic effects of serotonergic hallucinogens.
- 5-HT3 receptors are the only ionotropic serotonin receptors governing nausea and gut motility.
- 5-HT4 receptors in the gut promote intestinal peristalsis and are targets for prokinetic drugs.

## Gut-Brain Serotonin

- Enterochromaffin cells lining the gut produce 95 percent of body serotonin in response to mechanical and chemical stimuli.
- Gut serotonin activates vagal afferents signaling intestinal status to the brainstem.
- Gut microbiome composition influences tryptophan availability and enterochromaffin cell serotonin production.
- The gut-brain serotonergic axis provides a bidirectional communication pathway linking intestinal health and mood.
