# Cognitive Disorders

## Abstract

Cognitive disorders — encompassing Alzheimer disease, vascular dementia, Lewy body dementia, and frontotemporal dementia — represent the most significant and growing challenge in neurological medicine, with Alzheimer alone affecting over 50 million people globally and projected to triple by 2050 as populations age. Alzheimer disease is characterized by the accumulation of amyloid-beta plaques and tau neurofibrillary tangles that disrupt synaptic function, activate neuroinflammatory cascades through microglial NLRP3 inflammasome activation, and produce the progressive synaptic loss that underlies cognitive decline. The amyloid cascade hypothesis proposed by Dennis Selkoe holds that amyloid-beta accumulation is the initiating event triggering tau pathology, neuroinflammation, and synaptic loss — though this remains contested with tau propagation and neuroinflammation increasingly recognized as independent drivers. BDNF brain-derived neurotrophic factor deficiency is a consistent finding in Alzheimer — reduced BDNF impairs the synaptic plasticity, neuronal survival, and cognitive function that BDNF-TrkB signaling supports. Vascular dementia results from cerebrovascular insufficiency — small vessel disease, lacunar infarcts, and white matter hyperintensities — producing the stepwise cognitive decline pattern distinct from Alzheimer's gradual progression. Neuroinflammation as a driver of cognitive decline extends beyond Alzheimer to all dementia types — chronic microglial activation producing TNF-alpha, IL-1beta, and IL-6 creates a neuroinflammatory environment that accelerates synaptic loss and neuronal death. TCM brain-Kidney axis: the classical formulation that the Brain is the Sea of Marrow and the Kidney produces Marrow that fills the Brain corresponds to the modern understanding that constitutional factors governing neuronal maintenance, BDNF production, and cerebrovascular health are rooted in the body's fundamental regenerative capacity that TCM locates in the Kidney.

## Alzheimer Pathophysiology

- Amyloid-beta plaques disrupt synaptic transmission and activate microglial NLRP3 inflammasome.
- Tau neurofibrillary tangles propagate in a stereotyped pattern from entorhinal cortex through hippocampus to neocortex.
- Synaptic loss is the strongest correlate of cognitive decline — more predictive than plaque or tangle burden.
- BDNF deficiency impairs the synaptic maintenance that prevents cognitive decline.

## Neuroinflammation

- Chronic microglial activation produces TNF-alpha, IL-1beta, and IL-6 creating neurotoxic environment.
- NLRP3 inflammasome activation in microglia amplifies the neuroinflammatory cascade.
- Peripheral inflammation crosses the blood-brain barrier amplifying central neuroinflammation.
- Anti-inflammatory interventions — TCM herbs, exercise, sleep — reduce neuroinflammatory burden.
