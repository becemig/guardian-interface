# Dopamine Pathways

## Abstract

Dopamine is a catecholamine neurotransmitter synthesized from tyrosine that mediates reward learning, motivation, motor control, and endocrine regulation through four major anatomical pathways. The mesolimbic pathway from the ventral tegmental area VTA to the nucleus accumbens is the primary reward pathway mediating the motivational salience and wanting associated with reward-related stimuli. The mesocortical pathway from VTA to the prefrontal cortex mediates working memory, cognitive flexibility, and executive function — its disruption contributes to the negative symptoms and cognitive deficits of schizophrenia. The nigrostriatal pathway from the substantia nigra pars compacta to the striatum governs motor control — its degeneration produces Parkinson's disease. The tuberoinfundibular pathway from the hypothalamus to the pituitary inhibits prolactin secretion — its blockade by antipsychotics produces hyperprolactinemia. Wolfram Schultz discovered that dopamine neurons signal reward prediction error — firing when rewards exceed expectations, pausing when rewards are worse than expected, and remaining silent when rewards match predictions exactly. This prediction error signal drives associative learning across all domains. Zhi motivation — the Kidney spirit's capacity for sustained purposeful effort toward meaningful goals — corresponds to the mesolimbic dopamine system as its neurobiological substrate.

## Mesolimbic Pathway

- VTA dopamine neurons project to the nucleus accumbens, amygdala, hippocampus, and olfactory tubercle.
- This pathway mediates the motivational salience of reward-predicting stimuli — the wanting system.
- Phasic dopamine release in the nucleus accumbens signals reward prediction error driving approach behavior.
- Addiction hijacks this pathway through supraphysiological dopamine release producing pathological wanting.

## Mesocortical Pathway

- VTA dopamine projects to the prefrontal cortex mediating working memory and cognitive flexibility.
- Optimal prefrontal dopamine follows an inverted-U function — too little or too much impairs performance.
- Stress-induced mesocortical dopamine depletion contributes to cognitive impairment under chronic stress.
- Antipsychotics blocking mesocortical dopamine contribute to the cognitive side effects of these medications.

## Prediction Error Signal

- Dopamine neurons fire in response to unexpected rewards and reward-predicting stimuli.
- They pause below baseline when expected rewards fail to materialize signaling negative prediction error.
- This bidirectional prediction error signal drives reinforcement learning throughout the brain.
- The prediction error signal corresponds to the moment of surprising insight and discovery in learning contexts.
