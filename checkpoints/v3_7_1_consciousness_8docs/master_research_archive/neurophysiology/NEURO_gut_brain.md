# Gut-Brain Axis

## Abstract

The gut-brain axis is the bidirectional communication network between the gastrointestinal tract and the central nervous system operating through neural, hormonal, and immune pathways. Michael Gershon described the enteric nervous system ENS as the second brain — an autonomous neural network of approximately 500 million neurons lining the gastrointestinal tract capable of independent reflex activity. The vagus nerve provides the primary ascending pathway carrying interoceptive information from gut to brain with approximately 80 percent of vagal fibers being afferent — from gut to brain rather than brain to gut. The gut microbiome produces neurotransmitters, short-chain fatty acids, and immune signals that modulate brain function, mood, and behavior through the gut-brain axis. Approximately 90 percent of the body's serotonin is produced in the gut by enterochromaffin cells. The Spleen-Stomach as the middle Jiao transforming Gu Qi corresponds to the ENS and gut-brain axis as the neural substrate of digestive-mental integration.

## Enteric Nervous System

- The ENS contains approximately 500 million neurons organized in the myenteric and submucosal plexuses.
- It can coordinate gut motility, secretion, and blood flow independently of central nervous system input.
- ENS neurons include sensory neurons, interneurons, and motor neurons forming complete reflex arcs.
- The ENS communicates bidirectionally with the CNS through the vagus nerve and spinal afferents.

## Vagal Afferents

- Approximately 80 percent of vagal fibers are afferent carrying gut sensory information to the brainstem.
- Vagal afferents report on gut distension, chemical composition, and immune status to the nucleus tractus solitarius.
- This afferent information shapes interoceptive awareness, emotional experience, and cognitive function.
- Vagal tone measured by HRV reflects the strength of this bidirectional gut-brain communication.

## Microbiome-Brain Signaling

- Gut bacteria produce GABA, serotonin precursors, BDNF, and short-chain fatty acids affecting brain function.
- Psychobiotics — specific probiotic strains — reduce anxiety and depression through gut-brain axis signaling.
- Intestinal permeability leaky gut allows bacterial products to enter circulation triggering neuroinflammation.
- Dietary fiber fermentation producing butyrate supports gut barrier integrity and reduces neuroinflammation.
