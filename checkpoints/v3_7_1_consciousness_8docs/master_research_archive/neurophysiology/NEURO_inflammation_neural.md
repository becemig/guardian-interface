# Neuroinflammation

## Abstract

Neuroinflammation is inflammation within the brain and spinal cord involving microglial activation, cytokine production, and disruption of the blood-brain barrier. Robert Dantzer's sickness behavior research demonstrated that peripheral immune activation produces a coordinated behavioral syndrome — reduced activity, social withdrawal, anhedonia, cognitive slowing, and hyperalgesia — through cytokine signaling to the brain. This sickness behavior syndrome is mediated by prostaglandins, interleukin-1 beta, TNF-alpha, and IL-6 acting on brain circuits governing motivation, sleep, and pain. Chronic low-grade neuroinflammation is increasingly recognized as a mechanism in depression, Alzheimer's disease, and multiple psychiatric conditions. Kevin Tracey's inflammatory reflex demonstrated that vagal efferents suppress peripheral inflammation through the cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway. Damp-Heat in TCM — the combination of Dampness obstruction and Heat agitation — corresponds to the pathophysiological pattern of chronic neuroinflammation with its characteristic heavy foggy cognition and inflammatory activation.

## Sickness Behavior

- Peripheral immune activation by infection or injury signals the brain through vagal afferents and cytokines.
- The brain responds with a coordinated sickness behavior syndrome serving to redirect resources toward healing.
- Sickness behavior includes fatigue, anorexia, social withdrawal, anhedonia, hyperalgesia, and cognitive slowing.
- In chronic conditions this adaptive acute response becomes maladaptive producing depression-like syndromes.

## Cytokine-Brain Signaling

- IL-1 beta, TNF-alpha, and IL-6 produced by peripheral immune cells signal the brain through multiple pathways.
- Circumventricular organs lacking blood-brain barrier allow direct cytokine sensing in the brain.
- Vagal afferents carry cytokine signals from peripheral immune cells to the nucleus tractus solitarius.
- Brain endothelial cells produce prostaglandins and secondary cytokines mediating CNS effects.

## Vagal Anti-Inflammatory Pathway

- Tracey discovered that vagal efferents suppress peripheral inflammation through the spleen and macrophages.
- Acetylcholine released by vagal terminals activates alpha7 nicotinic receptors on macrophages suppressing cytokine release.
- This cholinergic anti-inflammatory reflex provides the brain with direct control over peripheral inflammation.
- HRV biofeedback and vagal nerve stimulation exploit this pathway to reduce inflammatory disease.
