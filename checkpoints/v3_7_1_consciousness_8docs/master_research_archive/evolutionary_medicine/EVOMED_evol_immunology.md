# Evolutionary Immunology

## Abstract

Evolutionary immunology — the application of evolutionary principles to understanding immune system development, function, and pathology — has produced the hygiene hypothesis and its more sophisticated successor the old friends hypothesis as the primary explanatory framework for the epidemic of autoimmune disease, allergy, and inflammatory conditions in modern populations. The hygiene hypothesis — proposed by David Strachan in 1989 based on the inverse relationship between family size and allergic disease — initially proposed that reduced childhood infection exposure in hygienic modern environments failed to drive the Th1 immune maturation needed to suppress the Th2-dominant allergic response, producing the allergic and autoimmune disease epidemic. Graham Rook's old friends hypothesis — a more nuanced reformulation — proposes that it is not childhood infections but the loss of chronic low-level exposure to the organisms that co-evolved with the human immune system throughout evolutionary history — the helminth parasites, commensal soil bacteria, and microorganisms of the ancestral environment — that disrupts the regulatory T cell networks needed to suppress inappropriate inflammatory responses. Helminth coevolution — the 300,000 year co-evolutionary relationship between humans and intestinal parasites — produced an immune regulatory circuit in which helminths induced strong IL-10 and regulatory T cell responses that suppressed inflammatory reactivity, protecting both the host from inflammatory pathology and the parasites from immune clearance. The removal of helminths from modern populations through sanitation and antiparasitic medicine disrupts this regulatory circuit, producing the inflammatory Th2 and Th17 dominance that underlies allergy, asthma, inflammatory bowel disease, and multiple sclerosis. The Wei Qi ecological calibration in TCM describes the understanding that the Wei Qi — the defensive Qi that circulates on the body's surface and governs immune function — requires appropriate ecological stimulation to develop proper calibration: isolation from natural environmental exposures produces Wei Qi imbalance corresponding to the immune miscalibration of the hygiene hypothesis.

## Hygiene Hypothesis

- Inverse relationship between infection exposure and allergic disease prevalence.
- Th1 Th2 balance: infection drives Th1 maturation suppressing allergic Th2 responses.
- Modern hygiene reduces infection exposure — Th2 dominance produces allergy and asthma.
- Limitation: cannot explain autoimmune Th1 and Th17 diseases also increasing in modern populations.

## Old Friends Hypothesis

- It is not acute infections but chronic low-level old friend organisms that calibrate immunity.
- Old friends: helminths, commensal soil bacteria, Lactobacillus, Mycobacterium — ancestral exposures.
- These organisms induced regulatory T cell networks suppressing inappropriate inflammation.
- Their removal disrupts regulatory immunity producing both allergic and autoimmune disease.

## Helminth Coevolution

- 300,000 year co-evolution produced mutual tolerance: helminths induce IL-10 and Tregs.
- Helminth-induced regulatory T cells suppress Th1 Th2 and Th17 inflammatory responses.
- Helminth therapy trials: inflammatory bowel disease multiple sclerosis allergy improvement.
- Microbiome diversity loss from sanitation and antibiotics produces similar regulatory T cell deficit.
