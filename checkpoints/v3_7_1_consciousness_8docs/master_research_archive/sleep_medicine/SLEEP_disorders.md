# Sleep Disorders

## Abstract

Sleep disorders — encompassing the spectrum of conditions that disrupt sleep initiation, maintenance, architecture, or timing — affect 30-50 percent of adults at some point in their lives and represent one of the most prevalent yet underdiagnosed categories of health conditions, with cascading consequences for cardiovascular, metabolic, immune, cognitive, and psychological function that make their effective treatment a major public health priority. Insomnia — the most common sleep disorder, affecting 10-15 percent of adults with chronic insomnia and 30-35 percent with occasional insomnia — is now understood through the hyperarousal model: chronic insomnia is maintained by elevated physiological, cognitive, and cortical arousal that prevents the normal dissipation of wakefulness at the circadian sleep gate, with the HPA axis hyperactivity, elevated cortisol, increased core body temperature, and high-frequency EEG activity of insomniacs reflecting a state of chronic hyperarousal rather than a sleep deficiency per se. Cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia CBT-I — the gold-standard evidence-based treatment for chronic insomnia, now recommended over pharmacotherapy in clinical guidelines — addresses the perpetuating factors of insomnia through sleep restriction, stimulus control, relaxation training, sleep hygiene, and cognitive restructuring of dysfunctional beliefs about sleep, producing durable improvement that persists after treatment ends unlike pharmacotherapy. Sleep apnea — the repetitive cessation of breathing during sleep producing chronic intermittent hypoxia, sleep fragmentation, and cardiovascular stress — affects 15-30 percent of middle-aged adults and is one of the most significant cardiovascular risk factors, with untreated moderate-to-severe sleep apnea producing a 2-3 fold increase in hypertension, a 2-4 fold increase in cardiovascular disease, and a 2 fold increase in all-cause mortality. Narcolepsy — the rare but mechanistically illuminating sleep disorder characterized by excessive daytime sleepiness, cataplexy, sleep paralysis, and hypnagogic hallucinations — is caused by the autoimmune destruction of the 70,000 orexin-producing neurons in the lateral hypothalamus, establishing orexin-hypocretin as the primary wake-stabilizing neuromodulator whose loss produces the inability to maintain stable wakefulness or stable sleep. TCM sleep disorder pattern differentiation encompasses the primary insomnia patterns: Heart-Kidney disharmony with Heart heat and Kidney deficiency producing inability to fall asleep with palpitations, Heart-Spleen deficiency with insufficient Blood failing to nourish the Shen producing light sleep and vivid dreams, and Liver Qi stagnation with Heat disturbing the Shen producing difficulty falling asleep with irritability.

## Insomnia Hyperarousal Model

- Elevated HPA activity, cortisol, core body temperature, and high-frequency EEG in chronic insomnia.
- Hyperarousal prevents the normal wakefulness dissipation required for sleep onset.
- Perpetuating factors: conditioned arousal to bed, anxiety about sleep, counterproductive sleep behaviors.
- CBT-I addresses perpetuating factors producing durable improvement — superior to pharmacotherapy long-term.

## Sleep Apnea

- Repetitive breathing cessation producing chronic intermittent hypoxia and sleep fragmentation.
- Sympathetic activation with each arousal — cumulative cardiovascular stress across the night.
- 2-3 fold hypertension risk, 2-4 fold cardiovascular disease risk in untreated moderate-severe apnea.
- CPAP treatment reduces cardiovascular risk and improves cognitive function and daytime alertness.

## Narcolepsy and Orexin

- Autoimmune destruction of 70000 lateral hypothalamic orexin neurons.
- Orexin stabilizes wakefulness by activating all major wake-promoting neurotransmitter systems.
- Loss of orexin produces unstable wakefulness with sleep intrusions and unstable sleep with REM intrusions.
- Cataplexy: sudden muscle atonia triggered by strong emotion — REM atonia intruding into wakefulness.
