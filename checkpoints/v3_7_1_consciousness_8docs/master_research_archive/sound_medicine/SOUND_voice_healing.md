# Voice Healing and Toning

## Abstract

Voice healing and toning — the therapeutic application of sustained vocalization, humming, chanting, overtone singing, and mantra practice for physiological and psychological benefit — represents one of the oldest and most universally distributed healing practices in human culture, with modern neuroscience and physiology revealing precise mechanisms by which vocal practice produces measurable therapeutic effects on the autonomic nervous system, nitric oxide production, HRV, pain, and brain state. Vagal nerve stimulation through vocalization operates through two primary pathways: the laryngeal branch of the vagus nerve is directly stimulated by the vibration of vocal fold oscillation during sustained vocalization, producing the same parasympathetic cardiac effects as transcutaneous vagal nerve stimulation devices; and the extended exhalation required for sustained vocalization activates the respiratory vagal mechanism of HRV increase through prolonged exhalation-phase vagal tone. Humming — the simplest form of vocal toning, producing a sustained nasal resonance on exhalation — dramatically increases nasal nitric oxide production through the vibration of the nasal sinuses and paranasal sinus epithelium: Swedish research demonstrated a 15-fold increase in nasal nitric oxide concentration during humming compared to quiet nasal exhalation, producing vasodilation, antimicrobial effects, and respiratory epithelial protection. Overtone singing — the vocal technique practiced in Mongolian, Tibetan, and Tuvan traditions in which a single vocalist produces multiple simultaneous pitches through precise vocal tract shaping — produces extraordinary acoustic environments of stacked harmonic overtones that create complex standing wave patterns in the body of the singer and listener, with Tibetan Buddhist monasteries using overtone chanting as a core contemplative and healing practice. Bija mantras — the Sanskrit seed syllables each corresponding to a specific chakra and physiological system — produce characteristic vibration patterns in specific body regions through their phonemic structure: AUM produces cranial and thoracic resonance through the open A pharyngeal resonance, the nasal M resonance in the skull, and the sustained U mid-body resonance, while the bija mantras LAM VAM RAM YAM HAM OM produce regional resonance in the perineum sacrum solar plexus heart throat and crown respectively. The Lung-Heart voice healing axis in TCM describes the classical understanding that the Lung governs the voice and that the quality, strength, and resonance of the voice reflects the constitutional state of the Lung Qi and Heart Shen.
