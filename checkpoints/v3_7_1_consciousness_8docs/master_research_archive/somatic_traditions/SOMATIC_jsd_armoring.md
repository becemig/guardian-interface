# Jin Shin Do Armoring

## Abstract

Wilhelm Reich's foundational insight that chronic emotional suppression produces corresponding muscular tension organized in horizontal body segments — and that releasing the muscular armor releases the suppressed emotion — forms the somatic psychology foundation that Jin Shin Do integrates with TCM theory. Reich identified seven primary armoring segments: ocular, oral, cervical, thoracic, diaphragmatic, abdominal, and pelvic — each holding characteristic emotional patterns expressed as chronic muscular tension and restricted movement in that body region. JSD systematically maps each Reich segment to corresponding TCM organ networks and their associated emotional holding patterns — creating a unified framework that connects the Western body-psychotherapy tradition with the Eastern meridian and organ system tradition. The ocular segment governs the eyes, forehead, and back of skull — corresponding to the TCM Liver governing the eyes, with fear and paranoia as the primary ocular segment emotional holding. The thoracic segment governs the chest, upper back, and arms — corresponding to the TCM Heart and Lung organ networks, with grief, sadness, longing, and heartbreak as primary thoracic segment holdings. The diaphragmatic segment governs the diaphragm, solar plexus, and lower costal area — corresponding to the TCM Liver governing the free flow of Qi, with frustration, anger, resentment, and suppressed expression as primary holdings. The abdominal segment governs the abdominal muscles and lower back — corresponding to TCM Spleen and Stomach, with anxiety, worry, and unprocessed nurturance issues as primary holdings. The pelvic segment governs the pelvis, hips, and legs — corresponding to TCM Kidney and lower Dan Tian, with sexual repression, fear, and disconnection from life force as primary holdings. Release sequencing in JSD typically begins with the upper segments and moves downward, following the natural downward flow of Qi and allowing each segment's release to create space for deeper segments.

## Seven Segments and TCM Mapping

- Ocular: eyes, forehead, occiput — Liver Hun, fear and paranoia, GB-1 and BL-1 gateway points.
- Oral: jaw, mouth, throat — Stomach and Large Intestine, shame and suppressed expression, ST-6 and LI-18.
- Cervical: neck and throat — Lung and Heart, grief and vulnerability, LU-1 and HT-1 gateway points.
- Thoracic: chest and upper back — Heart and Lung, heartbreak and sadness, BL-15 and BL-13 back-Shu.
- Diaphragmatic: diaphragm and solar plexus — Liver, frustration and anger, LV-14 and GB-24 alarm points.
- Abdominal: abdomen and lumbar — Spleen and Stomach, anxiety and worry, SP-16 and CV-12 alarm points.
- Pelvic: pelvis and legs — Kidney and lower Dan Tian, fear and life force suppression, KI-11 and CV-4.

## Release Sequencing

- Top-down sequencing follows the natural downward flow of Qi from upper to lower body.
- Releasing upper segments creates energetic space that allows lower segment patterns to surface safely.
- Ocular and cervical release often precedes diaphragmatic release — freeing the upper expression before the deeper holding.
- Complete pelvic release typically requires thorough work through all upper segments first.

## Somatic Trauma Patterns

- Traumatic experience produces characteristic armoring patterns that JSD maps to specific segment combinations.
- Shock trauma often produces ocular and cervical armoring as the freeze response locks in the upper segments.
- Developmental trauma produces thoracic and diaphragmatic armoring as the heart closes and expression suppresses.
- Neglect and abandonment produce abdominal and pelvic armoring as the nurturance and grounding connection severs.

## TCM Correspondence

- Each armor segment corresponding to specific organ networks connects somatic trauma directly to TCM pattern diagnosis.
- Releasing thoracic armor corresponds to TCM treatment of Heart and Lung patterns of grief and closed Shen.
- Diaphragmatic release corresponds to TCM Liver Qi stagnation treatment restoring free flow of expression.
- Complete armor dissolution from head to pelvis corresponds to the TCM cultivation of harmonious Qi flow through all organ networks.
