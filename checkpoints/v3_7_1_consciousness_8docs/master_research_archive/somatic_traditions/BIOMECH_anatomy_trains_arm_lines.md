# Anatomy Trains Arm Lines: Deep and Superficial Anterior and Posterior Arm Myofascial Chains

## Abstract

The four Arm Lines of Anatomy Trains describe the myofascial continuities linking the trunk to the hand through the upper extremity. Unlike the trunk lines which run longitudinally, the Arm Lines radiate from the axial skeleton through the shoulder girdle and down each arm creating four distinct myofascial pathways. These four pathways correspond closely to the six arm meridians of Traditional Chinese Medicine grouped into Yin anterior and Yang posterior pairs. The Arm Lines complete the Anatomy Trains myofascial body map providing a biomechanical framework for understanding upper extremity force transmission, shoulder dysfunction, and arm meridian treatment.

## Deep Front Arm Line

### Pathway

- Originates from the minor pectoralis and anterior ribcage fascia.
- Continues through the biceps brachii and anterior arm.
- Crosses the antecubital fossa into the anterior forearm flexors.
- Terminates at the thenar eminence and radial thumb side of the hand.

### Function

- Governs fine motor control and precision grip through the radial forearm flexors.
- Deep Front Arm Line tension produces medial epicondylitis and carpal tunnel syndrome patterns.
- Shortening draws the shoulder into internal rotation and the arm into flexion.

### TCM Correspondence

- Corresponds to the Lung meridian traversing the anterior lateral arm to the thumb.
- Lung meridian Wei Qi defensive function is expressed biomechanically through the grasping and protective arm postures of the Deep Front Arm Line.

## Superficial Front Arm Line

### Pathway

- Originates from the pectoralis major and latissimus dorsi at the anterior axilla.
- Continues through the biceps brachii and brachialis.
- Crosses into the medial forearm flexors.
- Terminates at the hypothenar eminence and ulnar little finger side of the hand.

### Function

- Governs power grip and medial forearm flexion strength.
- Superficial Front Arm Line tension produces medial elbow and wrist flexor overload patterns.
- Shortening produces the forward shoulder and rounded posture of the upper body.

### TCM Correspondence

- Corresponds to the Heart and Pericardium meridians traversing the anterior medial arm.
- Heart meridian Shen expression through touch and gesture is mediated biomechanically through the Superficial Front Arm Line.

## Deep Back Arm Line

### Pathway

- Originates from the rhomboids and medial scapular border.
- Continues through the infraspinatus and posterior rotator cuff.
- Crosses the posterior elbow through the triceps.
- Terminates at the radial posterior forearm extensors to the index finger.

### Function

- Governs scapular retraction, external rotation, and posterior shoulder stability.
- Deep Back Arm Line weakness produces the rounded shoulder pattern and posterior shoulder instability.
- Strengthening this line restores scapular position and posterior rotator cuff function.

### TCM Correspondence

- Corresponds to the Large Intestine meridian traversing the posterior lateral arm to the index finger.
- Large Intestine Metal element release function is expressed through the extension and retraction movements of the Deep Back Arm Line.

## Superficial Back Arm Line

### Pathway

- Originates from the trapezius and posterior neck fascia.
- Continues through the deltoid and posterior shoulder.
- Crosses the posterior upper arm through the triceps.
- Terminates at the ulnar posterior forearm extensors to the little finger.

### Function

- Governs shoulder abduction, posterior arm extension, and wrist extension strength.
- Superficial Back Arm Line tension produces posterior shoulder and upper trapezius overload.
- Shortening elevates the shoulder girdle producing the chronic upper trapezius tension pattern.

### TCM Correspondence

- Corresponds to the Small Intestine and Triple Burner meridians traversing the posterior medial arm.
- Triple Burner Yang Wei governance of the lateral surfaces is expressed through the Superficial Back Arm Line posterior lateral arm pathway.

## Integrated Shoulder Function

- All four Arm Lines converge at the shoulder girdle creating the force couple balance governing glenohumeral stability.
- Balanced tone across all four Arm Lines maintains the humeral head centered in the glenoid fossa.
- The six arm meridians in TCM similarly converge at the shoulder governing the full circular range of arm movement.
- Shoulder disorders in both systems reflect imbalanced tension across the Arm Line and arm meridian pairs.

## Practice Relevance

- Tai Chi arm movements cycle through all four Arm Lines in coordinated spiraling sequences.
- Silk reeling Chan Si Jin exercises specifically train balanced Arm Line coordination from trunk to fingertip.
- Ward-off Peng Jin engages Deep Back Arm Line posterior stabilization against Deep Front Arm Line anterior load.
- Push hands practice develops sensitivity across all four Arm Lines simultaneously.
