# Transcranial Direct Current Stimulation and Non-Invasive Brain Stimulation

## Abstract

Transcranial direct current stimulation tDCS and non-invasive brain stimulation — the application of weak direct currents through scalp electrodes to modulate cortical excitability and produce lasting changes in neuroplasticity through NMDA receptor-dependent and BDNF-mediated mechanisms — represents the most extensively studied non-invasive neuromodulation technique, with a growing evidence base for clinical applications in depression, chronic pain, stroke rehabilitation, and cognitive enhancement, and with interesting conceptual correspondence to the TCM tradition of governing vessel and crown point stimulation for mental clarity and constitutional activation. tDCS mechanisms — the subthreshold membrane polarization produced by transcranial direct current that does not directly trigger action potentials but shifts the resting membrane potential of cortical neurons, with anodal stimulation producing subthreshold depolarization increasing spontaneous firing rate and synaptic plasticity and cathodal stimulation producing hyperpolarization reducing excitability — produces effects that outlast the stimulation period by up to 90 minutes through NMDA receptor-dependent mechanisms corresponding to long-term potentiation and depression. Anodal-cathodal polarity effects — the fundamental principle that anodal tDCS over a cortical area increases the excitability and neuroplasticity of that area while cathodal tDCS decreases it, enabling targeted enhancement or suppression of specific brain regions — provides the theoretical basis for tDCS clinical protocols: anodal tDCS over left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex for depression, anodal over motor cortex for stroke rehabilitation, and bifrontal protocols for working memory enhancement. Transcranial alternating current stimulation tACS — the application of sinusoidal alternating current at specific frequencies to entrain endogenous brain oscillations to the stimulation frequency — has demonstrated gamma-band 40 Hz entrainment effects that reduce amyloid-beta and tau pathology in Alzheimer's mouse models, with the GENUS gamma entrainment using sensory stimuli and tACS showing promising results in early human Alzheimer's trials. The Governing Vessel crown point stimulation correspondence in TCM describes the classical practice of stimulating Baihui GV-20 the crown point of the Governing Vessel for mental clarity, depression, and constitutional Yang activation — a practice that corresponds to the prefrontal and vertex cortex stimulation protocols of tDCS for depression and cognitive enhancement.

## tDCS Mechanisms

- Weak direct current 1-2 mA through scalp electrodes — subthreshold membrane polarization.
- Anodal: subthreshold depolarization increases firing rate and NMDA-dependent plasticity.
- Cathodal: hyperpolarization reduces excitability and synaptic strength.
- After-effects: NMDA-dependent LTP-like changes lasting 30-90 minutes post-stimulation.

## BDNF and Plasticity

- Anodal tDCS increases BDNF release in stimulated cortex.
- BDNF activates TrkB signaling promoting dendritic spine growth and synaptic strengthening.
- Val66Met BDNF polymorphism: reduced activity-dependent BDNF release — reduced tDCS response.
- tDCS-exercise combination: synergistic BDNF increase for enhanced neuroplasticity.

## Clinical Applications

- Depression: anodal left DLPFC cathodal right DLPFC — multiple RCTs showing efficacy.
- Stroke rehabilitation: anodal motor cortex of affected hemisphere — enhanced motor learning.
- Chronic pain: cathodal motor cortex or anodal DLPFC — multiple pain applications.
- Cognitive enhancement: working memory attention executive function — mixed evidence.
