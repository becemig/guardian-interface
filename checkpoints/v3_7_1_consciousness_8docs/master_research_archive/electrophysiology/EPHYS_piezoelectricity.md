# Piezoelectricity in Bone and Fascia

## Abstract

Piezoelectricity in bone and fascia — the generation of electrical potentials by the mechanical deformation of collagen-containing biological tissues, first demonstrated in bone by Fukada and Yasuda in 1957 and subsequently identified in tendon, fascia, cartilage, and other collagen-rich tissues — provides a fundamental mechanism by which mechanical forces are transduced into electrical signals that regulate bone remodeling, tissue repair, and cellular behavior throughout the musculoskeletal system, corresponding to the TCM Jin sinew network as both a mechanical and an electrical transmission system. Collagen piezoelectric properties — the piezoelectric behavior of the collagen fibril arising from the asymmetric molecular structure of the triple helix and the ordered arrangement of collagen molecules in the fibril, producing electrical polarization when mechanically deformed — makes collagen the most widespread biological piezoelectric material, present in bone, tendon, fascia, cartilage, blood vessels, skin, and cornea and generating electrical signals proportional to mechanical strain in each of these tissues. Bone mechanotransduction through piezoelectricity — the mechanism by which mechanical loading of bone generates electrical potentials through the piezoelectric effect of mineralized collagen and through streaming potentials generated by fluid flow through bone canaliculi, activating osteoblasts and inhibiting osteoclasts to produce the bone remodeling response described by Wolff's law — provides the molecular basis for understanding why mechanical loading is essential for bone maintenance and why immobilization produces rapid bone loss. Fascial electrical signaling — the emerging evidence that fascia, as the most extensive collagen network in the body spanning all organs and tissues, generates piezoelectric signals during movement that propagate through the fascial network as a whole-body mechanosensory and electrical signaling system — provides a biophysical basis for understanding the myofascial force transmission and the proprioceptive function of the fascial network as an electrical as well as mechanical system. The Jin sinew network electrical transmission correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding of the Jin channels as the functional movement and force transmission network of the body — a network that corresponds not only to the myofascial anatomy of muscle-tendon-fascial chains but to the piezoelectric electrical transmission network generated by mechanical loading of the collagen matrix of these structures.

## Collagen Piezoelectricity

- Triple helix asymmetry: produces molecular dipoles that align under mechanical stress.
- Fibril organization: ordered arrangement amplifies piezoelectric effect at tissue level.
- Bone: mineralized collagen matrix generates 10-100 mV per strain unit under loading.
- Tendon and fascia: significant piezoelectric potentials during physiological loading.

## Bone Mechanotransduction

- Piezoelectric potentials: compression produces electronegativity on compressed surface.
- Streaming potentials: fluid flow through bone canaliculi generates additional electrical signals.
- Osteoblast activation: electronegative surfaces attract calcium and activate osteoblast deposition.
- Wolff law mechanism: bone remodels along lines of mechanical stress through piezoelectric signaling.

## Fascial Electrical Network

- Movement generates piezoelectric signals throughout fascial matrix.
- Fascial electrical signals may propagate through the connected fascial network.
