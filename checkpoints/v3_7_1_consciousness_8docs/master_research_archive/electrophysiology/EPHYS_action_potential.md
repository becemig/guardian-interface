# Action Potential Biophysics

## Abstract

Action potential biophysics — the mathematical and molecular understanding of how neurons generate and propagate electrical signals through the coordinated opening and closing of voltage-gated ion channels governing the membrane potential, first described quantitatively by Alan Hodgkin and Andrew Huxley in their landmark 1952 series of papers that earned them the Nobel Prize — provides the foundational understanding of neural communication that underlies all neuroscience and corresponds at a systems level to the TCM understanding of the Shen's capacity for clarity and rapid response as governed by the quality of the neural signal substrate. The resting membrane potential — the approximately -70 mV inside-negative electrical potential maintained across the neuronal cell membrane by the differential permeability of the resting membrane to potassium through leak channels and by the sodium-potassium ATPase that establishes the electrochemical gradients — represents the electrical readiness state of the neuron, with the magnitude and stability of the resting potential determining the neuron's excitability and the quality of its signaling. Voltage-gated sodium channels — the molecular machines responsible for the rapid depolarization phase of the action potential, opening within a fraction of a millisecond in response to membrane depolarization to allow sodium influx driven by both electrical and chemical gradients and then rapidly inactivating — are the primary determinants of action potential threshold, amplitude, and rise time, with sodium channel density and kinetics varying across neuron types to produce the diverse firing patterns of different neural circuits. Myelination and conduction velocity — the dramatic increase in action potential conduction speed produced by the myelin sheath of oligodendrocytes in the CNS and Schwann cells in the PNS, which insulates internodal membrane and confines active ion current to the nodes of Ranvier producing saltatory conduction — enables the high-speed neural communication required for real-time sensorimotor integration, with demyelinating diseases such as multiple sclerosis producing the slowed and disrupted neural conduction underlying their neurological symptoms. The Shen-clarity neural signal correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that the clarity and brightness of the Shen — the consciousness and mental clarity governed by the Heart — depends on the quality of the neural substrate, with Heart Blood and Yin nourishing the neural tissue corresponding to the membrane composition and myelination quality that determines action potential fidelity.

## Resting Membrane Potential

- Inside negative 70 mV: maintained by K+ leak channels and Na-K ATPase.
- Nernst potential: equilibrium potential for each ion determined by concentration gradient.
- Goldman equation: weighted average of ion permeabilities determines resting potential.
- Excitability: determined by distance between resting potential and action potential threshold.

## Action Potential Phases

- Depolarization: voltage-gated Na+ channels open — rapid Na+ influx to positive 30 mV.
- Repolarization: Na+ channels inactivate K+ channels open — K+ efflux restores negative potential.
- Hyperpolarization: K+ channels slow to close — brief undershoot below resting potential.
- Refractory period: absolute relative — determines maximum firing frequency.

## Saltatory Conduction

- Myelin sheath: electrical insulation of internodal membrane — reduces capacitance.
- Nodes of Ranvier: exposed membrane with high Na+ channel density — active conduction sites.
- Saltatory: action potential jumps from node to node — 10-100x faster than unmyelinated.
- Conduction velocity: 1-2 m per s unmyelinated to 70-120 m per s large myelinated fibers.
