# Gait Analysis

## Abstract

Human bipedal walking is one of the most sophisticated biomechanical achievements in the animal kingdom — a controlled fall forward that exploits the inverted pendulum mechanics of the stance limb to convert gravitational potential energy into kinetic energy with remarkable efficiency. The normal gait cycle consists of two phases: stance phase comprising approximately 60% of the cycle during which the foot is in contact with the ground, and swing phase comprising approximately 40% during which the foot is airborne. Within stance phase, double support — when both feet are simultaneously in contact with the ground — occurs twice per cycle and represents the transition between weight-bearing limbs. Ground reaction forces during walking reach approximately 1.2 times body weight at heel strike and 1.1 times body weight at push-off, with the vertical GRF tracing a characteristic double-humped curve that reflects the inverted pendulum mechanics of mid-stance. The six gait determinants described by Inman — pelvic rotation, pelvic tilt, knee flexion in stance, foot and ankle mechanisms, knee and hip interaction, and lateral displacement of the pelvis — collectively minimize the vertical displacement of the body's center of mass, reducing the energy cost of locomotion. Gait deviations from normal patterns — Trendelenburg lurch from weak hip abductors, steppage gait from foot drop, antalgic gait from pain — are diagnostic indicators of specific muscle weakness, joint dysfunction, or neurological impairment. TCM channel walking patterns observe the quality and character of a patient's gait as a diagnostic indicator of organ network health — the tentative shuffling of Kidney deficiency, the lateral lurching of Gallbladder Qi deficiency, the forward-leaning effortful walk of Spleen Qi deficiency.

## Gait Cycle

- Heel strike initiates stance phase — the foot meets the ground with controlled plantar flexion.
- Mid-stance is the single-limb support phase — the inverted pendulum converting potential to kinetic energy.
- Terminal stance and push-off generate the propulsive force for the next step through plantarflexion.
- Swing phase clears the foot and positions it for the next heel strike through hip flexion and knee flexion.

## Ground Reaction Forces

- Vertical GRF shows double-humped curve — first peak at loading response, valley at mid-stance, second peak at push-off.
- Anterior-posterior GRF shows braking force at loading and propulsive force at push-off.
- Medial-lateral GRF reflects the balance adjustments maintaining lateral stability throughout the cycle.
- Deviations from normal GRF patterns indicate specific biomechanical dysfunction and injury risk.

## Gait Deviations

- Trendelenburg: contralateral pelvis drops due to weak ipsilateral hip abductors — Gallbladder channel weakness.
- Steppage: exaggerated hip and knee flexion to clear foot drop from anterior tibialis weakness — Stomach channel.
- Antalgic: shortened stance on painful limb with rapid weight transfer — Qi and Blood stagnation pattern.
- Crouch: excessive knee flexion throughout stance from hip flexor contracture — Kidney-Spleen pattern.
