# Peripheral Circadian Clocks

## Abstract

Peripheral circadian clocks — the autonomous circadian oscillators present in virtually every cell and tissue of the body outside the SCN, including the liver, gut, adipose tissue, skeletal muscle, heart, lung, kidney, and immune cells, all running the same molecular clock machinery as the SCN but entrained by different zeitgebers including feeding time, temperature, and tissue-specific hormonal signals rather than primarily by light — govern the temporal organization of tissue-specific physiology and metabolism in ways that, when desynchronized from the SCN or from each other, produce the metabolic dysfunction, immune dysregulation, and disease risk of circadian misalignment. Liver clock metabolic timing — the hepatic circadian clock that governs the rhythmic expression of thousands of metabolic genes including those controlling glucose production, lipid metabolism, cholesterol synthesis, bile acid production, and detoxification enzyme expression, with clock gene disruption producing metabolic syndrome, fatty liver, and dysregulated glucose homeostasis, and with the liver clock entrained primarily by feeding time rather than light, making meal timing a critical determinant of hepatic metabolic rhythmicity. Gut clock digestive rhythms — the circadian clocks of the gastrointestinal tract governing the rhythmicity of intestinal permeability, nutrient absorption, gut motility, digestive enzyme secretion, mucus production, and the circadian oscillations of the gut microbiome composition, with mistimed eating disrupting gut clock function and contributing to intestinal permeability, dysbiosis, and the systemic inflammation of circadian misalignment. Immune circadian gating — the profound circadian regulation of immune function including the rhythmic trafficking of immune cells between blood and lymphoid tissues, the circadian peaks of pro-inflammatory cytokine production in the early morning hours, the circadian gating of vaccine responses and allergic reactions, and the circadian variation in susceptibility to infection and autoimmune flares — producing the clinical observations of morning symptom peaks in rheumatoid arthritis, asthma, and cardiovascular events. The Five Zang temporal resonance correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that each of the five organ systems has its own temporal rhythm governed by its Five Element resonances and its Zi Wu Liu Zhu two-hour peak period — a systems model of organ temporal coordination that corresponds to the modern understanding of peripheral circadian clocks coordinating tissue-specific physiological timing.

## Liver Clock

- Governs: glucose production lipid metabolism cholesterol bile acids detoxification.
- Primary zeitgeber: feeding time — liver clock follows meal timing not light.
- BMAL1 knockout liver: impaired glucose tolerance elevated lipids.
- REV-ERB alpha: links clock to lipid and bile acid metabolism — nuclear receptor.

## Gut Clock

- Intestinal permeability: circadian rhythm — highest in active phase lowest in rest.
- Nutrient absorption: glucose and lipid transporters rhythmically expressed.
- Gut microbiome: circadian oscillations in composition — feeding time dependent.
- Mistimed eating: disrupts gut clock — increases permeability and dysbiosis.

## Immune Circadian Gating

- NK cells T cells: peak blood levels at night — tissue trafficking circadian.
- TNF-alpha IL-6: peak production early morning — explains morning symptom peaks.
- Vaccine timing: morning vaccination produces higher antibody response.
- Infection susceptibility: circadian gating of innate immune activation.
