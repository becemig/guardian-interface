# Quantum Tunneling in Enzyme Catalysis

## Abstract

Quantum tunneling in enzyme catalysis — the quantum mechanical phenomenon by which hydrogen atoms protons and electrons pass through rather than over the energy barriers of chemical reactions — has emerged as a widespread and functionally important mechanism in biological catalysis, with evidence accumulated over the past three decades demonstrating that tunneling contributes to the catalytic rate enhancement of a diverse range of enzymes involved in central metabolic processes. Classical transition state theory predicts that chemical reaction rates are determined by the fraction of reactant molecules with sufficient thermal energy to surmount the activation energy barrier — a temperature-dependent Boltzmann distribution that places strict limits on how fast reactions can proceed at biological temperatures. Quantum tunneling allows particles to pass through energy barriers that would be classically insurmountable, contributing to reaction rates that exceed classical predictions and producing the characteristic kinetic isotope effects that serve as the primary experimental signature of tunneling: when hydrogen is replaced by the heavier isotope deuterium — which has a smaller de Broglie wavelength and therefore tunnels less efficiently — the reaction rate decreases by more than the classical prediction, revealing the quantum contribution. Alcohol dehydrogenase and dihydrofolate reductase DHFR are the most extensively studied tunneling enzymes: in both cases, proton or hydride transfer between substrate and cofactor occurs substantially by quantum tunneling, with the enzyme protein dynamics actively promoting tunneling by compressing the donor-acceptor distance to create tunneling-ready states. Tunneling-ready states — the specific protein conformations that bring the quantum mechanical donor and acceptor into close proximity for efficient tunneling — reveal that the protein scaffold is not merely a passive scaffold but an active quantum machine whose conformational dynamics are optimized for quantum mechanical catalysis. The correspondence between quantum enzyme tunneling and the TCM concept of Qi transformation — the efficient conversion of one form of vital substance into another through processes that exceed classical thermodynamic predictions — provides a quantum biological grounding for the TCM transformation model.
