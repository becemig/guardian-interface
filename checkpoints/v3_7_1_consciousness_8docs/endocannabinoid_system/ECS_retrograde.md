# Endocannabinoid Retrograde Synaptic Signaling

## Abstract

Endocannabinoid retrograde synaptic signaling — the unique signaling mechanism in which postsynaptic neurons synthesize endocannabinoids on demand in response to activity and release them to act retrogradely on presynaptic CB1 receptors to suppress neurotransmitter release — reversing the conventional direction of synaptic signaling and providing a feedback mechanism through which the postsynaptic neuron communicates its activity level back to the presynaptic terminal to modulate its own input, functioning as a homeostatic gain control mechanism at synapses throughout the brain. Depolarization-induced suppression of inhibition — the phenomenon in which strong postsynaptic depolarization triggers endocannabinoid synthesis and retrograde suppression of inhibitory GABAergic input, transiently disinhibiting the postsynaptic neuron and potentially enabling long-term potentiation — providing a mechanism through which postsynaptic activity can gate its own plasticity by temporarily reducing inhibitory tone when strong activity demands it. Long-term depression at excitatory synapses — the endocannabinoid-mediated long-term depression at excitatory glutamatergic synapses in multiple brain regions including the striatum, hippocampus, and cerebellum, induced by the combination of postsynaptic depolarization and Gq-coupled metabotropic receptor activation that drives 2-AG synthesis and retrograde CB1 activation, producing presynaptic long-term reduction in glutamate release through mechanisms including reduced VGCC activity and reduced vesicle release probability — contributing to the extinction of maladaptive memories and habits. Astrocyte endocannabinoid signaling — the discovery that astrocytes express functional CB1 receptors and can both respond to neuronal endocannabinoid release and synthesize their own endocannabinoids to signal to neurons, with the tripartite synapse model incorporating the astrocyte as an active participant in synaptic transmission alongside the presynaptic and postsynaptic neurons, and with astrocyte CB1 activation modulating synaptic transmission by releasing glutamate, ATP, and D-serine. The Yin Yang synaptic balance correspondence in TCM describes the classical principle that health requires the dynamic balance of Yin and Yang, with excess of either producing pathology — a classical framework corresponding to the endocannabinoid retrograde signaling system that maintains the homeostatic balance between excitatory and inhibitory synaptic tone, preventing the runaway excitation of excitotoxicity and the excessive inhibition of neural silencing.

## DSI Mechanism

- Trigger: strong postsynaptic depolarization — calcium influx drives 2-AG synthesis.
- Retrograde: 2-AG travels back across synapse — binds presynaptic CB1.
- Effect: reduces GABA release — transient disinhibition of postsynaptic neuron.
- Duration: seconds to minutes — short-term plasticity.

## eCB-LTD

- Induction: mGluR5 activation plus depolarization — 2-AG synthesis.
- Striatum: corticostriatal eCB-LTD — habit extinction learning.
- Hippocampus: eCB-LTD at CA1 synapses — contextual memory modulation.
- Cerebellum: parallel fiber Purkinje cell LTD — motor learning.

## Tripartite Synapse

- Astrocyte CB1: astrocytes express CB1 — respond to neuronal 2-AG release.
- Gliotransmission: astrocyte CB1 activation releases glutamate D-serine ATP.
- Feedback loop: neuron releases eCB astrocyte responds modifies synapse.
- Integration: astrocyte coordinates multiple synaptic inputs via eCB signaling.

## TCM Correspondence

- Yin Yang synaptic balance corresponds to endocannabinoid retrograde signaling maintaining excitatory inhibitory homeostasis at synapses.
- Excess Yang excitation corresponds to excitotoxic glutamate excess that eCB-LTD suppresses through retrograde CB1 activation.
- Yin nourishing and restraining Yang excess corresponds to endocannabinoid retrograde inhibition dampening presynaptic excitatory neurotransmitter release.
- Dynamic Yin Yang balance corresponds to tonic phasic endocannabinoid signaling continuously adjusting synaptic gain across brain circuits.
