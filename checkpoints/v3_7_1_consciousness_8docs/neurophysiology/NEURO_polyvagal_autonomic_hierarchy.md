# Polyvagal Theory and Autonomic Hierarchy in Somatic Regulation

## Abstract

Polyvagal Theory proposed by Stephen Porges describes the autonomic nervous system as a three-tiered phylogenetic hierarchy governing physiological state, social engagement, and survival response. The theory reframes HRV as a direct index of vagal brake function and provides a framework for understanding how movement practices regulate autonomic state through breath, posture, and social context.

## Physiological Mechanisms

### Three-Tiered Autonomic Hierarchy

- Ventral vagal complex: myelinated vagal pathways originating in the nucleus ambiguus, supporting social engagement, calm arousal, and respiratory sinus arrhythmia.
- Sympathetic adrenal system: mobilization response for fight-or-flight, suppressing ventral vagal tone and increasing cardiac output.
- Dorsal vagal complex: unmyelinated ancient vagal pathways originating in the dorsal motor nucleus, mediating immobilization and shutdown responses.

### Neuroception

- Neuroception is the subconscious neural evaluation of environmental safety and threat cues.
- The nervous system continuously scans interoceptive, proprioceptive, and exteroceptive signals to determine appropriate autonomic state.
- Mismatched neuroception underlies many trauma and anxiety presentations where the body signals danger in safe environments.

### Vagal Brake

- The ventral vagal pathway acts as a brake on the sinoatrial node, slowing heart rate and enabling HRV.
- Withdrawal of the vagal brake allows rapid sympathetic mobilization without full adrenal activation.
- Respiratory sinus arrhythmia reflects rhythmic engagement and release of the vagal brake during breathing cycles.

## Relevance to Internal Movement Arts

- Slow rhythmic breathing in Tai Chi and Qigong directly engages the ventral vagal pathway via pulmonary stretch receptors.
- Group practice environments provide social engagement cues that reinforce ventral vagal tone through neuroception of safety.
- The felt sense of groundedness and calm during practice corresponds to sustained ventral vagal dominance.
- HRV biofeedback at resonance frequency breathing maximizes vagal brake engagement and coherence.
