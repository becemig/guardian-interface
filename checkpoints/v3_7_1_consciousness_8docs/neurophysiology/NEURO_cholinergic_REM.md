# Cholinergic REM Generation

## Abstract

REM sleep generation depends on cholinergic neurons in the pedunculopontine tegmentum PPT and laterodorsal tegmentum LDT that activate the thalamus and cortex producing the characteristic EEG of REM sleep resembling waking. J. Allan Hobson and Robert McCarley's activation-synthesis hypothesis proposed that dreams arise from the brain's attempt to synthesize meaning from the random pontine-geniculate-occipital PGO wave activation during REM. PGO waves are phasic bursts of electrical activity originating in the pons traveling to the lateral geniculate nucleus and occipital cortex producing the rapid eye movements and generating the visual imagery of dreams. REM atonia — the near-complete paralysis of skeletal muscles during REM — is produced by glycinergic and GABAergic interneurons in the ventromedial medulla preventing the acting out of dream content. Lucid dreaming — the state of being aware that one is dreaming during REM sleep — provides a unique window into the neural correlates of self-awareness and metacognition during sleep. Stephen LaBerge developed reliable techniques for inducing lucid dreams and used eye movement signaling to communicate from within lucid dreams providing experimental verification of the lucid dream state. The threat simulation theory proposes that REM dreaming evolved as a rehearsal system for threatening situations. Hun night journey in TCM — the Liver Hun departing the body during sleep to travel through the dream world and returning with insight — corresponds to the REM dream state as the physiological substrate of the Hun's nocturnal activity.

## PGO Wave Generation

- PGO waves originate in cholinergic neurons of the pedunculopontine tegmentum firing in bursts during REM.
- These bursts propagate to the lateral geniculate nucleus then to the occipital cortex generating visual imagery.
- PGO waves produce the rapid eye movements of REM that give the state its name.
- PGO wave frequency correlates with dream vividness and emotional intensity.

## REM Atonia

- Glycinergic and GABAergic interneurons in the ventromedial medulla produce motor neuron hyperpolarization during REM.
- This near-complete skeletal muscle paralysis prevents acting out of dream content.
- REM behavior disorder involves failure of atonia allowing the sleeper to act out dream behavior.
- Atonia spares the diaphragm and extraocular muscles enabling breathing and eye movements during REM.

## Lucid Dreaming

- Lucid dreaming occurs when the dreamer becomes aware they are dreaming during REM sleep.
- EEG during lucid dreaming shows increased frontal gamma coherence corresponding to self-aware cognition.
- MILD mnemonic induction of lucid dreams uses prospective memory training to initiate dream lucidity.
- Lucid dreamers can signal their state using pre-agreed eye movements verifiable on polysomnography.
