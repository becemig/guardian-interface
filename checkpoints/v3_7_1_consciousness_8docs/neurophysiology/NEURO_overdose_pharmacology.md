# Overdose Pharmacology

## Abstract

Overdose is the pathological consequence of receptor saturation beyond the threshold at which the drug's primary pharmacological action produces life-threatening physiological disruption — with different drug classes producing their lethal effects through distinct but often complementary mechanisms. Opioid overdose kills through respiratory depression — mu opioid receptors in the pre-Botzinger complex of the brainstem respiratory rhythm generator are excessively activated, reducing respiratory drive to the point of apnea. The hypoxia cascade follows: respiratory failure leads to oxygen deprivation, cerebral hypoxia, cardiac arrhythmia, and death within minutes. Fentanyl and its analogs are 50-100 times more potent than morphine at mu receptors — extremely small doses produce lethal respiratory depression, and the drug's rapid onset leaves no time for the body's normal compensatory responses. Naloxone Narcan is a competitive mu receptor antagonist that rapidly reverses opioid respiratory depression — its action as an antidote corresponds to the TCM concept of rapidly opening the closed Lung Qi and restoring the descending function. Stimulant overdose kills through cardiotoxicity — cocaine and methamphetamine produce massive catecholamine surges that cause coronary vasospasm, ventricular arrhythmia, hypertensive crisis, and hyperthermia. The cocaine-lidocaine sodium channel blocking mechanism adds direct cardiac membrane toxicity to the catecholamine surge. Depressant synergy is the pharmacological basis of the most common fatal overdose combination — opioids with benzodiazepines or alcohol both of which enhance GABA-A receptor inhibition of the respiratory center, synergistically suppressing breathing beyond what either drug would produce alone. TCM organ impact of overdose: Lung Qi collapse in opioid overdose, Heart Yang collapse in stimulant overdose, Shen departure in the coma state.

## Opioid Respiratory Depression

- Mu opioid receptors in the pre-Botzinger complex reduce respiratory rate and tidal volume dose-dependently.
- Saturation of these receptors produces apnea — complete cessation of respiratory drive.
- Hypoxia cascade: apnea leads to hypoxemia, cerebral hypoxia, cardiac arrhythmia, and death.
- Naloxone competitively displaces opioids from mu receptors restoring respiratory drive within minutes.

## Stimulant Cardiotoxicity

- Cocaine blocks catecholamine reuptake producing massive norepinephrine and dopamine surges.
- Coronary vasospasm from norepinephrine surge produces myocardial infarction even in young healthy users.
- Ventricular arrhythmia from sodium channel blockade adds direct membrane toxicity to the catecholamine effect.
- Hyperthermia from massive sympathetic activation can exceed 42 degrees Celsius producing multi-organ failure.

## Depressant Synergy

- Opioids suppress respiratory drive through mu receptor activation of brainstem respiratory centers.
- Benzodiazepines and alcohol enhance GABA-A inhibition of the same brainstem respiratory centers.
- Combined opioid and benzodiazepine use multiplies respiratory depression beyond additive effects.
- The majority of fatal opioid overdoses involve concurrent benzodiazepine or alcohol use.
