# Prefrontal Cortex Executive Function

## Abstract

The prefrontal cortex PFC is the anterior portion of the frontal lobe comprising approximately one third of the human cortical surface and serving as the neural substrate of executive function — the higher-order cognitive capacities governing goal-directed behavior. Earl Miller and Jonathan Cohen's integrative theory proposes that the PFC exerts top-down biasing signals on posterior cortical areas and subcortical structures enabling goal-relevant information to guide perception, memory retrieval, and action selection. Three core executive functions identified by Adele Diamond are working memory — holding and manipulating information in mind, inhibitory control — suppressing prepotent responses and irrelevant information, and cognitive flexibility — shifting between rules and perspectives. The dorsolateral PFC dlPFC is the primary substrate of working memory and cognitive control. The ventromedial PFC vmPFC integrates emotional and reward information governing value-based decision-making. The orbitofrontal cortex OFC encodes stimulus-reward associations enabling flexible updating of behavioral strategies. ADHD involves impaired PFC executive function particularly working memory and inhibitory control corresponding to dopaminergic and noradrenergic dysregulation of PFC circuits. Shen governance in TCM — the Heart-Mind's integrating direction of all mental functions toward coherent purposeful action — corresponds to the PFC executive function as its neurobiological substrate.

## Working Memory

- Working memory maintains and manipulates information in mind over seconds to enable complex cognition.
- dlPFC persistent activity maintains representations active during delay periods between stimulus and response.
- Working memory capacity predicts fluid intelligence, academic achievement, and clinical outcomes.
- Stress and fatigue impair working memory through catecholamine dysregulation of dlPFC circuits.

## Inhibitory Control

- Inhibitory control suppresses prepotent responses enabling behavioral flexibility and self-regulation.
- Response inhibition involves right inferior frontal cortex and subthalamic nucleus stopping action sequences.
- Interference control suppresses distracting information enabling sustained attention to task-relevant stimuli.
- Impaired inhibitory control produces impulsivity, distractibility, and difficulty regulating emotional responses.

## Cognitive Flexibility

- Cognitive flexibility enables shifting between mental sets, rules, and perspectives as task demands change.
- Task switching involves anterior cingulate cortex and lateral PFC coordinating the shift between cognitive sets.
- Reversal learning requires updating reward associations when contingencies change — vmPFC and OFC dependent.
- Reduced cognitive flexibility characterizes depression, OCD, and autism through PFC circuit differences.
