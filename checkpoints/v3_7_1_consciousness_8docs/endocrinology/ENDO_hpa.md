# HPA Axis

## Abstract

The hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis is the central neuroendocrine stress response system, orchestrating the release of cortisol from the adrenal cortex in response to physical and psychological stressors. The cascade begins with CRH release from the hypothalamic paraventricular nucleus, which stimulates ACTH secretion from the anterior pituitary, which in turn drives cortisol synthesis and release from the adrenal cortex. Cortisol exerts negative feedback at both the hypothalamus and pituitary to terminate the response. Cortisol is essential for metabolic regulation, immune modulation, cognitive function, and cardiovascular homeostasis, but chronic HPA activation produces allostatic load with progressive organ damage. HPA dysregulation is found in PTSD (hypocortisolism), depression (hypercortisolism), burnout, and chronic fatigue syndrome. The cortisol awakening response (CAR) is a reliable biomarker of HPA reactivity and autonomic nervous system health.

## HPA Cascade

- Hypothalamus PVN: paraventricular nucleus releases CRH in response to stressor
- CRH: corticotropin-releasing hormone travels via hypophyseal portal system to anterior pituitary
- Anterior pituitary: CRH stimulates corticotroph cells to release ACTH
- ACTH: adrenocorticotropic hormone travels in bloodstream to adrenal cortex
- Adrenal cortex zona fasciculata: synthesizes and releases cortisol from cholesterol
- Latency: cortisol rises within 15-20 minutes of stressor onset
- AVP: arginine vasopressin potentiates CRH action - amplifies stress response

## Negative Feedback

- Fast feedback: cortisol rapidly inhibits CRH and ACTH release - membrane mechanism
- Slow feedback: cortisol binds glucocorticoid receptors in PVN and pituitary - genomic inhibition
- Hippocampus: dense GR expression - hippocampus provides inhibitory tone to HPA
- Chronic stress: sustained cortisol damages hippocampal neurons - reduces GR expression - feedback failure
- Dexamethasone suppression test: synthetic GC - tests integrity of HPA negative feedback
- Non-suppression: indicates impaired feedback - marker of melancholic depression

## Cortisol Physiology

- Diurnal rhythm: peaks 30-45 minutes after waking (CAR), declines through day, nadir at midnight
- Metabolic: mobilizes glucose via gluconeogenesis, lipolysis - provides energy for stress response
- Immune: anti-inflammatory at physiological levels - suppresses NF-kB and cytokine production
- Cognitive: enhances memory consolidation at moderate levels - impairs at high chronic levels
- Cardiovascular: increases blood pressure and heart rate - prepares for fight or flight
- Transport: 90 percent bound to CBG (corticosteroid-binding globulin) - 10 percent free and active

## Cortisol Awakening Response (CAR)

- Definition: 50-100 percent rise in cortisol in first 30-45 minutes after waking
- Function: anticipatory mobilization for the day - primes immune, metabolic, and cognitive systems
- Light amplification: morning bright light exposure amplifies CAR
- Blunted CAR: HPA exhaustion pattern - burnout, chronic fatigue, PTSD
- Exaggerated CAR: hyperreactive HPA - anxiety, early-stage chronic stress
- Guardian Interface: CAR as morning biomarker of HPA and ANS health

## HPA Dysregulation Patterns

- Melancholic depression: hypercortisolism - elevated diurnal cortisol, non-suppression on DST
- PTSD: hypocortisolism - low basal cortisol, enhanced negative feedback, blunted CAR
- Burnout: depleted diurnal curve - flat or inverted - loss of morning-evening differential
- Chronic fatigue syndrome: low cortisol - HPA hypoactivity - may reflect prior hyperactivation
- Cushing syndrome: pathological hypercortisolism - central obesity, muscle wasting, immune suppression
- Addison disease: adrenal insufficiency - fatigue, hypotension, hypoglycemia, salt craving

## Chronic Stress Consequences

- Hippocampal atrophy: chronic cortisol reduces dendritic arborization and neurogenesis
- Immune suppression: chronic cortisol suppresses NK cells, T cells, antibody production
- Metabolic syndrome: chronic cortisol drives visceral adiposity, insulin resistance
- Cardiovascular: sustained BP elevation accelerates atherosclerosis
- Sleep disruption: elevated evening cortisol prevents SWS and REM
- TCM parallel: chronic stress depletes Kidney Jing and Yin - root cause of premature aging
