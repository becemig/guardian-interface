# Insulin and Glucose Regulation

## Abstract

Insulin, secreted by pancreatic beta cells in response to rising blood glucose, is the primary anabolic hormone governing glucose uptake, glycogen synthesis, lipogenesis, and protein synthesis. Its counterregulatory hormone glucagon, secreted by alpha cells during hypoglycemia, opposes insulin by stimulating glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis. Insulin resistance — impaired cellular response to insulin signaling — is the central pathophysiology of metabolic syndrome and type 2 diabetes, driven by visceral adiposity, chronic inflammation, mitochondrial dysfunction, and excess dietary carbohydrate. Hyperinsulinemia compensating for insulin resistance has independent adverse effects on cancer risk, PCOS, cardiovascular disease, and neurodegeneration. Brain insulin signaling regulates synaptic plasticity, memory, and appetite — insulin resistance in the brain is implicated as a mechanism in Alzheimer disease, now sometimes termed type 3 diabetes. In TCM, glucose dysregulation maps to Spleen Qi deficiency with Dampness accumulation.

## Insulin Secretion

- Beta cells: pancreatic islets of Langerhans - glucose-sensing via GLUT2 and glucokinase
- First phase: rapid insulin spike within 2 minutes of glucose rise - clears preformed insulin
- Second phase: sustained secretion over 60-90 minutes - new insulin synthesis
- Potentiators: GLP-1, GIP (incretin hormones), acetylcholine, amino acids
- Inhibitors: epinephrine, norepinephrine, somatostatin - suppress insulin during stress
- Pulsatile: insulin secreted in 5-10 minute pulses - continuous infusion less effective

## Insulin Signaling Pathway

- Receptor: insulin receptor tyrosine kinase - autophosphorylation on binding
- IRS-1: insulin receptor substrate - scaffolds PI3K recruitment
- PI3K-Akt: primary metabolic signaling pathway - activates GLUT4 translocation
- GLUT4: insulin-sensitive glucose transporter - translocates to muscle and adipose membrane
- Glycogen synthesis: Akt activates glycogen synthase - stores glucose as glycogen
- mTOR: Akt activates mTOR - protein synthesis, cell growth
- MAPK pathway: parallel signaling - mitogenic and growth effects

## Glucagon Counterregulation

- Alpha cells: secrete glucagon in response to hypoglycemia and amino acids
- Actions: glycogenolysis (liver), gluconeogenesis, lipolysis, ketogenesis
- Insulin-glucagon ratio: determines net hepatic glucose output
- Stress hormones: epinephrine, cortisol, GH all counterregulatory - raise blood glucose
- Glucagonoma: rare alpha cell tumor - severe hyperglycemia, necrolytic migratory erythema

## Insulin Resistance

- Definition: impaired cellular response to insulin - compensatory hyperinsulinemia
- Mechanism: IRS-1 serine phosphorylation by inflammatory kinases (JNK, IKK) blocks signaling
- Drivers: visceral adiposity, saturated fat, fructose excess, physical inactivity, sleep deprivation
- Ectopic fat: intramyocellular and intrahepatic lipid accumulation impairs insulin signaling
- Mitochondrial dysfunction: reduced oxidative capacity - lipid intermediates accumulate
- Compensation: beta cells increase insulin output - eventual beta cell exhaustion

## Metabolic Syndrome and Type 2 Diabetes

- Metabolic syndrome: central obesity, hypertriglyceridemia, low HDL, hypertension, hyperglycemia
- HOMA-IR: homeostatic model assessment - fasting glucose x fasting insulin / 405
- Type 2 progression: insulin resistance + beta cell dysfunction + incretin deficiency
- Complications: retinopathy, nephropathy, neuropathy, cardiovascular disease
- Reversal: caloric restriction, low-carbohydrate diet, exercise - beta cell recovery possible

## Brain Insulin and Neurodegeneration

- Brain insulin: crosses BBB via saturable transport - local synthesis in neurons
- Synaptic function: insulin receptor in hippocampus and cortex - regulates AMPA trafficking
- Memory: intranasal insulin improves memory in healthy subjects and early AD
- Type 3 diabetes hypothesis: Alzheimer as insulin resistance of the brain
- Amyloid connection: insulin-degrading enzyme clears both insulin and amyloid-beta - competition
- TCM: Kidney-Brain axis - Spleen Qi deficiency produces Dampness clouding the orifices
