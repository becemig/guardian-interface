# Growth Hormone and IGF-1 Axis

## Abstract

Growth hormone GH — the 191 amino acid peptide hormone produced by somatotroph cells of the anterior pituitary — and its primary mediator insulin-like growth factor 1 IGF-1 produced primarily by the liver in response to GH stimulation — constitute the somatotropic axis governing somatic growth, body composition, protein anabolism, lipolysis, and the maintenance of lean mass and vitality across the lifespan. GH is released in episodic pulses governed by the opposing actions of growth hormone-releasing hormone GHRH from the hypothalamus stimulating GH release and somatostatin from the hypothalamus inhibiting GH release, with the largest GH pulse occurring during slow-wave sleep approximately 60-90 minutes after sleep onset — making sleep quality a primary determinant of GH secretion and anabolic recovery. IGF-1 — produced by the liver in response to GH stimulation and acting in endocrine, paracrine, and autocrine modes — mediates the growth-promoting effects of GH through the IGF-1 receptor signaling the same PI3K-Akt pathway as insulin, stimulating protein synthesis, cell proliferation, and survival while inhibiting apoptosis and protein degradation. GH decline in aging — somatopause — is one of the most consistent endocrine changes of aging: GH pulse amplitude declines by approximately 14 percent per decade after age 30, producing falling IGF-1, reduced lean mass, increased visceral adiposity, reduced bone density, impaired physical function, and reduced vitality. Daniel Rudman's landmark 1990 New England Journal of Medicine study demonstrating that GH administration in men over 60 reversed multiple markers of aging produced the modern anti-aging medicine movement's interest in growth hormone, though subsequent research has shown that exercise, sleep optimization, fasting, and cold exposure can substantially maintain GH secretion through lifestyle means without the risks of exogenous GH administration. The Kidney Jing growth vitality correspondence describes the TCM understanding of Kidney Jing as the constitutional substrate governing growth, development, reproductive capacity, and the vitality that declines with aging — a correspondence that maps closely to the somatotropic axis across the lifespan.

## Pulsatile GH Release

- GHRH from hypothalamus stimulates GH pulse; somatostatin inhibits.
- Largest pulse occurs 60-90 minutes after sleep onset during slow-wave sleep.
- Exercise: high-intensity exercise produces significant acute GH pulse 2-3 hours post-exercise.
- Fasting: acute fasting increases GH pulse frequency and amplitude through reduced IGF-1 feedback.

## IGF-1 Anabolic Effects

- Liver produces IGF-1 in response to GH — primary endocrine mediator of GH effects.
- Stimulates protein synthesis in muscle, bone, and all tissues through PI3K-Akt pathway.
- Promotes cell proliferation and survival — anti-apoptotic in most tissues.
- Bone: stimulates osteoblast proliferation and collagen synthesis — essential for bone density.

## Somatopause

- GH pulse amplitude declines approximately 14 percent per decade after age 30.
- Falling IGF-1 produces reduced lean mass, increased visceral fat, reduced bone density.
- Sleep optimization, high-intensity exercise, intermittent fasting maintain GH through lifestyle.
- Corresponds to the TCM Kidney Jing natural decline across the seven and eight year cycles.
