# Reproductive Endocrinology

## Abstract

Reproductive endocrinology — the endocrine regulation of reproductive function through the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal HPG axis — governs the menstrual cycle, fertility, sexual development, and the hormonal milieu that influences mood, cognition, bone density, cardiovascular health, and metabolic function across the lifespan. The HPG axis operates through the pulsatile release of gonadotropin-releasing hormone GnRH from the hypothalamic arcuate nucleus — the pulse frequency and amplitude encoding different physiological signals: slow pulses favor FSH release promoting follicular development, fast pulses favor LH release triggering ovulation. The menstrual cycle — a 28-day hormonal symphony coordinating ovarian follicular development, ovulation, and endometrial preparation for implantation — is governed by the cyclical interaction of estrogen, progesterone, FSH, and LH in precise temporal sequences that reflect the underlying health of the HPG axis and its integration with the HPA and HPT axes. Testosterone — the primary androgen in both males and females, produced by the testes in men and by the ovaries and adrenal cortex in women — governs libido, muscle mass, bone density, red blood cell production, mood, and cognitive function in both sexes, with its decline in aging men andropause and its dysregulation in polycystic ovary syndrome PCOS in women producing characteristic symptom clusters. PCOS — the most common endocrine disorder in reproductive-age women, affecting 8-13 percent globally — is characterized by androgen excess, oligo-ovulation, and polycystic ovarian morphology, with insulin resistance and hyperinsulinemia driving androgen excess through stimulation of ovarian androgen production and suppression of sex hormone-binding globulin. The TCM Kidney-Liver reproductive essence correspondence describes the dual organ network foundation of reproductive endocrinology: the Kidney Jing as the constitutional hormonal foundation providing the substrate for reproductive hormone production, and the Liver Blood as the functional resource that fills the Chong and Conception extraordinary vessels to support the menstrual cycle.

## HPG Axis

- GnRH from hypothalamic arcuate nucleus in pulses governs the entire reproductive cascade.
- Slow GnRH pulses: FSH dominant — follicular development phase.
- Fast GnRH pulses: LH dominant — ovulation trigger.
- FSH stimulates follicular growth and estrogen production; LH triggers ovulation and progesterone production.

## Menstrual Cycle Phases

- Follicular phase days 1-14: rising estrogen from developing follicles builds endometrium.
- Ovulation day 14: LH surge triggers follicle rupture and egg release.
- Luteal phase days 14-28: corpus luteum produces progesterone preparing endometrium for implantation.
- Menstruation: progesterone withdrawal triggers endometrial shedding if implantation did not occur.

## PCOS Pathophysiology

- Insulin resistance drives hyperinsulinemia stimulating ovarian androgen production.
- Androgen excess disrupts follicular development producing oligo-ovulation.
- Elevated LH-to-FSH ratio maintains follicles in immature state producing polycystic morphology.
