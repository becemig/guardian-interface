# Trauma Neuroscience

## Abstract

Psychological trauma produces lasting changes in brain structure and function that explain the persistent intrusive symptoms, emotional dysregulation, and somatic complaints that define post-traumatic stress disorder. Joseph LeDoux's research on fear memory consolidation established that the amygdala stores implicit emotional memories of traumatic experiences through a separate pathway from the hippocampal explicit memory system — these amygdala fear memories are formed rapidly during trauma through stress hormone-enhanced synaptic consolidation and are extremely resistant to extinction. The amygdala hijack occurs when current sensory input activates the amygdala fear memory before the prefrontal cortex can contextualize it as a past memory rather than a present threat — producing a full physiological threat response to a memory rather than an actual danger. Bessel van der Kolk's foundational research documented that trauma is stored somatically — in chronic muscular tension, altered breathing patterns, disrupted interoception, and the body postures and movement qualities that encode the unresolved defensive responses of the traumatic moment. Hippocampal shrinkage in PTSD results from chronic glucocorticoid exposure — cortisol is neurotoxic to hippocampal neurons — reducing the hippocampus's capacity to contextualize amygdala fear responses as past memories, creating a self-perpetuating cycle. Peritraumatic dissociation — the dissociative response during the traumatic event itself — is the strongest predictor of subsequent PTSD, corresponding to the dorsal vagal shutdown response that disconnects consciousness from somatic experience. TCM shock patterns correspond to the sudden Qi and Shen disruption of traumatic experience: Jing sinking from terror corresponds to the sympathoadrenal surge and subsequent depletion, Heart Shen scattering corresponds to the dissociative fragmentation of traumatic memory, and Liver Qi stagnation corresponds to the frozen unexpressed defensive response locked in the musculature.

## Amygdala Fear Memory

- Stress hormones epinephrine and cortisol enhance amygdala synaptic consolidation during trauma.
- Fear memories are stored as implicit procedural memories — the body knows the danger before the mind does.
- Amygdala fear memories are formed in a single trial and resist extinction — they do not fade with time alone.
- Reconsolidation window therapy allows fear memory updating during the brief period after retrieval.

## Somatic Imprint

- Unresolved defensive responses — the blocked run, the unexpressed fight — are stored in the musculature.
- Chronic muscular tension patterns correspond to the frozen defensive postures of the traumatic moment.
- Interoceptive disruption in PTSD corresponds to the disconnect from bodily sensation as protective numbing.
- Somatic therapies work by completing the interrupted defensive response and discharging the stored activation.

## Hippocampal Shrinkage

- Chronic cortisol exposure is neurotoxic to hippocampal CA3 neurons reducing hippocampal volume.
- Reduced hippocampus impairs contextualizing amygdala responses as past memories.
- This creates the PTSD cycle: amygdala fires, hippocampus cannot contextualize, threat response activates.
- Neuroplasticity interventions — BDNF upregulation, meditation, aerobic exercise — can restore hippocampal volume.
