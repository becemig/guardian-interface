# Essential Fatty Acid Physiology

## Abstract

Essential fatty acid physiology — the metabolic and signaling roles of the omega-3 and omega-6 polyunsaturated fatty acids that cannot be synthesized by humans and must be obtained from the diet, encompassing their structural roles in membrane composition, their roles as precursors for eicosanoid and specialized pro-resolving mediator synthesis, their effects on gene expression through nuclear receptor activation, and their critical roles in brain development and neurological function — establishes dietary fat quality as a primary determinant of inflammatory balance, neurological health, and cellular membrane function. Omega-3 omega-6 balance — the evolutionary perspective that hunter-gatherer diets contained approximately equal amounts of omega-6 and omega-3 fatty acids at a ratio near 1:1, while modern Western diets contain ratios of 15:1 to 20:1 omega-6 to omega-3 due to the displacement of omega-3-rich foods by omega-6-rich vegetable oils and grain-fed animal products — establishes the shift in dietary fatty acid balance as a primary driver of the chronic inflammatory diseases of modernity through the competitive displacement of omega-3 from membrane phospholipids and the skewing of eicosanoid production toward pro-inflammatory arachidonic acid metabolites. EPA and DHA neurological function — the critical roles of the long-chain omega-3 fatty acids eicosapentaenoic acid EPA and docosahexaenoic acid DHA in brain structure and function, with DHA comprising 40 percent of the polyunsaturated fatty acids in the brain and being essential for synaptic membrane fluidity, neurotransmitter receptor function, neurogenesis, and neuroprotection, and EPA providing the primary substrate for the anti-inflammatory and pro-resolving eicosanoid pathways that govern neuroinflammation — establish marine omega-3 consumption as essential for optimal neurological function across the lifespan. Specialized pro-resolving mediators — Charles Serhan's discovery that the resolution of inflammation is not a passive process but is actively driven by specialized lipid mediators including resolvins, protectins, and maresins derived from EPA and DHA, which actively halt neutrophil recruitment, promote macrophage clearance of apoptotic cells and debris, and restore tissue homeostasis — transforms the understanding of omega-3 fatty acids from anti-inflammatory to pro-resolving, explaining why chronic omega-3 deficiency impairs not just the inflammatory response but the resolution of inflammation, leading to chronic unresolved inflammation. The nutritive Jing fat essence correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding of fat and marrow as concentrated Jing essence that nourishes the Brain and Kidney — a correspondence with the modern understanding of DHA as the essential fatty acid that comprises brain structure and supports neurological function.

## Omega-3 Omega-6 Competition

- Both compete for FADS1 FADS2 desaturase enzymes — determine conversion efficiency.
- Arachidonic acid AA: primary omega-6 — substrate for pro-inflammatory eicosanoids.
- EPA: primary omega-3 — substrate for anti-inflammatory and pro-resolving eicosanoids.
- Western diet excess omega-6: competitively reduces EPA DHA incorporation into membranes.

## EPA DHA Functions

- DHA: 40 percent of brain PUFA — synaptic membrane fluidity receptor function.
- DHA: photoreceptor outer segment membranes — essential for visual function.
- EPA: primary substrate for E-series resolvins and anti-inflammatory prostaglandins.
- DHA: substrate for D-series resolvins protectins maresins.

## Specialized Pro-Resolving Mediators

- Resolvins: halt neutrophil recruitment promote macrophage phagocytosis.
- Protectins: neuroprotectin D1 protects neurons from oxidative stress apoptosis.
- Maresins: macrophage-derived mediators promoting tissue regeneration.
- Deficiency: impaired resolution leads to chronic unresolved inflammation.
