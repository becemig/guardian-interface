# Hakomi Method

## Abstract

The Hakomi Method developed by Ron Kurtz is a mindfulness-based somatic psychotherapy that works with the body as the primary access point to core material — the implicit beliefs, memories, and emotional states organized in the nervous system below the level of verbal awareness. The name Hakomi comes from a Hopi word meaning how do you stand in relation to these many realms — pointing to the method's inquiry into the deep organization of the self. The five principles of Hakomi are: mindfulness as the therapeutic medium, nonviolence in working with the person's defenses, organicity trusting the body's own healing wisdom, unity recognizing the interconnection of all elements of experience, and mind-body holism. Loving presence — the quality of genuine compassionate attention the therapist brings — is the essential therapeutic container that makes Hakomi possible. In Hakomi, the client enters a state of assisted mindfulness — a focused, inward, receptive attention to present-moment experience — in which probes are offered. Probes are carefully crafted verbal or touch experiments designed to evoke core material — the therapist offers a word, phrase, or gentle touch while the client tracks the internal response. Core material emerges through the probe response — spontaneous images, emotions, memories, and body sensations that reveal the underlying belief system. Nourishment — providing the missing experience that was absent in the original developmental wound — is the primary healing mechanism. Taking over reduces the effort and struggle the client habitually applies — the therapist physically supports a held position allowing the client to receive rather than effortfully maintain. Spleen Yi receptivity in TCM — the Spleen spirit's capacity to receive, process, and integrate new experience and nourishment — corresponds to the Hakomi principle of receptive mindful openness as the therapeutic medium.

## Mindfulness and Loving Presence

- Assisted mindfulness is a focused inward receptive attention to present-moment experience guided by the therapist.
- Loving presence is the quality of genuine compassionate attention that creates the therapeutic container.
- Mindfulness allows core material to surface from below verbal awareness where it is organized in the nervous system.
- The combination of loving presence and mindfulness produces safety that enables deep material to emerge.

## Probes and Core Material

- Probes are experiments — words, phrases, or gentle touch — offered while the client tracks internal response.
- The probe is designed based on the therapist's reading of the client's body, expression, and narrative.
- Core material emerges through the probe response as spontaneous images, emotions, memories, and body sensations.
- Core material reveals the implicit belief system organized below verbal awareness in the nervous system.

## Nourishment and Taking Over

- Nourishment provides the missing experience absent in the developmental wound — being held, seen, or supported.
- The experience of receiving what was missing produces a somatic shift and emotional release.
- Taking over physically supports a held position allowing the client to release effort and receive support.
- The release of effortful holding and the experience of being supported produces the primary healing moment.
