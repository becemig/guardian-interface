# Taijichuan Push Hands

## Abstract

Push hands Tui Shou is the two-person training practice of Taijichuan where partners maintain continuous contact and take turns applying and neutralizing force — developing the sensitivity, listening ability, and neutralization skill that constitute the martial and somatic intelligence at the heart of the Taijichuan tradition. Push hands is distinguished from sparring or fighting practice by its cooperative rather than competitive quality — the goal is mutual development of listening and neutralization sensitivity, not to defeat the partner. Listening Jin Ting Jin is the primary skill developed through push hands — the ability to sense through physical contact the quality, direction, weight, and intention of the partner's force, and to respond to what is actually present rather than what is anticipated or feared. Ting Jin is the Taijichuan equivalent of the JSD practitioner's hand sensitivity and the acupuncturist's pulse diagnosis sensitivity — a refined somatic awareness that reads the energetic state of another person through direct physical contact. Neutralization Hua Jin is the ability to redirect incoming force away from one's center without resisting it — the practical expression of the Taoist principle that the yielding overcomes the strong. Successful neutralization requires that the practitioner not know where the neutralization will go in advance — it arises spontaneously from the listening sensitivity meeting the incoming force. Four corner training Sijiao practices attack and neutralization from the four cardinal directions building comprehensive directional sensitivity. Fa Jin discharge is the expression of accumulated Neijin as a sudden release of force — the complete cycle of receive, listen, neutralize, and express that constitutes a complete Taijichuan martial exchange.

## Listening Jin

- Ting Jin develops through sustained two-person contact practice over many years.
- The listening is somatic not conceptual — the body knows before the mind analyzes.
- Quality of force, direction, weight, timing, and intention are all readable through skilled contact.
- This corresponds directly to the TCM practitioner's pulse diagnosis and palpation sensitivity.

## Neutralization

- Hua Jin successful neutralization arises spontaneously from the meeting of listening and incoming force.
- Premature neutralization based on anticipation rather than listening misses the actual force.
- True neutralization redirects without resistance — adding no force of one's own to the exchange.
- This corresponds to the Taoist principle of Wu Wei — responding to what is without adding what is not.

## Somatic Dialogue

- Push hands is a somatic conversation — two nervous systems communicating through physical contact.
- The quality of contact reveals the practitioner's internal state — tension, fear, aggression all transmit through touch.
- Developing equanimity in the face of incoming force develops equanimity in all challenging situations.
- This somatic development of equanimity corresponds to the TCM cultivation of Kidney Zhi unshakeable groundedness.

## TCM Correspondence

- Listening Jin sensitivity through contact corresponds to TCM practitioner palpation and pulse diagnosis refinement.
- Neutralization without resistance corresponds to the TCM principle of following the Qi rather than opposing it.
- Push hands somatic dialogue corresponds to the TCM practitioner-patient Qi field interaction during treatment.
- Equanimity development through challenging contact corresponds to Kidney Zhi cultivation as the constitutional stillness that cannot be moved.
