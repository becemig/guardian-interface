# Chi Nei Tsang Breath and Diaphragm

## Abstract

Breath is the primary medium through which Chi Nei Tsang activates and moves Qi through the abdominal organs — the diaphragm as the primary breathing muscle creates the rhythmic compression and release that massages the abdominal organs from above with every breath cycle, and CNT work dramatically amplifies this effect through skilled manual support of the breathing movement. The diaphragm is the central structure of CNT breath work — its restriction is the single most common finding in abdominal assessment, producing not only respiratory limitation but cascading effects on all abdominal organs through the peritoneal connections, on the heart through pericardial connections, and on the spine through the diaphragmatic crura. Abdominal breathing — using the diaphragm fully so that the belly expands on inhalation — is the foundational CNT breath practice, reversing the thoracic breathing pattern that chronic stress and postural collapse produce. Dan Tian breathing focuses the breath awareness on the lower abdominal Dan Tian cultivating the sensation of warm expanding Qi with each inhalation. Reverse breathing contracts the abdomen on inhalation and expands it on exhalation — the breathing pattern used in Qigong and martial arts to gather Qi inward and downward. Bone Marrow Washing breathing combines deep abdominal breath with specific visualization of Qi penetrating into the bones and marrow — cultivating the deepest constitutional Qi. The Six Healing Sounds combine specific exhalation sounds with organ-directed awareness to facilitate emotional and physical release from each organ. CNT diaphragm release through skilled direct contact with the diaphragm attachment points along the costal margin produces immediate improvement in breath depth and organ mobility throughout the abdomen.

## Diaphragm Work

- The diaphragm attaches along the entire costal margin, xiphoid, and lumbar vertebrae through the crura.
- CNT works along the costal margin attachment releasing the fascial restrictions that limit diaphragm excursion.
- Full diaphragm release produces immediate improvement in breath depth and abdominal organ mobility.
- The diaphragm crura attach to L1-L3 vertebrae — diaphragm restriction directly produces low back tension.

## Abdominal Breathing

- Abdominal breathing uses the diaphragm fully allowing the belly to expand freely on each inhalation.
- It reverses the thoracic stress-breathing pattern that elevates the chest and compresses the abdomen.
- Regular abdominal breathing massages the abdominal organs with every breath through diaphragm compression.
- Abdominal breathing activates the parasympathetic nervous system through vagal stimulation.

## Dan Tian Breathing

- Dan Tian breathing focuses awareness on the lower abdomen 2-3 inches below the navel.
- Each inhalation is felt as a warm expansion of Qi at the Dan Tian — filling the lower cauldron.
- Sustained Dan Tian breathing cultivates the warmth and fullness that indicates healthy lower Dan Tian Qi.
- This practice is the foundation of all Qigong and Taijichuan internal work.

## TCM Correspondence

- Diaphragm as the respiratory engine corresponds to the Lung governing Qi through its descending function.
- Abdominal breathing activating the lower Dan Tian corresponds to Lung Qi descending to support Kidney reception.
- Reverse breathing corresponds to the Taoist technique of leading Qi to the Dan Tian for cultivation.
- The breath-organ connection of CNT corresponds to the TCM principle of breath as the primary Qi mover.
