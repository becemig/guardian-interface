# Foundations of Biomechanics

## Abstract

Biomechanics is the application of mechanical principles to biological systems — the science of how physical forces shape, constrain, and enable movement. It is the bridge between anatomy and motion: where anatomy describes the structures, biomechanics describes what those structures do under load, in motion, and across time. The foundational framework is Newtonian mechanics applied to the human body, which is modeled as a system of rigid segments linked by joints whose motion is governed by forces, moments, inertia, and the conservation laws of energy and momentum. Statics analyzes the body at rest or in equilibrium — how forces balance across joints during standing, holding, and postural maintenance. Dynamics analyzes bodies in motion — how acceleration, inertia, and time-varying forces produce the trajectories of movement. Kinematics describes motion without reference to its causes — position, velocity, and acceleration of body segments in space. Kinetics describes the forces and moments that cause motion — muscle forces, joint reaction forces, ground reaction forces, and gravitational loads. The inverse dynamics approach — working backward from measured motion to infer forces — is the primary analytical method of clinical biomechanics. Forward dynamics — simulating motion from known forces — is the basis of musculoskeletal modeling and movement optimization. For the Guardian Interface project, biomechanics provides the physical science layer beneath the MCF framework: the Riemannian and sub-Riemannian geometries of MCF describe the shape of movement space, while biomechanics describes the forces that drive motion through that space. A complete model integrates both: the geometry tells you where natural paths are, the mechanics tells you how forces propel and constrain movement along them.

## Core Mechanical Principles

- Newton first law: body at rest or constant velocity unless net force acts — inertia
- Newton second law: F = ma — net force equals mass times acceleration — the primary equation
- Newton third law: every action has equal opposite reaction — ground reaction forces
- Torque: T = F x d — force times perpendicular moment arm — rotational equivalent of force
- Angular momentum: L = I x omega — moment of inertia times angular velocity
- Work: W = F x d — force times displacement — energy transferred to system
- Power: P = F x v — force times velocity — rate of work — athletic performance metric

## Kinematics

- Position: location of body segment in space — Cartesian or joint angle coordinates
- Velocity: rate of change of position — first derivative — direction and speed
- Acceleration: rate of change of velocity — second derivative — cause of net force
- Joint angles: primary kinematic variable — flexion extension abduction rotation
- Degrees of freedom: number of independent motion directions per joint
- Euler angles: three rotations describing 3D joint orientation — gimbal lock problem
- Quaternions: alternative 3D rotation representation — no gimbal lock — used in MCF
- MediaPipe: extracts 3D joint kinematics from video — primary MCF input stream

## Kinetics and Free Body Diagrams

- Free body diagram: isolate segment — draw all forces acting on it
- Joint reaction force: force transmitted through joint — compressive and shear components
- Muscle force: tension generated by muscle — magnitude direction and moment arm
- Ground reaction force: Newtons third law — force plate measures GRF directly
- Inverse dynamics: use measured kinematics and GRF to compute joint moments
- Indeterminate: more muscles than equations — optimization needed to resolve
- EMG: electromyography provides muscle activation timing — constrains optimization

## Lever Systems in the Body

- First class lever: fulcrum between effort and load — head on spine — nodding
- Second class lever: load between fulcrum and effort — standing on toes — rare in body
- Third class lever: effort between fulcrum and load — bicep curl — most common
- Mechanical advantage: MA = effort arm divided by load arm
- Body preference: third class levers sacrifice MA for speed and range of motion
- Muscle moment arm: perpendicular distance from muscle line of action to joint center
- Training: exercises that maximize moment arm optimize muscle mechanical advantage

## Musculoskeletal Modeling

- OpenSim: open source musculoskeletal modeling platform — industry standard
- Segment model: body as linked chain of rigid segments with defined mass and inertia
- Muscle model: Hill-type — contractile element plus series and parallel elastic elements
- Forward simulation: apply muscle activations — simulate resulting motion
- Optimization: find muscle activations that minimize metabolic cost — subject to constraints
- Personalization: scale generic model to individual anthropometry — patient-specific
- MCF link: musculoskeletal model provides DOF structure and constraint geometry for MCF manifold
