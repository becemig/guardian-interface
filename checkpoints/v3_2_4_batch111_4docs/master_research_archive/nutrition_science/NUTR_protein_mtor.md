# Protein Quality and mTOR Signaling

## Abstract

Protein quality and mTOR signaling — the molecular and nutritional understanding of how dietary protein quality, amino acid composition, digestibility, and feeding pattern determine muscle protein synthesis, tissue repair, immune function, and metabolic health through the mTORC1 nutrient-sensing pathway — establishes protein nutrition as far more nuanced than simple total intake, with leucine concentration, essential amino acid completeness, digestibility, and meal distribution all determining the anabolic response to dietary protein. Essential amino acid profiles — the determination of protein quality by the content and digestibility of the nine essential amino acids that cannot be synthesized by humans including histidine, isoleucine, leucine, lysine, methionine, phenylalanine, threonine, tryptophan, and valine, with the Digestible Indispensable Amino Acid Score DIAAS replacing the older PDCAAS as the gold standard measure of protein quality — establishes animal proteins generally as higher quality than individual plant proteins due to their more complete essential amino acid profiles and higher digestibility, while strategic plant protein complementation can achieve equivalent nutritional quality. Leucine mTORC1 threshold — the discovery that leucine serves as the primary amino acid signal activating mTORC1 to initiate muscle protein synthesis, with a minimum threshold leucine dose of approximately 0.05 grams per kilogram body weight required to maximally stimulate mTORC1 in skeletal muscle, and with whey protein's high leucine content explaining its superior anabolic response compared to casein and soy at equivalent total protein doses — establishes leucine as the key amino acid for optimizing muscle protein synthesis and explains why protein source and leucine content matter for anabolic response. Muscle protein synthesis timing and distribution — the evidence that distributing daily protein intake across 3-4 meals of 0.3-0.4 grams per kilogram each, rather than concentrating intake in one or two large meals, maximizes the total 24-hour muscle protein synthesis by repeatedly stimulating mTORC1 with leucine threshold-exceeding doses, with the post-exercise window providing enhanced mTORC1 sensitivity that makes protein timing particularly important after resistance training. The Gu Qi grain protein building essence correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding of Gu Qi as the essence extracted from grain and food by the Spleen's transformative function that provides the building material for Qi and Blood — a functional description of dietary protein as the primary substrate for tissue synthesis and repair.

## DIAAS Protein Quality

- Measures digestible essential amino acid content relative to reference protein.
- Animal proteins: DIAAS typically above 100 — complete and highly digestible.
- Isolated plant proteins: DIAAS typically 60-80 — limiting amino acids.
- Complementation: combining legumes and grains achieves complete essential amino acid profile.

## Leucine mTORC1 Activation

- Leucine sensor: Sestrin2 directly binds leucine to regulate GATOR2 mTORC1 activation.
- Threshold: approximately 2-3 grams leucine per meal required for maximal mTORC1 activation.
- Whey protein: 10-11 percent leucine content — superior anabolic response.
- Plant proteins: lower leucine content requires higher total dose for equivalent mTORC1 activation.

## Protein Distribution

- Optimal per-meal dose: 0.3-0.4 grams per kilogram body weight.
- 3-4 meals: maximizes 24-hour muscle protein synthesis.
- Post-exercise window: 2-4 hours of enhanced mTORC1 sensitivity.
- Aging: higher per-meal threshold required due to anabolic resistance.
