# Sub-Riemannian Geometry and TCM

## Abstract

The sub-Riemannian extension of the Motion Curvature Field framework reveals a profound geometric reinterpretation of Traditional Chinese Medicine's foundational concepts. Where the Riemannian MCF framework modeled TCM channels as geodesics of a freely-curved manifold, the sub-Riemannian framework offers a more precise correspondence: TCM channels are not merely preferred paths through movement space — they are the generators of the horizontal distribution D itself. Qi does not simply flow along channels as a fluid through pipes; Qi is the horizontal vector field — the field of permitted movement directions that defines what motion is natural, effortless, and structurally consonant with the body's geometry. Wu Wei — effortless action — is then precisely horizontal motion: movement whose velocity lies entirely within D at every instant, requiring no transverse force, no violation of constraint, no expenditure of energy fighting the body's own structure. Blockage — the stagnation of Qi — is the failure of a movement or energy vector to remain horizontal: a transverse component has appeared, meaning motion is occurring against the constraint geometry, forcing across the distribution rather than along it. Acupoints, understood in this framework, are singular points of the distribution — locations where D changes rank or character, where the constraint geometry is particularly concentrated, and where small perturbations (needle, pressure, intention) can shift the horizontal structure and restore horizontality to a stagnant vector field. The Dantian is the base point of the entire distribution — the origin from which all horizontal directions emanate and to which all channel paths return.
