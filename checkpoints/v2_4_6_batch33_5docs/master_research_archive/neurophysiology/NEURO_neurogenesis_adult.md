# Adult Neurogenesis

## Abstract

Adult neurogenesis — the generation of new neurons from neural stem cells in the mature brain — was considered impossible until Joseph Altman's autoradiographic studies in the 1960s demonstrated cell proliferation in the adult rat hippocampus. In the adult mammalian brain neurogenesis occurs constitutively in two regions: the subgranular zone SGZ of the hippocampal dentate gyrus and the subventricular zone SVZ adjacent to the lateral ventricles. Hippocampal neurogenesis contributes to pattern separation — the ability to distinguish similar experiences as distinct memories — spatial navigation, and the clearance of outdated memories. Henriette van Praag's research demonstrated that voluntary running dramatically increases hippocampal neurogenesis and improves memory performance. BDNF brain-derived neurotrophic factor is the primary growth factor supporting neurogenesis — exercise, antidepressants, and enriched environments all increase BDNF. The neurogenesis hypothesis of depression proposes that chronic stress suppresses hippocampal neurogenesis while effective antidepressant treatment restores it. Chronic stress, glucocorticoids, social isolation, and alcohol suppress neurogenesis while exercise, BDNF, antidepressants, caloric restriction, and learning enhance it. ## Dentate Gyrus Neurogenesis

- Neural stem cells in the subgranular zone generate new granule cells that integrate into the dentate gyrus circuit.
- Newly born neurons undergo a period of heightened synaptic plasticity making them preferentially recruited during learning.
- Pattern separation — distinguishing similar but distinct memories — is impaired when neurogenesis is suppressed.
- Approximately 700 new neurons are added to each human hippocampus per day under optimal conditions.

## Regulators of Neurogenesis

- Exercise is the most potent positive regulator of neurogenesis through BDNF upregulation and increased blood flow.
- Antidepressants including SSRIs increase neurogenesis through serotonin-BDNF signaling.
- Chronic stress and glucocorticoids suppress neurogenesis — a mechanism of stress-induced hippocampal damage.
- Environmental enrichment, learning, caloric restriction, and omega-3 fatty acids all support neurogenesis.

## Depression and Neurogenesis

- The neurogenesis hypothesis proposes that hippocampal neurogenesis loss contributes to depression pathophysiology.
- Chronic stress models that produce depression-like behavior all suppress hippocampal neurogenesis.
- Blocking neurogenesis prevents the behavioral effects of antidepressants in animal models.
- Hippocampal volume loss in depression may partly reflect neurogenesis suppression and dendritic atrophy.
