# Endocannabinoid System and Pain Modulation

## Abstract

Endocannabinoid system and pain modulation — the critical role of the endocannabinoid system in setting pain thresholds and modulating nociceptive processing at multiple levels of the pain neuraxis from peripheral nociceptors through the spinal dorsal horn to supraspinal pain modulatory centers, with CB1 receptors in the periaqueductal gray and rostral ventromedial medulla mediating the analgesic effects of stress and emotional states, spinal CB1 and CB2 receptors suppressing dorsal horn nociceptive transmission, and peripheral CB1 and CB2 receptors on primary afferent nociceptors raising the activation threshold for noxious stimuli. ECS in descending pain inhibition — the critical contribution of endocannabinoid signaling in the periaqueductal gray to stress-induced and emotionally-modulated analgesia, with PAG CB1 receptors mediating the analgesic effects of 2-AG release in response to stressful stimuli through activation of the descending inhibitory pathway to the rostral ventromedial medulla, with the stress-induced analgesia of footshock and fear conditioning being substantially reduced by CB1 antagonism and FAAH inhibition-induced anandamide elevation producing PAG-dependent analgesia. Peripheral CB1 CB2 analgesia — the analgesic potential of peripheral cannabinoid receptor activation without central psychoactive effects, with peripheral CB1 receptors on C fiber nociceptors being upregulated during inflammation and mediating the local anti-nociceptive effects of locally applied cannabinoids, with CB2 receptors on immune cells and peripheral sensory neurons reducing the release of pro-inflammatory mediators that sensitize nociceptors, and with peripherally restricted cannabinoid agonists demonstrating analgesic efficacy in inflammatory and neuropathic pain models without central side effects. Cannabis clinical pain evidence — the growing body of clinical evidence supporting cannabis-based medicines for chronic pain, with a 2018 systematic review finding moderate-quality evidence for cannabinoids improving chronic neuropathic pain, with Sativex oromucosal spray demonstrating efficacy in multiple sclerosis spasticity pain and cancer pain, and with observational studies consistently showing reduced opioid use in chronic pain patients using medical cannabis. The Tong Ze Bu Tong ECS pain relief correspondence in TCM describes the classical principle that where there is free flow there is no pain and where there is obstruction there is pain — a classical framework corresponding to the endocannabinoid system's facilitation of synaptic and neuronal function through retrograde inhibitory signaling that prevents the nociceptive transmission amplification underlying chronic pain.

## Clinical Evidence

- Neuropathic pain: moderate evidence for cannabinoids — systematic review 2018.
- MS spasticity: Sativex RCT efficacy — approved in multiple countries.
- Opioid sparing: cannabis use associated with 30-64 percent opioid dose reduction.
- Cancer pain: adjunctive cannabinoids improve pain control in cancer.
