# Ben Cao Gang Mu

## Abstract

The Ben Cao Gang Mu — Compendium of Materia Medica — compiled by Li Shi Zhen over 27 years and published in 1596 during the Ming dynasty, is the greatest achievement in the history of Chinese pharmaceutical literature and one of the most comprehensive works of natural history produced anywhere in the premodern world. Li Shi Zhen systematically documented 1,892 medicinal substances — 1,094 botanical, 444 animal, and 354 mineral — organized into 52 volumes with 11,096 prescriptions and 1,160 illustrations, correcting errors in prior materia medica texts and adding hundreds of previously undocumented substances from his own clinical observations and travels throughout China. The Ben Cao Gang Mu's classification system organized substances by their natural category rather than by their medical grade — a significant departure from prior Ben Cao texts that had classified substances by their efficacy grade. Each substance entry follows a systematic format: the name with etymological discussion, a collection of synonyms from prior texts, notes on the correct identification of the substance, records of the substance's processing and preparation, and the medical indications drawing on both classical sources and Li Shi Zhen's own clinical experience. Li Shi Zhen's empirical methodology — his insistence on direct observation, his willingness to correct authoritative prior texts when his observations contradicted them, and his systematic documentation of clinical outcomes — represents a remarkable pre-modern scientific approach to natural history and medicine. The Ben Cao Gang Mu was translated into multiple languages and influenced European natural history through its Japanese translations, contributing to the global understanding of East Asian flora and fauna. In the Guardian Interface context the Ben Cao Gang Mu represents the empirical foundation of the entire TCM herbal archive — every herb deep dive in the knowledge graph traces its lineage to the systematic documentation that Li Shi Zhen accomplished.

## Classification System

- 16 major categories: water, fire, earth, metals and stones, herbs, grains, vegetables, fruits, trees, garments, utensils, insects, fishes, crustaceans, birds, beasts, humans.
- Each category subdivided by natural properties — a pre-Linnaean systematic natural history.
- The botanical category alone contains 1,094 substances organized into 30 subcategories.
- This classification influenced both Chinese taxonomy and European natural history through Japanese translations.

## Empirical Methodology

- Li Shi Zhen corrected over 370 errors in prior materia medica texts based on direct observation.
- He personally tested substances on himself and documented clinical outcomes systematically.
- He traveled extensively to collect specimens and interview practitioners, miners, and farmers.
- This empirical approach corresponds to the modern evidence-based medicine principle of direct observation over authority.

## 1892 Substances

- 1,094 botanical: the complete Chinese medicinal plant pharmacopoeia as of the Ming dynasty.
- 444 animal: insects, fishes, crustaceans, birds, mammals including human-derived medicines.
- 354 mineral: metals, stones, earths, salts and their medicinal applications.
- Each substance documented with identification, processing, preparation, and clinical indications.
