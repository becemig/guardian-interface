# Addiction in TCM

## Abstract

Addiction in TCM is understood as a complex multi-pattern disorder that typically involves the simultaneous presence of multiple interacting pathological patterns — not a single disease entity but a dynamic pattern configuration that evolves through the stages of initiation, habituation, dependence, and withdrawal. The initiating pattern is most commonly Liver Qi stagnation — the frustrated, suppressed, or overwhelmed Qi circulation that seeks relief through the temporary relaxation and Qi-moving effect of substances. Alcohol, opioids, and stimulants all produce their initial attractive effects by temporarily releasing Liver Qi stagnation, which is why people with chronic Liver Qi stagnation are constitutionally more vulnerable to addiction. With sustained use the pattern evolves: Kidney Jing depletes as the constitutional essence is consumed by the chronic overstimulation; Heart Blood depletes as the Shen is repeatedly agitated and cannot rest; Spleen Qi weakens as digestion is disrupted by the substance; Phlegm-Fire mists the Heart as the combination of Spleen weakness and Liver heat generates phlegm that obscures the Shen producing the cognitive distortion and impaired judgment of addiction. Craving in TCM is understood as the Liver Hun seeking the substance that temporarily resolves its stagnation — the intelligent but misdirected seeking behavior of the creative spirit trying to find flow. Withdrawal corresponds to the acute re-emergence of the original Liver Qi stagnation plus the rebound patterns of deficiency that sustained use has created. The NADA National Acupuncture Detoxification Association five-point ear protocol — Shen Men, Sympathetic, Kidney, Liver, Lung — directly addresses the core TCM patterns of addiction through auricular acupuncture.

## Initiating Patterns

- Liver Qi stagnation is the most common constitutional vulnerability — the substance temporarily moves stagnant Qi.
- Heart Shen agitation from emotional trauma, suppression, or chronic stress drives self-medication.
- Kidney Yang deficiency — the cold constitutional depletion — drives stimulant and warming substance use.
- Spleen Qi deficiency producing emptiness and lack of satisfaction drives sugar, alcohol, and carbohydrate craving.

## Progression Patterns

- Initial: Liver Qi stagnation relieved temporarily — positive reinforcement through Qi movement.
- Intermediate: Kidney Jing depletion, Heart Blood deficiency, increasing tolerance requiring more substance.
- Advanced: Phlegm-Fire misting the Heart, cognitive distortion, Shen obscuration, loss of Zhi will to stop.
- Withdrawal: Acute Liver Qi stagnation rebound, Kidney deficiency tremors, Heart Shen agitation, sweating.

## NADA Protocol

- Shen Men calms the Shen and reduces anxiety and agitation during withdrawal.
- Sympathetic point regulates autonomic nervous system reducing withdrawal sympathetic hyperactivation.
- Kidney point tonifies the depleted constitutional Jing and addresses the constitutional root of addiction.
- Liver point moves stagnant Liver Qi and reduces the craving-driving stagnation.
- Lung point opens the corporeal soul Po and supports release of the grief and loss underlying addiction.

## TCM Correspondence

- Addiction as Liver Qi stagnation corresponds to dopamine deficiency in the reward system driving craving.
- Kidney Jing depletion in addiction corresponds to HPA axis burnout and neuroendocrine exhaustion.
- Phlegm misting the Heart corresponds to neuroinflammation and prefrontal cortex impairment in addiction.
- Withdrawal as Liver Qi stagnation rebound corresponds to dopamine receptor downregulation producing anhedonia.
