# pH Balance and Body Chemistry

## Abstract

The body maintains blood pH within the extraordinarily narrow range of 7.35-7.45 through three integrated buffer systems — the bicarbonate-carbonic acid buffer, the phosphate buffer, and the protein buffer including hemoglobin — coordinated by respiratory and renal compensation mechanisms. This tight pH regulation is essential because even small deviations profoundly affect enzyme function, protein conformation, oxygen-hemoglobin binding, and cellular metabolic processes. The potential renal acid load PRAL concept developed by Remer and Manz quantifies the acid or alkaline-forming tendency of foods based on their sulfur-containing amino acid, phosphate, chloride, organic acid, and mineral content — providing a quantitative basis for dietary acid-alkaline management. Acid-forming foods animal proteins, grains, most legumes, refined sugar, alcohol generate sulfuric acid, phosphoric acid, and organic acids as metabolic end-products requiring renal excretion. Alkaline-forming foods most vegetables, fruits, almonds, miso generate bicarbonate precursors that buffer dietary acid load. Metabolic acidosis from chronic high acid load has been associated with bone mineral loss as the skeleton releases calcium carbonate to buffer excess acid, muscle catabolism, kidney stone formation, and chronic fatigue. TCM damp-heat pattern corresponds to the systemic inflammatory and metabolic state of chronic mild acidosis — the tissue acidity, inflammatory cytokines, and reduced oxidative capacity that characterize both. TCM cold-damp pattern corresponds conversely to the low-metabolic-rate, reduced enzyme activity, and impaired circulation of hypothyroid and metabolically suppressed states where cellular pH is too low for optimal enzyme function. Kidney governing acid-base balance through its primary role in bicarbonate reabsorption and hydrogen ion excretion corresponds directly to the TCM Kidney governing Water metabolism and maintaining the body's fundamental equilibrium.

## Buffer Systems

- Bicarbonate-carbonic acid buffer: HCO3-/H2CO3 ratio maintained at 20:1 for pH 7.4 — primary blood buffer.
- Respiratory compensation: increased breathing rate blows off CO2 raising pH in metabolic acidosis.
- Renal compensation: kidneys reabsorb bicarbonate and excrete hydrogen ions adjusting pH over hours to days.
- Protein buffers: histidine residues in proteins and hemoglobin provide intracellular buffering capacity.

## PRAL and Dietary Chemistry

- Acid-forming: meat, fish, eggs, cheese, grains, legumes — high sulfur amino acids and phosphate.
- Alkaline-forming: vegetables, fruits, almonds, seaweed, miso — high organic acid anions that metabolize to bicarbonate.
- The Western diet is chronically acid-forming producing mild metabolic acidosis buffered by bone and muscle.
- TCM diet therapy recommending vegetable-dominant seasonal eating corresponds to alkaline-forming PRAL reduction.

## TCM Pattern Correspondence

- Damp-heat pattern corresponds to chronic mild metabolic acidosis with inflammatory cytokine elevation.
- Cold-damp pattern corresponds to reduced cellular enzyme activity from tissue acidosis and metabolic suppression.
- Kidney governing Water and acid-base balance corresponds to the kidney's primary role in bicarbonate-hydrogen ion regulation.
- Bitter cold herbs Huang Lian, Huang Bai clearing damp-heat correspond to anti-inflammatory alkalinizing effects.
