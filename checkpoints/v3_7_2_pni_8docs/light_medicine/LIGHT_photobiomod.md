# Photobiomodulation and Red Light Therapy

## Abstract

Photobiomodulation PBMT — the therapeutic application of red and near-infrared light at specific wavelengths and doses to stimulate cellular energy production, reduce inflammation, accelerate healing, and improve function — operates through the primary photoacceptor cytochrome c oxidase Complex IV of the mitochondrial electron transport chain, whose absorption spectrum in the red and near-infrared range corresponds precisely to the therapeutic wavelength windows of photobiomodulation, establishing a direct molecular mechanism for light-induced enhancement of mitochondrial function. Cytochrome c oxidase — the terminal enzyme of the mitochondrial electron transport chain that reduces oxygen to water while pumping protons across the inner mitochondrial membrane — contains copper centers and heme groups with strong absorption in the 600-1000 nm red and near-infrared range, with light absorption at these wavelengths dissociating inhibitory nitric oxide from the enzyme, increasing electron transfer efficiency, enhancing proton pumping, and increasing ATP production in cells with suboptimal mitochondrial function. Mitochondrial light response — the cascade of events following cytochrome c oxidase photoactivation — includes increased ATP production, increased mitochondrial membrane potential, modulation of reactive oxygen species, activation of transcription factors including NF-kB and Nrf2, and retrograde signaling to the nucleus producing anti-inflammatory, pro-survival, and regenerative gene expression changes. Near-infrared tissue penetration — the ability of 800-1000 nm near-infrared light to penetrate several centimeters into tissue, reaching muscles, joints, and even the brain through the skull — enables the therapeutic application of photobiomodulation to deep tissue injuries, joint inflammation, and transcranial brain stimulation, with the optical window of tissue transparency in the red and near-infrared range corresponding to the minimal absorption by water, hemoglobin, and melanin that would otherwise block shorter wavelengths. Clinical applications of photobiomodulation span wound healing, musculoskeletal pain, traumatic brain injury, stroke rehabilitation, depression, and Alzheimer's disease, with transcranial photobiomodulation using 810 nm light penetrating the skull to stimulate prefrontal cortex mitochondria showing promising results for depression, cognitive enhancement, and PTSD. The Yang warming activation correspondence in TCM describes the classical use of moxibustion — the burning of Artemisia mugwort over acupoints — as a form of thermal and infrared light therapy that activates Yang Qi through the infrared emission of the burning moxa, corresponding to the photobiomodulation mechanism of red and near-infrared light activating mitochondrial function.

## Cytochrome C Oxidase Mechanism

- Complex IV: terminal enzyme of electron transport chain — reduces O2 to H2O.
- Copper centers Cu-A and Cu-B: strong absorption at 600-900 nm — primary chromophores.
- NO inhibition: cytochrome c oxidase inhibited by NO in hypoxic or inflammatory tissue.
- Light dissociates NO: restores electron transfer and ATP production in inhibited enzyme.

## Therapeutic Wavelength Windows

- Red window 620-680 nm: strong cytochrome c oxidase absorption — superficial tissue effects.
- Near-infrared window 800-880 nm: deep tissue penetration through optical window.
- 1000-1100 nm: secondary window with less cytochrome c oxidase absorption.
- Biphasic dose response: low dose stimulates high dose inhibits — critical for clinical dosing.

## Transcranial Photobiomodulation

- 810 nm NIR light penetrates skull to reach cortical neurons 1-2 cm depth.
- Stimulates prefrontal cortex mitochondria in depression and TBI.
- Increases cerebral blood flow and neurotrophin production.
- Clinical trials: improved mood cognition and PTSD symptoms with transcranial PBMT.
