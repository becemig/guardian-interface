# Neurological Signatures of PTSD

## Abstract

Trauma produces measurable and reproducible neurological changes visible on fMRI and PET imaging, converging on a core circuit involving amygdala hyperreactivity, medial prefrontal cortex hypoactivation, hippocampal volume reduction, insula dysregulation, and disrupted default mode network coherence. The amygdala fire alarm becomes hypersensitive, the PFC override switch becomes less effective, the hippocampal context-tagging system fails, and the insula loses accurate interoceptive calibration. Two primary subtypes emerge: the hyperarousal-intrusive subtype with high sympathetic activation and flashback dominance, and the dissociative subtype with emotional overmodulation and depersonalization. Thalamic gating failure explains sensory flooding and fragmented traumatic memory encoding.

## Amygdala Hyperreactivity

- Finding: exaggerated amygdala BOLD response to threat-related stimuli in PTSD fMRI
- Mechanism: reduced GABA inhibitory tone, sensitized CRF input from hypothalamus
- Threshold lowering: amygdala fires to stimuli below conscious awareness
- Fear generalization: safe stimuli resembling original threat trigger full fear response
- BLA role: basolateral amygdala encodes fear memory with extreme durability
- CeA role: central amygdala outputs fear response to brainstem - autonomic and behavioral

## PFC Hypoactivation

- mPFC: medial prefrontal cortex normally inhibits amygdala fear response
- Finding: reduced mPFC activity during trauma cue exposure in PTSD
- vmPFC: ventromedial PFC is the extinction memory storage site - hypofunctional in PTSD
- Broca area: left inferior frontal gyrus deactivation during flashback - speech production fails
- Consequence: rational override of fear response is impaired - body reacts before mind intervenes
- van der Kolk: PFC goes offline - the smoke detector (amygdala) overrides the watchman (PFC)

## Hippocampal Dysfunction

- Volume: 8-12 percent bilateral hippocampal atrophy in chronic PTSD by MRI
- Context tagging failure: hippocampus normally marks memories with time and place context
- Flashback mechanism: traumatic memory retrieved without temporal context - feels present
- Pattern separation failure: similar but safe stimuli cannot be distinguished from threat
- Verbal memory: explicit narrative of trauma is fragmented and poorly organized

## Insula Dysregulation

- Insula role: primary interoceptive cortex - maps body state into conscious awareness
- Hyperarousal subtype: insula hyperactive - body sensations overwhelming and intrusive
- Dissociative subtype: insula hypoactive - body sensations muted, depersonalization
- Therapeutic target: body-based therapies (yoga, SE, breathwork) restore insula calibration
- van der Kolk yoga study: yoga normalizes insula activation and restores interoceptive contact

## Default Mode Network Disruption

- DMN: medial PFC, posterior cingulate, precuneus, angular gyrus - self-referential processing
- Normal: DMN active at rest, deactivates during task engagement
- PTSD: DMN fails to deactivate - trauma intrudes into task states
- Dissociative subtype: DMN hyperactive - excessive self-referential rumination
- Thalamic gating: trauma disrupts thalamic sensory filtering - sensory flooding results

## Freeze-Dissociation-Hyperarousal Spectrum

- Hyperarousal-intrusive subtype: high SNS, high amygdala, low mPFC, flashbacks, nightmares
- Dissociative subtype: high parasympathetic overmodulation, flat affect, depersonalization
- Freeze: dorsal vagal shutdown with simultaneous sympathetic activation - paralysis with terror
- Polyvagal mapping: hyperarousal = sympathetic dominance, dissociation = dorsal vagal shutdown
