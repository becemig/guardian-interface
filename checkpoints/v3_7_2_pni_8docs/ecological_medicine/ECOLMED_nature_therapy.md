# Nature Therapy and Ecotherapy Evidence

## Abstract

Nature therapy and ecotherapy evidence — the growing body of experimental and epidemiological evidence demonstrating that contact with natural environments including forests, coastlines, parks, and gardens produces measurable improvements in immune function, stress physiology, attention, mood, and cardiovascular health through multiple mechanisms including phytoncide inhalation, visual complexity of natural scenes, reduced noise pollution, increased physical activity, and the evolutionary resonance of natural environments with human sensory systems shaped by millions of years of evolution in natural settings. Shinrin-Yoku forest bathing research — Qing Li's research demonstrating that two-day forest bathing trips produce 40-50 percent increases in NK cell activity and number that persist for 30 days, attributed primarily to inhalation of phytoncides including alpha-pinene and limonene released by trees that directly activate NK cells and increase expression of anti-cancer proteins including perforin, granzyme, and granulysin, with the NK cell enhancement dose-dependently related to the urinary concentration of phytoncide metabolites. Attention Restoration Theory — Rachel and Stephen Kaplan's framework proposing that natural environments restore directed attention capacity depleted by urban cognitive demands through the four components of being away from ordinary demands, the extent of the natural environment providing a different cognitive world, the fascination of natural scenes capturing involuntary attention without effort, and compatibility between the natural environment and the restorative purpose. Stress Recovery Theory — Roger Ulrich's psychophysiological framework proposing that natural environments produce rapid automatic stress recovery through the activation of evolved aesthetic and safety responses to natural scenes including water, savanna-like vegetation, and gentle curves, with experimental evidence including the classic finding that surgical patients with window views of trees required less pain medication and recovered faster than those with views of a brick wall. The Mountain Forest Qi Field healing correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that different natural environments possess different Qi qualities that can be absorbed and that influence human health, with mountain and forest environments considered particularly rich in healing Qi — a traditional framework corresponding to the modern evidence for phytoncide, air ion, and reduced electromagnetic pollution mechanisms of forest health benefits.

## Shinrin-Yoku Evidence

- NK cells: 40-50 percent increase after 2-day forest bathing — persists 30 days.
- Phytoncides: alpha-pinene limonene — inhaled volatile compounds activate NK cells.
- Anti-cancer proteins: perforin granzyme granulysin — increased expression.
- Cortisol: reduced salivary cortisol after forest walking vs urban walking.
