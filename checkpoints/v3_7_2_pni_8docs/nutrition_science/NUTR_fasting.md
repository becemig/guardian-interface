# Intermittent Fasting Mechanisms

## Abstract

Intermittent fasting mechanisms — the molecular and physiological pathways activated by periodic abstention from food including autophagy induction, mTOR suppression, AMPK activation, ketogenesis, metabolic switching, circadian clock entrainment, and gut microbiome remodeling — establish intermittent fasting as one of the most potent and evolutionarily conserved interventions for metabolic health, longevity, cognitive function, and disease prevention, corresponding to the Daoist tradition of Bi Gu grain avoidance practices and the universal spiritual tradition of therapeutic fasting across all major healing traditions. Autophagy induction — the Nobel Prize-winning cellular self-eating process characterized by Yoshinori Ohsumi that is powerfully induced by fasting through mTOR suppression and AMPK activation, producing the engulfment of damaged organelles, protein aggregates, and intracellular pathogens in autophagosomes for lysosomal degradation and component recycling — provides the primary cellular mechanism of fasting's restorative effects, with autophagy representing the cellular housekeeping program that maintains proteostasis and organelle quality in the absence of anabolic signaling. mTOR-AMPK fasting switches — the opposing nutrient-sensing kinases that gate the transition between the fed anabolic state and the fasted catabolic state, with mTOR activated by amino acids insulin and growth factors promoting protein synthesis cell growth and autophagy inhibition, and AMPK activated by falling ATP and rising AMP during fasting to promote fat oxidation, mitochondrial biogenesis, autophagy, and SIRT1 activation — provide the primary molecular switching mechanism that translates fasting into its downstream cellular effects. Time-restricted eating circadian alignment — the emerging evidence that confining food intake to a consistent 8-10 hour window aligned with the active phase of the circadian cycle, regardless of caloric content, improves metabolic health through the reinforcement of circadian oscillations in peripheral metabolic organs, reduction in the metabolic consequences of eating during the circadian rest phase, and optimization of the circadian timing of digestive enzyme secretion and nutrient absorption. The Bi Gu grain avoidance cultivation correspondence in TCM describes the Daoist practice of progressive reduction and eventual elimination of grain consumption as a cultivation practice for refining the body's dependence on gross food and developing the capacity to sustain on Qi alone — a tradition that corresponds to the modern understanding of caloric restriction, fasting, and ketogenic metabolism as interventions that activate longevity pathways and reduce metabolic burden.

## Autophagy Induction

- mTOR suppression: primary trigger — mTOR inhibits ULK1 autophagy initiator.
- AMPK activation: secondary trigger — AMPK activates ULK1 and inhibits mTOR.
- Initiation complex: ULK1 Beclin1 VPS34 — forms phagophore membrane.
- Elongation: ATG proteins extend phagophore to encapsulate cargo.

## mTOR AMPK Switching

- Fed state: high amino acids insulin — mTOR active autophagy suppressed.
- Fasted state: low amino acids insulin — mTOR suppressed AMPK active autophagy induced.
- Transition timing: autophagy induction begins 12-14 hours after last meal.
- Deep autophagy: 18-24 hours fasting produces maximal autophagy induction.

## Time-Restricted Eating

- 8-10 hour eating window aligned with active circadian phase — daytime in humans.
- Reinforces peripheral circadian clocks in liver gut adipose.
- Metabolic benefits independent of caloric restriction in multiple rodent studies.
- Human RCTs: improved insulin sensitivity blood pressure and metabolic syndrome markers.
