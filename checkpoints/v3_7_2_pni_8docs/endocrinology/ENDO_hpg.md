# HPG Axis

## Abstract

The hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis governs reproductive function and sex hormone production through a pulsatile cascade from GnRH in the hypothalamus through LH and FSH in the pituitary to testosterone, estrogen, and progesterone in the gonads. Sex hormones exert profound effects on brain structure, neuroplasticity, mood, cognition, cardiovascular function, bone density, and body composition throughout the lifespan. The HPG axis is exquisitely sensitive to metabolic status and stress — chronic HPA activation suppresses GnRH pulsatility as a biologically adaptive response to perceived resource scarcity. Estradiol drives hippocampal synapse density fluctuations across the menstrual cycle. Testosterone supports muscle mass, erythropoiesis, libido, and mood in both sexes. In TCM, sex hormones and reproductive vitality are governed by Kidney Essence (Jing), with the seven-year cycles in women and eight-year cycles in men mapping the developmental trajectory of Kidney Jing.

## HPG Cascade

- Hypothalamus: GnRH (gonadotropin-releasing hormone) released in pulses from arcuate nucleus
- Pulse frequency: rapid pulses favor LH release - slow pulses favor FSH release
- LH: luteinizing hormone - stimulates testosterone in Leydig cells, triggers ovulation
- FSH: follicle-stimulating hormone - stimulates spermatogenesis, follicular development
- Gonads: testosterone from testes, estradiol and progesterone from ovaries
- Negative feedback: sex hormones inhibit GnRH and LH-FSH at hypothalamus and pituitary
- Positive feedback: estradiol surge mid-cycle triggers LH surge and ovulation

## Testosterone

- Production: Leydig cells (testes), theca cells (ovaries), adrenal cortex (both sexes)
- Transport: 98 percent bound to SHBG and albumin - 2 percent free and active
- Actions: muscle protein synthesis, erythropoiesis, bone density, libido, mood, cognition
- DHT: 5-alpha reductase converts testosterone to dihydrotestosterone - prostate, skin, hair
- Aromatization: testosterone converted to estradiol in adipose, brain, bone - important in males
- Decline: testosterone declines 1-2 percent per year after age 30 in men
- Low testosterone: fatigue, depression, reduced libido, muscle loss, cognitive decline

## Estrogens

- Types: estradiol (E2, most potent), estrone (E1), estriol (E3, pregnancy)
- Menstrual cycle: low follicular phase, surge at ovulation, moderate luteal phase
- Brain effects: estradiol increases hippocampal spine density, BDNF, serotonin, dopamine
- Neuroprotection: estradiol is neuroprotective - reduces Alzheimer risk in premenopausal women
- Cardiovascular: estradiol maintains endothelial function, vasodilation, lipid profile
- Menopause: estradiol decline causes hot flashes, sleep disruption, mood changes, cognitive fog

## Progesterone

- Source: corpus luteum after ovulation, placenta during pregnancy, adrenal cortex
- Luteal phase: prepares endometrium for implantation
- Neurosteroid: progesterone metabolizes to allopregnanolone - GABA-A positive modulator
- Calming: allopregnanolone is anxiolytic and sedating - explains luteal phase calm
- PMDD: dysregulated allopregnanolone sensitivity - anxiety and mood instability pre-menstrual
- Pregnancy: high progesterone maintains sedation and prepares breast tissue

## HPA-HPG Crosstalk

- Stress suppresses reproduction: CRH directly inhibits GnRH pulsatility
- Cortisol: reduces LH sensitivity at gonadal level
- Functional hypothalamic amenorrhea: chronic stress or undereating suppresses GnRH - loss of cycle
- Adaptive logic: reproduction suspended when resources insufficient or threat present
- Testosterone: suppressed by chronic cortisol - explains stress-related low libido in males

## Sex Hormones and Neuroplasticity

- Estradiol: hippocampal spine density peaks at estrogen surge - 30 percent more synapses
- Testosterone: promotes dendritic arborization in amygdala and cortex
- DHEA: adrenal androgen - neurosteroid with neuroprotective and mood-enhancing effects
- Menstrual cognitive variation: verbal memory peaks in high-estrogen phases
- TCM parallel: Kidney Jing nourishes Brain-Marrow - hormonal substrate is Jing expression
