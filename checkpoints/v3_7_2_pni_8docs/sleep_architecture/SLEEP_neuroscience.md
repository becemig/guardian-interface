# Sleep Neuroscience

## Abstract

Sleep is actively generated by dedicated neural circuits rather than being a passive default state. The ventrolateral preoptic area (VLPO) is the primary sleep-promoting nucleus, inhibiting wake-promoting systems through GABAergic and galaninergic projections. The orexin/hypocretin system in the lateral hypothalamus is the primary wake-stabilizing system, maintaining arousal and preventing inappropriate sleep intrusions. These two systems form a bistable flip-flop switch - either fully awake or fully asleep, with rapid transitions between states. The two-process model of sleep regulation describes the interaction between homeostatic sleep pressure (Process S, driven by adenosine accumulation) and circadian alerting signal (Process C, driven by the SCN) that together determine sleep timing and depth.

## VLPO - Sleep Promoting Nucleus

- Location: ventrolateral preoptic area of the anterior hypothalamus
- Neurotransmitters: GABA and galanin - inhibitory
- Targets: inhibits LC (norepinephrine), raphe (serotonin), TMN (histamine), orexin neurons
- Activation: VLPO fires during NREM and REM sleep - silent during waking
- Damage: VLPO lesions cause chronic insomnia proportional to lesion size
- Adenosine input: adenosine accumulation activates VLPO - homeostatic pressure signal

## Orexin/Hypocretin Wake System

- Location: lateral hypothalamus - 50,000-80,000 neurons in humans
- Projections: diffuse - LC, raphe, TMN, basal forebrain, cortex, spinal cord
- Function: stabilizes waking state and prevents inappropriate sleep intrusions
- Narcolepsy: orexin neuron loss (autoimmune) causes narcolepsy with cataplexy
- Orexin and feeding: integrates energy status, arousal, and reward
- Suvorexant: orexin receptor antagonist - FDA approved insomnia treatment

## Flip-Flop Switch Model

- Bistable: system is either fully awake or fully asleep - intermediate states unstable
- Wake side: LC, raphe, TMN, orexin mutually excite each other and inhibit VLPO
- Sleep side: VLPO inhibits all wake-promoting nuclei simultaneously
- Orexin role: stabilizes the wake side - prevents flip to sleep during waking
- Narcolepsy model: without orexin stabilization, switch flips spontaneously to sleep

## Two-Process Model of Sleep Regulation

- Process S (homeostatic): sleep pressure builds during waking via adenosine accumulation
- Adenosine: byproduct of neural activity - accumulates in basal forebrain during waking
- Caffeine mechanism: adenosine receptor antagonist - blocks sleep pressure signal
- Process C (circadian): SCN generates alerting signal opposing sleep pressure during day
- Wake maintenance zone: late afternoon SCN alerting signal peaks - fights adenosine
- Sleep gate: evening SCN signal drops - adenosine wins - sleep onset occurs

## REM Sleep Generators

- Sublaterodorsal nucleus (SLD): brainstem REM-on neurons - glutamatergic
- LC and raphe: REM-off neurons - noradrenergic and serotonergic - silent during REM
- Atonia circuit: SLD activates spinal glycinergic neurons - skeletal muscle paralysis
- PGO waves: ponto-geniculo-occipital waves drive rapid eye movements and dream imagery
- Acetylcholine: basal forebrain cholinergic activation drives REM EEG desynchronization

## Thalamic Gating

- Thalamus: sensory relay station - gates information to cortex
- Waking: thalamus in tonic firing mode - passes sensory signals to cortex
- NREM sleep: thalamus in burst firing mode - spindles block sensory throughput
- Sleep spindles: reticular thalamic nucleus generates 12-15 Hz oscillations that close sensory gate
- Trauma disruption: PTSD thalamic gating failure allows sensory flooding and fragmented encoding
