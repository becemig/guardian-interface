# Rolfing and Structural Integration

## Abstract

Rolfing Structural Integration developed by Ida Rolf is a manual therapy system that reorganizes the myofascial system to achieve optimal alignment with gravity. Rolf proposed that the human body functions best when its major segments — head, thorax, pelvis, legs — are stacked vertically in the gravitational field. Chronic postural misalignment from injury, habit, and emotional holding shortens and thickens the fascia pulling segments out of optimal relationship. The ten-series Rolfing protocol systematically addresses the fascial restrictions of the entire body in a specific sequence designed to free the major joints and restore vertical organization. Thomas Myers extended Rolf's work through Anatomy Trains demonstrating the specific myofascial continuities connecting body segments. The Governing Vessel Du Mai traversing the posterior spinal midline provides the TCM structural axis corresponding to Rolf's vertical line.

## Gravity and Structure

- Ida Rolf proposed that the body's relationship to gravity is the primary determinant of postural and functional health.
- When major body segments are vertically aligned the body moves through gravity with minimum effort.
- Misalignment forces muscles to work continuously against gravity producing chronic tension and fatigue.
- Rolfing reorganizes the fascial system to restore vertical alignment and gravity-efficient movement.

## Ten-Series Protocol

- Session 1 opens the breath by releasing the ribcage and diaphragmatic fascial restrictions.
- Sessions 2 and 3 establish the foundation by organizing the feet, legs, and lateral line.
- Sessions 4 through 7 address the core — inner leg, psoas, pelvic floor, and deep spinal fascia.
- Sessions 8 through 10 integrate the whole body in movement restoring coordinated three-dimensional organization.

## Fascial Remodeling

- Sustained manual pressure to fascial restrictions produces thixotropic softening of the ground substance.
- Fibroblast mechanosensing through piezoelectric signaling responds to manual pressure by remodeling collagen.
- Myofibroblast relaxation in chronically contracted fascia releases sustained holding patterns.
- The remodeling effects of Rolfing persist long-term through the reorganization of fascial architecture.
