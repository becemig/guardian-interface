# Taijichuan Form Practice

## Abstract

The solo Taijichuan form is a moving meditation that embodies the complete cosmological framework of Taoist philosophy in physical movement — each posture expressing a specific energetic quality, each transition embodying a principle of change, and the complete form cycling through the full range of Yin-Yang and five element qualities in a continuous flowing sequence. The form is simultaneously a moving meditation, a neigong internal cultivation practice, a martial training method, and a health cultivation system — these dimensions are inseparable in authentic Taijichuan practice. The Yi-Qi-Li sequence is the classical teaching of the order of development in Taijichuan: Yi intention leads, Qi follows, Li force expresses — corresponding to the TCM principle that Yi directs Qi and Qi directs Blood and substance. In early practice Li muscular force dominates because Yi and Qi are not yet developed. Through years of practice Yi becomes more refined, Qi becomes more available, and Li becomes the spontaneous expression of Yi-Qi rather than the primary method. Martial application understanding is essential to authentic form practice — without understanding what each movement is doing martially, the practitioner cannot correctly express the specific jins and energetic qualities of each posture. Each posture has specific martial applications that reveal its underlying energetic principle. The Yang 108 posture form and the Chen Laojia first form are the two primary classical form traditions — the Yang form emphasizes the development of Song relaxation and the smooth continuous flow of Qi, while the Chen form emphasizes silk reeling and alternates between slow refined movement and explosive Fa Jin discharge. Embodied cosmology is the understanding that the complete Taijichuan form is a physical enactment of the Taoist cosmological process — from Wuji through Taiji through the eight trigrams and five elements and back to Wuji.

## Yi-Qi-Li Development

- Beginning stage: Li force dominates — the practitioner uses muscular effort to move through the form.
- Intermediate stage: Qi becomes available — the practitioner begins to feel and direct Qi through the form.
- Advanced stage: Yi leads — a subtle intention produces Qi movement which produces effortless Li force expression.
- Mastery: Yi-Qi-Li are one — intention, energy, and force arise and express as a single integrated event.

## Form as Neigong

- Each posture held or moved through with correct alignment and intent is a neigong practice.
- The slow meditative movement quality enables internal awareness that fast movement obscures.
- Breathing coordinated with movement in the form circulates Qi through the primary and extraordinary channels.
- Daily form practice produces cumulative neigong development comparable to dedicated seated cultivation.

## Embodied Cosmology

- Opening from Wuji to Taiji in the first movement enacts the cosmological emergence of duality from unity.
- Each posture name — White Crane Spreads Wings, Single Whip, Grasp Sparrow Tail — describes an energetic quality not just a shape.
- The complete form cycles through all eight trigram energies and all five element qualities.
- Returning to Wuji at the end of the form completes the cosmological cycle — from unity through multiplicity back to unity.

## TCM Correspondence

- Yi-Qi-Li sequence corresponds to the TCM principle that Yi intention directs Qi movement which directs Blood.
- Form as whole-body Qi circulation practice corresponds to TCM preventive medicine through daily Qi cultivation.
- Embodied cosmology of the form corresponds to TCM theory as the same cosmological framework applied to the body.
- Martial application understanding corresponds to the TCM principle that true understanding requires knowing the function.
