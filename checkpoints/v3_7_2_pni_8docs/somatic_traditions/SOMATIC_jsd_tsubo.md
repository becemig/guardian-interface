# Jin Shin Do Tsubo Theory

## Abstract

Tsubo is the Japanese term for acupressure point — literally meaning vase or jar, evoking the image of a vessel that holds and concentrates Qi. JSD tsubo theory expands this concept to understand each point as a vortex of Qi concentration — a place where the channel Qi gathers, pools, and can be accessed, redirected, and released by sustained pressure. The holding technique that distinguishes JSD from most other acupressure systems is the sustained two-hand hold of local and distal point combinations — rather than brief stimulation of individual points, the JSD practitioner holds two points simultaneously for extended periods ranging from one to several minutes, waiting for the release response to occur. The local point is the area of primary tension or armoring; the distal point is selected from the same channel or an energetically related channel to create a current between the two points that dissolves the holding between them. The release response in JSD manifests as a spontaneous softening of the tissue under the fingers, often accompanied by a palpable pulse change at the point, a release of held breath, a surge of warmth or tingling, emotional release, or spontaneous imagery. The energetic release sequence describes the typical progression of a JSD session — beginning with gentle contact and presence, progressing through increasingly deep holding as the client's system relaxes and trusts the contact, reaching the core tension of the primary armor segment, and completing with integrative holding that allows the released energy to settle and reorganize. Ashi points are tender spots that are not classical acupuncture points but represent local Qi accumulation — JSD works extensively with ashi points in combination with classical points to address the unique pattern of each individual's holding.

## Point as Vortex

- Each tsubo is a vortex of Qi concentration where channel energy pools and can be accessed.
- The vortex model explains why sustained pressure produces deeper effects than brief stimulation.
- Pressure into the vortex redirects accumulated Qi back into channel flow dissolving the accumulation.
- Ashi tender points are temporary vortices created by trauma, stress, or emotional holding.

## Two-Hand Holding Technique

- One hand holds the local tension point; the other holds a distal point on the same or related channel.
- The two-point hold creates a circuit between local accumulation and distal channel flow.
- Sustained holding for 1-3 minutes allows the circuit to dissolve the holding between the two points.
- The practitioner waits for the release response rather than imposing a predetermined technique.

## Release Response

- Tissue softening under the fingers is the primary physical release indicator.
- Pulse change at the held points — from tight and wiry to soft and even — indicates energetic release.
- Spontaneous breath release, warmth surge, or tingling confirms Qi movement through the held circuit.
- Emotional release, imagery, or spontaneous memory often accompanies deep tsubo release.

## TCM Correspondence

- Tsubo as Qi vortex corresponds to the TCM understanding of acupuncture points as places where channel Qi concentrates.
- Two-hand holding creating a circuit corresponds to the TCM technique of connecting local and distal points.
- Release response waiting corresponds to the TCM principle of following the Qi rather than forcing it.
- Ashi points as temporary Qi accumulations corresponds to the TCM concept of Ah Shi points in local treatment.
