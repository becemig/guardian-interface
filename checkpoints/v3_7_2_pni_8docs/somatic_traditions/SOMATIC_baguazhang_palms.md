# Baguazhang Eight Mother Palms

## Abstract

The Eight Mother Palms are the foundational movement vocabulary of Baguazhang — eight primary palm postures and their associated movement qualities that correspond to the eight trigrams of the I Ching and embody the eight fundamental forces of the cosmos in martial form. Each of the eight mother palms expresses a specific trigram quality — Heaven, Earth, Water, Fire, Thunder, Wind, Mountain, and Lake — through a characteristic hand position, body alignment, and movement strategy. The Single Palm Change is the most fundamental movement of Baguazhang — the most basic direction change on the circle that trains the essential Baguazhang principle of turning to face in a new direction while maintaining continuous movement. The Double Palm Change adds a second palm change within the same turning sequence — training the capacity to shift strategy twice within a single encounter. The eight animal forms associated with the eight mother palms — lion, snake, bear, eagle, rooster, dragon, phoenix, and monkey in various lineages — provide embodied archetypes that help students access the specific quality of each trigram through animal movement imagination. The Cheng style and Yin style represent the two primary transmission lineages from the founder Dong Haichuan — Cheng style emphasizing linear piercing force and Yin style emphasizing circular flowing evasion. Palm change mechanics are the technical heart of Baguazhang training — the specific body mechanics of the turn that integrate footwork, spinal rotation, and palm transition into a single unified movement expressing the trigram quality.

## Single Palm Change

- The single palm change is the foundational direction reversal of Baguazhang — turning to face the opposite direction.
- It trains the core principle of continuous movement while changing direction and strategy.
- The mechanics involve a specific footwork pattern, spinal rotation sequence, and palm transition.
- All more complex Baguazhang movements are elaborations of the single palm change principle.

## Eight Trigram Embodiment

- Heaven Qian palm — maximum structural completion, full Yang expression, overhead expanding force.
- Earth Kun palm — maximum yielding, full Yin reception, downward gathering and rooting force.
- Water Kan palm — penetrating through the center, direct piercing force, danger and depth.
- Fire Li palm — expanding outward from center, radiating heat and light, clarity and brilliance.
- Thunder Zhen palm — initiating movement, explosive first strike, arousing from stillness.
- Wind Xun palm — following and penetrating, gentle but persistent, wind through all openings.
- Mountain Gen palm — stillness within movement, stopping force, immovable root.
- Lake Dui palm — joyful yielding, opening and receptivity, reflecting force back to its source.

## Lineage Differences

- Cheng Tinghua style emphasizes linear direct piercing force — the snake and dragon archetypes predominate.
- Yin Fu style emphasizes circular evasion and flowing redirection — the ox tongue palm is characteristic.
- Both lineages derive from Dong Haichuan but express different aspects of the trigram system.
- Modern Baguazhang practice often integrates both lineage qualities.

## TCM Correspondence

- Eight mother palms corresponding to eight trigrams corresponds to the pakua organization of organ networks.

- The eight trigram body map corresponds to the eight extraordinary vessels governing constitutional body organization.
- Animal archetype embodiment corresponds to the Taoist tradition of cultivating animal Shen qualities in practice.
