# Gyrokinesis and Gyrotonic

## Abstract

Gyrokinesis and Gyrotonic are somatic movement systems developed by Romanian dancer Juliu Horvath that emphasize three-dimensional spinal articulation, circular joint movement, and coordinated breathing. Horvath developed these systems through his own recovery from injury drawing on principles from yoga, tai chi, gymnastics, and swimming. Gyrokinesis is the mat-based floor practice using fluid circular spinal movements coordinated with breath. Gyrotonic uses specialized equipment with weighted pulleys and rotational discs enabling three-dimensional resistance training through circular movement patterns. The system emphasizes spinal spiraling movements — seven spinal principles including arching, curling, lateral flexion, twisting, and circular movement — that mobilize the entire spinal column in all planes. The dragon spine movement in Gyrotonic — a spiraling three-dimensional spinal wave — corresponds directly to the Qigong and Tai Chi cultivation of the spinal dragon representing Kidney Yang ascending through the Governing Vessel.

## Seven Spinal Principles

- Arching and curling mobilize the spine in the sagittal plane through full flexion and extension.
- Lateral flexion mobilizes the spine in the frontal plane developing side-bending range.
- Twisting rotates the spine in the transverse plane developing rotational mobility.
- Circular movement combines multiple planes producing three-dimensional spinal articulation.
- Each principle is performed with coordinated breathing using specific breath patterns for each direction.

## Gyrotonic Equipment

- The Gyrotonic tower uses weighted pulleys providing resistance through circular movement arcs.
- The handle unit provides rotating grips enabling three-dimensional arm and spinal movement.
- The leg extension unit provides hip and leg circular movement with resistance.
- Equipment resistance can be adjusted to support or challenge specific movement ranges.

## Breathing Coordination

- Each Gyrokinesis movement sequence uses specific breath patterns coordinated with movement direction.
- Expansion movements correspond to inhalation while contraction movements correspond to exhalation.
- Breath coordination develops the connection between respiratory and spinal movement rhythms.
- This breathing coordination corresponds to the Qigong principle of coordinating breath with Qi movement.
