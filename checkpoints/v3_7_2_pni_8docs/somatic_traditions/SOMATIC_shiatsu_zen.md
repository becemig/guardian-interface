# Zen Shiatsu and Macrobiotic Shiatsu

## Abstract

Zen Shiatsu developed by Shizuto Masunaga is a synthesis of traditional Japanese shiatsu with Chinese meridian theory, Zen philosophy, and Western psychology that significantly expanded the classical meridian system and developed distinctive diagnostic and treatment principles. Masunaga extended the twelve classical meridians beyond their classical endpoints throughout the body — the meridian extensions — based on his palpatory research and clinical experience. Hara diagnosis — assessment of the abdomen through skilled palpation — provides the primary diagnostic information in Zen Shiatsu, identifying Kyo empty-deficient and Jitsu full-excess areas that indicate the most significant meridian imbalances. The Kyo-Jitsu dynamic is the central diagnostic concept — Kyo represents the area of greatest deficiency and deepest need while Jitsu represents the secondary compensatory excess that developed to protect the Kyo. Treatment always addresses the Kyo-Jitsu pair simultaneously — the practitioner holds the Kyo point with one hand providing supportive tonification while the other hand works the Jitsu area dispersing the excess. The two-hand connection maintaining simultaneous contact with the Kyo and Jitsu points is the distinguishing treatment principle of Zen Shiatsu. Perpendicular pressure applied with relaxed body weight rather than muscular force enables the practitioner's Ki to penetrate deeply without strain. Ki flow in TCM — the movement of Qi through the meridian network — corresponds to the Zen Shiatsu understanding of Ki as the fundamental life energy accessed and balanced through skilled perpendicular pressure.

## Hara Diagnosis

- The hara abdomen is palpated systematically to identify areas of Kyo and Jitsu corresponding to meridian states.
- Each meridian has a specific hara zone — the Kidney zone in the lower abdomen, Liver zone in the right hypochondrium.
- Kyo areas feel empty, unresponsive, and slightly sunken with a subtle pulling quality into the depths.
- Jitsu areas feel full, reactive, and resistant with a pushing quality against the palpating hand.

## Kyo-Jitsu Dynamic

- Kyo is the area of deepest deficiency — the root cause requiring the most careful supportive attention.
- Jitsu is the compensatory excess that developed to protect or mask the underlying Kyo deficiency.
- The Kyo-Jitsu pair always involves one Yin and one Yang meridian from the five element system.
- Treating the Kyo-Jitsu pair simultaneously produces rapid system-wide rebalancing.

## Two-Hand Connection

- The mother hand maintains supportive contact with the Kyo while the active hand works the Jitsu.
- This two-hand connection creates a circuit between the two meridian poles enabling energetic communication.
- The mother hand listens and supports while the active hand moves and disperses excess.
- This principle corresponds to the classical needling principle of tonifying one point while dispersing another.

## TCM Correspondence

- Ki flow as the fundamental life energy accessed through perpendicular pressure corresponds to TCM Qi in the meridians.
- Hara diagnosis of meridian states corresponds to TCM abdominal palpation and the Fu diagnostic system.
- Kyo-Jitsu treatment of meridian pairs corresponds to TCM treatment of excess-deficiency patterns across related channels.
- Masunaga's meridian extensions correspond to the sinew channel pathways of classical TCM.
