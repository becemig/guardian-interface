# Myofascial Force Transmission: Superficial Back Line vs. Taiyang Sinew Channel

## Abstract

This node maps the structural and functional overlap between Thomas Myers Superficial Back Line (SBL) and the Foot Taiyang Sinew Channel (Bladder Jing Jin). Both frameworks describe a continuous network of connective tissue running along the posterior aspect of the body, acting as an integration system for kinetic strain, spinal extension, and postural balance.

## Structural Intersections

The anatomical paths trace a near-identical sequence of fascial bands and muscle insertions from the plantar surface to the epicranial fascia.

### Plantar-to-Calcaneal Link

- Anatomy Trains SBL: Plantar fascia connects to short digital flexors, wrapping the calcaneus to interface with the Achilles tendon and gastrocnemius complex.
- Taiyang Jing Jin: Originates at the lateral fifth digit, anchoring at the heel before ascending the calcaneal tendon network.

### Posterior Pelvic-to-Spinal Link

- Anatomy Trains SBL: Sacrotuberous ligament routes tensile force from biceps femoris into sacral fascia and erector spinae pathways.
- Taiyang Jing Jin: Binds at the ischial tuberosity before branching across gluteal fascia to ascend along paraspinal columns.

## Biomechanical Relevance to Internal Movement Arts

During slow eccentric loading cycles such as Tai Chi and Qigong, force transmission shifts from isolated muscle contractions toward integrated whole-body fascial planes. Coordinating long exhalations with deliberate spinal articulation tensions the entire posterior sheet, providing sensory feedback that bridges physical structure with autonomic regulation.
