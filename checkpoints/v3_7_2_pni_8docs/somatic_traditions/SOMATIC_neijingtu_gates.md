# Three Gates and Five Passes

## Abstract

The three gates and five passes are the specific points of potential blockage on the Governing Vessel pathway that must be opened for the microcosmic orbit to circulate freely and for the Neidan alchemical process to proceed through the inner landscape. The three gates Wei Lu the tail gate, Jia Ji the squeeze spine, and Yu Zhen the jade pillow are the three critical constrictions on the ascending Governing Vessel pathway where accumulated tension, old trauma, and constitutional blockage most commonly impede the free circulation of refined Qi up the spine. Wei Lu the tail gate at the sacrococcygeal junction is the first and often most difficult gate — it corresponds to the accumulation of Kidney fear, survival tension, and constitutional compression in the sacral and pelvic floor region. Clearing Wei Lu requires releasing the deep holding patterns of the pelvic floor, sacrum, and coccyx that chronic fear and survival stress produce. Jia Ji the squeeze spine at the thoracic-lumbar junction approximately T5-T6 is the second gate — it corresponds to the holding patterns of the heart-lung region where grief, suppressed emotion, and chronic postural collapse create thoracic kyphosis and energetic blockage. Yu Zhen the jade pillow at the occiput is the third gate — it corresponds to the suboccipital tension, atlanto-occipital compression, and cognitive holding patterns that prevent refined Qi from entering the brain. The five passes are elaborations of the three gates adding the Mingmen and the crown as additional cultivation checkpoints. Opening the three gates is the primary physical and energetic preparation for all advanced Taoist cultivation practice.

## Wei Lu Tail Gate

- Located at the sacrococcygeal junction — the lower terminus of the Governing Vessel.
- Corresponds to accumulated Kidney fear, survival tension, and constitutional compression.
- Physical correlates: chronic pelvic floor holding, sacral compression, coccyx tension.
- Opening Wei Lu releases the constitutional fear holding allowing Qi to begin ascending the spine.

## Jia Ji Squeeze Spine

- Located at the mid-thoracic spine approximately T5-T6 — the heart-lung level.
- Corresponds to suppressed grief, heart-lung holding, and chronic thoracic kyphosis.
- Physical correlates: thoracic kyphosis, tight erector spinae, compressed thoracic vertebrae.
- Opening Jia Ji releases the heart-lung holding allowing Qi to continue ascending through the thorax.

## Yu Zhen Jade Pillow

- Located at the occiput-atlas junction — the posterior cranial base.
- Corresponds to suboccipital tension, cognitive holding, and resistance to upper Dan Tian cultivation.
- Physical correlates: suboccipital muscle hypertonicity, atlanto-occipital compression, cervical tension.
- Opening Yu Zhen allows refined Qi to enter the brain and begin the nine palace cultivation sequence.

## TCM Correspondence

- Three gates correspond to the three primary regions of Governing Vessel pathology in TCM — sacral, thoracic, occipital.
- Wei Lu at the sacrum corresponds to GV-1 Changqiang Long Strong at the coccyx tip.
- Jia Ji corresponds to the Huatuo jiaji points along the thoracic spine used in TCM for organ back-shu access.
- Yu Zhen corresponds to GV-17 Naohu Brain Door and GB-12 Wangu Mastoid at the occiput.
