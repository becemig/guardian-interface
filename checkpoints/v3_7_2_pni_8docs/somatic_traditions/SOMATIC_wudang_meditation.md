# Wudang Taoist Meditation

## Abstract

Wudang Mountain in Hubei province is the sacred center of the Taoist internal martial arts tradition — the legendary home of Zhang Sanfeng who is credited with developing Taijichuan and the Wudang internal arts system from Taoist cultivation principles. Where Shaolin embodies the Buddhist approach to somatic cultivation through disciplined effort and progressive overload, Wudang embodies the Taoist approach through yielding, emptiness, and the cultivation of Wu Wei effortless action. The fundamental difference between the Shaolin and Wudang approaches is the element: Shaolin is associated with fire and metal — effort, discipline, forging — while Wudang is associated with water — yielding, penetrating, finding the lowest path. Water methods Shui Fa are the characteristic Wudang meditation and movement approach — instead of developing force through strength, the practitioner develops force through the yielding penetrating quality of water. Still Water practice is the primary Wudang sitting meditation — unlike Chan wall-gazing which investigates the nature of mind directly, Wudang still water practice cultivates the quality of still water as a metaphor and model: clear, reflective, undisturbed at the surface while moving powerfully in the depths. Three Treasures cultivation Jing-Qi-Shen through the Wudang Neidan tradition follows the inner landscape framework of the Nei Jing Tu — gathering Jing at the lower Dan Tian, refining it into Qi through cultivation practice, and further refining Qi into Shen in the upper Dan Tian. Xing Ming cultivation — the simultaneous development of Xing nature-spirit and Ming destiny-body — is the Wudang synthesis of the meditative and physical dimensions of practice. Wu Wei effortless action is both the goal and the method — practicing from a place of natural flow rather than forced effort.

## Water Methods

- Water methods use the natural yielding penetrating quality of water as the model for movement and force.
- Instead of meeting force with force, water flows around, under, and through all resistance.
- Wudang water methods develop force that penetrates through yielding rather than opposing directly.
- The water metaphor corresponds to the Tao Te Ching teaching that the softest overcomes the hardest.

## Still Water Practice

- Still water sitting meditation cultivates the quality of a still mountain lake — clear, undisturbed, reflective.
- Surface stillness enables deep perception — the disturbed mind cannot clearly perceive what arises.
- The practice cultivates the Kidney-Water quality of deep stillness underlying all movement.
- This corresponds to the TCM cultivation of Kidney Zhi as the deep constitutional stillness underlying all activity.

## Three Treasures Cultivation

- Lower Dan Tian gathering: collecting scattered Jing essence into the lower cauldron through still seated practice.
- Middle Dan Tian refining: transforming gathered Jing into refined Qi through breath and visualization work.
- Upper Dan Tian clarifying: further refining Qi into clear Shen spirit through deep meditation and inner landscape work.
- The three-stage process corresponds to the Neidan River Cart circulation through the three Dan Tian centers.

## TCM Correspondence

- Water methods corresponding to Kidney Water yielding-penetrating quality connects Wudang directly to Kidney cultivation.
- Wu Wei effortless action corresponds to the TCM concept of harmonious Qi flow without stagnation or forcing.
- Three Treasures cultivation corresponds to the Jing-Qi-Shen refinement sequence of Taoist internal alchemy.
- Xing Ming cultivation corresponds to the TCM integration of spirit Shen with constitutional vitality Jing.
