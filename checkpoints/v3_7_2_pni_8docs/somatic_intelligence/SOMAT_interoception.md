# Interoception Deep Dive

## Abstract

Interoception — the sense of the internal physiological state of the body, encompassing the detection, interpretation, and integration of signals from the viscera, cardiovascular system, respiratory system, and all internal organs — is now recognized as a fundamental sensory modality as important as the exteroceptive senses, governing emotion, homeostasis, self-awareness, decision-making, and the sense of bodily self that underlies all conscious experience. A.D. Craig's landmark framework distinguishing interoception as a distinct sensory system — with its own anatomical pathway from lamina I spinothalamic neurons through the posterior insula to the anterior insula — established the neuroanatomical basis for understanding interoception as the foundation of the subjective feeling state: the anterior insula integrates interoceptive signals with emotional, cognitive, and social context to produce the felt sense of the body that constitutes the substrate of emotional experience. Interoceptive accuracy — the objective performance on interoceptive detection tasks such as the heartbeat detection task — measures the correspondence between actual physiological signals and consciously perceived signals, with high interoceptive accuracy associated with better emotional regulation, more accurate emotional recognition, higher body awareness, and better clinical outcomes in somatic and psychological disorders. Interoceptive sensibility — the subjective confidence in one's own interoceptive ability, measured through questionnaires — is dissociable from interoceptive accuracy, with the relationship between sensibility and accuracy varying across individuals and clinical populations: high sensibility with low accuracy characterizes anxiety disorders where the patient feels hyperaware of body signals but misinterprets them; low sensibility with high accuracy may characterize alexithymia. Predictive interoception — Karl Friston's active inference framework applied to interoception — proposes that the brain does not passively receive interoceptive signals but actively generates predictions about the body's physiological state, with interoceptive experience arising from the comparison of predictions with actual afferent signals: interoceptive disorders arise from miscalibrated predictions rather than abnormal signals. The Zang Fu inner sensing correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that the practitioner cultivates the capacity to sense the state of each organ network through the pulse, the complexion, and the direct inner sensing of Qi — a tradition of systematic interoceptive cultivation that corresponds to the modern science of interoceptive accuracy training.

## Interoceptive Pathways

- Lamina I spinothalamic neurons: small-diameter afferents from viscera conveying physiological state.
- Posterior insula: primary interoceptive cortex receiving thalamic relay of lamina I input.
- Anterior insula: integrates interoceptive signals with emotional and cognitive context.
- Anterior insula right hemisphere: the primary substrate of the subjective feeling state of the body.

## Three Dimensions of Interoception

- Interoceptive accuracy: objective heartbeat detection task performance — signal detection.
- Interoceptive sensibility: subjective confidence in body awareness — questionnaire measure.
- Interoceptive awareness: metacognitive accuracy — knowing when your interoception is accurate.
- These three dimensions are dissociable and have different clinical profiles across disorders.

## Predictive Interoception

- Brain generates top-down predictions about body state based on prior experience and context.
- Interoceptive experience arises from prediction error: difference between prediction and signal.
- Chronic pain: high prediction of threat amplifies normal nociceptive signals into pain.
- Anxiety: high threat prediction amplifies normal interoceptive signals into alarm.
