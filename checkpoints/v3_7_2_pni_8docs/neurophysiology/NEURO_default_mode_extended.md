# Default Mode Network Extended

## Abstract

The default mode network DMN is a set of brain regions — medial prefrontal cortex mPFC, posterior cingulate cortex PCC, angular gyrus, and medial temporal lobe — that are consistently active during rest and deactivated during externally directed tasks. Randy Buckner's comprehensive review identified three primary DMN functions: self-referential processing — thinking about oneself, one's traits, and personal experiences, social cognition — thinking about other minds, their intentions and perspectives, and episodic future thinking — imagining future scenarios and possibilities. Mind wandering — the spontaneous drift of thought away from the current task — is mediated by the DMN and is associated with unhappiness in Killingsworth and Gilbert's experience sampling study. Depression involves hyperactivation of the DMN particularly the mPFC-PCC circuit producing excessive self-referential rumination. Mindfulness meditation consistently reduces DMN activity and increases task-positive network activity corresponding to present-moment attention. The DMN's role in narrative self-construction — the ongoing autobiographical story the mind tells about itself — makes it the neural substrate of the psychological self. Shen narrative in TCM — the ongoing story the Heart-Mind tells about itself and its experience constituting personal identity — corresponds to DMN self-referential processing as its neurobiological substrate.

## Self-Referential Processing

- The mPFC is the primary node for trait self-description — thinking about one's own characteristics.
- The PCC integrates self-relevant information with autobiographical memory and spatial context.
- Self-referential processing activates the DMN while externally directed attention deactivates it.
- Meditation reduces self-referential DMN activity corresponding to the reduced self-preoccupation of contemplative states.

## Social Cognition

- The DMN supports mentalizing — reasoning about other people's mental states and intentions.
- The temporoparietal junction within the DMN is specifically activated during theory of mind tasks.
- Social exclusion and ostracism activate the DMN's social pain circuits in the dACC and anterior insula.
- Impaired DMN social cognition contributes to the social difficulties of autism and schizophrenia.

## Mind Wandering

- Mind wandering occupies approximately 47 percent of waking thought based on experience sampling.
- Mind wandering is associated with unhappiness — people are less happy when their minds wander regardless of content.
- Prospective mind wandering — thinking about the future — is more common and less aversive than past-focused wandering.
- Mindfulness practice reduces mind wandering frequency and improves the quality of present-moment experience.
