# Cortisol Rhythm and HPA Axis Regulation

## Abstract

Cortisol is the primary glucocorticoid stress hormone produced by the adrenal cortex under hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis control. Cortisol follows a robust diurnal rhythm peaking within 30 to 45 minutes of awakening in the cortisol awakening response and declining through the day to nadir at midnight. This rhythm is entrained by the suprachiasmatic nucleus circadian clock and modulated by sleep quality, light exposure, stress, and lifestyle factors. Allostatic load describes the cumulative physiological cost of chronic stress adaptation as dysregulated HPA axis activity damages multiple organ systems. Burnout syndrome involving HPA axis exhaustion with flattened cortisol rhythm corresponds to severe Kidney Jing depletion in TCM. Practice-induced HPA axis normalization reduces allostatic load and supports Kidney Jing conservation.

## Diurnal Cortisol Rhythm

- The cortisol awakening response produces a 50 to 100 percent increase in cortisol within 30 minutes of waking.
- The CAR is entrained by the suprachiasmatic nucleus and represents preparation for the demands of the waking day.
- Cortisol declines through the morning and afternoon reaching nadir around midnight.
- Disrupted CAR — blunted or exaggerated — indicates HPA axis dysregulation from chronic stress or burnout.

## Allostatic Load

- Allostatic load is the cumulative wear from chronic HPA axis activation and its downstream effects.
- Chronic cortisol elevation damages the hippocampus reducing its negative feedback on the HPA axis.
- This hippocampal damage reduces cortisol suppression creating a vicious cycle of escalating HPA activation.
- Allostatic load manifests as cardiovascular disease, immune suppression, metabolic syndrome, and cognitive decline.

## Burnout and HPA Exhaustion

- Burnout syndrome involves progressive HPA axis exhaustion with flattening of the cortisol diurnal rhythm.
- Blunted CAR in burnout reflects adrenal exhaustion and loss of circadian HPA responsiveness.
- Burnout corresponds to severe Kidney Jing depletion with Kidney Yang deficiency in TCM.
- Recovery requires radical reduction of demands, restoration of sleep, and constitutional Kidney support.
