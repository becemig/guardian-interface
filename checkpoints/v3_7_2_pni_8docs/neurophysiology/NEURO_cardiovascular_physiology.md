# Cardiovascular Physiology: Cardiac Cycle, Frank-Starling, Coronary Circulation, and Heart Qi

## Abstract

The cardiovascular system is the primary transport network delivering oxygen, nutrients, and hormonal signals throughout the body while removing metabolic waste products. The heart is a dual pump driving pulmonary and systemic circulation through precisely coordinated electrical activation and mechanical contraction. The Frank-Starling mechanism governs cardiac output through the intrinsic relationship between ventricular filling and stroke volume. The coronary circulation supplies the myocardium itself and is the site of the most lethal cardiovascular pathology. Heart Qi in TCM governs the pumping and circulation functions corresponding to cardiac contractility and output while Heart Blood nourishes the vessels and tissues. Internal movement arts produce robust cardiovascular adaptations including reduced resting heart rate, improved baroreflex sensitivity, and enhanced HRV.

## Cardiac Cycle

### Systole

- Ventricular systole begins with isovolumetric contraction as the ventricles contract before the aortic and pulmonary valves open.
- Ejection phase follows as ventricular pressure exceeds arterial pressure opening the semilunar valves.
- The left ventricle ejects blood into the aorta at pressures of 120 mmHg systolic in normal adults.
- Stroke volume is the volume ejected per beat averaging 70 mL at rest with ejection fraction of 55 to 70 percent.

### Diastole

- Isovolumetric relaxation follows valve closure as the ventricles relax before the atrioventricular valves open.
- Rapid filling phase follows as the mitral and tricuspid valves open and blood flows from atria to ventricles.
- Atrial contraction contributes approximately 20 percent of ventricular filling at rest and more at high heart rates.
- Diastolic dysfunction reflecting impaired ventricular relaxation is the earliest cardiac manifestation of hypertension and aging.

### Cardiac Output

- Cardiac output equals heart rate multiplied by stroke volume averaging 5 liters per minute at rest.
- During maximal exercise cardiac output can reach 20 to 25 liters per minute in trained individuals.
- Autonomic regulation governs both heart rate through sinoatrial node modulation and stroke volume through contractility.
- HRV reflects the dynamic autonomic regulation of heart rate providing a window into cardiac autonomic health.

## Frank-Starling Mechanism

- The Frank-Starling law states that stroke volume increases with ventricular preload up to physiological limits.
- Greater diastolic filling stretches myocardial sarcomeres increasing actin-myosin overlap and contractile force.
- This intrinsic mechanism allows the heart to match output to venous return without requiring neural or hormonal input.
- Impaired Frank-Starling function in heart failure reduces the capacity to increase output with increased preload.

## Coronary Circulation

### Anatomy

- The left coronary artery divides into the left anterior descending and circumflex arteries supplying the left ventricle.
- The right coronary artery supplies the right ventricle, inferior left ventricle, and sinoatrial node in most individuals.
- Coronary blood flow occurs primarily during diastole when myocardial compression is released.
- Coronary flow reserve is the ratio of maximal to resting flow and reflects microvascular health and collateral development.

### Coronary Pathology

- Atherosclerotic plaque accumulation in coronary arteries produces progressive luminal narrowing and ischemia.
- Plaque rupture triggers acute thrombus formation producing myocardial infarction.
- Endothelial dysfunction the earliest atherosclerotic change is driven by oxidative stress, inflammation, and hemodynamic shear.
- Internal arts practice reduces atherosclerotic risk through anti-inflammatory, antihypertensive, and endothelial protective mechanisms.

## Baroreflex

- The arterial baroreflex is the primary short-term blood pressure regulatory mechanism.
- Baroreceptors in the carotid sinus and aortic arch detect arterial wall stretch and signal the nucleus tractus solitarius.
- Increased pressure activates baroreflex producing vagal bradycardia and sympathetic withdrawal reducing pressure.
- Baroreflex sensitivity BRS measured as the slope of the relationship between blood pressure and RR interval is a key cardiovascular health index.
- Tai Chi practice improves baroreflex sensitivity in older adults and hypertensive populations.

## TCM Correspondence

- Heart Qi governs the pumping force of the cardiac muscle corresponding to contractility and cardiac output.
- Heart Blood nourishes the vessel walls and tissues corresponding to coronary circulation and endothelial health.
- The pulse reflects Heart Qi strength, regularity, and the quality of Blood circulation through the vessels.
- Pericardium protects the Heart and governs circulation corresponding to pericardial anatomy and coronary protective function.
- Heart Yang corresponds to sympathetic cardiac drive while Heart Yin corresponds to parasympathetic and vagal tone.

## Practice Effects

- Tai Chi produces significant reductions in resting systolic and diastolic blood pressure in hypertensive populations.
- Resting heart rate reduction from regular practice reflects enhanced vagal tone and improved cardiac efficiency.
- HRV improvement from practice reflects enhanced baroreflex sensitivity and cardiac autonomic modulation.
- Endothelial function improvement from practice reduces atherosclerotic risk through nitric oxide pathway enhancement.
- Left ventricular diastolic function improves with regular internal arts practice in older adults.
