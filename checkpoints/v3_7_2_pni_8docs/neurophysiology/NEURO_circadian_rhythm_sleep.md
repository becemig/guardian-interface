# Circadian Rhythm, Sleep Architecture, and the TCM Organ Clock

## Abstract

Circadian rhythms are approximately 24-hour biological cycles governed by the suprachiasmatic nucleus of the hypothalamus and entrained by light-dark cycles. Sleep is organized into ultradian cycles of slow-wave and REM sleep stages each serving distinct neurological and somatic restoration functions. The TCM organ clock describes a two-hour peak activity cycle rotating through all twelve primary meridians over 24 hours. This traditional framework anticipates contemporary chronobiology by mapping organ system activity rhythms that correspond to measurable physiological parameters including cortisol, melatonin, and HRV diurnal variation.

## Circadian Biology

### Suprachiasmatic Nucleus

- The suprachiasmatic nucleus in the anterior hypothalamus is the master circadian pacemaker.
- Retinal light input via the retinohypothalamic tract entrains the suprachiasmatic nucleus to the solar day.
- The suprachiasmatic nucleus coordinates peripheral circadian clocks in every cell of the body through hormonal and neural signals.
- Disruption of suprachiasmatic nucleus function produces circadian misalignment underlying shift work disorder and jet lag.

### Melatonin and Cortisol Rhythm

- Melatonin secreted by the pineal gland rises after darkness onset peaking between 2 and 4 AM.
- Melatonin signals darkness and promotes sleep onset through hypothalamic temperature reduction.
- Cortisol rises sharply in the early morning hours peaking within 30 minutes of waking as the cortisol awakening response.
- The reciprocal melatonin-cortisol rhythm reflects the fundamental rest-activity cycle of the HPA and pineal systems.

## Sleep Architecture

### Slow Wave Sleep

- Slow wave sleep comprises stages N2 and N3 of non-REM sleep characterized by delta wave EEG activity.
- Slow wave sleep is the primary period of physical restoration including growth hormone release and tissue repair.
- Glymphatic system activation during slow wave sleep clears metabolic waste including amyloid beta from the brain.
- Disruption of slow wave sleep impairs physical recovery, immune function, and HPA axis regulation.

### REM Sleep

- Rapid eye movement sleep is characterized by theta wave EEG, muscle atonia, and vivid dreaming.
- REM sleep is the primary period of emotional memory consolidation and psychological integration.
- REM sleep deprivation produces emotional dysregulation, impaired threat assessment, and reduced empathy.
- The proportion of REM sleep increases in the later sleep cycles of the night peaking in the early morning hours.

### HRV During Sleep

- HRV is highest during slow wave sleep reflecting maximal parasympathetic dominance and cardiac restoration.
- HRV decreases during REM sleep as autonomic activity fluctuates with dream content.
- Sleep HRV quality predicts daytime cognitive performance, emotional regulation, and physical readiness.
- Consistent sleep timing improves HRV through circadian stabilization of autonomic nervous system tone.

## TCM Organ Clock

- The organ clock describes two-hour peak activity windows for each of the twelve primary meridians.
- Lung meridian peaks 3 to 5 AM corresponding to the transition from sleep to waking and optimal respiratory function.
- Large Intestine peaks 5 to 7 AM the optimal time for morning elimination.
- Stomach peaks 7 to 9 AM supporting optimal digestive function during breakfast.
- Heart peaks 11 AM to 1 PM during midday activity and social engagement.
- Kidney peaks 5 to 7 PM supporting restoration and Jing consolidation before evening rest.
- Gallbladder peaks 11 PM to 1 AM during the critical first slow wave sleep cycle.
- Liver peaks 1 to 3 AM during deep sleep when Blood returns to the Liver for purification.

## Practice Implications

- Morning Qigong practice during the Lung and Large Intestine peak hours optimizes respiratory and eliminative function.
- Evening Tai Chi during the Kidney and Pericardium hours supports pre-sleep autonomic downregulation.
- HRV measured at consistent times of day accounts for diurnal variation in autonomic baseline.
- Sleep hygiene practices that protect Gallbladder and Liver peak hours support the critical restorative functions of deep and REM sleep.
