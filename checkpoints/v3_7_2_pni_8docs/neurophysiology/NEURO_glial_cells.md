# Glial Cells and Neural Support

## Abstract

Glial cells comprising astrocytes, microglia, oligodendrocytes, and ependymal cells constitute approximately half the cells in the brain and perform essential functions in neural support, immune defense, myelination, and brain homeostasis. Astrocytes regulate synaptic transmission through the tripartite synapse, maintain the blood-brain barrier, support metabolic coupling with neurons, and drive glymphatic waste clearance. Microglia are the brain's resident immune cells performing surveillance, synaptic pruning, and inflammatory responses. Oligodendrocytes produce and maintain myelin while providing metabolic trophic support to axons. Glial dysfunction underlies neuroinflammation, neurodegeneration, and psychiatric disease. Kidney Marrow as the constitutional substrate of brain health corresponds to the glial support network maintaining the neural environment.

## Astrocytes

- Astrocytes form the tripartite synapse with pre and postsynaptic neurons modulating synaptic transmission.
- They clear excess glutamate preventing excitotoxicity and recycle it as glutamine for neuronal use.
- Astrocyte endfeet cover brain capillaries forming the blood-brain barrier and regulating cerebral blood flow.
- During sleep astrocyte aquaporin-4 channels drive glymphatic CSF flow clearing metabolic waste.

## Microglia

- Microglia are the brain's resident macrophages performing continuous surveillance of neural tissue health.
- They perform synaptic pruning during development eliminating weak synapses and refining neural circuits.
- Activated microglia produce inflammatory cytokines TNF-alpha IL-1beta and IL-6 driving neuroinflammation.
- Chronic microglial activation in neurodegeneration contributes to progressive neural damage.

## Oligodendrocytes

- Oligodendrocytes myelinate CNS axons and provide metabolic support through lactate transfer.
- Each oligodendrocyte myelinates up to 50 axon segments making it essential for white matter integrity.
- Oligodendrocyte loss in multiple sclerosis produces demyelination and axon degeneration.
- Exercise and sleep support oligodendrocyte health reducing neurodegeneration risk.
