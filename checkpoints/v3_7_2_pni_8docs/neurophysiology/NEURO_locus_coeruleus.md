# Locus Coeruleus and Norepinephrine

## Abstract

The locus coeruleus LC is a small pigmented nucleus in the pontine brainstem containing the largest concentration of noradrenergic neurons in the brain — approximately 15,000 neurons in humans that project to virtually every brain region providing the primary source of cortical and subcortical norepinephrine. Gary Aston-Jones and Jonathan Cohen's adaptive gain theory proposes that the LC-NE system modulates the gain of neural processing — at low tonic activity the system is in exploration mode with broad diffuse attention, at optimal intermediate tonic activity the system is in exploitation mode with focused task-related attention, and at high tonic activity the system shows degraded task performance despite high arousal. Phasic LC responses to task-relevant stimuli are superimposed on tonic firing and promote rapid task-relevant neural processing. The LC-NE system is the primary mediator of the stress arousal response preceding and accompanying the HPA axis activation. ADHD involves insufficient NE signaling in prefrontal cortex circuits — atomoxetine and guanfacine treat ADHD by targeting noradrenergic pathways. PTSD hyperarousal reflects chronically elevated LC-NE tone producing persistent hypervigilance, exaggerated startle, and sleep disruption. Shen vigilance in TCM — the Heart-Mind's alerting function that rapidly orients attention to significant stimuli — corresponds to the LC-NE system as its neurobiological substrate.

## Adaptive Gain Theory

- Tonic LC activity sets the mode of neural processing — exploration at low, exploitation at optimal, degradation at high.
- Phasic LC responses to relevant stimuli promote rapid processing of those stimuli through gain modulation.
- The optimal tonic level produces the best balance of focused attention and cognitive flexibility.
- Chronic stress shifts tonic LC activity to high levels producing the hyperarousal of anxiety and PTSD.

## Norepinephrine and Cognition

- NE in the prefrontal cortex enhances working memory and cognitive control through alpha-2A receptor activation.
- NE in the amygdala consolidates emotionally significant memories enhancing fear learning and threat memory.
- NE modulates sensory cortex gain enhancing signal-to-noise ratio for attended stimuli.
- Noradrenergic depletion produces deficits in attention, working memory, and cognitive flexibility.

## LC and Stress

- The LC receives direct input from the central amygdala enabling threat signals to rapidly elevate noradrenergic tone.
- LC activation precedes HPA axis activation providing the initial rapid arousal response to threat.
- Chronic stress produces LC sensitization — progressively stronger responses to equivalent stressors.
- Prazosin blocking alpha-1 NE receptors reduces PTSD nightmares by reducing LC-driven arousal during sleep.
