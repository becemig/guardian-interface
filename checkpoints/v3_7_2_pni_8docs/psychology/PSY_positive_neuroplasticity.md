# Positive Neuroplasticity

## Abstract

Positive neuroplasticity training developed by Rick Hanson applies the neuroscience of experience-dependent plasticity to the deliberate cultivation of positive mental states and traits — using the brain's capacity to rewire itself through experience to intentionally build neural resources for wellbeing, resilience, and wisdom. The foundational insight is the negativity bias — the brain's evolved tendency to register, store, and weight negative experiences more heavily than positive ones, as Hanson puts it the brain is like Velcro for negative and Teflon for positive experiences. This asymmetry served evolutionary survival but creates a default tendency toward anxiety, pessimism, and dissatisfaction that exceeds what circumstances warrant. Taking in the good is the core positive neuroplasticity practice — deliberately enriching and extending the experience of positive states to convert fleeting emotional experiences into lasting neural traits. The HEAL practice encodes this: Have a beneficial experience, Enrich it through sensory detail and emotional depth, Absorb it by sensing it sinking in, and optionally Link it with a related difficult experience to gradually transform it. Self-directed neuroplasticity refers to the use of intentional mental practice to shape the brain's structure and function — supported by research showing that meditation, gratitude practice, and positive visualization produce measurable structural brain changes. The three pillars of Hanson's approach are: growing inner strengths including safety, satisfaction, and connection, installing these resources through taking in the good, and using installed resources to regulate difficult states. Heart Shen cultivation in TCM — the progressive development of the Heart-Mind's luminous awareness, compassion, and wisdom through sustained cultivation practice — corresponds to positive neuroplasticity as its Western scientific framework.

## Negativity Bias

- The brain registers negative experiences faster, more intensely, and stores them more durably than positive ones.
- This asymmetry evolved for survival — better to overestimate threat than to miss a real danger.
- In modern low-threat environments it creates unnecessary suffering through chronic low-grade anxiety and dissatisfaction.
- Positive neuroplasticity training deliberately counterbalances this bias through intentional positive state cultivation.

## Taking in the Good

- Taking in the good enriches and extends positive experiences to convert them into lasting neural traits.
- HEAL: Have a beneficial experience, Enrich it, Absorb it, Link it with difficult material.
- Enriching involves staying with the positive experience for 20-30 seconds minimum to enable synaptic consolidation.
- Absorption involves sensing the experience sinking into the body as a physical felt sense.

## Self-Directed Neuroplasticity

- Mental practice produces structural brain changes — meditation thickens the insula and prefrontal cortex.
- Gratitude practice increases activity in the medial prefrontal cortex and ACC corresponding to positive reappraisal.
- Loving-kindness meditation increases positive affect, social connectedness, and vagal tone.
- Consistent practice over weeks to months produces measurable trait-level changes in brain function.
