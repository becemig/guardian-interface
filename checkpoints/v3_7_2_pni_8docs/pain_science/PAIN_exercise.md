# Exercise and Pain Neuroscience

## Abstract

Exercise and pain neuroscience — the investigation of exercise-induced hypoalgesia, the well-documented phenomenon of reduced pain sensitivity following acute aerobic exercise, and the mechanisms through which regular exercise training produces lasting improvements in chronic pain conditions including fibromyalgia, chronic low back pain, osteoarthritis, and neuropathic pain, through multiple mechanisms including endorphin and endocannabinoid release, enhancement of descending pain inhibitory controls, reduction of central sensitization through anti-inflammatory effects, and the reconditioning of the fear-avoidance cycle through graded exposure to movement. Exercise-induced hypoalgesia — the reduction in pain sensitivity measured by pressure pain threshold, heat pain threshold, and ischemic pain tolerance that occurs during and for up to 30 minutes following aerobic exercise at moderate to vigorous intensities, mediated through the activation of endogenous opioid, endocannabinoid, and serotonergic systems and through the activation of descending pain inhibitory pathways, with the magnitude of hypoalgesia dose-dependently related to exercise intensity and greatest at large muscle mass exercises. Endorphin and endocannabinoid exercise analgesia — the dual neuromodulator mechanisms of exercise-induced pain relief, with beta-endorphin released from the pituitary into the bloodstream during vigorous exercise activating peripheral and central mu-opioid receptors contributing to the runner's high and pain threshold elevation, and with anandamide the primary endocannabinoid also rising substantially during vigorous aerobic exercise and crossing the blood-brain barrier to activate CB1 receptors in pain-modulating circuits — with the runner's high now attributed primarily to the endocannabinoid system rather than endorphins based on molecular pharmacology studies. Graded exercise for chronic pain — the clinical application of progressive exercise despite pain using a time-contingent rather than pain-contingent pacing approach, gradually increasing activity duration and intensity according to a predetermined schedule rather than in response to pain levels, progressively reversing the deconditioning, fear-avoidance, and reduced pain inhibitory capacity that characterize chronic pain, with aerobic exercise, strength training, and mind-body exercises all demonstrating efficacy across different chronic pain conditions. The Wei Qi activating movement correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that movement activates and circulates Wei Qi the defensive Qi of the superficial body, opening the channels and preventing the stagnation that underlies pain — a classical framework corresponding to the modern understanding that exercise activates endogenous pain inhibitory systems and reverses the physiological changes that perpetuate chronic pain.

## Exercise Induced Hypoalgesia

- Pressure pain threshold: increases 15-40 percent following aerobic exercise.
- Heat pain threshold: elevated during and after moderate to vigorous exercise.
- Mechanism: endorphin endocannabinoid descending inhibitory activation.
- Dose response: higher intensity greater hypoalgesia — large muscle mass exercises.

## Endocannabinoid Analgesia

- Anandamide: rises significantly during vigorous aerobic exercise.
- Blood-brain barrier: AEA crosses BBB — central CB1 activation.
- Runner's high: now attributed primarily to endocannabinoid not endorphin.
- CB1 receptors: PAG and RVM — descending pain inhibitory activation.

## Graded Exercise

- Time-contingent: pacing by time not pain — breaks pain-contingent avoidance.
- Progressive: gradual increase in duration intensity — reconditioning.
- Aerobic: walking cycling swimming — broad chronic pain evidence base.
- Mind-body: Tai Chi yoga — pain disability quality of life improvements.

## TCM Correspondence

- Kidney Yang activating through exercise corresponds to exercise reconditioning reversing the hypo-arousal of chronic pain deconditioning.
