# Pain Measurement and Assessment

## Abstract

Pain measurement and assessment — the systematic evaluation of pain across its multiple dimensions including intensity, quality, temporal pattern, spatial distribution, functional impact, and psychological context using validated instruments ranging from simple numerical rating scales to comprehensive multidimensional batteries and objective quantitative sensory testing protocols, with the IMMPACT consensus recommendations identifying six core outcome domains for chronic pain clinical trials including pain intensity, physical functioning, emotional functioning, patient global impression, symptoms and adverse events, and participant disposition. Quantitative sensory testing — the standardized psychophysical protocol developed by the German Research Network on Neuropathic Pain applying a battery of thermal and mechanical stimuli to assess the function of specific sensory fiber types and central pain processing, with the QST protocol including measurements of thermal detection and pain thresholds, mechanical detection and pain thresholds, pressure pain thresholds, vibration detection thresholds, and temporal summation and conditioned pain modulation efficiency — providing a comprehensive profile of peripheral and central sensitization that informs diagnosis and treatment selection. Patient reported outcome measures — the validated self-report instruments capturing the patient's subjective experience of pain including the Brief Pain Inventory measuring pain intensity and interference with function, the McGill Pain Questionnaire capturing pain quality through sensory, affective, and evaluative word descriptors, the Pain Catastrophizing Scale measuring the three components of catastrophizing, and the PROMIS pain instruments providing computer-adaptive assessment of pain intensity and behavior. Conditioned pain modulation clinical application — the bedside assessment of descending pain inhibitory efficiency using a standardized conditioning stimulus such as cold pressor or ischemic tourniquet to measure the inhibition produced in test stimulus pain at a remote site, with reduced CPM efficiency predicting chronic pain development, poor response to NSAIDs, and better response to centrally-acting analgesics including SNRIs and tramadol — making CPM assessment a clinically useful guide to analgesic selection. The Four Examinations applied to pain syndrome correspondence in TCM describes the classical diagnostic framework of looking, listening and smelling, asking, and palpating applied to the assessment of pain — including the visual assessment of complexion and posture, the auditory assessment of voice quality and breathing, the systematic inquiry into pain character onset location quality and modifying factors, and the palpation of tender points, pulse, and abdomen — a comprehensive assessment framework corresponding to the modern multidimensional pain assessment approach.

## QST Protocol

- Thermal thresholds: cold detection heat detection cold pain heat pain thresholds.
- Mechanical thresholds: touch detection mechanical pain threshold allodynia.
- Pressure pain: PPT at multiple body sites — widespread sensitization assessment.
- Temporal summation: wind-up assessment — central sensitization indicator.

## Patient Reported Outcomes

- NRS: 0-10 numerical rating scale — simplest most widely used.
- BPI: Brief Pain Inventory — intensity and interference 7 domains.
- McGill: sensory affective evaluative descriptors — quality characterization.
- PCS: Pain Catastrophizing Scale — helplessness rumination magnification.

## CPM Clinical Use

- Protocol: cold pressor conditioning — test stimulus PPT before and after.
- Reduced CPM: predicts chronic pain risk and NSAID poor response.
- Enhanced CPM: predicts better response to peripherally acting analgesics.
- SNRIs: duloxetine improves CPM — guides analgesic selection.
