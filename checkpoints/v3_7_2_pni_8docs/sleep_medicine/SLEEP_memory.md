# Sleep and Memory Consolidation

## Abstract

Sleep-dependent memory consolidation — the process by which newly acquired memories are stabilized, integrated, and transferred from short-term hippocampal storage to long-term neocortical representation during sleep — is one of the most robust and well-characterized functions of sleep, providing the neurobiological explanation for why sleep deprivation impairs learning, why studying before sleep improves retention, and why a full night of sleep is required to realize the full benefit of daytime learning. Hippocampal replay — discovered by Wilson and McNaughton in 1994 through simultaneous recording of place cell ensembles in rats during waking exploration and subsequent sleep — demonstrates that the hippocampus reactivates the neural patterns of daytime experience during subsequent sleep, replaying the sequences of neural activity that occurred during learning at compressed timescales during slow wave sleep sharp-wave ripples. Systems consolidation — the gradual transfer of memories from hippocampal-dependent short-term storage to neocortical-dependent long-term storage — occurs through the repeated co-reactivation of hippocampal and neocortical representations during sleep: the hippocampal sharp-wave ripples that replay daytime experience drive corresponding neocortical spindle oscillations that gradually strengthen the neocortical representation until it becomes hippocampus-independent. Sleep spindle memory transfer — the specific mechanism by which sleep spindles mediate hippocampal-neocortical memory consolidation — involves the temporal coupling of hippocampal sharp-wave ripples with thalamocortical sleep spindles during N2 sleep: the sharp-wave ripple drives the spindle which drives the slow oscillation, creating a nested hierarchy of oscillations that packages hippocampal memory traces for neocortical integration. Procedural memory REM dependence — the enhancement of motor skill learning and perceptual learning during REM sleep — complements the declarative memory consolidation of NREM: motor sequence learning shows offline improvement after sleep with the degree of improvement correlating with REM sleep duration, and this improvement is specifically dependent on REM rather than NREM sleep. ## Declarative vs Procedural

- Declarative episodic and semantic: primarily consolidated during NREM slow wave sleep.
- Procedural motor and perceptual: primarily consolidated during REM sleep.
- Emotional declarative memories: consolidated during REM with reduced emotional charge.
