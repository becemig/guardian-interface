# Glymphatic System and Sleep

## Abstract

The glymphatic system — discovered by Maiken Nedergaard and colleagues at the University of Rochester in 2012 — is the brain's waste clearance system, a network of perivascular channels through which cerebrospinal fluid CSF flows into the brain parenchyma, exchanges with the interstitial fluid ISF surrounding neurons, and flushes metabolic waste products including amyloid-beta and tau proteins out of the brain through the lymphatic vessels of the dural sinuses, with glymphatic clearance being almost entirely dependent on slow wave sleep and providing the neurobiological explanation for why sleep disruption is the strongest modifiable risk factor for Alzheimer's disease. The perivascular space — the channel surrounding cerebral arteries and veins bounded by the astrocytic endfeet — is the primary conduit for glymphatic flow: CSF enters the brain along arterial perivascular spaces driven by arterial pulsation and pressure gradients, exchanges with ISF through aquaporin-4 AQP4 water channels densely expressed on astrocytic endfeet, and exits along venous perivascular spaces carrying interstitial waste toward the dural lymphatics. AQP4 water channels — the astrocytic membrane proteins that mediate the CSF-ISF exchange critical to glymphatic function — are distributed in a polarized fashion on astrocytic endfeet surrounding blood vessels, and their polarization is essential for directed glymphatic flow: AQP4 knockout mice show 65 percent reduction in interstitial amyloid clearance. Slow wave sleep glymphatic activation — the most important finding in glymphatic neuroscience — demonstrates that glymphatic flow increases approximately 60 percent during sleep compared to waking, driven by the expansion of the interstitial space by 60 percent during sleep that reduces resistance to CSF flow and by the slow wave oscillations that drive perivascular CSF pulsation. Lulu Xie's 2013 Science paper demonstrating that beta-amyloid clearance rates are nearly doubled during sleep established the direct link between sleep quality and Alzheimer's disease risk through glymphatic failure.
