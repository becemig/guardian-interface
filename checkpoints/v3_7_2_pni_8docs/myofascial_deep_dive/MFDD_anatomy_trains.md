# Anatomy Trains Update

## Abstract

Anatomy Trains — Thomas Myers' myofascial meridian model describing the whole-body fascial force transmission lines that connect distant body segments through continuous myofascial chains running from foot to head along predictable anatomical pathways — provides a clinically powerful framework for understanding how local dysfunction produces distant compensations and how fascial restriction in one region manifests as pain or movement limitation in anatomically unrelated areas, with remarkable correspondence to the TCM Jing Jin sinew channel pathways that describe the same whole-body myofascial organization from an empirical clinical tradition. The Superficial Back Line SBL — running from the plantar fascia and short toe flexors up through the gastrocnemius, hamstrings, sacrotuberous ligament, erector spinae, epicranial fascia, and frontalis to the brow — connects the entire posterior body surface in a continuous fascial chain that functions to extend and hyperextend the body and whose restriction produces the posterior chain tightness, forward head posture, and reduced hip flexion characteristic of sedentary lifestyles, corresponding to the Bladder sinew channel that runs the same posterior pathway. The Deep Front Line DFL — the deepest myofascial meridian running from the inner arch of the foot through the deep leg compartment, adductors, pelvic floor, psoas and iliacus, anterior spine, deep thorax, and pericardium to the cranial base — constitutes the body's core myofascial support system governing spinal stability, diaphragm function, and the deep postural tone that supports upright organization against gravity, corresponding to the Kidney sinew channel and the deep yin channels running the same medial and anterior pathway. The Spiral Line SBL — wrapping around the body in a double helix from the skull down the same-side rhomboids and serratus anterior across the opposite-side abdominal obliques to the iliac crest, down the iliotibial tract, across the tibialis anterior, under the foot and up the biceps femoris back to the ischium — governs rotational balance and the helical organization of movement, corresponding to the spiral wrapping pathways of several sinew channels. The Jing Jin sinew channel myofascial meridian correspondence — the remarkable structural and functional overlap between Myers' myofascial meridians and the TCM sinew channel pathways, including the Bladder sinew channel corresponding to the Superficial Back Line, the Kidney sinew channel corresponding to the Deep Front Line, and the Gallbladder sinew channel corresponding to the Lateral Line — suggests that both systems independently mapped the same underlying myofascial anatomy from different observational traditions.

- Erector spinae thoracolumbar fascia suboccipitals epicranial fascia frontalis.
- Function: extension hyperextension and postural support of posterior body.
- Bladder sinew channel: runs identical posterior pathway from toe to head.

## Deep Front Line

- Adductors pelvic floor psoas iliacus anterior longitudinal ligament.
- Respiratory diaphragm pericardium mediastinum scalenes longus colli to cranial base.
- Function: deep core support spinal stabilization respiratory integration.

## Spiral Line

- Splenius capitis and cervicis rhomboids serratus anterior.
- External and internal obliques ASIS iliotibial tract tibialis anterior.
- Peroneus longus under foot biceps femoris sacrotuberous ligament.
- Function: rotational balance helical movement organization.
