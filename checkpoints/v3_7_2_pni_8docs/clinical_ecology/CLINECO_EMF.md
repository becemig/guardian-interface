# Electromagnetic Sensitivity and EMF Biology

## Abstract

Electromagnetic sensitivity and EMF biology — the investigation of the biological effects of non-ionizing electromagnetic radiation from radiofrequency sources including cell phones, Wi-Fi, and smart meters, and the clinical syndrome of electrohypersensitivity in which individuals report multisystem symptoms temporally associated with EMF exposure — spans a genuinely contested scientific landscape in which established biological mechanisms including voltage-gated calcium channel activation, oxidative stress, and melatonin suppression coexist with provocation study evidence that EHS individuals cannot reliably detect EMF exposure above chance, suggesting that while EMF has real biological effects, the EHS symptom pattern may involve nocebo mechanisms, anxiety, and conditioning rather than direct EMF perception. Non-ionizing radiation biology — the established biological effects of radiofrequency electromagnetic radiation including thermal effects from energy absorption producing tissue heating, melatonin suppression by nighttime light-emitting device exposure disrupting circadian function, and the more contested non-thermal effects including Martin Pall's VGCC hypothesis proposing that pulsed EMF activates voltage-gated calcium channels producing intracellular calcium elevation and downstream oxidative stress through nitric oxide and peroxynitrite, with the International Agency for Research on Cancer classifying radiofrequency fields as Group 2B possibly carcinogenic based on epidemiological evidence for increased glioma risk with heavy cell phone use. Electrohypersensitivity research — the systematic review evidence showing that in double-blind provocation studies, EHS individuals consistently fail to detect actual EMF exposure at rates better than chance, with symptom reports correlating with perceived rather than actual exposure, supporting nocebo mechanisms and anxiety-mediated symptom generation while not excluding the possibility that real biological EMF sensitivity exists below the threshold of conscious detection and contributes to the initial sensitization. 5G and millimeter wave biology — the emerging biology of millimeter wave frequencies used in 5G networks, with penetration depths of less than 1 mm limiting exposure to skin and eye surfaces, producing established thermal effects at high power densities and less-characterized potential effects on skin nerve endings, sweat ducts, and surface microbiome at environmental exposure levels.
