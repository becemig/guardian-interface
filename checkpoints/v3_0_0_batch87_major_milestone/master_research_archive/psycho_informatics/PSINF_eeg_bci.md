# EEG and Brain-Computer Interface

## Abstract

Electroencephalography EEG — the non-invasive measurement of electrical brain activity through electrodes placed on the scalp — is the primary tool of brain-computer interface research and clinical neurofeedback, providing millisecond-resolution temporal information about neural oscillatory dynamics that reflect cognitive, emotional, and attentional states. Neural oscillations — the rhythmic fluctuations of electrical potential produced by synchronized firing of neural populations — are organized into canonical frequency bands each associated with specific cognitive and physiological states: delta 0.5-4 Hz associated with deep sleep and unconscious processing; theta 4-8 Hz associated with drowsiness, memory encoding, and meditative states; alpha 8-13 Hz associated with relaxed wakefulness, visual suppression, and attentional gating; beta 13-30 Hz associated with active thinking, motor preparation, and anxious arousal; gamma 30-100 Hz associated with conscious binding, attention, and high-level cognitive processing. Brain-computer interfaces BCIs create direct communication channels between the brain and external devices by decoding neural signals in real time and translating them into commands. The P300 BCI paradigm uses the event-related potential evoked by rare target stimuli — a positive deflection at 300ms after the target — to identify which item in a matrix the user is attending to without any motor output. The SSVEP steady-state visually evoked potential BCI uses the brain's resonant response to flickering visual stimuli — neural oscillations entrained to the stimulus frequency — to identify the target the user is looking at. Neurofeedback — the real-time display of the user's own neural activity with the instruction to modulate it in a desired direction — is the therapeutic application of BCI technology, enabling users to learn voluntary control of their neural oscillatory patterns. Alpha-theta neurofeedback training — rewarding increased alpha and theta power while suppressing beta — has demonstrated efficacy for anxiety, PTSD, addiction, and the enhancement of meditative and creative states.

## Frequency Bands

- Delta 0.5-4 Hz: deep sleep, unconscious processing, organ restoration, Shen withdrawal.
- Theta 4-8 Hz: drowsiness, memory encoding, creative ideation, meditative hypnagogia.
- Alpha 8-13 Hz: relaxed wakefulness, attentional gating, visual suppression, calm presence.
- Beta 13-30 Hz: active thinking, motor preparation, anxious arousal, Shen engagement.
- Gamma 30-100 Hz: conscious binding, attention, high-level integration, peak awareness.

## BCI Paradigms

- P300: rare target stimuli evoke a positive deflection at 300ms — attention decoded without movement.
- SSVEP: flickering visual stimuli entrain neural oscillations — gaze decoded from resonant frequency.
- Motor imagery: imagining movement produces sensorimotor desynchronization — decoded for motor BCI.
- Passive BCI: continuous classification of mental state without explicit user intent — affective monitoring.

## Neurofeedback

- Alpha-theta training: reward increased alpha and theta, suppress beta — relaxed alert meditation state.
- SMR training: reward sensorimotor rhythm 12-15 Hz — attention stability, reduced hyperarousal.
- Gamma training: reward high-frequency coherence — attention, binding, peak cognitive performance.
- Infra-low frequency training: reward slow cortical potentials — autonomic regulation and arousal control.
