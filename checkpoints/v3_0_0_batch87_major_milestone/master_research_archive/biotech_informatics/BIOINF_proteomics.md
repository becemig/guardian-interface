# Proteomics and Metabolomics

## Abstract

Proteomics and metabolomics — the comprehensive analysis of the complete protein and small molecule metabolite content of biological samples — provide the most functionally proximate molecular portraits of health state, revealing the actual molecular machinery operating in cells and tissues and the metabolic outputs of that machinery in ways that genomics and transcriptomics alone cannot. Mass spectrometry is the primary technology for both proteomics and metabolomics: it separates molecules by mass-to-charge ratio with exquisite precision, enabling the simultaneous identification and quantification of thousands of proteins or metabolites in a single experiment. The plasma proteome — the complete set of proteins circulating in blood — is the most accessible window into the body's proteome, containing proteins secreted by every organ and tissue that reflect their current functional state. The Human Plasma Proteome Project has catalogued over 4500 proteins in plasma, many of which serve as biomarkers of specific organ dysfunction or disease states. Protein interaction networks — the interactome — map the physical and functional interactions between all proteins in a cell, revealing the network architecture through which molecular signals are transmitted and the hub proteins whose disruption produces the most widespread functional consequences. Metabolomics provides the most downstream and phenotypically proximate molecular measurement: metabolites are the small molecules produced by cellular metabolism and reflect the integrated output of all genomic, transcriptomic, and proteomic activity as well as the direct influence of diet, gut microbiome, and environmental exposures. Nuclear magnetic resonance NMR spectroscopy and mass spectrometry are the two primary metabolomics platforms, each with complementary strengths in metabolite coverage and quantification precision. Metabolic flux analysis quantifies the rates of metabolic reactions rather than the steady-state concentrations of metabolites, revealing the dynamic metabolic activity of cells under different conditions. The correspondence between metabolomics profiles and TCM tongue and pulse diagnostics — both reflecting the current functional state of organ networks through indirect systemic indicators — represents one of the most promising bridges between molecular medicine and TCM.

- Organ-specific proteins leak into plasma during dysfunction — cardiac troponin, liver ALT, kidney creatinine.

## Protein Interaction Networks

- The interactome maps all physical and functional protein interactions in the cell.
- Hub proteins with many connections produce widespread dysfunction when disrupted.
- Disease genes cluster in specific interactome neighborhoods — the disease module concept.
- Corresponds to the TCM five element network of organ interactions and mutual influence.

## Metabolomics

- Small molecule metabolites are the most functionally proximate molecular measurement.
- Urine metabolomics reflects kidney filtration and whole-body metabolic output.
- Plasma metabolomics reflects systemic metabolic state and organ functional balance.
- Fecal metabolomics reflects gut microbiome metabolic activity and intestinal function.
