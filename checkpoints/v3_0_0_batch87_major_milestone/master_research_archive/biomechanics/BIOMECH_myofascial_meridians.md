# Myofascial Meridians

## Abstract

Thomas Myers' Anatomy Trains framework maps the body's continuous myofascial connections as functional lines of tension transmission — chains of muscle and fascia that run through the body in specific pathways, transmitting forces across multiple joints and body regions in coordinated movement patterns. The twelve anatomy trains lines correspond with striking accuracy to the twelve primary TCM meridian pathways — providing a biomechanical substrate for the traditional Chinese observation of channel pathways that connects ancient clinical wisdom to modern fascial anatomy. The Superficial Back Line runs from the plantar fascia through the calf, hamstrings, sacrotuberous ligament, erector spinae, and suboccipital muscles to the frontal fascia — corresponding closely to the Bladder meridian pathway running from the eye along the back to the foot. The Superficial Front Line runs from the dorsal foot through the anterior tibialis, quadriceps, rectus abdominis, and sternocleidomastoid to the skull — corresponding to the Stomach meridian pathway on the anterior aspect of the body. The Lateral Line runs from the foot through the peroneals, iliotibial band, lateral abdominal obliques, and lateral intercostals to the skull — corresponding to the Gallbladder meridian pathway along the lateral aspect of the body. The Spiral Line runs in a helical pattern from the skull through the splenius capitis, rhomboids, serratus anterior, external oblique, internal oblique, and iliotibial band back to the skull — corresponding to the extraordinary vessel pathways that cross the midline. The Deep Front Line runs from the inner arch of the foot through the deep posterior compartment, adductors, psoas, anterior spinal ligaments, and pericardium to the base of the skull — corresponding to the Kidney and Liver meridian pathways along the medial and deep aspects of the body.

- Plantar fascia to suboccipital muscles creates a continuous tensional line along the posterior body.
- Restriction anywhere in the SBL produces compensatory tension along the entire line.
- The SBL corresponds to the Bladder meridian governing the posterior body and the nervous system.

## Spiral Line

- It governs rotational balance and the helical force transmission of walking and twisting.
- Restriction in the spiral line produces the rotational compensations visible in posture assessment.
- The spiral line corresponds to the extraordinary vessel pathways that cross the midline governing constitutional balance.
