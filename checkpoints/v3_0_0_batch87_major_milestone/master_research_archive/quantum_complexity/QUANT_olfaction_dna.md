# Quantum Olfaction and DNA Mutation

## Abstract

Quantum biology extends beyond energy transfer and sensing into two further domains of fundamental biological function: olfaction and genetic mutation. The vibrational theory of olfaction — proposed by Luca Turin in 1996 — challenges the dominant shape theory of smell by proposing that odorant molecules are recognized not by their three-dimensional shape binding to receptor proteins but by their molecular vibrational frequencies, detected through inelastic electron tunneling in the olfactory receptor proteins. Inelastic electron tunneling — a quantum mechanical process in which an electron tunnels across a gap while losing energy to a molecular vibration, with the energy loss detectable as a change in tunneling current — would allow olfactory receptors to function as molecular spectrometers, detecting the characteristic vibrational fingerprint of each odorant molecule. Experimental evidence for the vibrational theory includes the finding that molecules with identical shapes but different vibrational frequencies due to isotopic substitution — replacing hydrogen with deuterium — smell different to human subjects and to Drosophila, a result that is difficult to explain by shape theory but predicted by vibrational theory. Quantum proton transfer in DNA — the quantum mechanical tunneling of protons between the complementary base pairs of the DNA double helix — was proposed by Per-Olov Lowdin in 1963 as a mechanism for spontaneous DNA mutation: tautomeric shifts in which a proton tunnels from its normal position to an alternative position on the complementary base produce mismatched base pairs that, if not repaired before replication, result in point mutations. The tautomeric mutation mechanism provides a quantum mechanical explanation for the spontaneous mutation rate that underlies both the baseline noise of genetic fidelity and the raw material of evolution. The correspondence between quantum olfaction and the TCM Lung-nose axis — the Lung governing the sense of smell and the nose as the opening of the Lung — provides a quantum biological grounding for the TCM organ-sense correspondence.
