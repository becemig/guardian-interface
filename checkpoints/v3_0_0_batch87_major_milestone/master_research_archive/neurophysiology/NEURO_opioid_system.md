# Endogenous Opioid System

## Abstract

The endogenous opioid system comprises three families of opioid peptides — endorphins, enkephalins, and dynorphins — acting at three receptor subtypes mu, delta, and kappa distributed throughout the central and peripheral nervous system. John Hughes and Hans Kosterlitz discovered enkephalins in 1975 as the brain's endogenous morphine-like substances. Beta-endorphin from the pituitary and hypothalamus is the most potent endogenous opioid acting primarily at mu receptors to produce analgesia, euphoria, and stress modulation. Enkephalins are widely distributed in the spinal cord and brainstem serving as local pain modulators at mu and delta receptors. Dynorphins act preferentially at kappa receptors producing analgesia with dysphoric rather than euphoric effects. The periaqueductal gray PAG is the master center of opioid-mediated descending pain inhibition. Diffuse noxious inhibitory controls DNIC — the phenomenon of pain inhibiting pain — operates through opioid and serotonergic descending pathways. Ji Sheng Han's research demonstrated that electroacupuncture at different frequencies releases different opioid peptides — 2Hz releases enkephalins and beta-endorphin while 100Hz releases dynorphins — providing mechanistic evidence for acupuncture analgesia.

## Three Opioid Families

- Endorphins including beta-endorphin are large peptides from POMC precursor acting at mu receptors.
- Enkephalins are small pentapeptides widely distributed in pain circuits acting at mu and delta receptors.
- Dynorphins are large peptides from prodynorphin precursor acting preferentially at kappa receptors.
- Each family has distinct distributions, receptor preferences, and physiological roles in pain and mood.

## Descending Pain Inhibition

- The PAG receives input from the frontal cortex, hypothalamus, and amygdala and projects to the raphe nuclei.
- PAG activation releases beta-endorphin and enkephalins activating descending inhibitory pathways to the spinal cord.
- Descending serotonergic and noradrenergic fibers inhibit spinal pain transmission at the dorsal horn.
- Placebo analgesia is substantially mediated by opioid release — naloxone blocks much of the placebo analgesic effect.

## Acupuncture and Opioids

- Han demonstrated frequency-dependent opioid release by electroacupuncture using naloxone blockade.
- 2Hz electroacupuncture releases enkephalins and beta-endorphin producing sustained analgesia.
- 100Hz electroacupuncture releases dynorphins producing rapid analgesia with faster tolerance development.
- Alternating 2-100Hz produces release of all three opioid families with optimal analgesic effect.
