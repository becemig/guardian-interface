# Enteric Nervous System

## Abstract

The enteric nervous system ENS is the intrinsic nervous system of the gastrointestinal tract — a vast network of approximately 500 million neurons organized into two plexuses: the myenteric Auerbach plexus between the circular and longitudinal muscle layers governing motor function, and the submucosal Meissner plexus in the submucosa governing secretion and blood flow. Michael Gershon called the ENS the second brain — it contains as many neurons as the spinal cord, uses all the same neurotransmitters as the central nervous system, and can function autonomously without input from the brain through its intrinsic reflex circuits. Approximately 90-95% of the body's serotonin is produced in the gut by enterochromaffin cells — gut serotonin regulates intestinal motility, secretion, and sensation and provides the primary serotonin signal to the vagus nerve ascending to the brainstem. The gut-brain axis is the bidirectional communication system between the ENS and the CNS — vagal afferents carrying gut information to the brainstem, descending autonomic pathways modulating ENS function, and the HPA axis responding to gut signals. The intestinal microbiome profoundly influences ENS function and behavior — germ-free animals show altered anxiety, stress responses, and cognitive function that are rescued by microbiome transplant. Gut feelings — the visceral sensations that guide intuitive decision-making — are generated by ENS activity transmitted via vagal afferents to the insula and ACC. Spleen-Stomach intelligence in TCM — the digestive organ network's role not only in food transformation but in generating Post-Heaven Qi that nourishes Shen and enables clear Yi cognitive function — corresponds to the ENS-gut-brain axis as its neurobiological substrate.

## Myenteric and Submucosal Plexuses

- Myenteric plexus between muscle layers coordinates the peristaltic reflex and other motor patterns.
- Submucosal plexus in the submucosa regulates secretion, absorption, and mucosal blood flow.
- The ENS contains all major CNS neurotransmitters including serotonin, dopamine, acetylcholine, and GABA.
- ENS intrinsic reflex circuits produce coordinated gut motility independently of CNS input.

## Gut Serotonin

- 90-95% of the body's serotonin is produced by enterochromaffin cells in the intestinal mucosa.
- Gut serotonin regulates intestinal motility — stimulating peristalsis and secretion through 5-HT3 and 5-HT4 receptors.
- Gut serotonin activates vagal afferents transmitting gut state information to the brainstem and insula.
- SSRIs affect gut function through peripheral serotonin — explaining the GI side effects of antidepressants.

## Microbiome-ENS Axis

- The intestinal microbiome produces short-chain fatty acids, neurotransmitter precursors, and immune signals.
- These signals influence ENS neuron activity, vagal tone, and CNS function through the gut-brain axis.
- Microbiome dysbiosis correlates with depression, anxiety, autism, and Parkinson's disease.
- Fecal microbiota transplant can transfer anxiety and depression-like behaviors between animal strains.
