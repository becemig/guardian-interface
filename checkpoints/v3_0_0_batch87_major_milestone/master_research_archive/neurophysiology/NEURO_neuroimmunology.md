# Neuroimmunology: Brain-Immune Communication, Microglial Function, and Psychoneuroimmunology

## Abstract

Neuroimmunology is the field studying the bidirectional communication between the nervous system and the immune system. The brain was long considered immunologically privileged but is now understood to be in continuous communication with peripheral immune cells through humoral, neural, and cellular pathways. Microglia are the resident immune cells of the central nervous system governing neuroinflammation, synaptic pruning, and neuroprotection. Psychoneuroimmunology established that psychological states including stress, depression, and social connection directly modulate immune function through autonomic and neuroendocrine pathways. Internal movement arts produce robust immunomodulatory effects through their integrated effects on the autonomic nervous system, HPA axis, and social engagement system.

## Microglia

### Development and Identity

- Microglia are derived from yolk sac progenitors that colonize the developing brain before birth.
- They are distinct from peripheral macrophages and maintain themselves through local self-renewal throughout life.
- Microglia comprise approximately 10 to 15 percent of all brain cells and are distributed throughout the central nervous system.
- Microglial density is highest in regions of high synaptic activity including hippocampus, basal ganglia, and cerebellum.

### Surveillance and Activation

- Resting microglia extend dynamic processes continuously sampling the local environment for signs of damage or infection.
- Pattern recognition receptors on microglia detect pathogen-associated and damage-associated molecular patterns.
- Microglial activation produces a spectrum of responses from neuroprotective to neuroinflammatory depending on context.
- Chronic microglial activation in the absence of infection underlies sterile neuroinflammation in aging and neurodegeneration.

### Synaptic Pruning

- Microglia actively prune synapses during development and throughout life through complement-mediated phagocytosis.
- Healthy synaptic pruning refines neural circuits by eliminating weak or inactive synapses.
- Excessive pruning in schizophrenia and stress reduces synaptic density in prefrontal cortex and hippocampus.
- Sleep deprivation drives excessive microglial synaptic pruning producing cognitive deficits.

## Blood-Brain Barrier

### Structure and Function

- The blood-brain barrier is formed by tight junctions between cerebrovascular endothelial cells limiting molecular passage.
- Astrocytic endfeet surrounding cerebral capillaries contribute to barrier properties and regulate transport.
- The blood-brain barrier selectively admits nutrients, oxygen, and hormones while excluding most pathogens and large molecules.
- Pericytes on cerebral capillaries regulate blood flow and contribute to barrier maintenance.

### Barrier Disruption

- Chronic systemic inflammation produces blood-brain barrier disruption allowing peripheral cytokines to enter brain tissue.
- Stress-induced cortisol elevation disrupts tight junction proteins increasing barrier permeability.
- Once disrupted the barrier allows peripheral immune cells and inflammatory mediators to access neural tissue.
- Restoration of blood-brain barrier integrity is a key mechanism through which anti-inflammatory interventions protect brain health.

## Psychoneuroimmunology

### Stress and Immune Suppression

- Robert Ader and Nicholas Cohen demonstrated in 1975 that immune responses could be classically conditioned.
- Acute stress produces transient immune enhancement redistributing immune cells to sites of potential injury.
- Chronic stress produces sustained immune suppression through cortisol-mediated lymphocyte apoptosis and cytokine suppression.
- Bereavement, social isolation, and depression all produce measurable immune suppression and increased infection susceptibility.

### Sickness Behavior

- Peripheral immune activation produces centrally mediated sickness behavior through cytokine signaling to the brain.
- IL-1beta, IL-6, and TNF-alpha signal the brain via vagal afferents and blood-brain barrier transport.
- Sickness behavior including fatigue, social withdrawal, reduced appetite, and sleep increase are adaptive responses to infection.
- Chronic low-grade inflammation produces sickness behavior that is clinically indistinguishable from depression.

### Social Connection and Immunity

- Social connection and positive emotion enhance NK cell activity, antibody production, and vaccine response.
- Loneliness and social isolation produce a conserved transcriptional response characterized by increased inflammatory gene expression.
- The social engagement system through ventral vagal activation directly modulates immune function through cholinergic pathways.
- Guardian Interface as a knowledge and practice community potentially supports immune health through social-somatic engagement.
