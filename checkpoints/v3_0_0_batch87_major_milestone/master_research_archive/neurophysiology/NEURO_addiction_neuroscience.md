# Addiction Neuroscience

## Abstract

Addiction is now understood as a chronic brain disorder involving pathological neural remodeling of the reward, motivation, memory, and inhibitory control circuits — with the transcription factor DeltaFosB as the molecular switch that converts acute drug exposure into the lasting neural changes underlying compulsive use. Eric Nestler's laboratory demonstrated that DeltaFosB uniquely accumulates with repeated drug exposure — unlike other transcription factors that return to baseline after stimulation, DeltaFosB is unusually stable and accumulates in the nucleus accumbens over weeks and months of repeated exposure, functioning as a molecular switch that persistently alters gene expression in reward circuits. The allostatic shift model developed by George Koob describes how addiction involves a progressive shift in the hedonic setpoint — repeated drug exposure elevates the reward threshold requiring more drug to achieve the same effect while simultaneously lowering the baseline mood below the normal setpoint, trapping the addict between insufficient reward from natural sources and the relief of withdrawal through renewed drug use. Incentive salience theory developed by Kent Berridge distinguishes wanting — the dopaminergic drive toward a stimulus — from liking — the opioid-mediated hedonic pleasure from it. In addiction wanting becomes pathologically amplified while liking decreases — producing the characteristic compulsive craving for something that no longer provides genuine pleasure. Withdrawal neurochemistry involves the rebound activation of stress systems CRF, dynorphin, norepinephrine that were suppressed by chronic drug use — producing the hyperkatifeia the intensified negative emotional state of withdrawal. These neurochemical mechanisms map precisely onto the TCM pattern progression from Liver Qi stagnation through Kidney Jing depletion to Phlegm-Fire Heart disturbance in chronic addiction.

## DeltaFosB

- DeltaFosB accumulates in nucleus accumbens with repeated drug exposure — stable for weeks to months.
- It alters expression of hundreds of genes involved in synaptic plasticity, dendritic morphology, and reward sensitivity.
- DeltaFosB overexpression produces increased sensitivity to drug reward and natural reward deficiency.
- It is the molecular mechanism of addiction memory — the reason the brain never fully forgets addictive experience.

## Allostatic Shift

- Repeated drug exposure elevates the reward threshold through opponent process neuroadaptation.
- Simultaneously the emotional baseline shifts below normal setpoint producing chronic dysphoria.
- The addict is trapped: natural rewards insufficient, only drug use relieves the allostatic dysphoria.
- Recovery requires the slow normalization of hedonic setpoint through sustained abstinence and alternative reward.

## Incentive Salience

- Wanting is dopaminergic — the motivational drive toward a reward cue — mediated by nucleus accumbens.
- Liking is opioidergic — the hedonic pleasure of consumption — mediated by hedonic hotspots.
- In addiction wanting is pathologically amplified while liking decreases — compulsive pursuit of diminishing pleasure.
- Cue-induced wanting — the craving triggered by drug-associated stimuli — is the primary relapse mechanism.
