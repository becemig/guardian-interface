# Sensory Integration and Processing

## Abstract

Sensory integration is the neural process of combining information from multiple sensory modalities — vision, hearing, touch, proprioception, vestibular, interoception — into coherent unified perceptions that guide adaptive behavior. Barry Stein and Alex Meredith's research on the superior colliculus established key principles of multisensory integration: spatial correspondence — signals from the same location are more effectively combined, temporal correspondence — signals arriving simultaneously are more effectively combined, and inverse effectiveness — weak unimodal signals produce greater multisensory enhancement than strong signals. The superior temporal sulcus STS is the primary cortical site for audiovisual integration and the convergence of social perceptual signals. The Bayesian brain framework proposes that the brain is a prediction machine that combines prior expectations with incoming sensory evidence according to Bayesian probability theory — perception is a form of inference. The rubber hand illusion — where stroking a visible rubber hand synchronously with the participant's hidden hand produces the illusion that the rubber hand is one's own — demonstrates the malleability of body schema through multisensory integration. Jean Ayres developed sensory integration therapy for children with processing difficulties demonstrating the clinical significance of sensory integration for development and function. Shen perception in TCM — the Heart-Mind's integrating witness that unifies all sensory experience into the field of conscious awareness — corresponds to the multisensory integration process as its experiential substrate.

## Multisensory Convergence

- The superior colliculus is the primary subcortical multisensory convergence site receiving visual, auditory, and somatosensory input.
- Multisensory neurons in the SC respond more strongly to combined stimuli than predicted by summing unimodal responses.
- Spatial and temporal correspondence between modalities determines the degree of multisensory enhancement.
- Inverse effectiveness means that weaker signals benefit more from multisensory integration than stronger signals.

## Bayesian Perception

- The brain generates predictions about sensory input based on prior experience and contextual priors.
- Incoming sensory signals are compared against predictions and the discrepancy constitutes the prediction error.
- Perception represents the brain's best inference about the cause of sensory signals given both prior and likelihood.
- Surprising stimuli generate large prediction errors driving learning and updating of the predictive model.

## Body Schema

- The body schema is the dynamic representation of the body's posture, boundaries, and spatial extent.
- It is constructed from proprioceptive, vestibular, visual, and tactile signals integrated in the posterior parietal cortex.
- The rubber hand illusion demonstrates that the body schema can incorporate non-biological objects through multisensory correlation.
- Phantom limb pain reflects a body schema that retains the amputated limb despite its physical absence.
