# Neural Oscillations

## Abstract

Neural oscillations are rhythmic patterns of electrical activity generated by synchronized neural population activity across defined frequency bands each with characteristic functional roles and anatomical generators. György Buzsáki's comprehensive framework identifies five primary oscillatory bands: delta 0.5-4Hz during deep sleep and large-scale integration, theta 4-8Hz during memory encoding and spatial navigation, alpha 8-12Hz during relaxed wakefulness and cortical inhibition, beta 13-30Hz during active cognition and motor preparation, and gamma 30-80Hz during local cortical processing and binding. Cross-frequency coupling — particularly phase-amplitude coupling between theta phase and gamma amplitude — enables hierarchical information organization across spatial and temporal scales. The hippocampal theta rhythm organizes memory encoding sequences while the prefrontal-hippocampal theta coherence supports working memory and decision-making. Alpha oscillations reflect active cortical inhibition — regions not currently processing show enhanced alpha while processing regions show alpha suppression. Meditation produces characteristic EEG signatures — increased frontal theta reflecting focused attention, increased parietal alpha reflecting open awareness, and increased gamma reflecting perceptual clarity. The TCM concept of Qi rhythms — the cyclical pulsation of Qi through the meridian system and organ clock — corresponds to neural oscillations as the rhythmic temporal organization of neural information processing.

## Delta Band

- Delta oscillations at 0.5-4Hz are the dominant rhythm of deep NREM sleep and large-scale cortical synchrony.
- Slow wave sleep delta reflects the synchronous cortical down-states of neural restoration and memory consolidation.
- Pathological delta in waking states indicates severe cortical dysfunction or deep anesthesia.
- Thalamo-cortical loops generate delta through reciprocal inhibitory-excitatory dynamics during sleep.

## Theta Band

- Hippocampal theta at 4-8Hz organizes memory encoding and spatial navigation sequences.
- Theta phase gates synaptic plasticity — LTP occurs preferentially at specific theta phases.
- Frontal theta increases during focused meditation, working memory, and cognitive control tasks.
- Theta-gamma phase-amplitude coupling packages information — gamma bursts nest within theta cycles.

## Alpha Band

- Alpha oscillations reflect active inhibition of task-irrelevant cortical regions.
- Eyes-closed resting alpha is generated in the occipital cortex and reflects visual cortical inhibition.
- Alpha increases in regions not processing current task demands enabling focused processing elsewhere.
- Meditation increases alpha amplitude corresponding to the cultivation of relaxed but alert awareness.
