# Gestalt Therapy

## Abstract

Gestalt therapy developed by Fritz Perls, Laura Perls, and Paul Goodman is a humanistic experiential psychotherapy grounded in Gestalt psychology's principles of perceptual organization, field theory, and phenomenology. The foundational principle is that psychological health consists in the organism's capacity for fluid figure-ground formation — the ability to form clear figures of interest from the ground of experience, make full contact, and complete the experience allowing it to recede back into ground. Unfinished business — incomplete gestalt formations from past experience that continue to interrupt present contact — is the primary source of psychological disturbance. The empty chair technique enables dialogue with absent others or disowned parts of the self making the past present for completion. Contact — full meeting at the boundary between self and other — is the medium of growth and healing. Retroflection — doing to oneself what one wants to do to another — produces chronic muscular tension and somatic symptoms. Confluence — the loss of the self-other boundary — produces the loss of individual perspective and authentic contact. Present moment awareness is the primary therapeutic orientation — what is happening now in awareness, sensation, and contact. Shen presence in TCM — the Heart-Mind's full luminous attendance to present experience — corresponds to the Gestalt therapeutic principle of present-centered awareness.

## Figure-Ground

- Psychological health involves fluid figure-ground formation — clear interest arising from background and receding after completion.
- Unfinished business interrupts figure-ground cycling by keeping past unresolved figures active in the foreground.
- These fixed gestalts consume attention and energy that would otherwise be available for present contact.
- Completion of unfinished business through present-moment re-experiencing allows the fixed figure to recede.

## Contact and Boundary

- Contact occurs at the boundary between organism and environment — the meeting point of self and other.
- Healthy contact involves full meeting while maintaining the distinction between self and other.
- Contact disturbances — retroflection, confluence, introjection, projection, deflection — interrupt authentic contact.
- The therapeutic relationship models and enables genuine contact as the medium of healing.

## Present Moment

- Gestalt therapy orients entirely to present-moment experience — what is happening now in awareness and sensation.
- Past and future are addressed as they appear in the present — how the past lives in current posture and breathing.
- The therapist tracks present-moment somatic and expressive indicators as primary therapeutic data.
- Experiment — trying something new in the session — enables direct present-moment exploration of new possibilities.
