# Alexander Technique

## Abstract

The Alexander Technique developed by Frederick Matthias Alexander is a somatic education method focused on improving the use of the self — the coordination of thought, posture, and movement through conscious inhibition and direction. Alexander discovered that his habitual vocal and postural patterns were maintained by faulty sensory appreciation — his kinesthetic sense was unreliable, confirming what felt right was actually wrong. Primary control describes the dynamic relationship between the head, neck, and back that governs the coordination of the whole body. Inhibition is the foundational practice — the deliberate pause before action that interrupts habitual patterns and creates the possibility of conscious choice. Direction refers to the thought-based instructions given to the primary control — neck free, head forward and up, back to lengthen and widen. End-gaining — focusing on the goal at the expense of the means — is identified as the primary cause of misuse and postural dysfunction. Governing Vessel governing the spinal axis and the head-spine relationship directly corresponds to the primary control of Alexander Technique.

## Primary Control

- Primary control is the dynamic relationship between the head, neck, and back governing whole-body coordination.
- When the head is free to balance on the atlas the spine can lengthen and the body organize optimally.
- Habitual startle pattern pulls the head back and down compressing the spine and disrupting primary control.
- Restoring primary control through inhibition and direction improves coordination throughout the whole body.

## Inhibition

- Inhibition is the deliberate pause before action that interrupts the habitual pattern before it begins.
- It is not suppression but the creation of a moment of choice between stimulus and response.
- Inhibition requires recognizing the end-gaining impulse and choosing not to act on it habitually.
- Regular inhibition practice builds the prefrontal capacity for deliberate conscious response.

## Direction

- Direction refers to thought-based instructions given to the primary control without direct muscular effort.
- The classic directions: neck free, head forward and up, back to lengthen and widen, knees forward and away.
- These directions are projections of intention rather than muscular commands — they work through neural intention.
- Direction combined with inhibition allows the body's natural coordination to emerge without interference.
