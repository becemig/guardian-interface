# Systema

## Abstract

Systema — the Russian martial art rooted in the training methods of the Russian Special Forces and developed publicly by Mikhail Ryabko and Vladimir Vasiliev — is one of the most psychophysiologically sophisticated martial systems in the world, organized around four foundational pillars: breathing, relaxation, structure, and movement. Unlike martial arts organized around techniques, Systema is organized around the cultivation of psychophysical states — the practitioner who can maintain relaxed breathing, structural alignment, and fluid movement under extreme pressure has developed the fundamental capacity from which all effective martial response emerges. Systema's approach to breath work is its most distinctive contribution — continuous breathing is maintained through all physical and psychological stress, and specific breath protocols are used to manage fear, pain, exhaustion, and psychological shock. The hold-your-breath training of Systema — systematically working under breath holds of increasing duration while performing demanding physical tasks — develops extraordinary CO2 tolerance and the capacity to function effectively in oxygen-depleted states. Strike absorption training — learning to receive strikes with the whole body rather than bracing against them — develops the capacity to distribute and dissipate force through the entire body structure, converting the kinetic energy of the strike into whole-body movement rather than localized tissue damage. Systema's psychological training — deliberate exposure to fear-inducing situations with maintained breath and relaxation — corresponds to the systematic desensitization of the threat response, training the practitioner to maintain prefrontal function and physical effectiveness in the presence of the autonomic arousal that normally produces freeze, fight, or flight. The Systema concept of the nervous system as weapon — the trained practitioner can transmit specific nervous system states through touch, strike, or presence — corresponds to the TCM concept of Yi intention and Shen presence as the primary instrument of both martial and healing arts.

## Four Pillars

- Breathing: continuous natural breath maintained through all stress — the foundation of all other qualities.
- Relaxation: absence of unnecessary tension at all times — tension is detected and released continuously.
- Structure: optimal skeletal alignment allowing force transmission without muscular bracing.
- Movement: continuous fluid movement that never stops — stillness creates vulnerability.

## Breath Work

- Continuous breathing through all exertion prevents the CO2 accumulation that produces panic.
- Breath holds under physical stress build CO2 tolerance and develop calm under oxygen deficit.
- Exhale on impact of strikes received: releasing breath prevents diaphragm spasm and shock.
- The breath is the primary tool for managing fear, pain, and psychological overwhelm in combat.

## Strike Absorption

- Receiving strikes with whole-body movement distributes force rather than concentrating it at the impact point.
- The relaxed body absorbs and dissipates kinetic energy through elastic deformation rather than rigid resistance.
- This corresponds to the Taijichuan principle of using softness to overcome hardness.
- Strike absorption training develops the capacity to function effectively after being hit.

## TCM Correspondence

- Systema breath work corresponds to TCM Lung governing the breath and the capacity to maintain Qi circulation under stress.
- Relaxation under pressure corresponds to the TCM cultivation of the Shen remaining centered while the body is under threat.
- Nervous system as weapon corresponds to TCM Yi intention and Shen presence as the primary healing and martial instrument.
- Strike absorption corresponds to the TCM softness and yielding principle of water overcoming stone.
