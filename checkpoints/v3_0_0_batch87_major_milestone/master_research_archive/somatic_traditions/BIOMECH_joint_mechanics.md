# Joint Mechanics and Articular Biology

## Abstract

Synovial joints are the primary load-bearing articulations of the musculoskeletal system, lined with hyaline cartilage and lubricated by synovial fluid. Articular cartilage is avascular and depends on cyclic mechanical loading to drive nutrient diffusion from synovial fluid. Joint lubrication occurs through boundary, fluid film, and weeping mechanisms that distribute load and reduce friction. Osteoarthritis results from imbalance between cartilage matrix synthesis and degradation driven by mechanical overload, inflammation, and aging. Tai Chi produces significant benefits for knee osteoarthritis through its gentle cyclic loading, proprioceptive training, and anti-inflammatory effects. The Kidney governs bone and the Liver governs sinew corresponding to cartilage and ligamentous joint health.

## Synovial Joint Anatomy

- Hyaline cartilage covers articular surfaces providing smooth load distribution and shock absorption.
- The synovial membrane produces synovial fluid that lubricates and nourishes the avascular cartilage.
- The joint capsule provides mechanical containment and houses proprioceptive Ruffini and Pacinian receptors.
- Ligaments provide passive stability while muscles provide dynamic stability through active tension.

## Cartilage Nutrition

- Articular cartilage is avascular and receives nutrition entirely through synovial fluid diffusion.
- Cyclic mechanical loading drives fluid into and out of the cartilage matrix delivering nutrients and removing waste.
- Immobilization produces cartilage atrophy while excessive loading produces cartilage damage.
- The gentle cyclic loading of Tai Chi optimally stimulates cartilage nutrition without damaging shear forces.

## Joint Lubrication

- Boundary lubrication is provided by lubricin glycoprotein adsorbed to cartilage surfaces.
- Fluid film lubrication occurs when moving surfaces are separated by a pressurized fluid layer.
- Weeping lubrication occurs when cartilage compression expresses fluid to the surface.
- Tai Chi slow controlled movement optimizes lubrication by maintaining synovial fluid distribution.
