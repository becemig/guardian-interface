# Central Pattern Generators

## Abstract

Central pattern generators — the neural networks capable of producing rhythmic patterned motor output without requiring rhythmic sensory input or descending commands, constituting the spinal and brainstem circuits that generate the fundamental rhythms of locomotion, respiration, chewing, and other stereotyped behaviors — represent the evolutionary foundation of coordinated movement, with the vertebrate locomotor CPG in the spinal cord generating the basic gait pattern that supraspinal centers modulate and guide, and the respiratory CPG in the brainstem generating the breathing rhythm that is modulated by chemoreceptor input, behavioral state, and voluntary control. Spinal locomotor CPG architecture — the half-center oscillator organization of the spinal locomotor CPG in which excitatory rhythm-generating interneurons drive flexor and extensor motor neuron pools alternately through reciprocal inhibitory interneurons, producing the alternating flexion-extension pattern of stepping without requiring supraspinal input, demonstrated by the classic experiments showing that spinal cord-isolated cats can produce coordinated treadmill locomotion with dopaminergic activation. Respiratory CPG and pre-Botzinger complex — the discovery by Jack Feldman of the pre-Botzinger complex, a small bilateral nucleus in the ventral medullary reticular formation containing the pacemaker interneurons that generate the inspiratory rhythm through persistent sodium current and synaptic interconnections, with its output modulated by the parafacial respiratory group generating active expiration, and with the entire network modulated by serotonin, norepinephrine, substance P, opioids, and the chemoreceptor drive from CO2 and O2 sensors. CPG neuromodulation — the profound effects of monoamine neuromodulators including dopamine, serotonin, and norepinephrine on CPG output, with dopamine activating the spinal locomotor CPG enabling locomotion in spinal animals, serotonin modulating respiratory CPG rhythm and pattern, and the loss of serotonergic drive from the raphe nuclei contributing to the respiratory irregularity of sudden infant death syndrome. The Zong Qi ancestral breath rhythm generator correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding of Zong Qi gathering Qi as the primordial respiratory Qi that animates the rhythmic movements of breathing and heartbeat — a functional description of the brainstem CPGs that generate the fundamental life-sustaining rhythms of respiration and cardiac activity.

## Locomotor CPG

- Half-center oscillator: flexor and extensor half-centers mutually inhibit — alternating output.
- Rhythm generation: intrinsic membrane properties and synaptic interconnections.
- Pattern formation: separate networks coordinate interlimb timing.
- Supraspinal modulation: corticospinal reticulospinal modulate speed direction adaptation.

## Pre-Botzinger Complex

- Location: ventral medulla — bilateral nucleus 600 neurons per side.
- Pacemaker: persistent sodium current drives rhythmic bursting.
- Inspiratory output: drives premotor interneurons motor neurons phrenic nerve diaphragm.
- Modulation: substance P serotonin NE activate opioids suppress.

## Treadmill Training Post-SCI

- Spinal CPG activation: treadmill with body weight support activates spinal locomotor CPG.
- Plasticity: repeated activation strengthens spinal locomotor circuits.
- Clinical evidence: locomotor training improves walking function after incomplete SCI.
- Principle: CPG can be trained even without supraspinal input — bottom-up rehabilitation.
