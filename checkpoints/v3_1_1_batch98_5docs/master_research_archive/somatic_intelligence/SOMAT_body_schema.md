# Body Schema and Body Image

## Abstract

Body schema and body image — the two primary conceptual frameworks for understanding the brain's internal representation of the body — provide the theoretical foundation for understanding how the brain constructs, maintains, and updates its model of the physical self, with clinical implications for phantom limb pain, eating disorders, depersonalization, somatic symptom disorders, and the embodied movement practices of martial arts, yoga, and somatic therapy. Body schema — the implicit, largely unconscious sensorimotor representation of the body that governs automatic movement, postural control, and action — is continuously updated by proprioceptive, tactile, and vestibular inputs and can be extended to incorporate tools, vehicles, and other objects that become functionally integrated into the agent's action space. Body image — the consciously accessible perceptual and conceptual representation of one's own body, including its appearance, boundaries, and social significance — is dissociable from body schema: a person can have an accurate body image poor description of body but intact body schema for action. Peripersonal space — the region of space immediately surrounding the body that is encoded by bimodal neurons responding to both tactile and visual stimuli — is defended by the body as a functional extension of the self, with threat to peripersonal space activating defensive responses even when the threat does not contact the body surface. The rubber hand illusion — the multisensory integration phenomenon in which synchronous tactile stimulation of a visible rubber hand and the hidden real hand produces the vivid illusion that the rubber hand is one's own — demonstrates the brain's use of temporal synchrony and spatial congruence to assign body ownership, revealing the conditional and constructive nature of the sense of body ownership. Body schema plasticity — the capacity for the body schema to reorganize in response to injury, training, and experience — underlies the effectiveness of mirror therapy for phantom limb pain, the expansion of peripersonal space in expert tool users, and the reorganization of cortical body maps in musicians. The Yi-Shen body map cultivation correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that the Yi — the intellect associated with the Spleen — and the Shen together construct and refine the internal body map through cultivation practice, with advanced practitioners developing an extraordinary precision of internal body awareness that corresponds to the high interoceptive accuracy and body schema refinement of elite somatic practitioners.

## Body Schema Architecture

- Implicit sensorimotor representation governing automatic movement and postural control.
- Continuously updated by proprioceptive tactile and vestibular inputs.
- Parietal cortex: primary cortical substrate for body schema — posterior parietal and somatosensory.
- Premotor cortex: predictive body schema for action planning — forward models of movement.

## Rubber Hand Illusion

- Synchronous tactile stimulation of rubber hand and hidden real hand produces ownership illusion.
- Temporal synchrony and spatial congruence are the critical multisensory integration cues.
- Ownership illusion accompanied by referred sensation, proprioceptive drift, and autonomic response.
- Demonstrates the brain's conditional construction of body ownership from multisensory evidence.

## Peripersonal Space

- Bimodal neurons respond to both touch on body and visual stimuli near body.
- Peripersonal space boundary varies with context: expands with tools, contracts with fear.
- Defensive responses activated by threat to peripersonal space — functional body extension.
- Social peripersonal space: interpersonal distance preferences reflect peripersonal space boundaries.
