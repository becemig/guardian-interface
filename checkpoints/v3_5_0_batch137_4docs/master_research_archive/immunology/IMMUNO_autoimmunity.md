# Autoimmunity and Tolerance Failure

## Abstract

Autoimmunity and tolerance failure — the breakdown of the immunological self-tolerance mechanisms that normally prevent the immune system from attacking the body's own tissues, producing the spectrum of autoimmune diseases affecting 5-8 percent of the population with a striking female predominance, including organ-specific autoimmune diseases such as type 1 diabetes targeting pancreatic beta cells, Hashimoto thyroiditis targeting the thyroid, and multiple sclerosis targeting myelin, and systemic autoimmune diseases such as rheumatoid arthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus, and Sjogren syndrome affecting multiple organ systems through autoantibodies and autoreactive T cells. Central and peripheral tolerance mechanisms — the two-stage system preventing autoimmunity, with central tolerance in the thymus and bone marrow deleting lymphocytes bearing high-affinity receptors for self-antigens through clonal deletion, with the AIRE transcription factor in thymic medullary epithelial cells enabling the expression of peripheral tissue antigens including insulin, thyroglobulin, and myelin antigens for tolerance induction, and with peripheral tolerance mechanisms including regulatory T cells, anergy, and immune ignorance maintaining tolerance to self-antigens not represented in the thymus. Molecular mimicry — the mechanism through which autoimmunity is triggered when immune responses against pathogen-derived antigens cross-react with structurally similar self-antigens, with the classic example of rheumatic fever in which antibodies against Streptococcus pyogenes M protein cross-react with cardiac myosin producing rheumatic carditis, and with multiple sclerosis associated with Epstein-Barr virus molecular mimicry between EBNA1 and the myelin antigen GlialCAM. Regulatory T cell failure — the critical role of FOXP3-expressing regulatory T cells in maintaining peripheral self-tolerance demonstrated by the IPEX syndrome in which FOXP3 loss-of-function mutations produce a devastating multi-organ autoimmune syndrome affecting the gut, thyroid, skin, and blood — and by the consistent finding of reduced Treg number and function in active autoimmune disease. The Zheng Qi fighting itself internal wind correspondence in TCM describes the classical category of Internal Wind arising from Liver Yin deficiency and Liver Yang rising, producing tremor, spasm, and erratic movement as the body's own Qi turns against itself in an uncontrolled fashion — a classical framework corresponding to the autoimmune pathophysiology in which the immune system's own adaptive machinery attacks self-tissues producing progressive organ destruction.

## Tolerance Mechanisms

- AIRE: thymic medullary epithelial cells — peripheral antigen expression for deletion.
- Clonal deletion: high affinity self-reactive thymocytes deleted — negative selection.
- Peripheral anergy: self-reactive T cells that escape thymus rendered unresponsive.
- Treg suppression: FOXP3 Tregs suppress self-reactive effector T cells peripherally.

## Molecular Mimicry

- Rheumatic fever: anti-streptococcal antibodies cross-react with cardiac myosin.
- Multiple sclerosis: EBV EBNA1 mimics GlialCAM — myelin autoimmunity.
- Type 1 diabetes: rotavirus VP7 mimics islet antigen — beta cell autoimmunity.
- Mechanism: shared epitopes between pathogen and self — tolerance bypass.

## Autoimmune Spectrum

- Organ specific: type 1 diabetes Hashimoto MS — single tissue target.
- Systemic: SLE RA Sjogren — multiple organ involvement.
- Female predominance: 78 percent of autoimmune patients female — sex hormones.
- Genetic: HLA alleles strongest genetic risk — MHC antigen presentation.
