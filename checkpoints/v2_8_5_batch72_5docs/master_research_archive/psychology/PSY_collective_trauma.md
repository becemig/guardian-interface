# Collective Trauma

## Abstract

Collective trauma — the psychological wound inflicted on an entire community, culture, or people through shared catastrophic experience — operates through mechanisms that differ fundamentally from individual trauma, propagating through generations via cultural transmission, epigenetic inheritance, and the disruption of the social fabric and meaning systems that give individual lives their coherence. Vamik Volkan's concept of chosen trauma describes how traumatic losses and humiliations that a large group was unable to mourn become transmitted to subsequent generations as a core element of group identity — the descendants unconsciously carry the unprocessed trauma of their ancestors as both a psychological burden and a badge of collective identity. Rachel Yehuda's landmark research on Holocaust survivors and their children demonstrated epigenetic transmission of trauma — the children of Holocaust survivors showed altered FKBP5 methylation patterns identical to those found in the survivors themselves, providing biological evidence for the intergenerational transmission of trauma through epigenetic mechanisms. Historical trauma theory developed by Maria Yellow Horse Brave Heart describes the cumulative multigenerational trauma experienced by indigenous peoples through colonization, genocide, forced relocation, and cultural destruction — proposing that the contemporary health disparities of indigenous communities are the direct psychological and biological consequence of unresolved historical trauma. Community healing from collective trauma requires approaches that operate at the collective level — truth and reconciliation processes, community ritual, cultural revitalization, and the restoration of the social fabric and meaning systems that collective trauma destroys. Ancestral Shen repair in the TCM and Chinese ancestral medicine tradition corresponds to the healing of the Shen disturbance transmitted through the lineage — through ancestor veneration practices, ritual healing, and the cultivation of the individual Shen as an act of ancestral healing.

## Epigenetic Transmission

- FKBP5 methylation changes from trauma are transmitted to offspring through epigenetic mechanisms.
- The transmitted epigenetic changes alter HPA axis reactivity producing stress sensitization in the next generation.
- This biological mechanism explains how trauma physiologically shapes descendants who did not directly experience the original trauma.
- Epigenetic changes are potentially reversible through therapeutic intervention corresponding to ancestral healing practices.

## Chosen Trauma

- Large groups transmit unprocessed traumatic losses to subsequent generations as a core identity element.
- Chosen traumas are periodically reactivated during periods of group stress or threat.
- They function simultaneously as a wound and as a source of group cohesion and identity.
- Resolution requires the collective mourning that the original generation was unable to complete.

## Community Healing

- Truth and reconciliation processes create the social container for collective mourning and acknowledgment.
- Cultural revitalization restores the meaning systems that collective trauma destroyed.
- Somatic community practices — ritual, dance, collective movement — address the embodied dimension of collective trauma.
- Individual healing from collective trauma is incomplete without addressing the collective dimension.
