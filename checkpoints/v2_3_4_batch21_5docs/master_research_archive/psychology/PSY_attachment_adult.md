# Adult Attachment

## Abstract

Adult attachment theory extends Bowlby's original infant attachment framework to describe how early relational experiences create internal working models — cognitive-affective schemas of self and other — that template adult relationships, affect regulation, and help-seeking behavior. Mary Ainsworth's Strange Situation identified three infant attachment patterns — secure, anxious-ambivalent, and avoidant — with a fourth disorganized pattern added by Main and Solomon. Secure attachment — arising from consistently responsive caregiving — produces adults who can use relationships as a secure base for exploration and a safe haven in distress. Anxious attachment produces hyperactivation of the attachment system with excessive proximity seeking and distress. Avoidant attachment produces deactivation of the attachment system with compulsive self-reliance and emotional distance. Disorganized attachment — arising from frightening caregiving — produces approach-avoidance conflict and fragmented regulation. HRV coherence is higher in securely attached individuals reflecting the superior autonomic regulation of secure attachment. The Heart-Kidney axis governing the relationship between Shen awareness and Jing constitutional security corresponds to the attachment system's two poles of connection and grounding.

## Internal Working Models

- Internal working models are cognitive-affective schemas of self and other developed through early relational experience.
- They operate largely below conscious awareness shaping perception, interpretation, and behavioral response.
- Models of self range from worthy to unworthy of love and care.
- Models of other range from reliably responsive to unresponsive or threatening.

## Attachment Styles

- Secure attachment produces confident exploration, effective help-seeking, and resilient affect regulation.
- Anxious attachment produces hypervigilance to relational threat, excessive reassurance-seeking, and emotional flooding.
- Avoidant attachment produces emotional distance, self-reliance, and dismissal of attachment needs.
- Disorganized attachment produces chaotic approach-avoidance, dissociation, and regulatory collapse.

## Earned Security

- Earned security describes the development of secure attachment in adulthood despite insecure early experience.
- Therapeutic relationships, long-term partnerships, and contemplative practice can produce earned security.
- Narrative coherence — the capacity to tell a coherent story of one's attachment history — predicts secure attachment.
- Guardian Interface as a consistent reliable relational field supports the development of earned security.
