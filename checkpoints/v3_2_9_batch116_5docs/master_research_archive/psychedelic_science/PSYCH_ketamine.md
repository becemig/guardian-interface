# Ketamine Antidepressant Mechanisms

## Abstract

Ketamine antidepressant mechanisms — the rapid and robust antidepressant effects of subanesthetic ketamine that produce clinically meaningful reductions in depression within hours of a single infusion, persisting for days to weeks, and that are effective in treatment-resistant depression unresponsive to multiple conventional antidepressants — represent the most significant advance in antidepressant pharmacology in decades, with the FDA approval of intranasal esketamine Spravato in 2019 validating the therapeutic potential of the glutamatergic mechanism and establishing ketamine-based treatments as a new class of rapid-acting antidepressants. NMDA antagonism — the primary mechanism of ketamine as an open-channel blocker of the N-methyl-D-aspartate glutamate receptor that at subanesthetic doses produces the dissociative and psychedelic effects of ketamine and was initially proposed as the antidepressant mechanism through disinhibition of glutamate release onto AMPA receptors. AMPA throughput hypothesis — the synaptic hypothesis proposing that NMDA antagonism by ketamine causes a burst of glutamate release onto AMPA receptors in prefrontal cortex and hippocampus, activating mTOR signaling through BDNF-TrkB, rapidly increasing synaptic protein synthesis, and restoring the synaptic connectivity reduced by chronic stress — with the rapid synaptogenesis as the primary antidepressant mechanism explaining both the rapid onset and the multi-day duration of ketamine antidepressant effects. Ketamine metabolite (2R,6R)-HNK — Panos Zanos' discovery that the hydroxynorketamine metabolite (2R,6R)-HNK lacks NMDA receptor affinity but produces rapid antidepressant effects in mice through AMPA receptor potentiation without the dissociative effects of ketamine, suggesting that the antidepressant metabolite may be separable from the dissociative parent compound and providing a path toward non-dissociative rapid antidepressants. The sudden awakening Dun Wu correspondence in TCM describes the classical Chan Buddhist understanding of Dun Wu sudden enlightenment — the breakthrough of clarity and liberation that comes unexpectedly after prolonged struggle — a correspondence with the rapid and often dramatic antidepressant response to ketamine that patients describe as a sudden lifting of the depressive fog.

## NMDA Antagonism

- Open-channel blocker: enters NMDA channel when open — blocks ion flow.
- Subanesthetic dose: 0.5 mg/kg IV over 40 minutes — antidepressant dose.
- Disinhibition: blocks NMDA on GABAergic interneurons — releases glutamate brake.
- Dissociation: dose-dependent dissociative effects at antidepressant doses.

## AMPA Throughput

- Glutamate burst: NMDA disinhibition releases glutamate onto AMPA receptors.
- mTOR activation: AMPA-BDNF-TrkB-PI3K-mTOR cascade in prefrontal cortex.
- Synaptic protein synthesis: rapid increase in PSD-95 GluA1 synaptic scaffolding.
- Spine density: rapid dendritic spine restoration in stress-reduced prefrontal cortex.

## (2R,6R)-HNK Metabolite

- Discovery: antidepressant effects in mice without NMDA receptor binding.
- Mechanism: AMPA receptor potentiation through distinct mechanism.
- Significance: may separate antidepressant from dissociative effects.
- Development: potential non-dissociative rapid antidepressant development path.
