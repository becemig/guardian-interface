# Functional Medicine Environmental Assessment

## Abstract

Functional medicine environmental assessment — the systematic clinical evaluation of an individual's total toxic burden, nutritional status, hormonal function, gut integrity, and metabolic capacity using advanced functional laboratory testing to identify the specific environmental and physiological contributors to chronic illness, and to guide personalized treatment protocols targeting the identified dysfunctions — represents the operational clinical framework of environmental medicine, translating the science of environmental toxicology, nutritional biochemistry, and systems biology into actionable clinical interventions for individuals with complex chronic illness related to environmental exposures. Toxin body burden testing — the laboratory assessment of accumulated environmental toxins in blood, urine, hair, and adipose tissue using GPL-TOX toxic non-metal profile measuring 172 environmental chemicals and their metabolites including organophosphate pesticides, phthalates, benzene, and styrene, heavy metals panels measuring whole blood and urine levels of lead, mercury, arsenic, cadmium, and aluminum, and mycotoxin urine testing for ochratoxin, aflatoxin, and trichothecenes, providing objective quantification of the patient's toxic burden for clinical decision-making and treatment monitoring. Organic acids testing — the urine organic acids profile measuring hundreds of metabolic intermediates that reflect mitochondrial function including Krebs cycle metabolites, fatty acid oxidation metabolites, and electron transport chain markers, neurotransmitter metabolism through markers of dopamine serotonin and GABA pathways, intestinal dysbiosis through markers of bacterial and fungal overgrowth, and nutritional cofactor adequacy through functional markers of B vitamin, CoQ10, and glutathione status, providing a comprehensive metabolic snapshot that guides targeted nutritional supplementation and treatment. Nutritional deficiency profiling — the functional nutritional assessment using intracellular micronutrient testing measuring nutrient adequacy within lymphocytes under conditions of replication stress that better reflects long-term functional adequacy than serum levels, amino acid profiles identifying deficiencies affecting neurotransmitter synthesis detoxification and protein structure, and fatty acid profiles identifying omega-3 omega-6 ratios and specific fatty acid deficiencies affecting inflammation and membrane function. The Four Examinations environmental pattern diagnosis correspondence in TCM describes the classical diagnostic methodology of looking listening smelling asking and touching to identify the patient's constitutional pattern and environmental pathogenic factors — a holistic diagnostic framework that corresponds to the functional medicine environmental history and systematic laboratory assessment that maps the patient's toxic burden and physiological dysfunction.

## Body Burden Testing

- GPL-TOX: 172 environmental chemicals in urine — organophosphates phthalates VOCs.
- Heavy metals: whole blood for recent exposure urine challenge for body burden.
- Mycotoxins: urine ochratoxin aflatoxin trichothecenes — CIRS mold illness.
- Hair analysis: long-term heavy metal exposure — arsenic mercury chronic exposure.

## Organic Acids

- Mitochondrial markers: citrate isocitrate succinate malate — Krebs cycle function.
- Dysbiosis markers: arabinose HPHPA D-arabinitol — bacterial fungal overgrowth.
- Neurotransmitter markers: HVA VMA 5-HIAA — dopamine serotonin metabolism.
- Nutritional markers: methylmalonate xanthurenate — B12 B6 functional status.

## Functional Lab Framework

- Optimal vs reference ranges: functional medicine uses narrower optimal ranges.
- Pattern recognition: multiple mildly abnormal markers form diagnostic patterns.
- Retesting: functional labs guide treatment and monitor response.
- Integration: labs interpreted in context of history symptoms and physical examination.
