# Oxidative Stress and Antioxidant Defense

## Abstract

Reactive oxygen species ROS are chemically reactive molecules produced as byproducts of mitochondrial respiration and immune activity that drive cellular aging and disease when in excess. The antioxidant defense system comprising superoxide dismutase, catalase, glutathione peroxidase, and the Nrf2 transcription factor maintains ROS homeostasis. Hormesis describes the paradoxical beneficial effects of low-dose stressors including exercise-induced ROS that activate adaptive responses exceeding the initial damage. Mitochondrial health is central to oxidative stress management with mitochondrial biogenesis through PGC-1alpha providing the key adaptive response to exercise. Kidney Jing preservation in TCM corresponds to minimizing unnecessary oxidative depletion while cultivating adaptive resilience through appropriate practice challenge.

## Reactive Oxygen Species

- Superoxide anion is the primary ROS produced at mitochondrial complexes I and III during electron transport.
- Superoxide is converted to hydrogen peroxide by superoxide dismutase and then to water by catalase.
- Hydroxyl radical produced by Fenton reaction is the most reactive and damaging ROS species.
- Nitric oxide reacts with superoxide to form peroxynitrite a highly reactive nitrogen species.

## Antioxidant Defense

- Superoxide dismutase is the first line antioxidant enzyme converting superoxide to hydrogen peroxide.
- Glutathione the master antioxidant is regenerated by glutathione reductase using NADPH.
- Nrf2 transcription factor is activated by mild oxidative stress driving expression of antioxidant and detoxification genes.
- Exercise activates Nrf2 producing a sustained upregulation of antioxidant capacity beyond the exercise period.

## Hormesis and Exercise

- Low-dose oxidative stress from exercise activates adaptive responses that exceed the initial damage.
- These adaptations include mitochondrial biogenesis, antioxidant upregulation, and heat shock protein induction.
- The result is a net improvement in cellular resilience and oxidative stress tolerance.
- Tai Chi and Qigong produce mild hormetic oxidative stress while also reducing chronic inflammatory ROS production.
