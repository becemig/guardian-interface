# Neuroinflammation

## Abstract

Neuroinflammation is the inflammatory response within the central nervous system mediated primarily by microglia — the resident immune cells of the brain — and infiltrating peripheral immune cells. Microglia survey the brain parenchyma in a ramified resting state and transform into activated amoeboid morphology upon detecting damage, infection, or systemic inflammatory signals crossing the blood-brain barrier. Activated microglia release pro-inflammatory cytokines including IL-1 beta, IL-6, and TNF-alpha that alter neuronal function, synaptic plasticity, and neurotransmitter metabolism. Robert Dantzer's cytokine hypothesis of depression proposes that inflammatory cytokines produce sickness behavior — fatigue, social withdrawal, anhedonia, and cognitive slowing — that overlaps substantially with major depression symptomatology. CRP and IL-6 elevation predicts depression onset and treatment resistance in clinical populations. Neuroinflammation in Alzheimer's disease reflects chronic microglial activation around amyloid plaques. Phlegm-Fire misting the Heart orifices in TCM — the turbid obstruction of Shen clarity by accumulated Phlegm combined with Heat — corresponds to the neuroinflammatory disruption of neural signal clarity and cognitive function.

## Microglial Activation

- Resting ramified microglia survey approximately 1400 cubic micrometers of brain tissue per hour.
- Activation stimuli include PAMPs from pathogens, DAMPs from cellular damage, and peripheral cytokines.
- M1-like activation produces pro-inflammatory cytokines promoting inflammation and phagocytosis.
- M2-like activation produces anti-inflammatory cytokines and growth factors promoting repair and resolution.

## Cytokine Effects on Brain

- IL-1 beta reduces hippocampal neurogenesis and impairs LTP corresponding to depression-related cognitive symptoms.
- TNF-alpha reduces AMPA receptor surface expression weakening synaptic transmission.
- Inflammatory cytokines shift tryptophan metabolism from serotonin toward kynurenine reducing serotonin availability.
- IDO activation by inflammation produces quinolinic acid — an NMDA receptor agonist with neurotoxic properties.

## Depression-Inflammation Link

- Approximately one third of depressed patients show elevated inflammatory markers.
- Anti-inflammatory treatment reduces depression symptoms in patients with elevated baseline inflammation.
- Inflammatory cytokines from peripheral infections, autoimmune disease, and obesity produce sickness behavior.
- The sickness behavior phenotype — fatigue, anhedonia, social withdrawal — is mediated by brain cytokine action.
