# Sleep Architecture

## Abstract

Sleep architecture describes the cyclical organization of sleep into approximately 90-minute cycles alternating between non-REM NREM sleep stages and REM sleep. NREM sleep progresses through three stages — N1 light sleep, N2 with sleep spindles and K-complexes, and N3 slow wave sleep — before transitioning to REM sleep with its characteristic rapid eye movements, muscle atonia, and vivid dreaming. Early sleep cycles are dominated by slow wave sleep while later cycles contain progressively more REM sleep. Robert Stickgold demonstrated that both NREM and REM sleep contribute to memory consolidation through distinct mechanisms — slow wave sleep enabling hippocampal-neocortical transfer of declarative memories and REM sleep enabling procedural and emotional memory integration. Maiken Nedergaard's glymphatic system research demonstrated that cerebrospinal fluid clearance of metabolic waste including amyloid beta is dramatically enhanced during slow wave sleep through the expansion of brain interstitial space. Liver storing Blood at night and Hun returning to the Liver during sleep in TCM corresponds to the restorative and integrative functions of sleep architecture — the Liver as the organ of nocturnal regeneration.

## NREM Sleep

- N2 sleep spindles reflect thalamocortical synchronization that actively suppresses external sensory processing.
- Sleep spindles correlate with overnight memory consolidation particularly for declarative and procedural learning.
- N3 slow wave sleep is characterized by high amplitude low frequency delta oscillations reflecting cortical synchrony.
- Slow wave sleep is the most restorative stage — growth hormone release peaks during the first slow wave episode.

## REM Sleep

- REM sleep is characterized by rapid eye movements, muscle atonia, and activated EEG resembling wakefulness.
- Hippocampal sharp wave ripples during REM replay waking experience supporting memory consolidation.
- Emotional memory processing during REM reduces the affective charge of emotionally significant memories.
- REM deprivation impairs emotional regulation and threat detection the following day.

## Glymphatic Clearance

- The glymphatic system uses CSF flowing along perivascular spaces to clear interstitial metabolic waste.
- Slow wave sleep increases interstitial space by 60 percent dramatically enhancing glymphatic flow.
- Amyloid beta clearance is substantially higher during sleep than during wakefulness.
- Chronic sleep deprivation accelerates amyloid accumulation corresponding to the increased Alzheimer's risk of poor sleep.
