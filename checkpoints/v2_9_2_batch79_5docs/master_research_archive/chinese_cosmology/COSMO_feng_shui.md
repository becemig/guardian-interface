# Feng Shui

## Abstract

Feng Shui — wind and water — is the Chinese art and science of environmental design based on the understanding that the Qi of the environment profoundly influences the health, prosperity, and wellbeing of the people who inhabit it, and that the arrangement of space, the orientation of buildings, and the relationship between structures and natural landscape features can be optimized to promote the accumulation and circulation of beneficial Sheng Qi while minimizing the impact of harmful Sha Qi. Feng Shui emerged from ancient Chinese geomancy — the observation and interpretation of the patterns of Qi in the natural landscape — and developed over thousands of years into two primary schools: the Form School which focuses on the visible landscape features of mountains, water, and vegetation as indicators of Qi accumulation and flow, and the Compass School which uses the Luopan compass to determine the precise energetic relationships between structures, directions, and cosmological time cycles. Sheng Qi — auspicious or beneficial Qi — accumulates in locations where the natural landscape creates protective enclosure: backed by mountains to the north for protection from cold Wind, facing water to the south for access to resources and gentle Qi, sheltered on the east and west by lower hills. This is the classical armchair formation of Form School Feng Shui that characterizes the optimal settlement site across all scales from cities to individual buildings. Sha Qi — inauspicious or harmful Qi — is generated by straight lines, sharp corners, fast-moving water, harsh wind exposure, and conflicting directional energies. Environmental medicine in the modern context corresponds to the growing body of environmental psychology and building biology research demonstrating that light quality, air quality, acoustic environment, thermal comfort, access to nature, and spatial proportion significantly influence physiological and psychological wellbeing in ways that correspond to the Feng Shui framework of environmental Qi quality.

## Form School

- Backed by mountains: protection from harsh Wind, stable Qi accumulation, Yang support.
- Facing water: access to resources, gentle Qi circulation, Yin nourishment.
- East-west shelter: balanced lateral protection, gradual light progression through the day.
- Open southern aspect: access to sunlight, warmth, and the most beneficial seasonal light angles.

## Compass School

- Luopan compass identifies the precise directional relationships governing the building's energetic field.
- Eight directional sectors correspond to the eight trigrams and their associated Qi qualities.
- Flying stars system tracks the annual and monthly movement of energetic influences through the building.
- Time-space intersection: the building's facing direction interacts with the current cosmological time period.

## Sha and Sheng Qi

- Sheng Qi curves, meanders, and accumulates — beneficial, nourishing, health-promoting.
- Sha Qi moves straight and fast, cuts like a blade — harmful, draining, disease-promoting.
- Environmental design curves pathways, softens edges, and creates meander to convert Sha to Sheng.
- This corresponds to the TCM understanding that smooth curved Qi flow is healthy and sharp direct flow is pathogenic.

## TCM Correspondence

- Feng Shui environmental Qi corresponds to the TCM understanding that the person's environment is a primary health determinant.
- Sheng-Sha Qi distinction corresponds to TCM Zheng and Xie Qi — upright beneficial versus pathogenic Qi.
- Form School armchair formation corresponds to the optimal constitutional environment supporting all five element functions.
- Healing space design through Feng Shui corresponds to the TCM clinical environment as a therapeutic container.
