# Nei Jing Ling Shu

## Abstract

The Huang Di Nei Jing Ling Shu — the Spiritual Pivot — is the companion volume to the Su Wen and together they constitute the complete Nei Jing. Where the Su Wen establishes the theoretical foundations of Chinese medicine, the Ling Shu provides the detailed clinical and technical knowledge of acupuncture — the channel pathways, the acupuncture point locations and indications, the nine needle types, the needling techniques, and the theory of how acupuncture works to regulate Qi and Blood through the Jing Luo network. The name Ling Shu meaning Spiritual Pivot reflects the text's understanding that acupuncture works not merely at the physical tissue level but at the level of the Shen — it pivots the spirit back to center. The Ling Shu's channel theory describes the primary channels Jing Mai, the divergent channels Jing Bie, the sinew channels Jing Jin, the cutaneous regions Pi Bu, and the Luo connecting vessels as a complete multi-layered network for the circulation of Qi and Blood throughout the body. The five Shu transporting points — Jing well, Ying spring, Shu stream, Jing river, He sea — are described in the Ling Shu as the five levels through which channel Qi flows from the periphery to the interior, each level with distinct clinical properties and indications. The nine needle types — from the fine filiform needle to the large round-headed needle to the three-edged needle — are described with specific indications for different tissue depths, pathological conditions, and clinical goals. The Ling Shu's discussion of spirit-body integration — particularly the relationship between the Five Shen and the organ networks — provides the deepest classical articulation of TCM psychosomatic medicine, establishing that treating the Shen is inseparable from treating the body.

## Channel Architecture

- Primary channels Jing Mai: the twelve bilateral channels governing organ network Qi circulation.
- Divergent channels Jing Bie: deeper pathways connecting paired Yin-Yang channels and reaching the Heart.
- Sinew channels Jing Jin: superficial pathways governing the musculoskeletal system and movement.
- Luo connecting vessels: transverse connections between paired channels enabling Qi-Blood overflow regulation.

## Five Shu Points

- Jing well points at the finger and toe tips: where channel Qi wells up — acute and emergency conditions.
- Ying spring points: where Qi glides — Heat conditions and febrile disorders.
- Shu stream points: where Qi pours — intermittent conditions and heaviness-dampness.
- Jing river points: where Qi flows strongly — respiratory and voice conditions.
- He sea points at the elbow and knee: where Qi enters the deep — organ conditions and rebellious Qi.

## Nine Needles

- Filiform needle Chan Zhen: the standard acupuncture needle for tonifying and regulating.
- Three-edged needle San Leng Zhen: for bloodletting and releasing acute excess conditions.
- Round-headed needle Yuan Zhen: for superficial massage and Qi activation without penetration.
- Each needle type matches a specific clinical situation — needle selection is itself a diagnostic statement.
