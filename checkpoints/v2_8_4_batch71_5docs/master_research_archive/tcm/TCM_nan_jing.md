# Nan Jing

## Abstract

The Nan Jing — Classic of Difficulties — attributed to the legendary physician Bian Que and composed during the Han dynasty, addresses 81 difficult questions arising from the Nei Jing, clarifying ambiguous passages, elaborating theoretical points, and introducing significant innovations in pulse diagnosis, acupuncture theory, and the understanding of the extraordinary vessels. The format of 81 numbered difficulties Nan provides a systematic refinement of Nei Jing theory through the dialectical examination of specific clinical and theoretical problems. The Nan Jing's innovations in pulse diagnosis represent one of its most enduring contributions — it established the three-position nine-level pulse examination system that refined the Nei Jing's pulse theory into the comprehensive diagnostic system that became standard in all subsequent TCM practice. The Nan Jing's development of five element acupuncture — particularly the Mother-Child law of tonifying the mother point to supplement deficiency and draining the child point to reduce excess — provided the theoretical basis for what became the Japanese acupuncture tradition's distinctive five element clinical approach. The Nan Jing's treatment of the eight extraordinary vessels Qi Jing Ba Mai — particularly its discussion of the Ren and Du vessels as the primary Yin-Yang poles — significantly advanced the extraordinary vessel theory that Li Shi Zhen would later systematize in the Qi Jing Ba Mai Kao. The discussion of Yuan source Qi and the Yuan source points established the theoretical foundation for using source points to access constitutional Kidney Jing and support the deepest level of treatment.

## 81 Difficulties

- Difficulties 1-22: pulse diagnosis theory and the three-position nine-level system.
- Difficulties 23-29: channel theory, Ying-Wei circulation, and extraordinary vessels.
- Difficulties 30-47: Zangfu organ theory and their relationships.
- Difficulties 48-81: acupuncture theory, five element needling, and clinical applications.

## Mother-Child Law

- Each of the five element points on every channel corresponds to one of the five elements.
- Deficiency: tonify the mother point — if Wood is deficient tonify the Water point on the Wood channel.
- Excess: drain the child point — if Wood is excess drain the Fire point on the Wood channel.
- This law provides a systematic five element acupuncture protocol derived from the generating cycle.

## Yuan Source Points

- Yuan source points are located near the wrist and ankle on each primary channel.
- They provide direct access to the Yuan source Qi — the constitutional Kidney Jing.
- Needling Yuan source points supports the deepest constitutional level of treatment.
- Combined with Luo connecting points they form the Host-Guest combination for channel pair treatment.
