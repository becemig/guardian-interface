# Sleep Architecture and TCM

## Abstract

Sleep is an active physiological process comprising alternating cycles of non-REM and REM sleep each serving distinct biological functions. Slow-wave sleep governs physical restoration, immune consolidation, and glymphatic brain clearance. REM sleep governs memory consolidation, emotional processing, and in TCM terms the activity of the Hun ethereal soul in dream space. The TCM organ clock maps organ Qi activity to specific two-hour intervals across the day and night cycle governing sleep quality and dream content. Heart Blood and Kidney Yin deficiency are the most common TCM patterns underlying chronic insomnia.

## Sleep Stages

- NREM Stage 1 is light transitional sleep with theta waves and hypnic jerks.
- NREM Stage 2 involves sleep spindles and K-complexes marking cortical memory consolidation.
- NREM Stage 3 slow-wave sleep involves high-amplitude delta waves and physical restoration.
- REM sleep involves cortical activation, atonia, and vivid dreaming corresponding to Hun soul activity.
- A full sleep cycle lasts approximately 90 minutes with 4 to 6 cycles per night.

## Glymphatic Clearance

- Slow-wave sleep drives maximum glymphatic CSF flow through periarteriolar spaces.
- Brain interstitial space expands 60 percent during sleep enabling enhanced waste clearance.
- Amyloid beta and tau protein are cleared during sleep — chronic sleep deprivation drives neurodegeneration.
- Tai Chi and Qigong improve sleep quality thereby enhancing glymphatic brain detoxification.

## TCM Correspondence

- Heart Blood houses the Shen during waking and must be sufficient for the Shen to rest during sleep.
- Liver stores Blood at night and houses the Hun which travels during REM dreaming.
- The organ clock places Heart activity at 11am to 1pm and Liver activity at 1am to 3am corresponding to dream peaks.
- Heart Blood deficiency produces insomnia with difficulty falling asleep and excess dreaming.
- Kidney Yin deficiency produces waking at 3am to 5am with night sweats and restlessness.
