# Nutrigenomics and Personalized Nutrition

## Abstract

Nutrigenomics and personalized nutrition — the field studying how individual genetic variation determines differential responses to dietary components, and how dietary components regulate gene expression, epigenetic marks, and metabolic phenotypes — establishes the scientific basis for truly individualized dietary recommendations that account for the genetic, epigenetic, and microbiome variation that produces dramatically different responses to the same foods across different individuals, corresponding to the TCM constitutional medicine approach that has always recognized individual differences in dietary needs based on constitutional type. Gene-diet interactions — the modification of dietary effects by genetic variation, with well-characterized examples including MTHFR polymorphisms determining folate requirements for DNA methylation, APOE4 genotype dramatically increasing cardiovascular risk from saturated fat, FADS1 polymorphisms determining conversion efficiency of plant omega-3s to EPA and DHA, and lactase persistence versus non-persistence determining adult dairy tolerance — demonstrate that optimal diet is not universal but depends on individual genetic makeup, with nutrigenetic testing potentially enabling truly personalized dietary guidance. Epigenetic nutrition — the regulation of epigenetic marks including DNA methylation, histone modification, and microRNA expression by dietary components, with methyl donor nutrients including folate, choline, methionine, and B12 governing DNA methylation patterns, polyphenols including resveratrol and EGCG modulating histone deacetylases, and caloric restriction producing widespread epigenetic changes associated with longevity — establishes that diet influences gene expression at the epigenetic level in ways that can persist across cell generations and potentially across generations through germline epigenetic inheritance. Nutrient-sensing pathways — the molecular switches including mTOR sensing amino acid abundance, AMPK sensing energy deficit, SIRT1 sensing NAD+ and caloric restriction, and FoxO transcription factors sensing insulin and growth factor signaling — translate nutritional status into transcriptional programs governing metabolism, cellular maintenance, stress resistance, and longevity, providing the molecular basis for understanding why dietary interventions including caloric restriction, intermittent fasting, and protein restriction produce profound effects on aging and disease. The constitutional diet correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that dietary recommendations must be individualized according to the patient's constitutional type — with Kidney Yang deficient constitutions requiring warming foods and Liver Qi stagnant constitutions requiring moving foods — a clinical tradition of personalized nutritional medicine that corresponds to the modern nutrigenomics framework.

## MTHFR and Methylation

- MTHFR C677T polymorphism: reduces enzyme activity 30-70 percent.
- Consequence: impaired folate conversion reduced methylation capacity.
- Dietary implication: higher folate B12 B6 riboflavin requirements.
- Clinical relevance: cardiovascular risk neural tube defects psychiatric vulnerability.

## APOE Genotype

- APOE2: protective against cardiovascular disease and Alzheimer's.
- APOE3: neutral — most common genotype.
- APOE4: increased cardiovascular risk from saturated fat increased Alzheimer's risk.
- Dietary implication: APOE4 carriers benefit more from Mediterranean-type low saturated fat diet.

## Epigenetic Nutrition

- Methyl donors: folate choline methionine B12 — govern DNA methylation patterns.
- Polyphenols: EGCG resveratrol quercetin — modulate HDAC and DNMT activity.
- Caloric restriction: widespread hypomethylation changes associated with longevity genes.
- Early life nutrition: maternal diet programs offspring epigenome with lasting health effects.
