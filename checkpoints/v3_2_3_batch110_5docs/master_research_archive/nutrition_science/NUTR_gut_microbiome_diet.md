# Gut Microbiome and Diet

## Abstract

Gut microbiome and diet — the bidirectional relationship between dietary composition and the gut microbial ecosystem that together determine metabolic health, immune function, and disease risk — establishes dietary fiber fermentation, the production of short-chain fatty acids, and the maintenance of microbial diversity as central mechanisms through which plant-rich diets promote health, corresponding to the TCM Spleen-Earth network's governance of transformation and transportation as the central digestive and metabolic function that processes not only macronutrients but the entire ecology of the gut microbiome. Dietary fiber fermentation — the anaerobic fermentation of non-digestible plant polysaccharides including inulin, pectin, resistant starch, and beta-glucan by the colonic microbiome to produce short-chain fatty acids primarily acetate, propionate, and butyrate — is the primary mechanism by which dietary fiber produces its diverse health benefits, with fiber fermentation supporting microbial diversity, maintaining colonocyte health through butyrate production, and producing the systemic metabolic effects of SCFA absorption into the portal circulation. Butyrate as colonocyte fuel — the four-carbon SCFA produced by Firmicutes including Roseburia and Faecalibacterium prausnitzii through fiber fermentation that serves as the primary energy substrate for colonocytes, maintaining the energy-intensive tight junction barrier that prevents translocation of microbial products into the circulation, suppressing NF-kB to reduce colonocyte inflammation, activating HDAC inhibition to regulate colonocyte gene expression, and suppressing aberrant colonocyte proliferation to reduce colorectal cancer risk — establishes butyrate as the primary mechanism through which dietary fiber maintains colonic health. Microbiome diversity diet — the consistent finding across diverse populations that higher dietary diversity, particularly plant food diversity, is the strongest dietary predictor of gut microbiome alpha diversity, with each additional 10 plant species per week associated with significantly higher microbial diversity, and with low-diversity microbiomes associated with obesity, type 2 diabetes, inflammatory bowel disease, and cardiovascular disease — establishes plant food diversity as the primary nutritional target for microbiome health. The Spleen Earth microbiome cultivation correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding of the Spleen and Stomach Earth network as the central digestive axis governing the transformation and transportation of food Qi — a functional description that encompasses not only the physiological digestion processes but the entire microbial ecosystem that extends the digestive capacity of the host.

## Fiber Fermentation

- Firmicutes: primary butyrate producers — Roseburia Faecalibacterium Clostridium butyricum.
- Bacteroidetes: primary propionate and acetate producers.
- Fermentation capacity: determined by microbiome composition and substrate availability.
- Resistant starch: potato starch green banana oats — highly fermentable microbiome substrate.

## Butyrate Functions

- Colonocyte fuel: 70 percent of colonocyte energy from butyrate oxidation.
- Barrier maintenance: supports tight junction protein expression TEER.
- HDAC inhibition: regulates colonocyte gene expression anti-inflammatory anti-cancer.
- Systemic effects: portal absorption activates hepatic GPR109A FFAR2 signaling.

## Microbiome Diversity

- Plant food diversity: strongest dietary predictor of microbiome alpha diversity.
- 30 plants per week: target associated with highest microbial diversity in American Gut Project.
- Mediterranean diet: highest microbiome diversity and health outcomes in multiple cohort studies.
- Western diet: low diversity high processed food intake — associated with dysbiosis.
