# CB1 and CB2 Receptor Distribution and Function

## Abstract

CB1 and CB2 receptor distribution and function — the two primary G protein-coupled receptors of the endocannabinoid system, both coupling primarily through Gi/o proteins to inhibit adenylyl cyclase, activate inwardly rectifying potassium channels, and inhibit voltage-gated calcium channels — producing net inhibitory effects on neuronal excitability and neurotransmitter release at CB1, and anti-inflammatory and immune-modulatory effects at CB2 — with distinct anatomical distributions reflecting their distinct physiological roles in the central nervous system and immune periphery. CB1 neuronal expression and synaptic modulation — the most abundantly expressed G protein-coupled receptor in the brain, found at high density in the basal ganglia, cerebellum, hippocampus, prefrontal cortex, anterior cingulate cortex, amygdala, and hypothalamus, localized predominantly on the presynaptic terminals of both excitatory glutamatergic and inhibitory GABAergic neurons where endocannabinoid binding suppresses neurotransmitter release through Gi-mediated inhibition of presynaptic calcium channels — making CB1 the primary mediator of endocannabinoid retrograde synaptic modulation. CB2 immune and peripheral expression — originally identified as the peripheral cannabinoid receptor, expressed at high levels on immune cells including macrophages, B cells, natural killer cells, and microglia, with activation producing anti-inflammatory effects through reduced cytokine production, reduced immune cell migration, and induction of apoptosis in activated immune cells, and with CB2 expression on microglia being upregulated in neuroinflammatory conditions making CB2 a therapeutic target for neuroinflammation without the psychoactive effects of CB1 activation. Receptor heterodimers — the formation of functional CB1-CB2, CB1-dopamine D2, CB1-opioid mu receptor, and CB1-adenosine A2A heterodimers that modify the pharmacological properties of each receptor in the pair, with CB1-mu opioid heterodimers potentially underlying the opioid-sparing effects of cannabinoids in pain management and CB1-D2 heterodimers modulating reward circuit function. The Triple Burner regulatory network correspondence in TCM describes the classical Triple Burner as the organ system with no fixed form that governs the transformation and movement of Qi and fluids throughout the three body cavities — upper, middle, and lower — integrating the functions of all organ systems — a classical framework corresponding to the endocannabinoid system's role as a ubiquitous regulatory network modulating virtually every physiological system through the widespread distribution of CB1 and CB2 receptors.

## CB1 Distribution

- Basal ganglia: highest density — movement coordination reward.
- Cerebellum: motor coordination fine-tuning.
- Hippocampus: learning memory anxiety — CB1 modulates LTP.
- Prefrontal cortex: executive function stress response emotional regulation.

## CB1 Signaling

- Gi/o coupling: inhibits adenylyl cyclase — reduces cAMP.
- GIRK channels: activates inward rectifying K+ — hyperpolarization.
- VGCC: inhibits voltage-gated Ca2+ channels — reduces neurotransmitter release.
- ERK MAPK: activates proliferative survival signaling.

## CB2 Distribution and Function

- Immune cells: macrophages B cells NK cells microglia — primary expression.
- Peripheral: liver spleen thymus — immune regulation sites.
- Neuroinflammation: CB2 upregulated on activated microglia — therapeutic target.
- Anti-inflammatory: reduced TNF IL-6 cytokine production — immune modulation.
