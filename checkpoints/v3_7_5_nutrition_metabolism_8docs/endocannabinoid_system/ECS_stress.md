# Endocannabinoid System and Stress Response

## Abstract

Endocannabinoid system and stress response — the critical role of endocannabinoid signaling in modulating the physiological and behavioral responses to stress, with the ECS serving as a regulatory buffer that limits the magnitude and duration of HPA axis activation, facilitates the extinction of fear memories through CB1-mediated plasticity in the basolateral amygdala, and mediates the anxiolytic effects of exercise and positive social experience — with chronic stress reducing endocannabinoid tone and CB1 receptor expression contributing to the development of anxiety and depressive disorders, and with ECS augmentation representing a promising therapeutic target for stress-related conditions. ECS regulation of HPA axis — the bidirectional interaction between the HPA stress axis and the endocannabinoid system, with glucocorticoids rapidly stimulating 2-AG synthesis in the hypothalamus and other limbic structures through a membrane-initiated non-genomic mechanism that provides fast negative feedback on CRH neurons through CB1-mediated retrograde inhibition — with CB1 knockout mice showing exaggerated and prolonged HPA responses to stress, and with chronic stress reducing CB1 receptor expression and 2-AG levels in the prefrontal cortex and hippocampus undermining this negative feedback. Fear extinction and amygdala ECS — the essential role of CB1 receptors in the basolateral amygdala for the extinction of conditioned fear memories, with CB1 antagonism blocking fear extinction and FAAH inhibition facilitating fear extinction in rodent models, with the mechanism involving eCB-LTD at excitatory synapses onto basolateral amygdala GABAergic interneurons that reduces inhibitory tone and enables plasticity — with implications for PTSD treatment through ECS augmentation to facilitate fear extinction. Endocannabinoid buffering of stress — the concept of the ECS as a stress buffer system that is activated by acute stress to limit the physiological and behavioral stress responses through multiple mechanisms including HPA negative feedback, suppression of locus coeruleus noradrenergic activity, modulation of amygdala fear circuits, and suppression of hypothalamic CRH release — with chronic stress depleting this buffer and FAAH or MAGL inhibition restoring it. The Kidney Yang stress mobilization ECS buffer correspondence in TCM describes the classical Kidney Yang as the warming activating force that mobilizes constitutional resources in response to stress and challenge — with the Kidney also governing the storage of Jing that provides the constitutional reserve — corresponding to the ECS buffering of HPA stress responses through the Kidney-associated constitutional Jing reserve.

## HPA Regulation

- Glucocorticoid: rapidly stimulates 2-AG synthesis — non-genomic membrane mechanism.
- Retrograde inhibition: 2-AG suppresses CRH neuron activity — fast negative feedback.
- CB1 knockout: exaggerated prolonged HPA stress response — ECS required for termination.
- Chronic stress: reduces CB1 expression and 2-AG in PFC hippocampus — buffer depleted.

## Fear Extinction

- BLA CB1: essential for fear extinction — CB1 antagonism blocks extinction.
- FAAH inhibition: facilitates fear extinction — elevated anandamide.
- Mechanism: eCB-LTD at BLA interneuron synapses — enables plasticity for extinction.
- PTSD: endocannabinoid deficiency in PTSD — reduced AEA — augmentation therapeutic.

## Stress Buffering

- Acute stress: ECS activated — limits magnitude and duration of stress response.
- Noradrenergic: CB1 in locus coeruleus — suppresses norepinephrine stress response.
- Chronic depletion: repeated stress reduces ECS tone — anxiety depression vulnerability.
- Exercise: ECS mediates anxiolytic effects of exercise — anandamide elevation.
