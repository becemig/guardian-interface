# HPA Axis Dysregulation in Trauma

## Abstract

The hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis is the primary neuroendocrine stress response system, and its dysregulation is a central feature of trauma and PTSD. Acute trauma activates CRF release from the hypothalamus, driving ACTH from the pituitary and cortisol from the adrenal cortex. In PTSD the system does not return to baseline - instead showing paradoxical cortisol suppression with heightened glucocorticoid receptor sensitivity, meaning the body becomes hypersensitive to low cortisol levels rather than producing high ones. Chronic stress causes allostatic load - cumulative wear on regulatory systems from repeated or prolonged activation. Hippocampal damage from glucocorticoid excess impairs negative feedback, perpetuating dysregulation. Epigenetic transmission of HPA sensitivity across generations is documented in Holocaust survivor offspring and animal models.

## Normal HPA Axis Function

- Hypothalamus: CRF (corticotropin-releasing factor) released in response to stress or low cortisol
- Pituitary: CRF triggers ACTH (adrenocorticotropic hormone) release
- Adrenal cortex: ACTH drives cortisol synthesis and release
- Negative feedback: cortisol binds GR in hippocampus and hypothalamus to suppress CRF and ACTH
- Diurnal rhythm: cortisol peaks 30 minutes after waking (CAR), troughs at midnight
- Acute stress response: cortisol elevation is adaptive - mobilizes glucose, suppresses inflammation, consolidates memory

## PTSD HPA Pattern - Paradoxical Suppression

- Finding: PTSD shows LOW baseline cortisol - opposite of expected stress response
- Mechanism: upregulated glucocorticoid receptor (GR) sensitivity - system hypersensitive to small cortisol amounts
- Enhanced negative feedback: even low cortisol strongly suppresses CRF and ACTH
- Dexamethasone suppression: PTSD shows enhanced DST suppression - hallmark finding
- Distinction from depression: major depression shows elevated cortisol and blunted DST
- Implication: PTSD is not chronic stress activation but chronic stress sensitization

## Hippocampal Damage

- Chronic cortisol excess causes dendritic atrophy in CA3 hippocampal neurons
- Volume loss: 8-12 percent hippocampal volume reduction documented in PTSD by MRI
- Function impact: impaired explicit memory, context discrimination, fear extinction
- Cannot distinguish past from present: traumatic memories intrude without temporal tagging
- Neurogenesis suppression: chronic cortisol reduces hippocampal neurogenesis
- Recovery: BDNF-promoting interventions, exercise, and ECS activation support hippocampal recovery

## Allostatic Load

- Definition: cumulative physiological cost of adapting to chronic stressors
- McEwen model: allostasis is adaptive change, allostatic load is the cost of repeated allostasis
- Biomarkers: cortisol AUC, DHEA-S, IL-6, CRP, blood pressure, HRV, BMI, fibrinogen
- Four types: frequent stress, failed shutdown, inadequate response, inappropriate response
- TCM parallel: Jing essence depletion through chronic Wei Qi overactivation - constitutional exhaustion

## Epigenetic Transmission

- Holocaust offspring: lower cortisol levels and higher GR sensitivity than controls
- Mechanism: methylation of NR3C1 glucocorticoid receptor gene alters GR expression
- Animal models: early life stress methylates CRF promoter regions - transmitted to offspring
- Reversal: enriched environment, nurturing care, and HDAC inhibitors can reverse methylation
- TCM parallel: pre-heaven Jing depletion transmitted through parental constitutional weakness
