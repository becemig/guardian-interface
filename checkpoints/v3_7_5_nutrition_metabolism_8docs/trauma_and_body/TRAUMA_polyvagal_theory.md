# Polyvagal Theory

## Abstract

Polyvagal Theory, developed by Stephen Porges, proposes that the autonomic nervous system operates as a three-tiered phylogenetic hierarchy governing safety, danger, and life-threat responses. The ventral vagal complex (myelinated vagus) supports social engagement and calm physiological states. The sympathetic nervous system mediates mobilization for fight-or-flight. The dorsal vagal complex (unmyelinated vagus) mediates immobilization, shutdown, and dissociation in response to life threat. These three circuits activate in a hierarchical sequence from newest to oldest - social engagement first, sympathetic mobilization second, dorsal shutdown last. The concept of neuroception describes the nervous system continuously scanning the environment for cues of safety or danger below the level of conscious awareness, determining which circuit is engaged at any moment.

## The Three-Circuit Hierarchy

### Ventral Vagal Complex - Safe and Social
- Phylogeny: newest circuit - uniquely mammalian myelinated vagus
- Anatomy: nucleus ambiguus, myelinated vagal fibers to heart and bronchi
- Function: social engagement, calm alertness, prosocial behavior, co-regulation
- Physiological state: HRV high, heart rate moderate, face muscles engaged, voice melodic
- Trigger: cues of safety - familiar faces, prosodic voice, safe environment
- Vagal brake: ventral vagal inhibition of heart rate allows rapid mobilization when needed

### Sympathetic Nervous System - Mobilization
- Phylogeny: second circuit - shared with reptiles and early mammals
- Anatomy: thoracolumbar spinal cord, adrenal medulla, sympathetic chain ganglia
- Function: fight-or-flight mobilization, increased metabolic output
- Physiological state: HRV low, heart rate elevated, muscles primed, digestion suppressed
- Trigger: neuroception of danger - threat cues activate sympathetic without cortical approval
- Adaptive: appropriate for genuine threat - maladaptive when chronically activated

### Dorsal Vagal Complex - Shutdown
- Phylogeny: oldest circuit - shared with reptiles and all vertebrates
- Anatomy: dorsal motor nucleus of vagus, unmyelinated vagal fibers
- Function: immobilization, shutdown, dissociation, metabolic conservation
- Physiological state: heart rate drops, blood pressure falls, dissociation, freeze
- Trigger: neuroception of life threat when fight-flight has failed
- In trauma: chronic dorsal activation manifests as depression, dissociation, chronic fatigue

## Neuroception

- Definition: subconscious detection of safety, danger, or life threat by the nervous system
- Operates below conscious awareness - body responds before mind registers
- Cues of safety: prosodic voice, soft eyes, relaxed face, familiar smell, rhythmic movement
- Cues of danger: loud unpredictable sounds, flat affect, averted gaze, irregular rhythm
- Faulty neuroception: trauma history causes nervous system to detect danger in safe contexts
- TCM parallel: Wei Qi defensive surface scanning as somatic neuroception analog

## Social Engagement System

- Anatomy: ventral vagal complex plus cranial nerves V, VII, IX, X, XI
- Components: facial muscles (CN VII), middle ear (CN V), larynx/pharynx (CN IX-X), head turning (CN XI)
- Middle ear: ventral vagal tone tunes stapedius muscle to filter human voice frequencies
- Prosody: melodic voice activates social engagement system in listener
- Co-regulation: nervous systems of two people in safe contact mutually regulate each other
- Therapeutic: safe therapeutic relationship is primary vehicle of ANS re-regulation

## HRV as Vagal Tone Biomarker

- High HRV: ventral vagal dominance - flexible, responsive, regulated
- Low HRV: sympathetic dominance or dorsal shutdown - rigid or collapsed
- RSA (respiratory sinus arrhythmia): HRV linked to breathing cycle - primary vagal tone measure
- Trauma reduces HRV: chronic low HRV predicts poor stress resilience and health outcomes
