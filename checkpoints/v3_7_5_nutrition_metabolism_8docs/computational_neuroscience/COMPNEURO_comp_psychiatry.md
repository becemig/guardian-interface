# Computational Psychiatry

## Abstract

Computational psychiatry — the application of computational and mathematical models to understand the mechanisms underlying psychiatric disorders and to develop more precise diagnostic and treatment approaches, with the predictive coding and reinforcement learning frameworks providing mechanistic accounts of depression as stuck prior beliefs resistant to updating by prediction errors, anxiety as miscalibrated precision weighting overestimating the reliability of threat signals, schizophrenia as aberrant precision weighting of prediction errors producing excessive attribution of salience to irrelevant stimuli, and addiction as hijacking of the temporal difference learning system by substances that produce supraphysiological dopamine prediction error signals. Depression as stuck inference — the computational account of depression as a failure of belief updating in which excessively strong prior beliefs about the world and the self resist updating by positive prediction errors, producing the characteristic cognitive features of negative bias, hopelessness, and anhedonia as consequences of a generative model that is too precise and too pessimistic to be corrected by positive experiences, with antidepressants and psychotherapy understood as interventions that reduce the precision of stuck priors and increase the rate of belief updating from positive experience. Anxiety as precision dysregulation — the computational account of anxiety disorders as miscalibrated interoceptive and exteroceptive precision weighting in which the brain over-estimates the reliability of threat-related signals and under-estimates the precision of safety signals, producing excessive prediction error responses to ambiguous stimuli and the characteristic hypervigilance, avoidance, and somatic symptoms of anxiety through the downstream consequences of miscalibrated precision on both perception and action. Schizophrenia as aberrant prediction error — the aberrant salience hypothesis proposed by Shitij Kapur and formalized computationally as excessive precision weighting of prediction errors in the dopaminergic system, causing irrelevant stimuli to acquire excessive motivational salience and be misinterpreted as meaningful, producing the positive symptoms of psychosis including hallucinations as perceptions without appropriate sensory prediction errors and delusions as fixed false beliefs maintained by aberrant precision of predictive inference. The Shen disorder computational pattern correspondence in TCM describes the classical framework of Shen disturbance producing the psychiatric manifestations of disturbed cognition, emotion, and consciousness — a correspondence with the computational psychiatry understanding that psychiatric disorders reflect specific patterns of dysregulated inference, precision weighting, and prediction error processing in the neural systems governing cognition and affect.

## Depression Computational Model

- Stuck priors: overly precise pessimistic beliefs resist updating by positive experience.
- Anhedonia: reduced precision of reward prediction errors — blunted learning from rewards.
- Rumination: high-precision negative self-model iterating without updating.
- Treatment: antidepressants reduce prior precision psychotherapy introduces disconfirming evidence.

## Anxiety Computational Model

- Interoceptive precision: over-weighted visceral signals — somatic symptoms amplified.
- Threat precision: over-weighted ambiguous threat signals — hypervigilance.
- Safety precision: under-weighted safety signals — extinction impaired.
- Avoidance: active inference to confirm threat predictions — reduces updating.

## Schizophrenia Aberrant Salience

- Dopamine: excessive phasic release — aberrant precision of prediction errors.
- Irrelevant salience: neutral stimuli acquire excessive motivational significance.
- Hallucinations: perceptions generated by top-down predictions without adequate sensory error correction.
- Delusions: fixed false beliefs maintained by aberrant precision weighting.

## TCM Correspondence

- Shen disturbance producing cognitive and affective disorder corresponds to dysregulated predictive inference in psychiatric conditions.
- Heart Fire disturbing Shen corresponds to excessive precision weighting of threat and arousal signals in anxiety.
- Phlegm misting Heart orifices corresponds to aberrant salience and signal-noise disruption in psychosis.
- Kidney failing to anchor Shen corresponds to loss of stable deep priors grounding higher-level inference in depression.
