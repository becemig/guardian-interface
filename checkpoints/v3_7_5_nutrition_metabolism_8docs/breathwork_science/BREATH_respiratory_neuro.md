# Respiratory Neurophysiology

## Abstract

Respiratory neurophysiology — the study of the neural circuits that generate, regulate, and modulate the breathing rhythm — reveals that respiration is governed by one of the most sophisticated and evolutionarily ancient neural control systems in the vertebrate brain, a distributed network of brainstem nuclei that generates the breathing rhythm, integrates afferent feedback from the lungs and chemoreceptors, and broadcasts respiratory phase information throughout the brain to coordinate breathing with cardiovascular, vocal, emotional, and cognitive functions. The pre-Botzinger complex — identified by Jack Smith and colleagues in 1991 in the ventrolateral medulla — is the primary respiratory rhythm generator: a compact cluster of interneurons whose intrinsic membrane properties and mutual synaptic connections produce self-sustaining rhythmic bursting that drives the phrenic nerve and diaphragm contraction. The nucleus tractus solitarius NTS — the primary relay station for vagal afferent information — receives mechanosensory feedback from pulmonary stretch receptors activated by lung inflation and integrates this feedback with chemoreceptor input from the carotid and aortic bodies to regulate the timing and depth of each breath. The Hering-Breuer reflex — the inhibition of inhalation by pulmonary stretch receptor activation during lung inflation, transmitted through the vagus nerve to the NTS and respiratory pattern generator — is the primary feedback mechanism that terminates inhalation and initiates the transition to exhalation, setting the tidal volume and respiratory rate through the interaction of central rhythm generation and peripheral feedback. Slow deep breathing influences the prefrontal cortex through multiple ascending pathways: the pre-Botzinger complex projects to the locus coeruleus regulating norepinephrine and arousal, the NTS projects to the parabrachial nucleus and limbic system regulating emotional tone, and the slow respiratory rhythm itself entrains prefrontal oscillations through vagal afferent feedback, producing the calm alert prefrontal-dominant cognitive state associated with slow deep breathing practices.

## Pre-Botzinger Complex

- Primary respiratory rhythm generator in the ventrolateral medulla.
- Intrinsic membrane properties and mutual synaptic connections produce self-sustaining rhythmic bursting.
- Drives phrenic nerve and diaphragm contraction setting the baseline respiratory rhythm.
- Projects to locus coeruleus directly linking breathing rate to whole-brain arousal state.

## Vagal Afferent Integration

- Pulmonary stretch receptors activated by lung inflation send feedback through the vagus nerve.
- NTS integrates vagal stretch receptor feedback with chemoreceptor input to regulate breath timing.
- Hering-Breuer reflex terminates inhalation through stretch receptor activation — sets tidal volume.
- Slow deep breathing maximizes vagal afferent feedback producing maximum parasympathetic tone.

## Breath-Prefrontal Coupling

- Slow breathing entrains prefrontal cortical oscillations through vagal and ascending pathway feedback.
- Prefrontal entrainment produces calm alert executive function — the cultivated breathing state.
- Fast anxious breathing produces subcortical dominant state — limbic over prefrontal.
- Voluntary slow breathing shifts neural hierarchy from subcortical to prefrontal dominance.
