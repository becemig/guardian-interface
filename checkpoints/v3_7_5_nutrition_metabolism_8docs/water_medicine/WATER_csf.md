# Cerebrospinal Fluid Hydraulics and the Glymphatic System

## Abstract

Cerebrospinal fluid hydraulics and the glymphatic system — the integrated fluid system governing the production, circulation, and absorption of CSF and the AQP4-dependent bulk flow of interstitial fluid through the brain parenchyma that clears metabolic waste products including amyloid-beta and tau during sleep — represents one of the most important recent discoveries in neuroscience, providing the mechanistic basis for the long-observed connection between sleep deprivation and neurodegenerative disease and corresponding to the TCM understanding of the brain as the Sea of Marrow governed by Kidney Jing whose nightly replenishment during deep sleep is essential for cognitive vitality. Cerebrospinal fluid production — the approximately 500 ml of CSF produced daily by the choroid plexus epithelium of the lateral third and fourth ventricles through active secretion of sodium chloride and bicarbonate driving osmotic water flow across AQP1 aquaporin channels — circulates from the ventricles through the subarachnoid space surrounding the brain and spinal cord and is absorbed at the arachnoid granulations into the venous sinuses, maintaining an intracranial pressure of approximately 10-15 mmHg and providing mechanical protection, buoyancy, and the chemical microenvironment for the brain. The glymphatic system — the brain-wide network of perivascular spaces surrounding penetrating arteries and veins through which CSF and interstitial fluid flow in a convective bulk flow pattern driven by arterial pulsatility and facilitated by AQP4 water channels in astrocyte end-feet — was discovered by Maiken Nedergaard and colleagues in 2012 and fundamentally changed the understanding of how the brain clears its metabolic waste products, with the glymphatic system providing the primary clearance mechanism for amyloid-beta, tau, alpha-synuclein, and other neurotoxic proteins that accumulate in neurodegenerative diseases. Sleep-dependent glymphatic clearance — the dramatic increase in interstitial space volume by approximately 60 percent during sleep, corresponding to enhanced glymphatic bulk flow and accelerated clearance of metabolic waste products including amyloid-beta — provides the mechanistic basis for the sleep deprivation-amyloid accumulation relationship observed in human studies, with even one night of sleep deprivation producing measurable increases in amyloid-beta in the human brain detectable by PET imaging. CSF as brain mirror — the biochemical composition of CSF reflecting the metabolic state of the brain with a delay of hours to days, used clinically in diagnostic lumbar puncture to measure amyloid-beta tau neurofilament light and other biomarkers of neurodegeneration — provides a window into brain health that corresponds to the TCM pulse diagnosis reading the state of deep constitutional systems through peripheral accessible signals. The Brain Marrow Sea Kidney Jing correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding of the brain as the Sea of Marrow whose substance is generated by Kidney Jing and replenished during deep sleep, corresponding to the glymphatic replenishment and waste clearance of the brain that occurs during slow wave sleep.

## CSF Production and Circulation

- Choroid plexus: secretes approximately 500 ml CSF per day through active transport.
- AQP1 in choroid plexus: facilitates water movement driven by ionic secretion.
- Circulation: lateral ventricles third ventricle cerebral aqueduct fourth ventricle subarachnoid space.
- Absorption: arachnoid granulations into venous sinuses and via cribriform plate to nasal lymphatics.

## Glymphatic System

- Periarterial CSF inflow: CSF enters brain along penetrating arteries driven by pulsatility.
- AQP4 facilitation: astrocyte end-feet AQP4 enables rapid water movement across parenchyma.
- Perivenous outflow: ISF exits along perivenous spaces carrying amyloid-beta tau and metabolites.
- Sleep enhancement: 60 percent increase in interstitial space volume during sleep.

## Sleep-Dependent Clearance

- Amyloid-beta clearance 2x faster during sleep than waking in mice.
- One night sleep deprivation: measurable amyloid-beta increase in human brain by PET.
- Slow wave sleep: greatest glymphatic activity during N3 deep sleep.
- Sleep position: lateral position enhances glymphatic flow compared to supine or prone.
