# Polyvagal Theory and Social Engagement

## Abstract

Polyvagal theory developed by Stephen Porges proposes a hierarchical model of autonomic nervous system organization based on the evolutionary development of the vagus nerve. Three neural circuits provide adaptive responses to safety and threat in a phylogenetically determined hierarchy: the ventral vagal system enabling social engagement, the sympathetic system enabling fight-flight mobilization, and the dorsal vagal system enabling immobilization and shutdown. Neuroception is the below-conscious process by which the nervous system detects safety and threat through facial cues, voice prosody, posture, and environmental signals — distinct from conscious perception. The ventral vagal complex innervates the face, larynx, pharynx, and heart enabling the coordinated social engagement of facial expression, prosody, listening, and heart rate variability. Social engagement produces co-regulation — the mutual regulation of nervous system states through attuned relational contact. Heart Shen safety — the Heart-Mind's sense of fundamental relational safety — corresponds to the ventral vagal social engagement state as its TCM equivalent.

## Three Circuit Hierarchy

- The ventral vagal circuit is the newest evolutionary development enabling mammalian social engagement.
- The sympathetic circuit is intermediate enabling fight-flight mobilization against active threats.
- The dorsal vagal circuit is the oldest enabling immobilization shutdown in life-threatening situations.
- These circuits activate in reverse phylogenetic order — ventral first, sympathetic second, dorsal last.

## Neuroception

- Neuroception detects safety and threat below the threshold of conscious awareness.
- It operates through detection of facial cues, voice prosody, posture, movement, and environmental sounds.
- Safe neuroception activates the ventral vagal social engagement system enabling connection and regulation.
- Threat neuroception activates sympathetic or dorsal vagal responses suppressing social engagement.

## Social Engagement System

- The ventral vagal complex coordinates facial expression, prosody, listening, and cardiac vagal tone.
- This integrated social engagement system evolved to support mammalian caregiving and co-regulation.
- High HRV reflects strong ventral vagal tone and the capacity for flexible social engagement.
- Social engagement produces co-regulation — mutual nervous system regulation through attuned contact.
