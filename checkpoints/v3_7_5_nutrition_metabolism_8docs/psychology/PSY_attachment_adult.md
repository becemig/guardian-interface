# Adult Attachment

## Abstract

Adult attachment theory extends John Bowlby's developmental attachment framework to adult romantic and social relationships demonstrating that the internal working models of self and other established in early attachment relationships continue to shape adult relational patterns, emotion regulation, and health outcomes. Cindy Hazan and Philip Shaver's landmark 1987 study demonstrated that the three infant attachment patterns — secure, anxious-ambivalent, and avoidant — have direct parallels in adult romantic relationship styles. Secure adults are comfortable with intimacy and interdependence, describe relationships positively, and show coherent attachment narratives. Anxious adults show hyperactivation of the attachment system — preoccupied with relationship security, fear abandonment, and show heightened emotional reactivity. Avoidant adults show deactivation of the attachment system — uncomfortable with closeness, value independence defensively, and show physiological arousal masked by behavioral suppression. Mary Main developed the Adult Attachment Interview AAI — a semi-structured interview assessing the adult's current state of mind regarding childhood attachment experiences — finding that coherence of narrative predicts parenting behavior and infant attachment classification. Disorganized attachment — arising from frightening or frightened caregiving — produces the most severe regulatory difficulties and psychopathology risk. Earned security — developing secure attachment functioning through healing relationships despite insecure childhood experiences — demonstrates the plasticity of attachment organization. Heart resonance patterns in TCM — the characteristic ways the Heart Shen opens or guards in intimate relationship reflecting constitutional and developmental patterning — corresponds to adult attachment styles as their psychophysiological substrate.

## Adult Attachment Styles

- Secure attachment involves comfort with intimacy, positive models of self and other, and effective emotion regulation.
- Anxious attachment involves hyperactivation — preoccupation with relationship security and fear of abandonment.
- Avoidant attachment involves deactivation — discomfort with closeness and defensive independence.
- Disorganized attachment involves collapse of organized strategy — approach-avoidance conflict in relationship.

## Internal Working Models

- Internal working models are mental representations of self, other, and relationship expectations formed in early attachment.
- They operate largely implicitly — shaping perception, emotion, and behavior outside conscious awareness.
- Secure models expect responsiveness, support independent exploration, and enable affect regulation through relationship.
- Insecure models expect unavailability or danger, restrict exploration, and impair co-regulatory capacity.

## Adult Attachment Interview

- The AAI assesses current state of mind regarding childhood attachment through narrative coherence.
- Coherent, collaborative, and reasonably brief narratives classify as secure-autonomous regardless of experience quality.
- Earned security involves coherent narrative despite difficult childhood experiences — healing through reflection.
- AAI classification predicts parenting behavior and infant attachment classification with 75-80% accuracy.
