# Developmental Psychology

## Abstract

Developmental psychology maps the systematic changes in cognition, emotion, social capacity, and identity across the lifespan — providing frameworks for understanding how the person we are at any moment reflects the accumulated developmental history of all the stages we have passed through. Jean Piaget's cognitive developmental stage theory proposes four universal stages of cognitive development: the sensorimotor stage from birth to two years where knowledge is constructed through sensory experience and motor action, the preoperational stage from two to seven years where symbolic representation and language develop but thinking remains egocentric and non-logical, the concrete operational stage from seven to eleven years where logical operations become possible with concrete objects, and the formal operational stage from eleven years onward where abstract hypothetical reasoning becomes available. Lev Vygotsky's sociocultural theory emphasizes that cognitive development occurs through social interaction — the zone of proximal development ZPD is the range between what a child can accomplish independently and what they can accomplish with skilled guidance, and learning occurs optimally in this zone through the scaffolding provided by more competent others. Erik Erikson's eight-stage psychosocial development theory maps the central developmental challenge of each life period: trust versus mistrust in infancy, autonomy versus shame in toddlerhood, initiative versus guilt in early childhood, industry versus inferiority in middle childhood, identity versus role confusion in adolescence, intimacy versus isolation in young adulthood, generativity versus stagnation in middle adulthood, and integrity versus despair in late life. TCM seven-year cycles of constitutional development — seven-year cycles for women and eight-year cycles for men governed by Kidney Jing maturation — provide a parallel developmental framework that maps constitutional rather than cognitive development across the lifespan.

## Piaget Stages

- Sensorimotor: birth to 2 years — knowledge through action, object permanence develops by 8 months.
- Preoperational: 2-7 years — symbolic thought, language, egocentrism, magical thinking.
- Concrete operational: 7-11 years — logical operations with concrete objects, conservation.
- Formal operational: 11 years onward — abstract reasoning, hypothetical thinking, systematic problem-solving.

## Vygotsky ZPD

- The ZPD is the productive learning zone between independent capability and guided capability.
- Scaffolding by more competent others enables development through the ZPD.
- The teacher-student relationship in somatic traditions is precisely the ZPD scaffolding relationship.
- This corresponds to the TCM lineage transmission model where the master scaffolds the student's development.
