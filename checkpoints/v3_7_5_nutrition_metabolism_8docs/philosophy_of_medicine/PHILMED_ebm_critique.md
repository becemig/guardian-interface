# Evidence-Based Medicine Critique

## Abstract

Evidence-based medicine critique — the systematic philosophical and empirical examination of the assumptions, limitations, and unintended consequences of the EBM movement that has dominated clinical medicine since the 1990s — reveals that while EBM's core commitment to rigorous evaluation of clinical interventions is valid, its implementation has produced significant distortions including the marginalization of clinical expertise and patient values, the privileging of RCT evidence that systematically excludes complex interventions and individualized medicine, the surrogate endpoint problem, the publication bias and reproducibility crisis that undermines the reliability of the evidence base, and the number needed to treat NNT problem that obscures the modest average effects of most medical interventions. EBM hierarchy of evidence — the formal hierarchy placing systematic reviews and meta-analyses of RCTs at the apex of evidence quality, followed by individual RCTs, cohort studies, case-control studies, case series, and expert opinion — reflects valid statistical reasoning about internal validity but systematically biases toward pharmacological interventions amenable to blinded RCT design and against complex interventions including lifestyle, surgery, psychotherapy, and traditional medicine whose efficacy cannot be evaluated by blinded RCT. RCT limitations in complex interventions — the fundamental methodological problem that complex interventions including multicomponent lifestyle programs, traditional medical systems, surgical techniques, and psychotherapeutic approaches cannot be meaningfully evaluated by the double-blind placebo-controlled RCT because blinding is impossible, the active components are inseparable, individualization is essential, and the intervention itself changes through practitioner learning and patient engagement. John Ioannidis' reproducibility critique — the mathematical demonstration that given typical statistical power levels, prior probabilities of hypothesis truth, researcher degrees of freedom, and publication bias in biomedical research, most published positive findings are false positives, with the reproducibility crisis in psychology, preclinical biomedical research, and nutrition science providing empirical confirmation of this mathematical prediction and fundamentally undermining confidence in the EBM evidence base. The clinical wisdom empirical knowledge correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that the highest level of medical knowledge is the integrated clinical wisdom of the experienced physician who has synthesized thousands of patient encounters into pattern recognition that cannot be reduced to explicit rules — a form of expertise that EBM's operator-independent guideline approach explicitly attempts to replace but that complexity medicine recognizes as irreplaceable for individualized care.

## EBM Hierarchy Critique

- Internal validity vs external validity: RCTs maximize internal validity at cost of external validity.
- Exclusion criteria: typical RCT excludes elderly comorbid polypharmacy patients — most clinical patients.
- Average treatment effects: RCT shows average effect in population — irrelevant for individual.
- Complex intervention blindness: EBM hierarchy systematically disadvantages non-pharmacological treatments.

## Surrogate Endpoint Problem

- Surrogate endpoints: biomarker proxies for clinical outcomes — LDL cholesterol blood pressure.
- Assumption: improving surrogate improves clinical outcome — often invalid.
- Examples: antiarrhythmics reduced arrhythmia but increased mortality.
- Required: trials powered for patient-relevant outcomes mortality morbidity quality of life.

## Reproducibility Crisis

- Ioannidis 2005: most published research findings are false.
- Psychology replication: 36-61 percent of published findings replicate.
- Preclinical biomedicine: 11-25 percent of landmark papers replicate.
- Nutrition science: observational epidemiology particularly unreliable.
