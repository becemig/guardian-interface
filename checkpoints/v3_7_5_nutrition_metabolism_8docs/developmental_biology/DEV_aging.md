# Hallmarks of Aging

## Abstract

The hallmarks of aging framework, first proposed by Lopez-Otin in 2013 and expanded in 2023, identifies nine primary mechanisms that drive the progressive functional decline and increased disease vulnerability that characterize aging. These hallmarks — genomic instability, telomere attrition, epigenetic alterations, loss of proteostasis, deregulated nutrient sensing, mitochondrial dysfunction, cellular senescence, stem cell exhaustion, and altered intercellular communication — are deeply interconnected, with each hallmark amplifying others in a feedforward cascade of deterioration. The framework has catalyzed a new field of geroscience that treats aging itself as a modifiable process rather than an inevitable fate, identifying leverage points for intervention that could compress morbidity and extend healthspan. Inflammaging — the chronic low-grade sterile inflammation that accumulates with age — is increasingly recognized as both a cause and consequence of multiple hallmarks simultaneously. In TCM, aging is understood as the progressive depletion of Kidney Jing through the seven and eight year cycles, with accumulation of pathological products (Phlegm, Blood stasis, Dampness) as secondary consequences.

## Hallmark 1 - Genomic Instability

- Accumulation: DNA damage accumulates faster than repair mechanisms can correct
- Sources: reactive oxygen species, replication errors, environmental mutagens, radiation
- Repair systems: base excision repair, nucleotide excision repair, homologous recombination
- Decline: DNA repair efficiency declines with age - damage accumulates exponentially
- Consequences: mutations, chromosomal aberrations, transposon activation
- Cancer link: accumulated genomic instability is primary driver of age-related cancer

## Hallmark 2 - Telomere Attrition

- Shortening: telomeres lose 50-200 base pairs per cell division
- Crisis: critically short telomeres trigger permanent cell cycle arrest - senescence
- Telomerase: expressed in germ cells and stem cells - maintains telomere length
- Somatic cells: most somatic cells lack telomerase - telomeres shorten with each division
- Stress acceleration: chronic psychological stress and inflammation accelerate shortening
- Exercise: aerobic exercise upregulates telomerase activity - slows shortening

## Hallmark 3 - Epigenetic Alterations

- Drift: epigenetic patterns established in development gradually erode with age
- Methylation: global hypomethylation with local hypermethylation at CpG islands
- Histone marks: loss of repressive H3K27me3 - activation of silenced repetitive elements
- Horvath clock: methylation pattern predicts biological age with high precision
- Reversibility: epigenetic age can be reversed - Yamanaka factors, fasting, exercise
- Chromatin remodeling: loss of chromatin architecture allows transposon activation

## Hallmark 4 - Loss of Proteostasis

- Proteostasis: protein homeostasis - proper folding, trafficking, and degradation
- Chaperones: HSP70, HSP90 - assist protein folding - decline with age
- UPS: ubiquitin-proteasome system - degrades misfolded proteins - impaired in aging
- Autophagy: lysosomal degradation of damaged organelles and aggregates - declines with age
- Aggregates: misfolded proteins accumulate - amyloid, tau, alpha-synuclein
- ER stress: unfolded protein response activated chronically - drives inflammation

## Hallmark 5 - Mitochondrial Dysfunction

- mtDNA mutations: mitochondrial DNA accumulates mutations - no histone protection
- ETC: electron transport chain efficiency declines - less ATP, more ROS
- ROS: reactive oxygen species damage mtDNA, proteins, lipids - positive feedback
- Mitophagy: selective autophagy of damaged mitochondria - declines with age
- Biogenesis: PGC-1 alpha drives new mitochondrial production - exercise is primary stimulant
- NAD+: NAD+ declines with age - impairs sirtuin and PARP activity

## Hallmark 6 - Deregulated Nutrient Sensing

- mTOR: hyperactivated in aging - drives anabolism at expense of autophagy and repair
- Insulin-IGF1: elevated signaling associated with accelerated aging - caloric restriction reduces
- AMPK: energy sensor - declines with age - reduced stimulation of autophagy and mitochondria
- Sirtuins: NAD-dependent deacetylases - longevity regulators - require NAD+ availability
- Caloric restriction: reduces mTOR and IGF1 - extends lifespan in all organisms tested

## Hallmarks 7-9

- Cellular senescence: permanent cell cycle arrest - SASP drives local and systemic inflammation
- Stem cell exhaustion: reduced regenerative capacity - tissue maintenance fails
- Altered intercellular communication: inflammaging, reduced hormonal signals, microbiome dysbiosis
- Inflammaging: sterile chronic low-grade inflammation - driven by senescent cells, mtDNA, LPS
- Interconnection: each hallmark amplifies others - aging as positive feedback cascade
- TCM: Kidney Jing depletion as root - Phlegm and Blood stasis as secondary accumulation
