# Cellular Senescence

## Abstract

Cellular senescence is a state of permanent cell cycle arrest triggered by various forms of stress including telomere shortening, DNA damage, oncogene activation, and oxidative stress. Senescent cells resist apoptosis and persist in tissues, where they secrete a complex mixture of pro-inflammatory cytokines, chemokines, proteases, and growth factors known as the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP). The SASP drives local tissue dysfunction, recruits immune cells, induces paracrine senescence in neighboring cells, and contributes to systemic inflammaging. While senescence is acutely beneficial as a tumor suppressor mechanism and in wound healing, the accumulation of senescent cells with age becomes a major driver of tissue deterioration and age-related disease. Senolytics — drugs that selectively kill senescent cells — and senomorphics — drugs that suppress the SASP without killing senescent cells — represent a rapidly advancing therapeutic frontier. Animal studies show that clearing senescent cells extends healthspan and reduces multiple age-related pathologies simultaneously. In TCM, the SASP maps to the accumulation of Phlegm-Heat and Blood stasis as secondary pathological products of Jing depletion.

## Triggers of Senescence

- Replicative: telomere shortening reaches critical length - Hayflick limit
- DNA damage: double-strand breaks and persistent damage foci - genotoxic stress
- Oncogene activation: RAS, RAF overactivation triggers senescence as tumor suppression
- Oxidative stress: ROS-induced DNA damage and protein oxidation
- Epigenetic: loss of repressive chromatin marks - SAHF formation
- Developmental: transient senescence during embryogenesis - wound healing senescence
- Therapy-induced: chemotherapy and radiation induce senescence in tumor and normal cells

## Molecular Mechanisms

- p53-p21 axis: DNA damage activates p53 - p53 induces p21 - CDK inhibitor - G1 arrest
- p16-RB axis: p16INK4a inhibits CDK4-6 - RB remains hypophosphorylated - E2F repressed
- Both pathways: reinforcing dual brake - makes senescence permanent
- SAHF: senescence-associated heterochromatin foci - condense proliferative gene loci
- Lysosomal enlargement: increased SA-beta-gal activity - classic senescence marker
- cGAS-STING: cytosolic DNA from damaged chromatin activates innate immune signaling
- NF-kB: primary driver of SASP gene expression - activated by cGAS-STING and ATM

## SASP Components

- Cytokines: IL-1 alpha, IL-1 beta, IL-6, IL-8, TNF-alpha - pro-inflammatory
- Chemokines: CXCL1, CXCL2, CCL2 - recruit neutrophils and macrophages
- Proteases: MMP-3, MMP-9 - degrade extracellular matrix - impair tissue architecture
- Growth factors: amphiregulin, VEGF - can paradoxically promote tumor growth
- Paracrine senescence: SASP factors induce senescence in neighboring cells - spread
- Systemic: SASP factors enter circulation - contribute to systemic inflammaging
- Immune evasion: some SASP components prevent NK cell clearance of senescent cells

## Beneficial Functions of Senescence

- Tumor suppression: oncogene-induced senescence prevents malignant transformation
- Wound healing: transient senescent cells at wound edge promote tissue remodeling
- Embryonic: senescent cells guide morphogenesis and are cleared by immune system
- Liver fibrosis resolution: hepatic stellate cell senescence limits fibrosis
- Paradox: the same mechanism that suppresses cancer in youth drives aging in old age
- Timing: transient senescence cleared by immune system - chronic persistence is pathological

## Senolytics and Senomorphics

- Senolytics: drugs that selectively kill senescent cells - exploit senescent survival pathways
- Dasatinib plus quercetin: first senolytic combination - clears senescent cells in fat and lung
- Navitoclax (ABT-263): BCL-2 inhibitor - kills senescent cells - also reduces platelets
- Fisetin: flavonoid - senolytic properties - extends median lifespan in old mice
- Senomorphics: rapamycin, JAK inhibitors - suppress SASP without killing senescent cells
- Intermittent dosing: senolytics effective with intermittent dosing - cells do not rapidly return
- Human trials: multiple clinical trials ongoing - frailty, IPF, diabetic kidney disease
