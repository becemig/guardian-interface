# Mantric Traditions and Neuroscience

## Abstract

Mantric traditions and neuroscience — the scientific investigation of the physiological and neurological effects of repetitive sacred sound recitation including Sanskrit mantras, Christian prayer, Buddhist chanting, and Islamic dhikr — revealing measurable effects on autonomic function, neural synchrony, immune response, and psychological wellbeing through the combined mechanisms of slow rhythmic breathing imposed by chanting, vagal stimulation from laryngeal vibration and resonance, attentional focus reducing default mode network activity, and the psychologically meaningful content activating reward and social bonding systems. Sanskrit mantra acoustic properties — the linguistic and acoustic analysis of Sanskrit mantras including the primordial sound Om revealing their construction around specific phoneme sequences producing particular resonance patterns in the vocal tract and cranial cavities, with the elongated M of Om producing sustained nasopharyngeal vibration that may mechanically stimulate the vagus nerve through its auricular branch, and with traditional Sanskrit grammar encoding specific intentions of sound-meaning correspondence. Repetitive chanting neuroscience — the EEG studies demonstrating that repetitive mantra chanting produces increased alpha and theta power reflecting internalized attention and relaxed awareness, reduced default mode network activity and mind wandering, and with prolonged practice producing the gamma synchrony associated with focused contemplative states, and with the attentional demands of maintaining correct pronunciation and rhythm providing a concentration anchor preventing habitual thought patterns. Om and vagal stimulation — the neuroimaging study by Kalyani demonstrating that Om chanting produces activation of limbic structures and deactivation of amygdala, anterior cingulate cortex, and prefrontal cortex similar to vagal nerve stimulation, suggesting that the vibration produced during Om chanting stimulates the vagus nerve through its auricular branch in the ear canal, providing a non-invasive form of vagal stimulation with measurable effects on limbic processing. The Qi Transmission Through Sacred Sound correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that sound and vibration carry Qi and that the Qi of the healer or teacher can be transmitted through sound, with healing sounds used therapeutically in the Six Healing Sounds practice of Daoist Qigong — a tradition corresponding to the modern neuroscience demonstrating that sacred sound practices produce measurable physiological and neurological changes through both acoustic mechanical and attentional mechanisms.
