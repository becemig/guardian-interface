# Movement Disorders Neuroscience

## Abstract

Movement disorders neuroscience — the investigation of the neural circuit mechanisms underlying conditions that impair the initiation, execution, and coordination of voluntary movement including Parkinson's disease, cerebellar ataxia, dystonia, and Huntington's disease — reveals the specific roles of the basal ganglia in movement selection and initiation, the cerebellum in movement coordination and error correction, and the corticospinal system in movement execution, with the characteristic movement abnormalities of each disorder reflecting the specific computational function lost when each node in the motor circuit is damaged. Parkinson's disease dopamine circuits — the loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta that project to the striatum producing the cardinal motor features of bradykinesia, rigidity, and resting tremor through the disruption of the direct and indirect basal ganglia pathways, with dopamine depletion causing excessive inhibition of the thalamus by the internal globus pallidus that reduces thalamocortical drive and impairs movement initiation, with the alpha-synuclein aggregation pathology of Lewy bodies spreading through the nervous system in a prion-like manner following Braak staging. Basal ganglia motor loops — the cortico-striato-thalamo-cortical loops through which the basal ganglia select and facilitate desired movements while suppressing competing motor programs, with the direct pathway through D1 receptor-expressing striatal neurons disinhibiting the thalamus to facilitate movement, and the indirect pathway through D2 receptor-expressing striatal neurons inhibiting the thalamus to suppress competing movements, with the balance between direct and indirect pathways determining the fluency and selectivity of voluntary movement. Cerebellar ataxia mechanisms — the loss of Purkinje cell-mediated error correction in the cerebellum producing the characteristic features of ataxia including dysmetria, intention tremor, dysdiadochokinesia, and gait ataxia through the loss of the forward model and error-correction functions that normally ensure smooth accurate coordinated movement. The Wind Tremor Liver Yang Rising correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding of movement disorders including tremor, rigidity, and incoordination as manifestations of internal Wind arising from Liver Yang rising or Liver Blood deficiency failing to nourish the sinews — a clinical framework that corresponds to the modern understanding of basal ganglia and cerebellar circuit dysfunction producing movement disorders.

## Parkinson Circuits

- Substantia nigra: dopaminergic neurons project to striatum — movement initiation and selection.
- Direct pathway: D1 MSNs excite GPi — disinhibit thalamus — facilitate movement.
- Indirect pathway: D2 MSNs inhibit GPe — disinhibit STN — inhibit thalamus — suppress movement.
- Dopamine loss: indirect pathway overactive — excessive thalamic inhibition — bradykinesia.

## Basal Ganglia Function

- Movement selection: focus neural activity on desired movement suppress alternatives.
- Action sequencing: link movement elements into coordinated sequences.
- Habit formation: basal ganglia mediate stimulus-response habit learning.
- DBS: subthalamic nucleus stimulation — disrupts pathological oscillations — improves motor function.

## Cerebellar Error Correction

- Purkinje cells: integrate mossy fiber input and climbing fiber error signals.
- Climbing fibers: inferior olive — carry movement error signals for synaptic plasticity.
- Output: via deep cerebellar nuclei to thalamus motor cortex — correct ongoing movement.
- Ataxia: loss of error correction — dysmetric movements overshoot undershoot targets.
