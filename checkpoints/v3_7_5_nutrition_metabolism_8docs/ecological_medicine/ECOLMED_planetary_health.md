# Planetary Health and Human Health

## Abstract

Planetary health and human health — the Rockefeller Foundation-Lancet Commission framework establishing that human health is fundamentally dependent on the health of the natural systems of the Earth including stable climate, functioning biodiversity, clean air and water, productive soils, and intact ocean systems, with the accelerating degradation of these planetary systems through climate change, biodiversity loss, land use change, nitrogen and phosphorus pollution, and freshwater depletion generating a growing burden of human disease through the disruption of the ecosystem services on which human health ultimately depends. Ecosystem services and human wellbeing — the four categories of ecosystem services including provisioning services providing food water and medicines, regulating services providing climate regulation, flood control, water purification, and disease regulation, cultural services providing recreation, spiritual connection, and mental health benefits from nature contact, and supporting services providing nutrient cycling, soil formation, and primary production that underlie all other services, with the economic value of global ecosystem services estimated at 125 trillion dollars annually and the health costs of their degradation already measurable in increased infectious disease burden, respiratory illness, malnutrition, and mental health deterioration. Climate change health impacts — the WHO projection that climate change will cause 250,000 additional deaths annually between 2030 and 2050 through heat stress, malaria, diarrheal disease, and undernutrition, with the health impacts distributed inequitably affecting the most vulnerable populations in low-income countries who have contributed least to greenhouse gas emissions, and with additional impacts through extreme weather events, air quality deterioration from ozone and wildfire smoke, and the mental health consequences of ecological grief and climate anxiety. Biodiversity loss and disease emergence — the evidence that biodiversity loss increases the risk of zoonotic disease spillover through the dilution effect in which diverse host communities reduce pathogen transmission by diluting infectious contacts among competent reservoir hosts, with deforestation and habitat fragmentation increasing human contact with wildlife reservoir species and with the COVID-19 pandemic and prior zoonotic emergences including Ebola, SARS, MERS, and Nipah all linked to disruption of wildlife habitat. The Heaven Earth Human Triad ecological correspondence in Daoist cosmology describes the classical understanding that the human being exists within and depends upon the integrity of the natural order — that human health is inseparable from the health of the Heaven and Earth that surround and sustain it — a cosmological framework corresponding to the planetary health science demonstrating that human health is fundamentally dependent on planetary ecological integrity.

## Ecosystem Services

- Provisioning: food water timber fish medicines — direct material benefits.
- Regulating: climate flood disease air quality water purification — indirect benefits.
- Cultural: recreation spiritual aesthetic mental health — non-material benefits.
- Supporting: nutrient cycling soil formation primary production — foundational services.

## Climate Health Impacts

- Heat: excess mortality in vulnerable populations — cardiovascular respiratory.
- Vector-borne: malaria dengue range expansion with warming.
- Nutrition: crop yield reduction — undernutrition in vulnerable regions.
- Mental health: ecological grief climate anxiety eco-anxiety emerging burden.

## Biodiversity and Disease

- Dilution effect: diverse communities reduce pathogen transmission to humans.
- Zoonotic spillover: habitat destruction increases wildlife human contact.
- Reservoir hosts: rodents and bats amplified when diversity lost.
- COVID lesson: pandemic preparedness requires biodiversity conservation.
