# Acetylcholine System

## Abstract

Acetylcholine ACh is the primary neurotransmitter of the cholinergic system serving both the peripheral and central nervous systems in distinct roles. In the periphery, ACh is the neuromuscular junction transmitter and the primary parasympathetic postganglionic transmitter. In the central nervous system, the basal forebrain cholinergic system — comprising the nucleus basalis of Meynert, the medial septal nucleus, and the diagonal band of Broca — provides the primary cholinergic innervation of the entire neocortex and hippocampus. Marcel Mesulam's research identified basal forebrain cholinergic neurons as the primary substrate of cortical attention and cognitive arousal — their degeneration in Alzheimer's disease produces the characteristic cognitive decline of that condition. Michael Hasselmo proposed that ACh modulates cortical signal-to-noise ratio — increasing ACh during attention enhances the response to external inputs while suppressing recurrent cortical activity, shifting the balance from memory retrieval to new learning. ACh is critical for REM sleep generation and the encoding of new memories — cholinergic blockade profoundly impairs both. Muscarinic M1 receptors in the cortex mediate cognitive enhancement while nicotinic receptors in the VTA and striatum mediate the reinforcing effects of nicotine. Shen clarity in TCM — the luminous unobstructed awareness of the Heart-Mind — corresponds to the cholinergic modulation of cortical signal-to-noise ratio enabling clear, present-focused perception.

## Basal Forebrain System

- The nucleus basalis of Meynert provides the primary cholinergic innervation of the neocortex.
- The medial septum provides cholinergic innervation of the hippocampus driving the theta rhythm for memory encoding.
- Basal forebrain neurons are among the first to degenerate in Alzheimer's disease preceding cortical atrophy.
- Acetylcholinesterase inhibitors used in Alzheimer's treatment partially restore cortical cholinergic tone.

## Attention and Signal-to-Noise

- ACh release during attention increases the response of cortical neurons to external sensory inputs.
- Simultaneously ACh suppresses recurrent intracortical connections reducing noise from internal processing.
- This dual action sharpens the cortical representation of attended stimuli improving perceptual clarity.
- Low ACh tone shifts the balance toward recurrent activity and memory retrieval over new sensory encoding.

## Memory and REM Sleep

- Hippocampal ACh from the medial septum drives the theta rhythm essential for synaptic plasticity and encoding.
- ACh levels are highest during waking attention and REM sleep and lowest during NREM slow wave sleep.
- During NREM the low ACh enables hippocampal-neocortical memory consolidation through sharp wave ripple replay.
- Cholinergic blockade with scopolamine produces a reversible amnesia for new information.
