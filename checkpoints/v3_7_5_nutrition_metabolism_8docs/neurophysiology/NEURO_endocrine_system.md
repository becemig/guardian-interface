# Endocrine System: Hormonal Regulation, HPA Axis Anatomy, and Jing-Hormone Correspondence

## Abstract

The endocrine system is a distributed chemical signaling network governing metabolism, growth, reproduction, stress response, and homeostatic regulation through hormones secreted into the bloodstream. The hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis is the central stress response system coordinating cortisol release in response to psychological and physiological threat. The hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis governs reproductive hormone cycles in both sexes corresponding closely to TCM Kidney Jing and Tian Gui essence. Internal movement arts consistently normalize HPA axis function, support thyroid regulation, and preserve sex hormone levels through their integrated neuroendocrine effects.

## Major Endocrine Axes

### HPA Axis

- The hypothalamus releases corticotropin-releasing hormone CRH in response to stress signals.
- CRH stimulates the anterior pituitary to release adrenocorticotropic hormone ACTH.
- ACTH stimulates the adrenal cortex to synthesize and release cortisol.
- Cortisol feeds back to the hypothalamus and pituitary inhibiting further CRH and ACTH release.
- Chronic stress disrupts this negative feedback producing sustained cortisol elevation and allostatic overload.

### HPG Axis

- The hypothalamus releases gonadotropin-releasing hormone GnRH in pulsatile fashion.
- GnRH stimulates the anterior pituitary to release LH and FSH governing gonadal function.
- LH stimulates testosterone production in Leydig cells of the testes and ovulation and progesterone in the ovary.
- FSH governs follicle development in the ovary and spermatogenesis in the testes.
- Chronic stress suppresses GnRH pulsatility reducing sex hormone production corresponding to Kidney Jing depletion.

### HPT Axis

- The hypothalamus releases thyrotropin-releasing hormone TRH governing thyroid function.
- TRH stimulates pituitary TSH release which drives thyroid T3 and T4 production.
- Thyroid hormones govern metabolic rate, temperature regulation, cardiac function, and neurological development.
- Chronic stress and cortisol excess suppress thyroid function producing fatigue and metabolic slowing.

## Adrenal Hormones

### Cortisol

- Cortisol is the primary glucocorticoid governing stress response, glucose metabolism, and immune suppression.
- Acute cortisol release mobilizes energy, reduces inflammation, and enhances memory consolidation of threatening events.
- Chronic cortisol elevation produces muscle catabolism, bone resorption, immune suppression, and hippocampal atrophy.
- Cortisol follows a circadian rhythm peaking at morning awakening and declining through the day corresponding to TCM organ clock Yang cycle.

### DHEA

- Dehydroepiandrosterone DHEA is an adrenal androgen precursor with anti-aging and neuroprotective properties.
- DHEA counterbalances cortisol effects and its ratio to cortisol is a key index of adrenal resilience.
- DHEA declines progressively with age corresponding to Kidney Jing depletion and reduced constitutional vitality.
- Qigong practice has been shown to increase DHEA and reduce cortisol improving the DHEA to cortisol ratio.

### Epinephrine and Norepinephrine

- The adrenal medulla releases epinephrine and norepinephrine as part of the acute sympathetic stress response.
- These catecholamines produce rapid cardiovascular activation, bronchodilation, and metabolic mobilization.
- Chronic sympathetic activation depletes adrenal medullary reserve contributing to fatigue and reduced stress resilience.

## Sex Hormones

### Testosterone

- Testosterone governs muscle protein synthesis, bone density, libido, and assertive behavior in both sexes.
- Testosterone corresponds to Kidney Yang Jing the warming activating constitutional essence.
- Chronic stress, poor sleep, and sedentary lifestyle suppress testosterone production.
- Resistance exercise and internal arts practice support healthy testosterone levels through multiple mechanisms.

### Estrogen

- Estrogens govern female reproductive cycles, bone density, cardiovascular protection, and cognitive function.
- Estrogen corresponds to Kidney Yin Jing the nourishing cooling constitutional essence.
- Estrogen decline at menopause produces hot flashes, bone loss, and cardiovascular risk corresponding to Kidney Yin deficiency.
- Tai Chi and Qigong produce estrogen-like effects in postmenopausal women supporting bone density and quality of life.
