# Cerebral Blood Flow and Neurovascular Coupling

## Abstract

The brain receives approximately 15 percent of cardiac output despite comprising only 2 percent of body weight reflecting its exceptional metabolic demand. Cerebral blood flow CBF is maintained by cerebral autoregulation — the intrinsic capacity of cerebral vasculature to maintain constant perfusion despite changes in systemic blood pressure. Neurovascular coupling describes the tight spatial and temporal linkage between neural activity and local cerebral blood flow — active brain regions receive increased blood flow within seconds through a process mediated by astrocytes and nitric oxide. This neurovascular coupling is the physiological basis of the BOLD signal measured by fMRI. CO2 reactivity — the vasodilation produced by elevated CO2 — provides a powerful regulatory mechanism linking respiratory physiology to cerebral perfusion. Heart Qi governing the circulation of Blood throughout the body and the Governing Vessel governing the brain and spinal cord together correspond to the cardiac and neural regulation of cerebral blood flow.

## Cerebral Autoregulation

- Cerebral autoregulation maintains constant CBF over a mean arterial pressure range of approximately 60 to 150 mmHg.
- Myogenic response of vascular smooth muscle, metabolic autoregulation, and neural control all contribute.
- Autoregulation failure produces pressure-passive CBF with risk of ischemia or hemorrhage at extremes.
- Chronic hypertension shifts the autoregulatory curve rightward requiring higher pressure for adequate perfusion.

## Neurovascular Coupling

- Neural activity produces local vasodilation within 1 to 2 seconds through astrocyte-mediated signaling.
- Astrocyte calcium waves triggered by synaptic glutamate release vasoactive substances including prostaglandins.
- Nitric oxide from nNOS and eNOS contributes to activity-dependent vasodilation.
- This coupling enables the metabolic demands of active neurons to be precisely met by increased delivery.

## BOLD Signal

- Blood oxygen level dependent BOLD signal reflects the ratio of oxyhemoglobin to deoxyhemoglobin.
- Neural activity increases CBF more than oxygen consumption producing a local increase in oxyhemoglobin.
- This hemodynamic response peaks at 5 to 6 seconds after neural activity onset with a temporal resolution limitation.
- BOLD fMRI maps neural activity through its vascular correlate rather than measuring electricity directly.
