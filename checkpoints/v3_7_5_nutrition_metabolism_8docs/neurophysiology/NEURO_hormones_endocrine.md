# Hormones and Endocrine Signaling

## Abstract

The endocrine system coordinates physiological function through hormonal signaling molecules released into the bloodstream to act on distant target organs. The hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis governs the stress response through cortisol while the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis governs reproductive function through sex hormones. Thyroid hormones regulate metabolic rate and thermogenesis. Chronic stress produces allostatic load through sustained cortisol elevation driving inflammation, immune suppression, and metabolic dysfunction. Kidney Jing in TCM provides the constitutional substrate for all endocrine function with Kidney Yang corresponding to adrenal and thyroid function and Kidney Yin corresponding to anabolic and restorative hormonal activity.

## HPA Axis and Cortisol

- The hypothalamus releases CRH activating the pituitary to release ACTH stimulating adrenal cortisol production.
- Cortisol mobilizes glucose, suppresses inflammation acutely, and prepares the body for fight-or-flight demands.
- Chronic cortisol elevation produces hippocampal atrophy, immune suppression, and metabolic syndrome.
- Diurnal cortisol rhythm peaks at awakening and declines through the day — disruption indicates HPA dysregulation.
- Tai Chi and Qigong reduce morning cortisol and flatten the diurnal curve in chronically stressed populations.

## Sex Hormones

- Testosterone governs anabolic function, libido, bone density, and assertive motivation in both sexes.
- Estrogen governs reproductive cycling, bone protection, cardiovascular health, and mood regulation.
- DHEA the adrenal androgen precursor declines with aging and correlates with vitality and immune function.
- Testosterone and DHEA correspond to Kidney Yang and Jing supporting constitutional vitality and anabolic capacity.

## Thyroid Hormones

- T3 and T4 govern metabolic rate, thermogenesis, cardiac output, and cognitive function.
- Hypothyroidism produces fatigue, cold intolerance, weight gain, and cognitive slowing corresponding to Kidney Yang deficiency.
- Hyperthyroidism produces anxiety, heat intolerance, weight loss, and palpitations corresponding to Yin deficiency with fire.
- The Spleen and Kidney Yang together govern metabolic rate in TCM corresponding to thyroid function.

## TCM Correspondence

- Kidney Jing provides the constitutional substrate for all hormonal production and regulation.
- Kidney Yang governs the warming metabolic functions corresponding to thyroid and adrenal hormonal drive.
- Kidney Yin governs the cooling restorative functions corresponding to anabolic and anti-inflammatory hormonal activity.
- Kidney Jing depletion with aging corresponds to the progressive decline of DHEA, sex hormones, and GH with age.
