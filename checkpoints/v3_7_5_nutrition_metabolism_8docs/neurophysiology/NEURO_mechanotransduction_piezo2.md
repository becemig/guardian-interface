# Mechanotransduction and Piezo2 Channels in Somatic Sensing

## Abstract

Mechanotransduction converts mechanical forces into electrochemical signals within biological tissues. Piezo2 is the primary mechanically activated ion channel responsible for proprioception, light touch, and interoception in mammals. Understanding Piezo2 provides a molecular foundation for somatic awareness and the physiological basis of embodied practices such as Tai Chi and Qigong.

## Physiological Mechanisms

### Piezo2 Channel Structure

- Piezo2 is a trimeric ion channel embedded in sensory neuron plasma membranes.
- Membrane deformation directly gates the channel, allowing calcium and sodium influx within milliseconds.
- Expressed in dorsal root ganglion neurons, Merkel cells, muscle spindles, and pulmonary stretch receptors.

### Proprioceptive Role

- Piezo2 knockout produces complete loss of limb proprioception with preserved nociception.
- Proprioceptive Ia afferents in muscle spindles require Piezo2 for stretch transduction.
- Loss of Piezo2 in humans produces severe proprioceptive ataxia with intact pain sensitivity.

### Interoceptive Role

- Piezo2 expressed in vagal afferents innervating lung, gut, and cardiovascular system.
- Pulmonary Piezo2 channels transduce lung inflation into vagal signals regulating breathing rhythm.
- Cardiac mechanosensation via Piezo2 contributes to baroreflex modulation of heart rate.

### Fascial Mechanosensation

- Fascial fibroblasts express Piezo1 and Piezo2, implicating connective tissue in whole-body mechanical signaling.
- Sustained fascial loading during slow movement may activate Piezo2 in periosteum and deep fascia.
- This provides a molecular mechanism for felt internal structure during Tai Chi practice.

## Relevance to Internal Movement Arts

- Tai Chi slow loading operates within the activation range of Piezo2 low-threshold mechanoreceptors.
- Coordinated breath-movement cycles may entrain pulmonary Piezo2 vagal discharge with proprioceptive input.
- HRV coherence during practice may reflect synchronized Piezo2-mediated vagal and proprioceptive activity.
- De Qi sensation in acupuncture may involve Piezo2 activation in fascial mechanoreceptors at acupoints.
