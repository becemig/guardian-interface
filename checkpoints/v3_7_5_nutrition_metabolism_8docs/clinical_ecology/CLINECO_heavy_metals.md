# Heavy Metal Toxicology

## Abstract

Heavy metal toxicology — the study of the mechanisms by which toxic metals including lead, mercury, arsenic, cadmium, and aluminum damage biological systems through enzyme inhibition, oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, epigenetic disruption, and displacement of essential metals from their biological binding sites — establishes these ubiquitous environmental contaminants as significant contributors to neurodevelopmental impairment, cardiovascular disease, renal dysfunction, cancer, and immune dysregulation, with no safe level of exposure identified for lead or mercury and global exposure occurring primarily through contaminated food, water, and air. Lead neurotoxicity — the most extensively studied developmental neurotoxicant, with epidemiological evidence demonstrating that blood lead levels below 5 micrograms per deciliter produce measurable IQ reductions, attention deficits, and behavioral problems in children, with the neurotoxic mechanisms including displacement of calcium from synaptic signaling, inhibition of NMDA receptors during critical developmental windows, disruption of heme synthesis, and epigenetic modifications at neuroplasticity genes persisting into adulthood. Methylmercury mechanisms — the organomercury compound produced by microbial methylation of inorganic mercury that bioaccumulates through aquatic food chains to reach toxic concentrations in large predatory fish, causing the devastating neurological syndrome of Minamata disease from high-dose exposure and subtler neurodevelopmental deficits at low prenatal exposures, through mechanisms including binding to sulfhydryl groups of critical enzymes, disruption of microtubule polymerization, and mitochondrial dysfunction in energy-intensive neurons. Arsenic carcinogenesis — the metalloid arsenic occurring naturally in groundwater at toxic concentrations in Bangladesh, West Bengal, and other regions exposing hundreds of millions of people, causing the characteristic arsenical keratoses and Mees lines at high doses and bladder, lung, and skin cancers through mechanisms including epigenetic silencing of tumor suppressor genes, disruption of DNA repair, and generation of oxidative stress. The Kidney Jing metal poison depletion correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that heavy metal poisons deplete Kidney Jing — the constitutional essence governing developmental capacity and longevity — corresponding to the irreversible developmental neurotoxicity of heavy metals that permanently impairs the child's constitutional developmental potential.

## Lead Mechanisms

- Calcium displacement: Pb2+ mimics Ca2+ — disrupts synaptic calcium signaling.
- NMDA inhibition: lead blocks NMDA receptors during critical developmental windows.
- Heme inhibition: inhibits delta-aminolevulinic acid dehydratase — reduces heme synthesis.
- Epigenetics: lead exposure alters DNA methylation at neuroplasticity genes.

## Mercury Toxicity

- Methylmercury: bioaccumulates in tuna swordfish shark — prenatal exposure highest risk.
- Mechanism: sulfhydryl binding enzyme inhibition microtubule disruption.
- Minamata: industrial methylmercury discharge — devastating neurological syndrome.
- Threshold: no safe prenatal exposure established — global fish consumption dilemma.

## Chelation

- DMSA: oral chelator for lead mercury arsenic — FDA approved for childhood lead poisoning.
- DMPS: intravenous chelator for mercury — used in functional medicine.
- EDTA: intravenous chelator for lead — cardiovascular chelation controversial.
- Principles: chelate after removing exposure source — mobilization without removal worsens toxicity.
