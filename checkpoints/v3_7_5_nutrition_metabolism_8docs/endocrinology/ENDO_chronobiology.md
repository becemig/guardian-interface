# Chronobiology of Hormones

## Abstract

The endocrine system is a temporally orchestrated system in which every major hormone follows a characteristic circadian, ultradian, or infradian rhythm — timed to anticipate physiological demands across the 24-hour cycle. The circadian neuroendocrine orchestra is conducted by the SCN, which coordinates the diurnal cortisol curve, the nocturnal GH pulse, the melatonin darkness signal, the morning testosterone peak, and the nocturnal prolactin and TSH rises into a coherent hormonal symphony. Ultradian pulsatility — the minute-to-minute pulsatile release of GnRH, GH, cortisol, and insulin — provides temporal information that is distinct from the mean hormone level and cannot be replaced by continuous infusion. Circadian disruption through shift work, social jetlag, or chronic light pollution desynchronizes this orchestra, producing metabolic, reproductive, immune, and cognitive consequences within days. The TCM twelve organ clock maps the sequential energetic activation of organ systems through the 24-hour cycle in striking parallel to the chronoendocrine profile.

## The 24-Hour Hormonal Profile

- 6-8 AM: cortisol awakening response peaks - primes immune, metabolic, cognitive systems
- 7-9 AM: testosterone peaks in males - highest androgen action window
- 8-10 AM: TSH begins diurnal decline from nocturnal peak - thyroid activity rising
- 12-2 PM: post-lunch insulin sensitivity dips - circadian glucose tolerance reduction
- 3-5 PM: core body temperature peaks - highest physical performance window
- 6-8 PM: cortisol at daily nadir - immune activity rising
- 9-10 PM: melatonin onset (DLMO) - darkness signal begins
- 11 PM - 1 AM: first GH pulse during initial SWS - peak anabolic window
- 2-4 AM: prolactin peaks - immune modulation and tissue repair
- 4-6 AM: cortisol begins rising - prepares body for waking

## Ultradian Pulsatility

- GnRH: pulses every 60-90 minutes - frequency encodes FSH vs LH release ratio
- GH: discrete pulses every 3-5 hours - trough levels near zero between pulses
- Cortisol: superimposed ultradian pulses every 60-90 minutes on diurnal curve
- Insulin: pulsatile every 5-10 minutes - continuous infusion less effective than pulses
- Information content: pulse frequency and amplitude encode independent signals
- Disruption: continuous hormone exposure (exogenous or pathological) desensitizes receptors

## Cortisol Diurnal Curve

- Shape: steep morning rise, gradual afternoon decline, nadir around midnight
- CAR: 50-100 percent spike in first 30-45 minutes after waking
- Light dependence: morning light steepens the rise - sets the daily amplitude
- Flat curve: burnout and PTSD pattern - loss of morning-evening differential
- Inverted curve: shift workers - cortisol peaks at night - metabolic consequences
- Measurement: cortisol AUC from waking samples captures diurnal exposure

## Sleep-Hormone Architecture

- SWS coupling: GH pulse tightly coupled to first SWS episode - both decline with age together
- REM coupling: testosterone pulses during REM - nocturnal penile tumescence marker
- TSH nocturnal peak: TSH peaks before sleep onset - drives morning thyroid activity
- Prolactin: rises sharply after sleep onset - falls on waking
- Cortisol suppression: SWS actively suppresses cortisol - disrupted SWS raises night cortisol
- Sleep deprivation: one week of 5-hour sleep reduces testosterone by 10-15 percent

## Chrono-Nutrition

- Morning eating: highest insulin sensitivity - calories most efficiently metabolized
- Evening eating: reduced insulin sensitivity - same meal causes higher glucose response
- Time-restricted eating: aligning feeding to daytime improves metabolic markers
- Breakfast skipping: increases cortisol and reduces insulin sensitivity across the day
- Meal timing and microbiome: microbiome circadian oscillation entrained by meal timing
- TCM: breakfast corresponds to Stomach time 7-9 AM - largest meal should be morning

## Circadian Disruption Consequences

- Shift work: desynchronizes peripheral clocks from SCN - metabolic syndrome risk doubles
- Social jetlag: weekend schedule shift disrupts hormonal synchrony
- Light at night: suppresses melatonin - disrupts GH and prolactin nocturnal peaks
- Jet lag: 1-2 weeks to fully resynchronize endocrine system after transmeridian travel
- WHO classification: shift work as probable carcinogen - disrupted melatonin rhythm
