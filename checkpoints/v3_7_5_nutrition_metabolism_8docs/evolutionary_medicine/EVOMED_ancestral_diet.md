# Ancestral Diet and Nutritional Evolution

## Abstract

Ancestral diet science — the reconstruction of the nutritional environment in which human metabolic physiology evolved and the application of that knowledge to understand contemporary dietary health — reveals that modern industrial diets deviate from ancestral nutritional patterns in virtually every measurable dimension, and that these deviations correspond precisely to the nutritional drivers of the chronic disease epidemic. Human digestive and metabolic physiology co-evolved with a specific nutritional environment over millions of years: diverse plant foods providing 50-100 grams of fiber per day feeding a diverse gut microbiome; wild animal foods providing a balanced omega-3 to omega-6 fatty acid ratio of approximately 1:1 to 1:4 compared to the modern ratio of 1:15 to 1:20; a low glycemic load diet based on whole unprocessed foods without refined grains or sugars; and a rich phytochemical environment providing thousands of bioactive plant compounds that activate xenohormetic stress response pathways. Fiber coevolution — the 300,000 year co-evolutionary relationship between the human microbiome and dietary fiber — produced a gut microbial ecosystem exquisitely tuned to ferment diverse plant fibers into short-chain fatty acids that nourish the colonic epithelium, regulate immune function, produce B vitamins and vitamin K2, and signal the enteroendocrine system to regulate appetite and metabolism. The omega-3 to omega-6 ratio in ancestral diets — reflecting the high alpha-linolenic acid content of wild plant foods and the high EPA and DHA content of wild animal foods — supported an anti-inflammatory eicosanoid balance that modern diets invert through the dominance of omega-6 rich refined vegetable oils, producing a pro-inflammatory baseline that underlies cardiovascular disease, depression, and autoimmune conditions. Phytochemical xenohormesis — the hormetic activation of animal stress response pathways by plant-derived bioactive compounds including polyphenols, glucosinolates, and alkaloids — describes the mechanism by which diverse plant food consumption activates Nrf2, AMPK, and sirtuin pathways producing the same constitutional strengthening as other hormetic stressors. The Five Flavors nutritional wisdom of TCM — the classification of foods by their predominant flavor and its corresponding organ network tropism — represents an empirically developed nutritional ecology that encodes millennia of observation about the constitutional effects of diverse food consumption.

## Ancestral Macronutrient Ratios

- Protein: 19-35 percent of calories from diverse animal and plant sources.
- Carbohydrate: 22-40 percent from whole unprocessed plant foods with high fiber and low glycemic load.
- Fat: 28-58 percent with balanced omega-3 to omega-6 ratio from wild sources.
- Fiber: 50-100 grams per day — 5-10 times modern average intake.

## Omega-3 Omega-6 Evolution

- Ancestral ratio approximately 1:1 to 1:4 omega-3 to omega-6.
- Modern Western diet ratio approximately 1:15 to 1:20 — profoundly pro-inflammatory.
- Omega-6 excess from refined vegetable oils produces pro-inflammatory arachidonic acid eicosanoids.
- EPA and DHA deficiency impairs brain development, mood regulation, and cardiovascular health.

## Phytochemical Xenohormesis

- Plant bioactive compounds activate animal cellular stress response pathways.
- Polyphenols: resveratrol quercetin curcumin activating Nrf2 and sirtuins.
- Glucosinolates from cruciferous vegetables activating Phase II detoxification enzymes.
- Diversity of plant foods provides diverse xenohormetic signals — corresponds to microbiome diversity benefit.
