# Microbiome Testing and Interventions

## Abstract

Microbiome testing and interventions — the methods for characterizing gut microbiome composition and function and the clinical and dietary interventions available to restore and optimize microbiome health, with 16S rRNA amplicon sequencing providing taxonomic identification through the nine hypervariable regions of the bacterial 16S ribosomal RNA gene, whole genome shotgun metagenomics providing strain-level resolution and functional gene characterization, and metabolomics providing direct measurement of the microbial metabolites including SCFAs, secondary bile acids, and tryptophan metabolites that mediate microbiome effects on host health. 16S rRNA sequencing methods — the most widely used clinical microbiome assessment method targeting specific hypervariable regions of the 16S rRNA gene to identify bacterial taxa present in a sample through comparison to reference databases, with the V3-V4 region being most commonly used in clinical services, with limitations including the inability to distinguish between viable and dead bacteria, the reference database dependence limiting identification of novel taxa, and the variable region-dependent taxonomic resolution that typically allows genus but not species-level identification. Metagenomics and functional analysis — the shotgun sequencing of all DNA in a microbiome sample providing species and strain-level resolution, functional gene content characterization identifying the metabolic pathways encoded by the community, and the ability to identify antibiotic resistance genes and virulence factors, with the Human Microbiome Project establishing reference metagenomes for healthy adults across body sites and the MetaHIT consortium establishing the gene catalog of 3.3 million non-redundant gut microbial genes. Fecal microbiota transplantation clinical evidence — the therapeutic transfer of fecal microbiome from a healthy donor to a dysbiotic recipient, with greater than 90 percent cure rates for recurrent Clostridioides difficile infection representing the best-supported clinical indication, moderate evidence for ulcerative colitis remission including the Paramsothy multidonor intensive FMT trial showing 32 percent remission versus 9 percent placebo, and emerging evidence for metabolic syndrome, autism, and IBS. The Clinical Microbiome Assessment Four Examinations correspondence in TCM describes the classical four examination framework of looking listening asking and palpating applied to the assessment of digestive and microbiome-related conditions — including the visual assessment of tongue coating and complexion, the inquiry into bowel habits, digestive symptoms, diet history, and medication use, and the palpation of the abdomen — a holistic assessment framework corresponding to the clinical integration of microbiome testing results with conventional history and examination.

## 16S Sequencing

- Variable regions: V3-V4 most common in clinical services.
- Taxonomic resolution: genus level typical — species level limited.
- Limitations: dead bacteria detected — culture or viability staining needed.
- Clinical reports: diversity metrics relative abundance dysbiosis indicators.

## Metagenomics

- Shotgun: all DNA sequenced — species strain level resolution.
- Functional: metabolic pathway genes — SCFA bile acid tryptophan capacity.
- Resistance genes: antibiotic resistance gene detection — clinical relevance.
- Reference: Human Microbiome Project MetaHIT gene catalog.

## FMT Evidence

- C. difficile: greater than 90 percent cure rate recurrent infection — gold standard.
- Ulcerative colitis: 32 percent remission multidonor intensive FMT — emerging.
- Donor matching: donor composition predicts recipient engraftment and outcome.
- Safety: serious adverse events rare — donor screening critical.
