# Short Chain Fatty Acids and Microbiome Metabolites

## Abstract

Short chain fatty acids and microbiome metabolites — the primary bioactive products of colonic microbial fermentation of dietary fiber and resistant starch, with the three principal SCFAs being butyrate, propionate, and acetate produced in an approximate ratio of 15:25:60, collectively reaching millimolar concentrations in the colon and serving as the primary fuel for colonocytes, the primary signals regulating colonic motility and mucosal defense, the primary immune regulatory signals inducing regulatory T cell differentiation and suppressing inflammatory cytokine production, and as circulating metabolites reaching the liver, muscle, adipose tissue, and brain to influence systemic metabolism and immune function. Butyrate colonocyte fuel — the four-carbon SCFA produced primarily by Firmicutes species including Faecalibacterium prausnitzii, Roseburia, and Clostridium butyricum that serves as the obligate fuel for colonocytes providing 60-70 percent of their energy needs, maintaining the hypoxic environment of the colon that prevents pathobiont expansion, strengthening tight junction proteins to maintain intestinal barrier integrity, inducing goblet cell mucin secretion, and stimulating regulatory T cell differentiation in the lamina propria — making butyrate a master regulator of colonic health whose depletion in dysbiosis underlies intestinal barrier dysfunction and mucosal inflammation. Butyrate epigenetics and HDAC inhibition — the mechanism by which butyrate at physiological concentrations inhibits histone deacetylases, enzymes that remove acetyl groups from histone tails and condense chromatin to silence gene transcription, with HDAC inhibition by butyrate increasing histone acetylation and opening chromatin at the promoters of anti-inflammatory genes, tumor suppressor genes, and differentiation genes — providing the mechanism for butyrate's anti-inflammatory, anti-cancer, and immune regulatory effects in the colon and systemically at the concentrations that reach portal circulation. Secondary bile acids — the gut microbiome transformation of primary bile acids synthesized in the liver into the secondary bile acids deoxycholic acid and lithocholic acid through bacterial 7-alpha-dehydroxylation, with secondary bile acids serving as signaling molecules activating TGR5 and FXR receptors on enteroendocrine cells, hepatocytes, and immune cells to regulate metabolic rate, glucose homeostasis, immune function, and GLP-1 secretion — making the gut microbiome a critical regulator of bile acid metabolism and its downstream metabolic effects. The Grain Qi fermentation Post-Heaven essence correspondence in TCM describes the classical teaching that Grain Qi extracted from food by the Spleen is the primary source of Post-Heaven Qi — a correspondence with the production of SCFAs and other bioactive metabolites through the microbial fermentation of dietary grain and fiber in the colon, representing the ultimate biochemical mechanism through which dietary substrates are transformed into the metabolic signals sustaining bodily function.

## Butyrate Functions

- Colonocyte fuel: 60-70 percent of colonocyte energy from butyrate.
- Barrier: strengthens tight junctions ZO-1 claudin occludin expression.
- Mucin: stimulates goblet cell MUC2 secretion — protective mucus layer.
- Treg: induces FOXP3+ regulatory T cells — immune tolerance in lamina propria.

## HDAC Inhibition

- Mechanism: butyrate inhibits class I and II HDACs.
- Histone acetylation: increased H3K9ac H3K27ac — chromatin opening.
- Anti-cancer: tumor suppressor gene expression — colorectal cancer prevention.
- Anti-inflammatory: NF-kB pathway inhibition — reduced cytokine production.

## SCFA Receptors

- GPR41: propionate acetate — enteroendocrine PYY GLP-1 secretion.
- GPR43: acetate — immune cell adipocyte signaling.
- GPR109a: butyrate niacin receptor — colonocyte immune regulation.
- Systemic: portal circulation — liver muscle adipose tissue effects.
