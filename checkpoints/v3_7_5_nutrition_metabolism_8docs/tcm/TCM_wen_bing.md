# Wen Bing Xue

## Abstract

The Wen Bing — warm disease school — emerged in the Qing dynasty as a systematic extension and partial revision of the Shang Han Lun cold damage framework to address the febrile epidemic diseases that the cold damage framework could not adequately treat. The foundational insight of Wen Bing theory is that warm-heat pathogens enter the body through the mouth and nose rather than through the skin surface as cold pathogens do — they invade first into the Lung and upper Jiao and progress downward through the body rather than inward from the exterior as in the Shang Han six channel model. Ye Tian Shi's four level Wei-Qi-Ying-Xue framework — which became the primary diagnostic system of Wen Bing — maps the progression of warm disease through four increasingly deep levels: Wei defensive level with fever, slight chills, and sore throat; Qi level with high fever, thirst, and sweating; Ying nutritive level with fever worse at night, delirium, and rash appearance; and Xue Blood level with hemorrhage, convulsions, and unconsciousness. Wu Ju Tong's three burner San Jiao framework provides a complementary spatial mapping of warm disease progression — upper burner Lung-Pericardium, middle burner Spleen-Stomach, lower burner Liver-Kidney — that organizes the clinical presentations and formulas of Wen Bing by anatomical level. The Wen Bing school developed a body of classical formulas specifically for warm disease — Yin Qiao San, Sang Ju Yin, Qing Ying Tang, Xi Jiao Di Huang Tang, Da Ding Feng Zhu — that address the specific pathophysiology of warm-heat pathogen invasion and its progressive deepening into the Blood level and extraordinary vessels.

## Four Level Diagnosis

- Wei level: pathogen at the body surface — fever dominant over chills, sore throat, floating rapid pulse.
- Qi level: pathogen in the Qi system — high fever, thirst, sweating, no chills, surging rapid pulse.
- Ying level: pathogen in the nutritive layer — fever worse at night, restlessness, delirium, faint rash.
- Xue Blood level: pathogen in the Blood — hemorrhage, convulsions, loss of consciousness, deep rapid pulse.

## Three Burner Framework

- Upper Jiao Lung and Pericardium: early stage febrile disease affecting respiratory and cardiac function.
- Middle Jiao Spleen and Stomach: mid-stage affecting digestion, fluid metabolism, and consciousness.
- Lower Jiao Liver and Kidney: late stage affecting the constitutional Yin and producing internal Wind.

## Wen Bing Formulas

- Yin Qiao San: Wei level Wind-Heat — acrid cool exterior releasing with Honeysuckle and Forsythia.
- Qing Ying Tang: Ying level — clearing nutritive Heat and cooling the Blood with Rhinoceros Horn substitute.
- Da Ding Feng Zhu: late stage Liver-Kidney Yin exhaustion with internal Wind — heavy nourishing formula.
- These formulas address the specific pathophysiology of warm-heat invasion unavailable in cold damage formulas.
