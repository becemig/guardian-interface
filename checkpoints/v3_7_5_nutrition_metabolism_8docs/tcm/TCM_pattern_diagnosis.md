# Pattern Diagnosis in TCM: Eight Principles, Pattern Differentiation, and Clinical Reasoning

## Abstract

Pattern diagnosis Bian Zheng is the foundational clinical reasoning methodology of Traditional Chinese Medicine. Rather than diagnosing named diseases the TCM clinician identifies patterns Zheng of disharmony in the body mind and spirit that explain the totality of the patient presentation. The Eight Principles Ba Gang provide the primary analytical framework organizing all clinical data into four paired polarities: Yin and Yang, Interior and Exterior, Hot and Cold, and Excess and Deficiency. Pattern diagnosis integrates findings from the four examinations — looking, listening and smelling, asking, and palpation — into a coherent clinical picture that directly guides treatment strategy. Guardian Interface somatic telemetry provides real-time pattern-relevant data that can support and augment traditional diagnostic reasoning.

## Eight Principles — Ba Gang

### Yin and Yang

- Yin and Yang are the master principles encompassing all other pairs in the Eight Principles framework.
- Yang patterns tend toward activity, heat, excess, and exterior presentation.
- Yin patterns tend toward stillness, cold, deficiency, and interior presentation.
- Complex presentations may show mixed Yin and Yang features requiring careful differentiation.
- HRV reflects the dynamic Yin-Yang balance of autonomic tone with low HRV suggesting Yang excess or Yin deficiency.

### Interior and Exterior

- Exterior patterns arise from invasion of external pathogenic factors at the body surface Wei level.
- Symptoms include aversion to cold or wind, fever, stiff neck, and floating pulse.
- Interior patterns arise from dysfunction of internal organ systems Zang Fu.
- Symptoms reflect the specific organ system affected and tend toward chronicity and deeper pathology.
- The Tai Yang exterior stage of six-channel diagnosis corresponds to early Bladder and Small Intestine meridian involvement.

### Hot and Cold

- Hot patterns present with heat sensations, thirst, dark urine, red face, rapid pulse, and yellow tongue coat.
- Cold patterns present with cold sensations, no thirst, pale face, slow pulse, and white tongue coat.
- True Heat corresponds to excess inflammatory and sympathetic activation states.
- True Cold corresponds to deficient metabolic, circulatory, and Yang Qi states.
- Pseudo-Heat from Yin deficiency produces empty heat signs with underlying deficiency requiring careful differentiation.

### Excess and Deficiency

- Excess patterns arise from pathogenic factors, stagnation, or accumulation that impede normal Qi flow.
- Deficiency patterns arise from insufficiency of Qi, Blood, Yin, or Yang in specific organ systems.
- Excess presentations are typically acute, strong, and respond well to draining and dispersing treatment.
- Deficiency presentations are typically chronic, weak, and require tonifying and nourishing treatment.
- Mixed excess and deficiency patterns are common in clinical practice requiring simultaneous addressing of both.

## Four Examinations — Si Zhen

### Looking — Wang

- Observation of spirit Shen, complexion, body constitution, posture, and tongue body and coating.
- Tongue diagnosis is the most objective and reproducible visual diagnostic tool in TCM.
- Tongue body color reflects Blood and Yin status while tongue coat reflects digestive function and pathogenic factors.
- Posture and movement quality reveal constitutional strength and myofascial pattern.

### Listening and Smelling — Wen

- Voice quality, breath sounds, and body odors provide diagnostic information about organ function.
- Weak thin voice suggests Lung Qi or overall Qi deficiency.
- Loud aggressive voice suggests Liver Yang rising or Liver Qi stagnation.
- Specific body odors correspond to specific organ imbalances in TCM diagnostic tradition.

### Asking — Wen

- Systematic questioning about chief complaint, pain character, sleep, appetite, digestion, urination, bowel habits, and emotional state.
- Temperature preferences reveal Yin-Yang balance and Hot-Cold pattern.
- Sleep quality reflects Heart Shen stability and Heart Blood sufficiency.
- Emotional tendencies reveal the predominant organ system involved in the pattern.

### Palpation — Qie

- Pulse diagnosis at the radial artery provides information about Qi, Blood, and organ system status.
- Twenty-eight pulse qualities are distinguished in classical TCM pulse diagnosis.
- Abdominal palpation Hara diagnosis in Japanese acupuncture tradition provides additional organ system information.
- Palpation of acupoint tenderness and tissue quality guides local and distal point selection.

## Common Pattern Clusters

### Kidney Yin Deficiency

- Symptoms: afternoon fever, night sweats, tinnitus, lower back soreness, dark scanty urine, red tongue no coat, thin rapid pulse.
- Corresponds to: reduced parasympathetic tone, elevated cortisol, reduced sex hormones, sleep disruption.

### Liver Qi Stagnation

- Symptoms: hypochondriac distension, irritability, sighing, irregular periods, wiry pulse, slightly purple tongue.
- Corresponds to: elevated sympathetic tone, HRV reduction, diaphragmatic tension, stress-mediated digestive dysfunction.

### Heart Blood Deficiency

- Symptoms: palpitations, insomnia, poor memory, anxiety, pale face, thin pale tongue, thin weak pulse.
- Corresponds to: reduced cardiac output, low HRV, anemia, reduced cerebral perfusion.

### Spleen Qi Deficiency with Dampness

- Symptoms: fatigue after eating, loose stools, bloating, heavy limbs, pale swollen tongue, soggy pulse.
- Corresponds to: impaired gut motility, dysbiosis, metabolic syndrome, lymphatic congestion.
