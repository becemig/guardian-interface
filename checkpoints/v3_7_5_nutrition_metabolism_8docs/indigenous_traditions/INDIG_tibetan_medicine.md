# Tibetan Medicine

## Abstract

Sowa Rigpa — the science of healing — is the traditional medicine of Tibet, developed over more than a thousand years through the synthesis of indigenous Bon healing practices, Indian Ayurvedic medicine, Chinese medicine, and the uniquely Tibetan contribution of Buddhist medical tantra, creating a comprehensive system of medicine that integrates physical, psychological, and spiritual dimensions of health in a manner perhaps unparalleled among the world's medical traditions. The Gyushi — the Four Medical Tantras compiled in the 12th century — is the foundational text of Tibetan medicine, presenting its teachings through an elaborate tree metaphor with three roots, nine trunk branches, and hundreds of leaves representing the complete body of Tibetan medical knowledge. The three Nyes-pa humors — rLung wind, mKhris-pa bile, and Bad-kan phlegm — govern all physiological processes in a manner analogous to the Ayurvedic doshas but with distinctly Tibetan characteristics reflecting Buddhist cosmology and Himalayan environmental medicine. rLung wind is the most important and frequently imbalanced of the three humors in Tibetan medicine — it governs the movement of all bodily processes including the circulation of Qi in the subtle body channels, the function of the nervous system, the movement of thought, and the expression of emotion. rLung imbalance produces the characteristic Tibetan disease category of rLung disorder: anxiety, insomnia, excessive thinking, trembling, dizziness, tinnitus, and a wide range of psychosomatic conditions that correspond remarkably to the contemporary understanding of autonomic nervous system dysregulation and anxiety disorders. The Tibetan subtle body system — the central channel Uma, the right channel Ro-ma, and the left channel Rkyang-ma, with their 72,000 subsidiary channels and the five chakra wheels — provides the subtle anatomical framework for both medical treatment and tantric meditation practice.

## Three Nyes-pa

- rLung wind: movement, nervous system, thought, breath, elimination — seat in the lower abdomen.
- mKhris-pa bile: transformation, digestion, vision, complexion, intelligence — seat in the liver and gallbladder.
- Bad-kan phlegm: cohesion, lubrication, memory, sleep, joint health — seat in the upper body and brain.
- Each humor has five functional subdivisions governing specific physiological domains.

## rLung Disorder

- The primary epidemic condition of Tibetan medicine — corresponding to autonomic dysregulation and anxiety.
- Excessive thinking, emotional volatility, insomnia, and somatic symptoms from rLung imbalance.
- Treatment: warming foods and lifestyle, butter and meat diet, social warmth, specific rLung-pacifying herbs.
- The prevalence of rLung disorder in modern Tibetan communities corresponds to the epidemic of anxiety disorders.

## Subtle Body System

- Uma central channel corresponds to the Governing and Conception vessels of TCM.
- Five chakra wheels at the crown, throat, heart, navel, and secret center correspond to Dan Tian system.
- Tantric meditation practices work directly with the subtle body to purify channels and enhance Qi flow.
- This subtle body anatomy bridges Tibetan medicine, Indian tantra, and TCM channel theory.

## TCM Correspondence

- rLung wind disorder corresponds to TCM Liver Qi stagnation, Kidney deficiency, and Heart Shen disturbance combined.
- mKhris-pa bile corresponds to TCM Liver-Gallbladder Fire and the transforming ministerial fire.
- Bad-kan phlegm corresponds to TCM Spleen-Stomach Phlegm-Damp and the cohesive earth element.
