# Jin Shin Do Body Focusing

## Abstract

The verbal body focusing component of Jin Shin Do represents its most distinctive contribution to the somatic therapy field — the systematic use of guided verbal awareness to help the client attend to and articulate the somatic experience arising during acupressure point work. Eugene Gendlin's focusing methodology provides the psychological foundation for the JSD verbal component: the felt sense is Gendlin's term for the vague, holistic bodily sense of a situation or issue that contains more information than can be immediately articulated. In JSD sessions the practitioner holds a tsubo combination while verbally guiding the client to attend to the felt sense arising at the held points — inviting awareness of sensations, temperature, pressure, movement, images, memories, and emotions without forcing interpretation or premature closure. The psychosomatic bridge concept in JSD holds that acupressure point work loosens the somatic holding that has been maintaining the psychological holding — creating a window during which the client can access and process material that was previously locked in the body armor and unavailable to conscious awareness. Imagery arising during JSD sessions is treated as somatic intelligence — the body's way of communicating the content of what is being released. A client who sees blue water during kidney point work, or feels heat during liver point release, or has a memory of childhood during thoracic segment work is receiving information directly from the somatic system about the nature of the pattern being released. Emotional release in JSD is welcomed as a sign of successful point work — tears, trembling, sighing, and spontaneous sound are the body's natural release mechanisms that complete the cycle of activation and discharge. Integration of released material through verbal processing, movement, or simply resting in awareness completes the JSD session process.

## Felt Sense

- The felt sense is the vague holistic bodily knowing of a situation that contains more than can be articulated.
- Attending to the felt sense at held acupressure points connects physical sensation to psychological meaning.
- The practitioner invites rather than directs — following the client's felt sense rather than interpreting it.
- This non-directive approach corresponds to the Taoist principle of following the natural Qi movement.

## Imagery as Somatic Intelligence

- Images arising during point work are treated as direct communication from the somatic system.
- Color imagery often corresponds to organ network associations: green for Liver, red for Heart, white for Lung.
- Temperature sensations, movement impulses, and spatial imagery are all somatic intelligence signals.
- Processing imagery verbally during point holding deepens the release and adds psychological meaning.

## Emotional Release Integration

- Tears, trembling, sighing, and sound are natural completion mechanisms for the activation-discharge cycle.
- Suppressing emotional release during point work reinstates the armor that is being dissolved.
- The JSD practitioner maintains steady holding through emotional release rather than withdrawing or redirecting.
- Post-release integration through verbal processing allows the client to contextualize the released material.

## TCM Correspondence

- Verbal body focusing corresponds to the TCM cultivation of Yi intention attending to the body's Qi movement.
- Felt sense as somatic intelligence corresponds to the TCM understanding that the body is always communicating its Qi state.
- Emotional release as natural completion corresponds to the TCM seven emotions needing expression to maintain Qi flow.
- Psychosomatic bridge corresponds to the TCM non-dual understanding that Shen disturbance and Qi stagnation are inseparable.
