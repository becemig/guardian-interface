# Anatomy Trains Deep Front Line and the Core Myofascial Meridian

## Abstract

The Deep Front Line is the innermost myofascial meridian in Thomas Myers Anatomy Trains framework. It runs from the plantar foot through the inner leg, psoas complex, and anterior spine to the cranial base, enveloping the visceral cavity and forming the structural core of the body. The Deep Front Line corresponds closely to the trajectory of the Kidney and Liver meridians in Traditional Chinese Medicine and provides a structural substrate for understanding internal power generation in Tai Chi.

## Structural Pathway

### Foot and Leg Segment

- Originates at the plantar surface via the tibialis posterior and long toe flexors.
- Ascends along the posterior tibia and fibula, passing deep to the superficial calf.
- Enters the knee via the popliteus and posterior knee capsule.
- Continues along the inner thigh via the adductor group and medial intermuscular septum.

### Pelvic and Core Segment

- Passes through the pelvic floor via the levator ani and obturator internus fascia.
- Connects to the psoas major and iliacus forming the iliopsoas complex at the lumbar spine.
- The psoas is the only muscle connecting the lumbar vertebrae directly to the femur.
- Integrates with the anterior longitudinal ligament and crural diaphragm at the thoracolumbar junction.

### Thoracic and Cranial Segment

- The respiratory diaphragm forms the central node of the Deep Front Line connecting thoracic and abdominal segments.
- Ascends via the pericardium, mediastinum, and anterior cervical fascia.
- Terminates at the cranial base via the hyoid bone, scalenes, and cervical fascia.

## TCM Convergence

- The Deep Front Line pathway closely parallels the Kidney meridian ascending the inner leg and anterior spine.
- The psoas region corresponds to the lower Dan Tian energy center in internal martial arts theory.
- The diaphragm as central node mirrors the Middle Dan Tian role in breath-based Qi cultivation.
- Fascial continuity from foot to cranium maps the structural pathway through which internal force is transmitted in Tai Chi.

## Relevance to Internal Movement Arts

- Root and ground in Tai Chi are mechanically expressed through Deep Front Line tension regulation.
- Psoas release and lengthening during slow eccentric movement allows fuller lumbar decompression and spinal elongation.
- Diaphragmatic breathing directly loads and unloads the central node of the Deep Front Line with each breath cycle.
- Coordinating breath with movement in Tai Chi may produce fascial pre-tensioning along the entire Deep Front Line.
