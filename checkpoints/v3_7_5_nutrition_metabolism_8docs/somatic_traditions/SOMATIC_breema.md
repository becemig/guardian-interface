# Breema Bodywork

## Abstract

Breema is a bodywork system and philosophy developed at the Breema Center in Oakland California drawing on ancient Kurdish healing traditions emphasizing the nine principles of harmony as the governing framework for all practice. Unlike most bodywork systems that focus primarily on technique, Breema emphasizes the quality of presence and the inner attitude of the practitioner as the primary therapeutic medium. The nine principles of harmony are: body comfortable, no extra, firmness and gentleness, full participation, mutual benefit, no hurry no pause, single moment single activity, no judgment, and support. Body comfortable means the practitioner arranges their body in a comfortable position before performing any movement — comfort in the body enables natural presence. No extra means doing only what is necessary — no exertion beyond what the movement requires. Single moment single activity means complete presence to the one activity of the present moment without mental commentary. Mutual benefit is the key principle — Breema treats practitioner and recipient simultaneously as a single system such that both benefit from every exchange. This mutual benefit principle corresponds to the TCM concept of the practitioner cultivating their own Qi through treatment — a recognized principle in classical acupuncture tradition. Present body awareness as the ground of all healing corresponds to the Shen presence of the Heart-Mind fully attending to the living moment of contact.

## Nine Principles

- Body comfortable establishes the foundation of natural uncontrived presence through physical ease.
- No extra eliminates unnecessary effort revealing the minimum action necessary for full therapeutic effect.
- Firmness and gentleness are held simultaneously — neither collapsing into softness nor hardening into force.
- Full participation means the whole body participates in each movement rather than isolated limb action.

## Mutual Benefit

- Mutual benefit is Breema's most distinctive principle — both giver and receiver benefit simultaneously.
- The practitioner uses the recipient's body as a support for their own stretching and movement.
- This transforms the therapeutic relationship from practitioner-acting-on-patient to shared movement practice.
- Mutual benefit prevents practitioner depletion — energy is generated rather than expended through the session.

## Self-Breema

- Self-Breema is the solo practice of Breema principles applied to one's own body through self-sequences.
- Self-Breema sequences develop present body awareness and the nine principles through self-directed practice.
- Regular Self-Breema practice cultivates the inner quality that makes Breema bodywork effective.
- Self-Breema corresponds to Qigong self-cultivation as the foundation of therapeutic capacity.

## TCM Correspondence

- Mutual benefit principle corresponds to the classical acupuncture understanding that treatment cultivates the practitioner's Qi.
- Present body awareness as the ground of healing corresponds to Shen presence of the Heart-Mind.
- No extra principle corresponds to the Taoist principle of Wu Wei — effortless action without unnecessary force.
- Triple Burner distributing warmth through relationship corresponds to the mutual benefit field of Breema practice.
