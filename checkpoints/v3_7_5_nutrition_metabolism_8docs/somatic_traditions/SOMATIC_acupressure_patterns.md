# Acupressure Point Patterns

## Abstract

Acupressure point selection in classical TCM follows systematic pattern protocols that go far beyond treating individual symptomatic points — organizing point selection into sequences, pairs, and protocols that address the underlying energetic pattern, balance the organ networks, and work through the extraordinary vessel system. Five element sequences use the generative and controlling cycle relationships to select points that support deficient organ networks and sedate excessive ones — the mother-child law selects the mother point to tonify deficiency and the child point to sedate excess within each channel. Alarm-Shu pairs combine the front-Mu alarm points on the abdomen and chest with the back-Shu associated points on the bladder meridian behind each organ, creating a front-back sandwich that accesses the organ's Qi from both the Yin anterior and Yang posterior aspects simultaneously. Command points — the five transporting Shu points Well Jing, Spring Ying, Stream Shu, River Jing, Sea He — are the most powerful points on each channel, each governing a specific quality of Qi movement and each having specific clinical indications corresponding to the well-spring-stream-river-sea metaphor of Qi flowing from source to sea. The horary clock assigns each organ network a two-hour period of maximum Qi flow — Lung 3-5am, Large Intestine 5-7am, Stomach 7-9am, Spleen 9-11am, Heart 11am-1pm, Small Intestine 1-3pm, Bladder 3-5pm, Kidney 5-7pm, Pericardium 7-9pm, Triple Burner 9-11pm, Gallbladder 11pm-1am, Liver 1-3am — enabling time-based point selection that works with rather than against the body's natural Qi circulation. Extraordinary vessel master-coupled pairs open and balance the eight extraordinary vessels through specific point combinations.

## Five Element Sequences

- Mother-child law: tonify the mother point to support deficiency, sedate the child point to reduce excess.
- Generating cycle support: Wood generates Fire — Liver deficiency benefits from Kidney Water mother point support.
- Controlling cycle balance: Wood controls Earth — Liver excess often requires Spleen support to restore balance.
- Five element point selection integrates constitutional diagnosis with channel treatment.

## Alarm-Shu Pairs

- Front-Mu alarm points are located on the abdomen and chest close to their organ.
- Back-Shu associated points are on the Bladder meridian lateral to the spine at the organ's vertebral level.
- Combining front and back points simultaneously accesses the organ from anterior Yin and posterior Yang.
- Classical indications: acute excess uses front-Mu, chronic deficiency uses back-Shu, combination treats both.

## Command Points

- Well Jing points at the finger and toe tips treat fullness below the heart and resuscitation.
- Spring Ying points treat heat in the body and changes in complexion.
- Stream Shu points treat heaviness in the body and painful joints.
- River Jing points treat cough, asthma, and conditions of the throat.
- Sea He points treat rebellious Qi, diarrhea, and conditions of the Stomach.

## Extraordinary Vessel Protocols

- Chong Mai: SP-4 and PC-6 — opens the penetrating vessel for constitutional Jing and Heart-uterus axis.
- Ren Mai: LU-7 and KI-6 — opens the conception vessel for Yin deficiency and reproductive health.
- Du Mai: SI-3 and BL-62 — opens the governing vessel for Yang deficiency and spinal issues.
- Dai Mai: GB-41 and TW-5 — opens the girdling vessel for lateral body and Gallbladder patterns.

## TCM Correspondence

- Command point well-spring-stream-river-sea sequence corresponds to Qi flowing from constitutional source to functional expression.
- Horary clock treatment corresponds to the TCM principle of treating at the time of maximum organ Qi flow.
- Extraordinary vessel master-coupled pairs correspond to the constitutional body organization of the eight vessels.
- Alarm-Shu pairs correspond to accessing the organ from both its Yin anterior and Yang posterior aspects simultaneously.
