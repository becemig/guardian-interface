# I Ching Meditation Practice

## Abstract

The I Ching as meditation practice reclaims the hexagram system as a contemplative technology for Shen clarification — using the symbolic imagery of the hexagrams, trigrams, and line texts as objects of meditation that illuminate the nature of the present situation and cultivate the quality of consciousness that can navigate change wisely. Hexagram contemplation is the primary practice — sitting with a received hexagram for an extended period of meditation, allowing its images, judgment, and line texts to resonate through the body and reveal their meaning through felt sense rather than intellectual analysis. The image of each hexagram — Water over Mountain produces Meng Youthful Folly, Thunder over Earth produces Zhen Arousing — provides the primary contemplative entry point: not analyzing the image but inhabiting it, allowing the body to resonate with the natural forces depicted. Line meditation focuses on a single significant line — particularly a moving line — dwelling with its specific text until the body reveals what it means in this situation. Trigram embodiment meditation uses the physical qualities of the trigrams as somatic practices: sitting like Mountain keeping still, breathing like Wind penetrating gently, standing like Heaven reaching to the utmost. Yi Jing Gong is the movement practice derived from I Ching meditation — embodying each of the sixty-four hexagrams through specific postures and movement sequences corresponding to their symbolic meanings. Shen clarification through I Ching meditation works because the hexagram system maps the same territory as the mind — sitting with a hexagram that resonates with the present situation clarifies the mind's understanding of its own situation.

## Hexagram Contemplation

- Sit quietly with the received hexagram after casting — do not immediately analyze or interpret.
- Allow the judgment text and image to resonate through the body as felt sense.
- Notice which aspects of the hexagram produce bodily response — warmth, contraction, opening, recognition.
- The body's response to the hexagram text is the guidance — more reliable than intellectual interpretation.

## Trigram Embodiment

- Heaven — embody full structural expansion, reaching upward, creative initiating force.
- Earth — embody full yielding receptivity, sinking into the ground, receptive nurturing force.
- Water — embody fluid penetrating persistence, finding the lowest path, inexorable depth.
- Fire — embody radiant clarity, expanding outward from center, brilliant illuminating warmth.
- Thunder — embody sudden arousing initiation, the shock of beginning, explosive first movement.
- Wind — embody gentle persistent penetration, following every opening, soft but relentless.
- Mountain — embody absolute stillness, immovable keeping still, the mountain that does not move.
- Lake — embody open joyful receptivity, reflecting surface, the joy of completion and exchange.

## Shen Clarification

- I Ching meditation clarifies Shen by providing a symbolic mirror of the mind's own situation.
- The hexagram does not tell the mind what to think but shows it what it already knows.
- Sustained contemplation with a resonant hexagram produces the felt sense of clarity — Shen settling.
- This corresponds to the TCM practice of cultivating Shen through stillness and contemplative inquiry.

## TCM Correspondence
