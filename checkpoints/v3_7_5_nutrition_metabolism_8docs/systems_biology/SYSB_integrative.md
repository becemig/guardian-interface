# Integrative Systems Medicine

## Abstract

Integrative systems medicine applies complex network and systems biology frameworks to bridge traditional healing paradigms with modern molecular medicine. Network pharmacology maps drug and herb compound interactions onto biological networks to predict therapeutic effects and toxicity, revealing that multi-target interventions — characteristic of herbal formulas — can rebalance diseased networks more effectively than single-target drugs. Polypharmacology recognizes that most effective drugs hit multiple targets simultaneously, and that synergistic multi-target combinations outperform additive single-target effects. Personalized medicine uses multi-omic profiling to individualize treatment to each patient biological network state. Digital phenotyping continuously captures behavioral and physiological biomarkers to track network state over time. These convergent developments validate core TCM principles of individualized pattern diagnosis and multi-target formula design.

## Network Pharmacology

- Hopkins 2008: drug-target-disease networks reveal new therapeutic opportunities
- Disease modules: disease genes cluster in network neighborhoods - drugs should target the module
- Polypharmacology: most approved drugs hit multiple targets - single-target design is incomplete
- Off-target effects: side effects and drug repurposing opportunities both arise from off-target hits
- Network distance: closer a drug target is to disease module in network, more effective the drug
- Combination therapy: drugs targeting different nodes in same disease module show synergy

## TCM Network Pharmacology

- Li systems approach: TCM formulas as multi-target network interventions
- Herb-compound-target-disease network: computational mapping of formula mechanisms
-君臣佐使 formula structure: Jun (emperor) hits primary targets, Chen amplifies, Zuo modulates side effects, Shi guides
- Network validation: Liu Wei Di Huang formula shown to hit 243 targets in aging network
- Synergy: herbal combinations produce synergistic effects not predictable from single herbs
- Antagonism identification: network analysis identifies herb combinations that cancel each other

## Polypharmacology and Synergy

- Definition: intentional design of compounds that modulate multiple targets simultaneously
- Synergy: combined effect greater than sum of individual effects - Bliss independence model
- Mechanisms: parallel pathway inhibition, sequential cascade targeting, network hub disruption
- Resistance: single-target drugs prone to resistance - multi-target harder to escape
- Examples: HIV triple therapy, cancer combination chemotherapy, antibiotic combinations
- TCM validation: computational synergy analysis confirms traditional formula empirical synergies

## Personalized Medicine

- P4 medicine: Hood framework - Predictive Preventive Personalized Participatory
- Multi-omics: genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, metabolomics integrated to characterize individual network state
- Wellness trajectory: longitudinal personal omics profiling tracks health-disease transitions
- N-of-1 trials: single-subject crossover trials determine optimal individual intervention
- TCM parallel: individualized pattern diagnosis and formula customization is personalized medicine
- Convergence: constitutional types (TCM) and omics subtypes map to overlapping categories

## Digital Phenotyping

- Definition: continuous passive capture of behavioral and physiological data via devices
- Data streams: HRV, sleep staging, activity, location, screen time, voice acoustics, gait
- Psychiatric application: digital biomarkers detect mood episodes before clinical presentation
- Guardian Interface: HRV, respiration, skin conductance as real-time digital phenotype streams
- Feedback loops: digital phenotype data enables closed-loop personalized intervention
- Privacy: continuous monitoring raises significant consent and data sovereignty issues

## Systems Medicine Clinical Applications

- Cancer: tumor network analysis guides combination therapy and predicts resistance
- Autoimmune: network-based patient stratification identifies responders to biologics
- Psychiatry: computational psychiatry models mental illness as network state transitions
- Aging: systems geroscience targets multiple aging hallmarks simultaneously
- TCM integration: network pharmacology provides mechanistic bridge for herb-drug interactions
