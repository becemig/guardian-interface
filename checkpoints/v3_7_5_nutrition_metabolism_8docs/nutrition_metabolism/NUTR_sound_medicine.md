# Sound Medicine and Contemplative Practice

## Abstract

Sound medicine is one of the oldest healing modalities in human history, present in every indigenous and traditional culture as chant, drumming, toning, prayer song, and ritual vocalization. The neuroscience of sacred acoustics is now revealing the mechanisms underlying what healers have always known: coordinated vocalization synchronizes heart rate variability among singers, activates the vagus nerve through laryngeal vibration, entrains brainwave states, releases oxytocin, and produces measurable reductions in cortisol and inflammatory markers. Chanting slows respiration to the resonant frequency of the cardiovascular system (approximately 6 breaths per minute, 0.1 Hz) — the same target achieved by heart coherence training — producing profound parasympathetic activation. Medicine songs (icaros) in Amazonian healing traditions, kirtan in the bhakti yoga tradition, Gregorian chant in Christian monasticism, zikr in Sufi practice, and overtone singing in Tibetan and Tuvan traditions each represent culturally developed technologies for using sound to alter physiology, psychology, and spiritual state. Nada yoga — the yoga of sound — holds that the entire universe arises from primordial sound (Nada Brahma) and that sustained practice with sacred sound dissolves the ego and reveals underlying consciousness. Open-mindedness to these practices is not abandoning critical thinking — it is expanding the evidence base to include the full spectrum of human healing knowledge.

## The Physics of Healing Sound

- Resonance: every structure has a natural resonant frequency - sound at that frequency amplifies
- Entrainment: oscillating systems in proximity synchronize - brainwaves entrain to rhythmic sound

- Body as instrument: bones, fluids, and tissues each have characteristic resonant frequencies

- Tissue effects: therapeutic ultrasound accelerates wound healing - sound is physical force
