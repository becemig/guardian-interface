# TCM Nutritional Theory

## Abstract

TCM dietary therapy (Shi Liao) is one of the oldest and most sophisticated food-as-medicine systems in the world, classifying foods by flavor, thermal nature, color, organ affinity, and direction of action rather than by macronutrient content alone. The Spleen and Stomach are the postnatal root — the central axis of Qi production that transforms food into usable energy and transports it to all organs and tissues. Every food choice is understood as having specific therapeutic effects: warming foods build Yang and move Qi; cooling foods clear Heat and nourish Yin; sour foods astringe and enter Liver; bitter foods descend and dry Dampness; sweet foods tonify and enter Spleen; pungent foods disperse and open the exterior; salty foods soften and enter Kidney. Seasonal eating aligns the diet with the prevailing Qi of each season, protecting the associated organ and harmonizing with environmental change. Congee (rice porridge) is the foundational healing food of East Asian medicine — easily digestible, deeply nourishing to Spleen Qi, and the base into which therapeutic herbs and foods are added for targeted healing. Modern nutritional science increasingly validates TCM dietary principles through microbiome research, anti-inflammatory nutrition, and circadian eating science.

## Spleen-Stomach as Postnatal Root

- Central axis: Spleen transforms food - Stomach receives and ripens - together they produce Gu Qi
- Gu Qi: food essence - combines with air (Kong Qi) to produce Zong Qi and ultimately Zheng Qi
- Upward bearing: Spleen bears clear Yang upward to Heart and Lung
- Downward descending: Stomach descends turbid downward to Small and Large Intestine
- Injury: cold raw foods, overthinking, worry, irregular eating - all injure Spleen
- Signs of Spleen deficiency: fatigue after eating, loose stools, bloating, poor appetite, cold limbs
- Priority: in virtually all chronic conditions - strengthen Spleen first - build the foundation

## Five Flavors

- Sour: astringes and consolidates - enters Liver and Gallbladder - vinegar, lemon, hawthorn
- Bitter: descends, dries, and clears Heat - enters Heart and Small Intestine - bitter melon, coffee
- Sweet: tonifies, harmonizes, and moistens - enters Spleen and Stomach - rice, yam, dates, honey
- Pungent: disperses, moves, and opens - enters Lung and Large Intestine - ginger, garlic, onion
- Salty: softens hardness and enters Kidney - seaweed, miso, salt - used in small amounts
- Balance: each flavor in balance nourishes its organ - excess injures - sour excess injures Spleen
- Five color parallel: green-Liver, red-Heart, yellow-Spleen, white-Lung, black-Kidney

## Thermal Nature of Foods

- Hot: lamb, venison, chili, cinnamon, dried ginger - strongly warming Yang - use in cold deficiency
- Warm: chicken, oats, walnuts, leeks, onion, ginger fresh - gently warming - most tonics
- Neutral: rice, potato, beef, egg, carrot - neither warming nor cooling - safe for all
- Cool: peppermint, cucumber, tofu, pears, mung beans - gently cooling - summer and Heat patterns
- Cold: watermelon, raw foods, iced drinks, crab, seaweed - strongly cooling - injures Spleen Yang
- Clinical rule: raw and cold foods damage Spleen Yang - cooked food universally easier to digest
- Cooking method: roasting and baking add warmth - steaming neutral - raw food adds cooling

## Seasonal Eating

- Spring: lighter foods - sprouts, young greens - sour flavor - support Liver Qi rising
- Summer: cooling foods - cucumber, watermelon, mung beans - bitter flavor - protect Heart
- Late summer: sweet nourishing foods - squash, yam, millet - build Spleen and Earth
- Autumn: warming and moistening - pears, sesame, honey, white foods - protect Lung Yin
- Winter: deeply warming and nourishing - lamb, bone broth, black beans, walnuts - build Kidney
- Circadian eating: largest meal midday when Spleen Qi peaks (9-11am Spleen, 11am-1pm Stomach)
- Modern validation: circadian nutrition research confirms benefits of earlier eating window

## Congee and Healing Foods

- Congee: rice cooked in 5-10 times water - easily digestible - rests and nourishes Spleen
- Foundation: base into which therapeutic additions are made - ginger, jujube, goji, astragalus
- Convalescence: universal East Asian healing food for illness, surgery, postpartum recovery
- Jujube dates (Da Zao): tonify Qi and Blood - calm Shen - used in virtually every tonic formula
- Goji berries (Gou Qi Zi): nourish Liver and Kidney Yin - brighten eyes - daily tonic food
- Black sesame: tonify Liver and Kidney - nourish Blood and Jing - moisten intestines
- Bone broth: builds Kidney Jing and marrow - collagen and minerals - ancestral wisdom validated

## Dietary Patterns for Common Conditions

- Qi deficiency: cooked warm foods - congee, soups, yam, chicken, oats - avoid cold and raw
- Blood deficiency: dark foods - black beans, beets, liver, mulberry, longan, leafy greens
- Yin deficiency: moistening foods - pears, lily bulb, tofu, sesame, barley, eggs
- Yang deficiency: warming foods - lamb, ginger, cinnamon, leeks, walnut, chestnuts
- Dampness: avoid dairy, sugar, greasy foods, alcohol, cold raw - eat aduki beans, barley, turnip
- Heat: cooling foods - bitter melon, mung beans, cucumber, peppermint tea, chrysanthemum
- Stagnation: moving foods - turmeric, hawthorn, vinegar, rose petals, moderate amounts of pungent
