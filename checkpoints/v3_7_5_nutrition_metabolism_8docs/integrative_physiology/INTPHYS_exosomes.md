# Exosomes and Extracellular Vesicles

## Abstract

Exosomes and extracellular vesicles — nano-scale membrane-enclosed particles released by virtually every cell type that carry protein, RNA, DNA, and lipid cargo between cells and organs — have emerged as a major inter-organ communication system that may be as important as classical endocrine signaling, providing a mechanism for the transfer of functional biological information between cells and organs that does not require direct cell contact or diffusion of free molecules. Exosomes — the smallest class of extracellular vesicles at 30-150 nm diameter — are formed through the inward budding of the endosomal membrane to create multivesicular bodies MVBs, which then fuse with the plasma membrane to release their vesicular contents into the extracellular space, with the composition of exosome cargo reflecting the physiological state of the parent cell and encoding information about that state that can be transferred to recipient cells. Cargo sorting — the selective packaging of specific proteins, miRNAs, mRNAs, and lipids into exosomes — is a regulated process governed by specific sorting signals, with the miRNA cargo of exosomes representing a particularly potent form of intercellular communication: miRNAs transferred from one cell to another through exosomes can regulate gene expression in the recipient cell, allowing one organ to epigenetically regulate the function of another. Inter-organ exosome communication has been demonstrated across multiple organ axes: muscle-derived exosomes during exercise carry miRNAs that regulate hepatic glucose metabolism; adipose-derived exosomes carry miRNAs that regulate insulin signaling in liver and muscle; brain-derived exosomes carry neuroprotective proteins and miRNAs that regulate peripheral immune function. Exosome therapeutics — one of the most rapidly growing areas of biomedical research — exploits the natural intercellular communication function of exosomes for therapeutic delivery: stem cell-derived exosomes carry the same regenerative signals as their parent stem cells without the safety concerns of cell therapy, and exosomes can be engineered to carry therapeutic cargo to specific target cells. The Jing essence intercellular transmission correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding of Jing as the finest constitutional substance that transmits hereditary and constitutional information between generations and between organ systems — a correspondence with the exosome's function as carrier of fundamental biological information between cells and organs.

## Exosome Biogenesis

- Endosomal membrane invaginates into multivesicular bodies containing intraluminal vesicles.
- MVBs fuse with plasma membrane releasing intraluminal vesicles as exosomes.
- Cargo sorting: specific proteins and miRNAs selectively packaged through sorting signals.
- Release regulated by cellular stress, activation state, and physiological context.

## miRNA Intercellular Transfer

- Exosomal miRNAs transferred to recipient cells are functional — regulate gene expression.
- Muscle exercise exosomes: carry miR-1 miR-133 regulating hepatic glucose metabolism.
- Adipose exosomes: carry miR-155 miR-34a regulating insulin signaling in liver.
- Cross-kingdom: plant-derived exosome-like vesicles carry miRNAs that regulate animal gene expression.

## Stem Cell Exosomes

- Mesenchymal stem cell exosomes carry regenerative miRNAs and growth factors.
- Produce tissue repair effects comparable to parent stem cells without cell engraftment.
- Cardiac repair: MSC exosomes reduce infarct size and improve cardiac function post-MI.
- Neural repair: exosomes promote axon regeneration and reduce neuroinflammation.
