# Psilocybin Neuroscience

## Abstract

Psilocybin — the prodrug of psilocin found in over 200 species of mushrooms distributed across every continent — has become the most extensively researched classic psychedelic in the contemporary renaissance of psychedelic science, with a growing body of randomized controlled trial evidence demonstrating remarkable therapeutic efficacy for treatment-resistant depression, end-of-life distress, addiction, and OCD. Psilocin — the active metabolite produced when psilocybin is dephosphorylated in the body — is a potent 5-HT2A serotonin receptor agonist that produces its profound effects primarily through activation of 5-HT2A receptors in the prefrontal cortex and default mode network. Robin Carhart-Harris' REBUS model — Relaxed Beliefs Under Psychedelics — proposes that psilocybin flattens the precision weighting of high-level priors in the predictive processing hierarchy, allowing bottom-up sensory and emotional information to exert greater influence on perception and cognition than is normally permitted. This flattening of the prior hierarchy produces the characteristic dissolution of ordinary cognitive categories, the increased emotional openness, and the enhanced neuroplasticity of the psychedelic state — creating a window of heightened learning and change during which therapeutic interventions can produce lasting effects. The default mode network disruption produced by psilocybin — measured as dramatically reduced DMN connectivity and activity — corresponds to the subjective experience of ego dissolution: the temporary dissolution of the ordinary sense of being a separate self that characterizes mystical experience across traditions. Roland Griffiths' landmark 2006 study established that psilocybin can reliably occasion mystical-type experiences — characterized by unity, noetic quality, sacredness, deeply felt positive mood, transcendence of time and space, and ineffability — that are rated among the most meaningful experiences of participants' lives and that produce lasting positive changes in attitudes, behavior, and wellbeing. Shen opening describes the TCM framework for understanding the psilocybin experience: a temporary dissolution of the habitual Shen patterns that ordinarily organize experience, allowing a direct encounter with the luminous awareness that is always present beneath the surface.
