# Amygdala and Fear Circuitry

## Abstract

The amygdala is an almond-shaped subcortical structure in the medial temporal lobe that serves as the brain's primary threat detection and fear processing center. Joseph LeDoux's research established the amygdala as the critical site for fear conditioning — the association of neutral stimuli with aversive outcomes that produces conditioned fear responses. The basolateral amygdala receives sensory input and forms fear associations through synaptic plasticity. The central amygdala outputs fear responses to the hypothalamus, brainstem, and cortex producing the full spectrum of fear physiology. The low road — direct thalamo-amygdala pathway — enables rapid below-conscious threat response while the high road — thalamo-cortical-amygdala pathway — enables contextual regulation of fear. Fear extinction is the learning that a previously feared stimulus is no longer dangerous — mediated by prefrontal inhibition of amygdala activity. PTSD involves impaired extinction and enhanced fear generalization from deficient prefrontal-amygdala regulation. Kidney governing fear in TCM corresponds to the amygdala-kidney axis of constitutional fear regulation and threat response.

## Fear Conditioning

- Conditioned fear arises when a neutral conditioned stimulus CS is paired with an aversive unconditioned stimulus US.
- The basolateral amygdala forms the CS-US association through LTP at glutamatergic synapses.
- Conditioned fear responses include freezing, autonomic activation, and HPA axis engagement.
- Fear conditioning is evolutionarily conserved and extremely resistant to erasure once formed.

## Extinction

- Extinction involves presenting the CS repeatedly without the US reducing conditioned fear responses.
- Extinction is new learning — inhibition of the original fear memory — not erasure of the original association.
- The prefrontal cortex, specifically vmPFC and prelimbic cortex, provides inhibitory control over amygdala.
- Context dependence of extinction explains fear renewal when the extinguished stimulus appears in a new context.

## PTSD

- PTSD involves impaired extinction with exaggerated fear responses and generalization to safe stimuli.
- Hyperactivation of the amygdala with reduced prefrontal inhibition characterizes the PTSD neural profile.
- Intrusive memories and flashbacks reflect amygdala-driven fear memory reactivation without prefrontal modulation.
- EMDR, prolonged exposure, and somatic therapies all target the fear extinction deficit of PTSD.
