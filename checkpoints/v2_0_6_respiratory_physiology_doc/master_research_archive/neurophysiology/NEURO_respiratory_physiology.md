# Respiratory Physiology: Gas Exchange, Chemoreceptors, CO2 Tolerance, and Lung Qi

## Abstract

Respiration is the fundamental life process exchanging oxygen and carbon dioxide between the atmosphere and the body tissues. The lungs provide the gas exchange surface while the respiratory control centers in the brainstem regulate breathing rate and depth to maintain blood gas homeostasis. Carbon dioxide rather than oxygen is the primary driver of respiratory drive through chemoreceptor activation making CO2 tolerance a key variable in respiratory efficiency. Slow resonance frequency breathing at 5 to 6 breaths per minute maximizes respiratory sinus arrhythmia and HRV through optimal baroreflex and vagal entrainment. The Lung in TCM governs Qi descent, Wei Qi distribution, and the Po corporeal soul providing the somatic feeling dimension of conscious embodied life.

## Pulmonary Gas Exchange

### Alveolar Architecture

- The lungs contain approximately 300 million alveoli providing a gas exchange surface area of 70 square meters.
- Alveolar walls are one cell thick allowing rapid diffusion of oxygen and carbon dioxide across the blood-gas barrier.
- Type I pneumocytes form the thin gas exchange surface while Type II pneumocytes produce surfactant reducing surface tension.
- Surfactant deficiency in premature infants produces respiratory distress syndrome through alveolar collapse.

### Oxygen Transport

- Oxygen diffuses from alveolar air into pulmonary capillary blood down a partial pressure gradient.
- Hemoglobin in red blood cells binds oxygen cooperatively with a sigmoidal oxygen-hemoglobin dissociation curve.
- The Bohr effect describes rightward shift of the dissociation curve with increased CO2, temperature, and acidity.
- This shift promotes oxygen unloading in metabolically active tissues where CO2 and temperature are elevated.

### Carbon Dioxide Transport

- Carbon dioxide is transported as dissolved CO2, carbaminohemoglobin, and bicarbonate in plasma.
- The bicarbonate buffer system is the primary CO2 transport mechanism involving carbonic anhydrase in red blood cells.
- CO2 diffuses 20 times faster than oxygen across the blood-gas barrier ensuring efficient elimination.
- Arterial CO2 partial pressure PaCO2 is the primary regulated variable in respiratory control.

## Respiratory Control

### Central Pattern Generator

- The pre-Botzinger complex in the medulla is the primary respiratory rhythm generator producing the basic breathing pattern.
- The dorsal and ventral respiratory groups in the medulla coordinate inspiratory and expiratory muscle activity.
- The pneumotaxic center in the pons modulates respiratory rate and inspiratory duration.
- Voluntary override of breathing through cortical pathways allows conscious control as in Qigong breath regulation.

### Chemoreceptors

- Central chemoreceptors in the medulla respond to CO2-induced changes in cerebrospinal fluid pH.
- Peripheral chemoreceptors in the carotid and aortic bodies respond to arterial PO2, PCO2, and pH.
- CO2 is the dominant respiratory drive — rising CO2 produces powerful urge to breathe even with adequate oxygen.
- Chronic mouth breathing and overbreathing reduce CO2 tolerance producing hypocapnia and paradoxical respiratory symptoms.

## CO2 Tolerance

### Hypocapnia and Overbreathing

- Chronic overbreathing reduces arterial CO2 below optimal levels producing hypocapnia.
- Hypocapnia causes cerebral vasoconstriction reducing brain oxygenation despite adequate blood oxygen saturation.
- The Bohr effect shifts leftward in hypocapnia reducing oxygen unloading in tissues producing cellular hypoxia.
- Symptoms of chronic overbreathing include anxiety, fatigue, cognitive fog, cold extremities, and paradoxical breathlessness.

### CO2 Tolerance Training

- Patrick McKeown and the Buteyko method propose that CO2 tolerance training normalizes chronic overbreathing.
- Nasal breathing reduces breathing volume and increases CO2 exposure improving tolerance over time.
- Breath hold training progressively extends comfortable breath hold time as a measure of CO2 tolerance.
- Slow breathing at 5 to 6 breaths per minute with extended exhalation increases CO2 and improves tolerance.

## Resonance Frequency Breathing

- Resonance frequency breathing at approximately 0.1 Hz 6 breaths per minute maximizes HRV and baroreflex amplitude.
- At this frequency respiratory sinus arrhythmia and baroreceptor oscillations synchronize producing maximum amplitude HRV.
- Resonance frequency breathing produces the largest increases in HRV of any intervention studied.
- Qigong and Tai Chi breathing patterns naturally approximate resonance frequency through their slow rhythmic movement.

## Practice Effects

- Qigong and Tai Chi breathing training improves CO2 tolerance through extended exhalation and reduced breathing rate.
- Resonance frequency breathing in practice maximizes HRV amplitude through baroreflex entrainment.
- Diaphragmatic breathing training restores normal breathing mechanics and reduces accessory muscle overuse.
- Nasal breathing cultivation in practice filters, warms, and humidifies air while increasing nasal nitric oxide production.
- Nitric oxide from nasal breathing produces bronchodilation and pulmonary vasodilation improving gas exchange efficiency.
