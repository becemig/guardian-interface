# Bioelectricity and Wound Healing

## Abstract

Bioelectricity and wound healing — the field studying the endogenous electrical fields generated by living tissues and their roles in guiding cell migration, directing tissue repair, and governing developmental pattern formation — reveals the body as an electrical system in which steady-state voltage gradients across epithelia and organ boundaries provide instructive positional information that guides the spatial organization of cells and tissues throughout development and repair, corresponding to the TCM concept of Qi flow through meridians as an information-carrying electrical or electromagnetic signal. Endogenous electric fields — the steady direct current electric fields of tens to hundreds of millivolts per millimeter that exist across intact epithelia and are generated by the differential ion transport activities of polarized epithelial cells maintaining transepithelial potentials — serve as guidance cues for cell migration, with cells sensing these fields through voltage-sensitive membrane mechanisms and migrating directionally toward either the cathode or anode depending on cell type in a process called galvanotaxis. Wound current — the dramatic increase in endogenous electric field strength at wound edges when the transepithelial potential is short-circuited by epithelial disruption, producing an outward wound current that serves as the initial signal for wound healing by recruiting inflammatory cells, fibroblasts, and keratinocytes toward the wound through galvanotaxis — provides the primary mechanism by which the body knows where healing is needed and directs the cellular resources required for repair. Bioelectric pattern formation — Michael Levin's research demonstrating that bioelectric signals mediated by voltage-gated ion channels and gap junctions govern not only wound healing but the fundamental pattern formation of embryonic development, organ polarity, and regeneration, with manipulation of bioelectric gradients sufficient to induce the formation of ectopic organs or correct developmental defects — reveals bioelectricity as a fundamental organizing principle of biological morphogenesis operating above the level of individual gene expression. The Qi flow electrical substrate correspondence in TCM describes the long-hypothesized and partially validated correspondence between the meridian system as a low-resistance electrical pathway governing the flow of bioelectrical signals through the body and the endogenous bioelectric field network governing cellular and tissue organization — a correspondence that remains productive as a theoretical framework for understanding the biophysical basis of meridian phenomena.

## Transepithelial Potential

- Polarized epithelial cells: differential ion transport creates voltage gradient across epithelium.
- Skin TEP: inside negative 10-60 mV relative to outside — primary bioelectric gradient.
- Maintained by Na-K ATPase sodium channels and potassium channels.
- Disruption by wound: short circuits TEP producing outward wound current at edges.

## Galvanotaxis

- Cells migrate directionally in applied and endogenous electric fields.
- Keratinocytes: migrate toward cathode — toward wound center in healing.
- Macrophages dendritic cells: galvanotaxis recruits immune cells to wound.
- Nerve fibers: galvanotactic guidance contributes to neural regeneration.

## Bioelectric Pattern Formation

- Voltage gradients encode positional information governing tissue organization.
- Left-right asymmetry: H+K ATPase activity determines embryonic left-right axis.
- Tail regeneration in planaria: bioelectric manipulation can override genetic default.
- Tumor suppression: normal bioelectric fields maintain tissue organization against cancer.
