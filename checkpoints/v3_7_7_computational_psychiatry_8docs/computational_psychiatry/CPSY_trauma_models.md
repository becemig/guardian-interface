# Computational Models of Trauma

## Abstract

Post-traumatic stress disorder is among the most treatment-resistant psychiatric conditions and one of the most illuminated by computational psychiatry. The core computational insight: PTSD is not a disorder of memory formation but of memory updating — specifically a failure of extinction learning, the process by which previously conditioned fear associations are suppressed by new safety learning. In Bayesian terms, the traumatic event creates an overprecise, highly confident prior belief that a specific class of stimuli predicts danger. This prior is so strong that subsequent contradictory evidence — safety experiences — cannot update it: the likelihood of new safe experiences is overwhelmed by the precision of the trauma prior. The result is a belief system that remains locked in threat mode regardless of actual environmental safety. Reconsolidation research suggests that memories are briefly destabilized when retrieved and can be updated with new information during this window — a finding with profound therapeutic implications. The somatic dimension of trauma — van der Kolk's insight that the body keeps the score — maps naturally onto the interoceptive predictive coding framework: traumatic memories are stored not only as cortical narratives but as interoceptive prediction errors, bodily states that carry threat signal independently of conscious recollection. Guardian Interface, as a somatic and movement-based system, has direct relevance to trauma-informed practice: MCF attunement monitoring during movement work, HRV tracking of autonomic regulation, and knowledge node activation along trauma-relevant pathways.

## Fear Conditioning Framework

- Classical conditioning: CS (neutral stimulus) paired with US (trauma) becomes threat signal
- Conditioned fear response: CS alone triggers full threat response - autonomic, behavioral, cognitive
- Generalization: threat response spreads to stimuli resembling CS - hypervigilance
- Amygdala: basolateral amygdala encodes CS-US associations - fear memory storage
- Hippocampus: contextual conditioning - place and context as CS - context-dependent fear
- Computational model: Rescorla-Wagner rule governs fear acquisition and extinction
- Individual differences: learning rate parameters predict who develops PTSD after trauma

## Extinction and Its Failure in PTSD

- Extinction: repeated CS presentation without US - new inhibitory CS-no-US association learned
- Not erasure: extinction does not delete fear memory - creates competing safety memory
- vmPFC: ventromedial prefrontal cortex drives extinction recall - inhibits amygdala
- PTSD: vmPFC hypoactivation - extinction memory fails to suppress amygdala response
- Renewal: extinction is context-specific - fear returns in non-extinction contexts
- Reinstatement: unexpected shock reinstates extinguished fear - fragility of safety learning
- Bayesian: safety prior insufficiently precise to override trauma prior - precision imbalance

## PTSD as Overprecise Prediction

- Core computational account: trauma creates maximally precise threat prediction
- High precision prior: incoming safety evidence cannot shift posterior - Bayes update fails
- Flashback: spontaneous recall as prediction - brain generates trauma prediction without CS
- Hypervigilance: high precision on threat detection channel - everything scanned for danger
- Numbing: suppression of all prediction error to avoid trauma signal - emotional blunting
- Dissociation: radical suppression of interoceptive prediction - body becomes unfamiliar
- Treatment target: reduce precision of trauma prior OR increase precision of safety prior

## Reconsolidation Window

- Discovery: retrieved memories enter labile state - can be updated before re-consolidation
- Window: approximately 6 hours post-retrieval - protein synthesis dependent
- Therapeutic potential: retrieve trauma memory then introduce new information during window
- EMDR: may work via reconsolidation - bilateral stimulation during trauma memory retrieval
- Propranolol: beta blocker during reconsolidation window weakens emotional memory
- MDMA-assisted: MDMA reduces amygdala reactivity during retrieval - safer reconsolidation
- Bayesian: reconsolidation is a forced precision reduction - prior briefly made updatable

## Somatic Trauma Storage

- Van der Kolk: traumatic memory stored in body - not only in cortical narrative
- Interoceptive prediction: body carries threat signal as interoceptive state
- Tonic immobility: freeze response stored as muscular and autonomic pattern
- Myofascial holding: chronic tension patterns as somatic trauma encoding
- Polyvagal: dorsal vagal shutdown in severe trauma - embodied collapse response
- Somatic therapies: SE, TRE, body-oriented EMDR target interoceptive prediction errors
- MCF link: high chronic A(t) in specific body regions as somatic trauma marker
