# Bayesian Brain and Active Inference

## Abstract

The Bayesian brain hypothesis proposes that the brain is fundamentally a prediction machine — a hierarchical generative model that continuously infers the hidden causes of sensory data by minimizing prediction error between top-down predictions and bottom-up sensory signals. Karl Friston's free energy principle extends this into a unified theory of biological self-organization: all living systems resist entropy by minimizing variational free energy — a mathematical bound on surprise. Active inference — the action-oriented extension — proposes that action and perception are unified under the same free energy minimization imperative: we act not just to gather information but to fulfill predictions, confirming our model of the world through embodied engagement. Predictive processing provides a powerful framework for understanding psychiatric disorders as alterations in the precision weighting of predictions and prediction errors: schizophrenia as aberrantly high precision on prediction errors, depression as excessively strong negative priors, anxiety as inflated precision on threat predictions. The Markov blanket formalism provides the mathematical boundary that separates self from world — and its perturbation may underlie the boundary dissolution experiences of meditation, psychedelics, and mystical states. In TCM, the Bayesian brain maps to the Shen's function as the arbiter of all incoming experience — the Heart-Mind that interprets, responds to, and acts upon the world through a continuously updated model.

## Predictive Processing Architecture

- Core claim: the brain generates predictions about sensory input - compares to actual input
- Prediction error: discrepancy between prediction and input - the primary signal that propagates
- Hierarchy: cortical hierarchy - each level predicts the level below - errors propagate upward
- Top-down: predictions flow downward from higher to lower areas
- Bottom-up: prediction errors flow upward - only the unexpected propagates
- Minimization: brain continuously updates model to minimize prediction error
- Efficiency: only errors propagated - not raw sensory data - massive compression

## Free Energy Principle

- Variational free energy: F = E_q[log q(s) - log p(s,o)] - upper bound on surprise
- Surprise: -log p(o) - how unexpected the sensory observation is under the generative model
- Minimizing F: equivalent to maximizing model evidence - making the world more predictable
- Two ways: update beliefs (perception) or change sensory input (action) - both minimize F
- Biological imperative: living systems must resist entropy - equivalent to minimizing surprise
- Unification: perception, action, learning, attention all special cases of free energy minimization
- Allostasis: interoceptive free energy minimization - maintaining physiological homeostasis

## Precision Weighting

- Precision: inverse variance - confidence in a prediction or prediction error signal
- Precision weighting: determines relative influence of top-down vs bottom-up signals
- High precision top-down: strong prior - prediction dominates - hallucinatory tendency
- High precision bottom-up: strong sensory signal - prediction error dominates - anxiety
- Attention: precision allocation to specific prediction error channels - top-down attention
- Dopamine: proposed to encode precision of prediction errors in basal ganglia
- Acetylcholine: proposed to encode precision of sensory prediction errors in cortex

## Psychiatric Disorders as Precision Disturbances

- Schizophrenia: aberrant precision of prediction errors - random stimuli gain salience
- Depression: high precision on negative priors - world seen through overly certain negative lens
- Anxiety: inflated precision on threat prediction errors - danger signal amplified
- Autism: reduced precision on top-down predictions - overwhelming sensory world
- OCD: excessive precision on interoceptive prediction errors - persistent sense of wrongness
- Addiction: hijacked precision on reward prediction - drug cue predictions dominate
- PTSD: trauma creates overprecise threat prior - prediction error fails to update model

## Active Inference

- Extension: action as free energy minimization - acting to confirm predictions
- Expected free energy: G - guides action selection - minimize expected future surprise
- Epistemic foraging: seeking information to reduce uncertainty - curiosity as active inference
- Pragmatic action: acting to achieve preferred outcomes - goal-directed behavior
- Markov blanket: statistical boundary separating internal from external states
- Selfhood: self as inference - the model that minimizes free energy over time
- Dissolution: meditation and psychedelics may relax Markov blanket boundaries

## Interoceptive Predictive Coding

- Interoception: prediction of internal body states - not just external sensory input
- Allostatic model: brain predicts metabolic needs and issues motor commands to meet them
- Emotion: interoceptive prediction error felt as emotion - surprise from body state
- Depression as interoceptive: depressive symptoms as distorted interoceptive predictions
- HRV: high HRV indicates flexible interoceptive prediction - good vagal top-down control
- Guardian Interface: telemetry channels directly measure interoceptive prediction accuracy
- TCM: Shen governing Heart - Heart as primary interoceptive organ - Shen as predictive model
