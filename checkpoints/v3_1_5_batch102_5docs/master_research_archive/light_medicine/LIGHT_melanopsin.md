# Melanopsin and ipRGCs

## Abstract

The discovery of intrinsically photosensitive retinal ganglion cells ipRGCs expressing the photopigment melanopsin by David Berson in 2002 resolved a century-long mystery about how blind individuals and rodents without functional rods and cones could still entrain their circadian rhythms to the light-dark cycle, establishing a third class of ocular photoreceptors dedicated to non-visual phototransduction functions including circadian entrainment, the pupillary light reflex, and the acute suppression of melatonin. Melanopsin — the opsin encoded by the OPN4 gene, belonging to the rhabdomeric opsin family found in invertebrate photoreceptors rather than the ciliary opsin family of vertebrate rods and cones — confers intrinsic photosensitivity on ipRGCs through a Gq-coupled phototransduction cascade, with peak spectral sensitivity at approximately 480 nm in the blue region of the visible spectrum, corresponding to the peak wavelength of the clear dawn sky and the most effective wavelength for circadian phase shifting and acute melatonin suppression. ipRGCs are a heterogeneous population comprising at least five subtypes M1 through M5 with different morphologies, projection targets, and physiological properties: M1 cells project to the SCN for circadian entrainment and to the olivary pretectal nucleus for the pupillary light reflex, M2-M4 cells project to the dorsal lateral geniculate nucleus and superior colliculus influencing visual processing, and all subtypes also receive rod and cone input enabling the integration of classical and non-visual phototransduction. The sustained nature of melanopsin phototransduction — with ipRGCs continuing to respond to sustained light long after rods and cones have adapted — explains why prolonged light exposure is more effective for circadian entrainment and melatonin suppression than brief flashes, and why the light history of the preceding hours influences the magnitude of the acute melatonin suppression response. The Ming Men gate of vitality light correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding of the Ming Men fire as the root Yang that is kindled by the first light of dawn — the moment of Yang activation corresponding to the activation of the melanopsin-ipRGC-SCN pathway by morning light.

## Melanopsin Photopigment

- OPN4 gene: rhabdomeric opsin family — Gq-coupled phototransduction.
- Peak sensitivity: approximately 480 nm blue-cyan wavelength.
- Intrinsic photosensitivity: ipRGCs respond to light without rod and cone input.
- Bistable photopigment: may be reisomerized by long wavelength light without retinal regeneration.

## ipRGC Subtypes

- M1: most intrinsically photosensitive — projects to SCN and OPN for entrainment and PLR.
- M2-M4: less intrinsically photosensitive — project to dLGN and SC for image-forming visual influence.
- M5: small field cells with high spatial acuity — role in color vision and spatial visual processing.
- All subtypes receive rod and cone input — integrate classical and non-visual phototransduction.

## Non-Visual Phototransduction Functions

- Circadian entrainment: M1 ipRGCs to SCN via RHT.
- Pupillary light reflex: M1 ipRGCs to olivary pretectal nucleus.
- Acute melatonin suppression: light at 480 nm most potent — blue-enriched light at night disrupts sleep.
- Mood regulation: ipRGC projections to perihabenular nucleus influence mood and depression.
