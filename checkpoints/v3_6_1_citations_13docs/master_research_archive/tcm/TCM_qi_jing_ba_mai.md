# Qi Jing Ba Mai Kao

## Abstract

The Qi Jing Ba Mai Kao — Investigations into the Eight Extraordinary Vessels — written by Li Shi Zhen in 1578, is the definitive classical text on the eight extraordinary vessels Qi Jing Ba Mai, synthesizing all prior references in the Nei Jing, Nan Jing, and clinical literature into a comprehensive systematic treatment of these deepest channels of the body. Li Shi Zhen describes the eight extraordinary vessels as the reservoirs of constitutional Qi and Blood that regulate the overflow and deficit of the twelve primary channels — when the primary channels are full the extraordinary vessels receive the excess, and when the primary channels are depleted the extraordinary vessels supply the deficiency. The Du Mai Governing Vessel is the Sea of Yang — it governs all Yang channels and carries the constitutional Yang up the spine to nourish the brain and marrow. The Ren Mai Conception Vessel is the Sea of Yin — it governs all Yin channels and carries the constitutional Yin nourishing the anterior body and reproductive system. The Chong Mai Penetrating Vessel is the Sea of Blood and the Sea of the Twelve Channels — it is the deepest reservoir of constitutional Essence and Blood, the root of all channel circulation, and the primary vessel through which the constitutional Jing is mobilized for reproduction and growth. The Dai Mai Girdle Vessel encircles the waist binding all vertical channels — it regulates the relationship between the upper and lower body and governs the lateral aspects of the torso. The four Qiao and Wei vessels govern the bilateral symmetry of movement, the coordination between Yin and Yang aspects of the body, and the Wei defensive layer. Li Shi Zhen's synthesis established the extraordinary vessel theory used in clinical practice to this day.

## Eight Vessels

- Du Mai: Sea of Yang — posterior midline ascending spine to brain, governs all Yang channels.
- Ren Mai: Sea of Yin — anterior midline governing all Yin channels and reproductive system.
- Chong Mai: Sea of Blood — deepest constitutional reservoir, root of all channel Qi and Blood.
- Dai Mai: Girdle Vessel — horizontal encirclement binding all vertical channels at the waist.
- Yin Qiao and Yang Qiao: Heel vessels governing bilateral symmetry of movement and sleep-wake cycle.
- Yin Wei and Yang Wei: Linking vessels maintaining cohesion of Yin and Yang aspects across the body.

## Constitutional Reservoir Function

- Extraordinary vessels receive excess from primary channels preventing pathological accumulation.
- Extraordinary vessels supply deficiency to primary channels preventing collapse of circulation.
- This reservoir function corresponds to the constitutional buffer that maintains homeostasis under extreme conditions.
- Deep constitutional treatment works through the extraordinary vessels rather than the primary channels.

## Opening Points

- Each extraordinary vessel has a master opening point on a primary channel that activates the vessel.
- Chong Mai opens through SP-4 Gongsun paired with PC-6 Neiguan for the Heart-chest-stomach axis.
- Du Mai opens through SI-3 Houxi paired with BL-62 Shenmai for the spine-brain-occiput axis.
- Ren Mai opens through LU-7 Lieque paired with KD-6 Zhaohai for the throat-chest-reproductive axis.
