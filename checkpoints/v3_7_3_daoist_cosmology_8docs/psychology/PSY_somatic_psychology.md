# Somatic Psychology

## Abstract

Somatic psychology encompasses the body-centered psychotherapy traditions that work directly with bodily sensation, movement, posture, and physiological state as the primary medium of therapeutic change — in contrast to talk therapies that work primarily through verbal cognitive processing. The foundational insight of somatic psychology is that psychological distress is held in the body — in chronic muscular tension, altered breathing, disrupted posture, and the somatic symptoms that represent the body's attempt to manage unprocessed psychological material. Bottom-up processing is the somatic psychology concept for therapeutic approaches that work from body sensation upward to emotion, meaning, and insight — in contrast to top-down approaches that work from cognitive insight down to emotional and somatic change. The bottom-up approach is particularly important for trauma because traumatic memory is stored in subcortical implicit memory systems that are not accessible through verbal cognitive processing alone. Pat Ogden's Sensorimotor Psychotherapy integrates somatic processing techniques with attachment theory and cognitive approaches — tracking the client's body sensations, impulses, and movement patterns as direct expressions of the nervous system state and working with these somatic resources to complete interrupted defensive responses and develop new somatic patterns of regulation. Ron Kurtz's Hakomi method uses mindfulness and gentle touch to access the body's habitual patterns of holding — proposing that every person has a characteristic body organization that expresses their core beliefs, defenses, and relational patterns. Hakomi works by evoking these patterns in the therapeutic relationship and creating experiments that allow the client to experience new possibilities beyond their habitual organization. The TCM psychosomatic integration framework — where psychological patterns are simultaneously Qi and Blood patterns and emotional clearing is Qi clearing — corresponds to the somatic psychology understanding that psychological and somatic are not separate domains but two perspectives on the same living process.

## Bottom-Up Processing

- Bottom-up: body sensation to emotion to meaning — working with the nervous system in its own language.
- Top-down: cognitive insight to emotional shift to somatic change — the talk therapy sequence.
- Trauma stored in subcortical implicit memory requires bottom-up access — talking about it is insufficient.
- Combining bottom-up somatic work with top-down meaning-making produces the most complete resolution.

## Sensorimotor Psychotherapy

- Tracks body sensation, posture, gesture, and movement as primary therapeutic data.
- Identifies incomplete defensive responses — the blocked run, the unexpressed push-away — and completes them.
- Develops somatic resources — grounding postures, self-regulation movements — that build capacity.
- Integrates somatic processing with attachment repair and narrative meaning-making.

## Hakomi

- Mindful body awareness allows the practitioner to observe the body's habitual patterns without interference.
- Gentle touch experiments evoke the characteristic body organization revealing core organizing beliefs.
- The therapeutic relationship provides the corrective attachment experience within which change becomes possible.
- Working at the edge of the window of tolerance — neither overwhelmed nor shut down — enables integration.
