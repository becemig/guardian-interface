# Jungian Psychology

## Abstract

Analytical psychology developed by Carl Gustav Jung is a depth psychological framework centered on the individuation process — the lifelong development toward psychological wholeness through the progressive integration of unconscious contents into conscious awareness. Jung's map of the psyche comprises the ego — the center of conscious awareness, the personal unconscious containing repressed and forgotten personal material organized into complexes, and the collective unconscious containing the universal inherited structures of the human psyche — archetypes. Archetypes are universal inherited patterns of psychological organization — the Shadow containing the repressed and unlived aspects of the personality, the Anima and Animus as the contrasexual aspects of the psyche, the Self as the archetype of wholeness and the center of the total personality, and many others including the Wise Old Man, Great Mother, and Trickster. Active imagination is Jung's primary therapeutic technique — consciously engaging with unconscious figures and images through dialogue, drawing, and movement. Synchronicity describes meaningful coincidences that cannot be explained by causality — the acausal connecting principle underlying the relationship between psyche and matter. The Shadow is the most practically significant archetype — projection of the Shadow onto others is the primary source of interpersonal conflict and collective violence. Hun-Po spirit dynamics in TCM — the Liver Hun as the projective creative soul and the Lung Po as the grounding somatic soul — correspond to the Jungian anima-animus dynamic as the primary spirit polarity of the Chinese psychological system.

## Archetypes and Collective Unconscious

- Archetypes are universal inherited patterns appearing in myths, dreams, and religious imagery across cultures.
- The Shadow contains all that the ego has rejected — both negative qualities and unlived positive potential.
- The Anima in men and Animus in women represent the contrasexual principle that must be integrated for wholeness.
- The Self is the archetype of totality — it transcends and encompasses the ego as the ocean transcends a wave.

## Individuation

- Individuation is the lifelong process of becoming more fully oneself through integration of unconscious contents.
- It requires confronting and integrating the Shadow rather than projecting it onto others.
- The mid-life transition is typically the point where the individuation process becomes the primary psychological task.
- Individuation produces increased tolerance for ambiguity, reduced projection, and deepened authentic expression.

## Active Imagination

- Active imagination involves consciously engaging with unconscious figures through sustained dialogue.
- The ego maintains its position while allowing unconscious figures to speak and move autonomously.
- This technique produces direct encounter with complexes and archetypes enabling their conscious integration.
- Dreams, paintings, movement, and writing are all valid media for active imagination practice.

## TCM Correspondence

- Hun-Po dynamics as the spirit polarity of projective Hun and grounding Po corresponds to the Jungian Anima-Animus polarity.
- Liver Hun as the projective creative soul corresponds to the Jungian creative imagination and anima projection.
- Lung Po as the somatic grounding soul corresponds to the Jungian body and instinctual ground of the psyche.
- The Shadow as the unlived dimension of the psyche corresponds to the dark face of the Liver Hun in TCM.
