# Complement System and Innate Defense

## Abstract

Complement system and innate defense — the ancient proteolytic cascade of over 50 plasma and membrane proteins constituting the complement system that provides immediate non-specific defense against pathogens through three activation pathways converging on the formation of C3 convertase, with the effector functions of complement including opsonization of pathogens for phagocytosis through C3b deposition, direct killing of gram-negative bacteria through membrane attack complex formation, recruitment of phagocytes and mast cells through the anaphylatoxins C3a and C5a, clearance of immune complexes and apoptotic cells, and modulation of adaptive immune responses through complement receptor signaling on B cells and dendritic cells. Three activation pathways — the classical pathway activated by antibody-antigen complexes binding C1q initiating the classical C3 convertase C4b2a, the lectin pathway activated by mannose-binding lectin and ficolins recognizing carbohydrate patterns on pathogen surfaces initiating the same C4b2a convertase, and the alternative pathway constitutively active at low levels with spontaneous C3 hydrolysis amplified dramatically on pathogen surfaces lacking the complement regulatory proteins that protect host cells — together ensuring that virtually any pathogen surface activates complement within seconds of exposure. Membrane attack complex — the terminal complement complex C5b-6789 assembled on pathogen membranes following C5 convertase cleavage of C5 to C5b, with the sequential addition of C6, C7, C8, and multiple C9 molecules forming the transmembrane pore that disrupts membrane integrity and directly kills gram-negative bacteria and enveloped viruses — a purely innate immune killing mechanism requiring no prior sensitization. Complement and Alzheimer's disease — the disturbing discovery that complement proteins including C1q and C3 tag synapses for elimination by microglia in the normal brain, with this complement-mediated synaptic pruning being excessive in Alzheimer's disease and other neurodegenerative conditions, with C1q deposition increasing dramatically with aging and with inhibition of complement-mediated synaptic pruning protecting against cognitive decline in mouse models. The Defensive Qi complement cascade ancient defense correspondence in TCM describes the classical Wei Qi as a fast-acting ancient defensive system providing immediate protection without the need for recognition and learning — corresponding to the complement system's ancient phylogenetically conserved rapid non-specific defense mechanism operating through spontaneous activation on pathogen surfaces.

## Three Pathways

- Classical: C1q binds antibody-antigen — adaptive immune amplifier.
- Lectin: MBL ficolins bind pathogen carbohydrates — innate pattern recognition.
- Alternative: spontaneous C3 hydrolysis — amplified on pathogen surfaces.
- Convergence: all three produce C3 convertase — C3b deposition on targets.

## Effector Functions

- Opsonization: C3b coating — CR1 receptor phagocytosis enhancement.
- MAC: C5b-6789 pore — direct gram-negative bacteria killing.
- Anaphylatoxins: C3a C5a — mast cell activation neutrophil recruitment.
- Immune complex: C1q clearance of immune complexes — prevents autoimmunity.

## Complement Dysregulation

- PNH: paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria — complement attacks own RBCs.
- Age-related macular degeneration: complement dysregulation in retina.
- Alzheimer: C1q C3 tagging synapses for microglial pruning — excess in AD.
- Treatment: eculizumab anti-C5 — approved for PNH and aHUS.
