# ECS Clinical Applications

## Abstract

Clinical endocannabinoid deficiency (CED) is a proposed syndrome wherein chronic underactivity of the ECS underlies treatment-resistant conditions including migraine, fibromyalgia, and irritable bowel syndrome. Beyond CED, the ECS is an established therapeutic target across pain, epilepsy, nausea and vomiting, anxiety, PTSD, sleep disorders, and neurodegeneration. FDA-approved cannabinoid medications include Epidiolex (CBD) for Dravet and Lennox-Gastaut syndromes, Sativex (THC:CBD 1:1) for MS spasticity, and dronabinol (synthetic THC) for chemotherapy-induced nausea and AIDS-related anorexia. The clinical evidence base supports CB1 and CB2 as high-value therapeutic targets with a broad safety window compared to opioids and benzodiazepines.

## Pain

- Mechanism: CB1 at PAG, spinal dorsal horn, and peripheral nociceptors reduces pain signal transmission
- CB2 on immune cells reduces peripheral sensitization and neuroinflammation
- Neuropathic pain: strongest clinical evidence - THC:CBD combination reduces central sensitization
- Fibromyalgia: CED hypothesis - low AEA correlates with pain amplification
- Migraine: ECS modulates trigeminovascular system - FAAH inhibition reduces migraine frequency in models
- Opioid sparing: ECS activation reduces opioid requirements by 30-50 percent in chronic pain

## Epilepsy

- Epidiolex (CBD): FDA approved 2018 for Dravet syndrome and Lennox-Gastaut syndrome
- Mechanism: GPR55 antagonism, sodium channel modulation, adenosine reuptake inhibition
- Dravet trial: 39 percent reduction in convulsive seizures vs 13 percent placebo
- ECS endogenous role: DSI provides seizure-terminating inhibitory tone at CB1
- Status epilepticus depletes local 2-AG - replacement is a therapeutic strategy

## Anxiety and PTSD

- Amygdala CB1: AEA-mediated fear extinction is the core mechanism
- CBD clinical evidence: reduces anxiety in public speaking, SAD, PTSD nightmares
- THC biphasic: low dose anxiolytic, high dose anxiogenic - dose precision required
- PTSD: deficient AEA at amygdala CB1 prevents extinction - FAAH inhibition is therapeutic target
- Nightmare suppression: nabilone (synthetic THC) reduces PTSD nightmare frequency

## Nausea and Appetite

- Dronabinol: FDA approved for chemotherapy-induced nausea and AIDS anorexia
- CB1 in dorsal vagal complex suppresses emetic reflex
- Appetite: hypothalamic CB1 increases hedonic feeding and ghrelin sensitivity
- Cachexia: THC arrests weight loss in cancer and HIV patients

## Sleep

- CB1 in hypothalamus and brainstem modulates sleep architecture
- THC reduces REM sleep, increases slow-wave sleep - useful for PTSD nightmares
- CBD at higher doses promotes sleep via 5-HT1A and adenosine mechanisms
- Yinqiao-Yangqiao TCM parallel: ECS governs sleep-wake balance analogous to yin-yang motility vessel cycle

## Neurodegeneration

- Alzheimer: CB2 activation reduces amyloid-beta-induced neuroinflammation and tau pathology
- Parkinson: CB1 in basal ganglia modulates dopamine-motor circuit dysregulation
- Multiple sclerosis: Sativex approved for spasticity - CB1 reduces motor neuron hyperexcitability
- TBI: 2-AG rises acutely after injury as neuroprotective response - exogenous support is therapeutic

## Clinical Endocannabinoid Deficiency (CED)

- Proposed by Russo 2004: chronic ECS hypofunction underlies treatment-resistant syndromes
- Conditions: migraine, fibromyalgia, IBS, cyclic vomiting, interstitial cystitis
- Evidence: low CSF AEA in migraine patients, reduced CB1 density in fibromyalgia
- Treatment logic: restore ECS tone via FAAH inhibition, low-dose cannabinoids, lifestyle
- Lifestyle ECS support: exercise (runner high is ECS-mediated), stress reduction, sleep
