# Myokines and Exercise Endocrinology

## Abstract

Myokines — the cytokines, peptides, and other signaling molecules produced and secreted by contracting skeletal muscle — constitute an endocrine organ system that Bente Pedersen identified as the muscle secretome, fundamentally transforming the understanding of skeletal muscle from a passive mechanical effector to an active endocrine organ that communicates with virtually every other organ in the body through exercise-induced secretion of systemic regulatory signals. The discovery that contracting muscle is the primary source of circulating IL-6 during exercise — with plasma IL-6 rising 100-fold during prolonged endurance exercise — established the concept of IL-6 as an exercise hormone with pleiotropic anti-inflammatory and metabolic effects: muscle-derived exercise IL-6 stimulates hepatic glucose production, promotes fat oxidation in adipose tissue, stimulates anti-inflammatory IL-10 and IL-1ra production, and inhibits the pro-inflammatory TNF-alpha and IL-1beta produced by macrophages, providing the mechanistic basis for the anti-inflammatory effects of regular exercise. The irisin-BDNF axis — the muscle-brain communication pathway activated by aerobic exercise — involves the cleavage of the muscle membrane protein FNDC5 to produce irisin, which crosses the blood-brain barrier and stimulates hippocampal BDNF production, neurogenesis, and synaptic plasticity, providing the molecular mechanism for the well-documented effects of exercise on cognitive function, depression, and neurodegeneration prevention. Muscle-brain crosstalk through myokines including irisin, BDNF, cathepsin B, and lactate explains the acute cognitive enhancement following exercise, the antidepressant effects of regular exercise comparable to medication, and the neuroprotective effects of lifelong physical activity against Alzheimer's disease. Myokine anti-cancer effects — the reduction in cancer risk and improved cancer outcomes associated with regular physical activity — are mediated in part through IL-6-driven NK cell mobilization, irisin-induced cancer cell apoptosis, and the myokine-mediated reduction in the inflammatory and metabolic environment that promotes tumor growth. The movement Qi cultivation systemic correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that appropriate physical movement cultivates and circulates Qi through the entire organ network system — a principle now supported by the myokine evidence that contracting muscle sends systemic signals that maintain the health of every organ.

## IL-6 as Exercise Hormone

- Contracting muscle produces IL-6 proportional to exercise duration and intensity.
- Plasma IL-6 rises 100-fold during prolonged endurance exercise.
- Anti-inflammatory: stimulates IL-10 and IL-1ra production, inhibits TNF-alpha.
- Metabolic: stimulates hepatic glucose production and fat oxidation during exercise.

## Irisin-BDNF Axis

- Exercise activates PGC1-alpha in muscle driving FNDC5 expression.
- FNDC5 cleaved to irisin — crosses blood-brain barrier.
- Irisin stimulates hippocampal BDNF production and neurogenesis.
- Explains exercise-induced cognitive enhancement and antidepressant effects.

## Muscle-Brain Crosstalk

- Cathepsin B: muscle-derived protease that crosses BBB and enhances memory consolidation.
- Lactate: exercise-derived brain fuel that stimulates BDNF and VEGF in hippocampus.
- Exercise neurogenesis: hippocampal neurogenesis driven by BDNF irisin and IGF-1.
- 30 minutes aerobic exercise: acute improvement in memory, attention, and executive function.
