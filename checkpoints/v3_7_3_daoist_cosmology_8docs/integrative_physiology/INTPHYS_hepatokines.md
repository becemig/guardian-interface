# Hepatokines and Liver Systemic Signaling

## Abstract

The liver — the body's primary metabolic organ processing virtually all absorbed nutrients, producing most circulating proteins, and performing central detoxification and biotransformation functions — is also a major endocrine organ secreting dozens of hepatokines that regulate the metabolism, growth, and function of virtually every other organ, corresponding to the TCM Liver's role in coursing and draining the free circulation of Qi and Blood through the entire organ network. FGF21 — fibroblast growth factor 21, the most extensively studied hepatokine — is produced by the liver in response to fasting, ketogenic diet, and protein restriction and acts as a systemic metabolic regulator promoting fat oxidation in adipose tissue and muscle, stimulating ketogenesis, reducing lipogenesis, improving insulin sensitivity, and signaling the brain's hypothalamus to reduce carbohydrate preference and increase thermogenesis, making it a master metabolic switch coordinating the organism's response to nutritional stress. ANGPTL proteins — angiopoietin-like proteins secreted by the liver — regulate lipoprotein lipase activity and thereby control the distribution of circulating lipids between tissues, with ANGPTL3 ANGPTL4 and ANGPTL8 forming a nutrient-sensing system that directs lipids to adipose tissue in the fed state and to oxidative tissues in the fasted state. Fetuin-A — a liver-secreted glycoprotein that inhibits insulin receptor signaling by acting as an endogenous ligand of toll-like receptor 4 — is elevated in fatty liver disease and obesity and represents a direct hepatokine mechanism linking hepatic steatosis to peripheral insulin resistance, with fetuin-A elevation producing systemic insulin resistance independent of circulating free fatty acids. The liver-brain glucose axis — the hepatic portal glucose sensing system in which portal vein glucose sensors transmit nutrient information to the brainstem and hypothalamus through the hepatic vagal afferents, governing meal size, appetite, and the cephalic phase of insulin release — demonstrates that the liver is a sensory organ communicating nutritional state to the brain in addition to its executive metabolic functions. The Liver coursing and draining free circulation correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that the Liver governs the smooth and free circulation of Qi and Blood throughout the organ network — a systemic regulatory function that corresponds to the hepatokine network governing the systemic distribution and utilization of metabolic substrates.

## FGF21 Metabolic Regulation

- Produced by liver in response to fasting ketogenic diet and protein restriction.
- Acts on adipose tissue: promotes fat oxidation and ketone production.
- Acts on brain hypothalamus: reduces carbohydrate preference increases thermogenesis.
- Acts on muscle: promotes fatty acid oxidation and glucose uptake.

## ANGPTL Lipid Distribution

- ANGPTL3: inhibits LPL in oxidative tissues — channels lipids to adipose in fed state.
- ANGPTL4: activated in fasting — releases LPL inhibition in muscle for fat oxidation.
- ANGPTL8: fed-state signal — restores adipose LPL activity for lipid storage.
- Genetic ANGPTL3 loss of function: dramatically reduced cardiovascular risk — therapeutic target.

## Liver-Brain Glucose Axis

- Portal vein glucose sensors: detect absorbed glucose concentration after meals.
- Hepatic vagal afferents: transmit portal glucose signal to NTS and hypothalamus.
- Governs meal size through hepatic satiety signal independent of systemic glucose.
- Cephalic phase insulin release: brain-liver axis releases insulin before glucose absorption.
