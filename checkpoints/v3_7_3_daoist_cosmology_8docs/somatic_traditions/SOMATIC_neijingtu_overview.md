# Nei Jing Tu Overview

## Abstract

The Nei Jing Tu Inner Scripture Diagram is one of the most remarkable and visually stunning documents in the history of human spirituality — a detailed diagram of the human body depicted as an inner landscape populated with mountains, rivers, fields, gates, celestial bodies, and divine inhabitants that together constitute a complete map of Taoist internal alchemy practice. The diagram depicts the body in profile showing the spinal column as the central mountain range, the head as the upper mountain peaks of the Kun Lun range, the abdomen as the lower fields and rivers, and the entire body as a self-contained cosmos through which the practitioner navigates in meditation. The three Dan Tian are the primary organizing centers of the inner landscape: the lower Dan Tian at the lower abdomen is the cauldron where Jing essence is stored and refined; the middle Dan Tian at the heart center is where refined Qi is gathered and circulated; the upper Dan Tian at the brain-third eye is where refined Shen spirit is cultivated and ultimately liberated. The governing and conception vessels running up the back and down the front are the primary pathways of the inner landscape — the mountain path ascending and the river descending. The Nei Jing Tu was preserved at the Baiyun Guan White Cloud Temple in Beijing and represents the synthesis of centuries of Taoist inner landscape visualization practice. Internal alchemy Neidan uses the Nei Jing Tu map as the foundation for systematic meditation practice — the practitioner visualizes and inhabits the inner landscape, cultivating the refinement of Jing to Qi to Shen through the three Dan Tian sequence.

## Three Dan Tian

- Lower Dan Tian at CV-4 to CV-7 is the cauldron of Jing — constitutional essence stored and refined.
- Middle Dan Tian at the heart center CV-17 is the field of Qi — refined energy gathered and circulated.
- Upper Dan Tian at the third eye between the eyebrows is the palace of Shen — spirit cultivated and clarified.
- The three Dan Tian cultivation sequence — Jing to Qi to Shen — is the fundamental Neidan alchemical path.

## Inner Landscape Features

- The spinal column is depicted as the Kun Lun mountain range — the central axis of the inner cosmos.
- The head contains the nine palaces of the brain — each a specific cultivation center of upper Dan Tian work.
- The heart field contains the paired divine governors — the red child and the jade maiden of the middle Dan Tian.
- The lower abdomen contains the ocean of Qi, the gate of life Mingmen, and the cauldron of refinement.

## Microcosmic Correspondence

- The body mirrors the cosmos — the inner landscape contains the same forces as the outer universe.
- The Milky Way flows through the body as the Governing Vessel ascending the spine.
- The sun and moon govern the eyes — right eye sun, left eye moon in some traditions.
- The inner cosmos is self-sufficient — complete cultivation is possible entirely within the body.

## TCM Correspondence

- Three Dan Tian correspond precisely to the TCM lower-middle-upper energetic centers governing Jing-Qi-Shen.
- Governing Vessel as central mountain axis corresponds to the Du Mai as the primary Yang cultivation channel.
- Mingmen Gate of Life in the lower landscape corresponds to the TCM Mingmen between the kidneys.
- The nine brain palaces of the upper Dan Tian correspond to the TCM brain as the sea of marrow.
