# Push Hands Tui Shou Science

## Abstract

Push hands Tui Shou science — the investigation of the two-person practice of Tai Chi push hands in which partners maintain continuous contact through the wrists and forearms while practicing the application of the four primary energies of Peng Lu Ji An in a flowing interactive format, developing listening force sensitivity through continuous tactile feedback, proprioceptive responsiveness, and the neuroscience of interpersonal motor coordination through which two nervous systems synchronize through touch — representing both a martial training methodology and a profound somatic practice for developing sensitivity, responsiveness, and interpersonal attunement. Listening force Ting Jin neuroscience — the highly trained tactile and proprioceptive sensitivity developed through years of push hands practice that enables the practitioner to detect through the point of contact the direction, magnitude, and intention of the partner's force before it becomes visible in their external movement, processed through the enhanced mechanoreceptive and proprioceptive pathways whose plasticity through training has been demonstrated in tactile discrimination studies of expert practitioners. Yielding and neutralizing biomechanics — the fundamental push hands skill of receiving incoming force without resistance by aligning the body's structure to redirect rather than oppose the force vector, using rotation around a central axis and weight shifting to change the angle of contact and nullify the incoming force's mechanical advantage, requiring the precise timing and spatial awareness developed through thousands of hours of partner practice. Social neuroscience of partner practice — the interpersonal neurobiology of sustained physical contact and coordinated movement, with the mirror neuron system continuously modeling the partner's movement intentions, the oxytocin system mediating the social bonding of collaborative practice, and the autonomic systems of two practitioners co-regulating through the physical and social feedback of continuous contact. The Two Become One relational Qi correspondence in Tai Chi philosophy describes the classical teaching that in advanced push hands practice the boundary between self and partner dissolves and the two practitioners move as a single system — a somatic experience of interpersonal unity corresponding to the neuroscience of interpersonal synchrony through which two nervous systems become functionally coupled through sustained physical contact and coordinated movement.

## Ting Jin Development

- Tactile threshold: expert Tai Chi practitioners show enhanced tactile discrimination.
- Force direction: detection of force vector through minimal contact point.
- Anticipation: Ting Jin enables response before force becomes overt movement.
- Training mechanism: thousands of hours of contact feedback trains mechanoreceptive pathways.

## Yielding Biomechanics

- Rotation: receiving force through rotation changes angle eliminates mechanical advantage.
- Weight shifting: transferring weight changes the structural geometry of contact.
- Song requirement: tension prevents yielding — relaxation enables structural redirection.
- Timing: yielding timed to force peak — too early or late loses neutralization.

- Mirror neurons: continuous modeling of partner movement intentions.
- Oxytocin: physical contact and collaborative practice — social bonding.
- Co-regulation: autonomic co-regulation through sustained physical social contact.
