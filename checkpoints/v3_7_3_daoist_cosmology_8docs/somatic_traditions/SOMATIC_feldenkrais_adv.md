# Feldenkrais Advanced

## Abstract

Feldenkrais Method advanced practice develops the foundational ATM and FI principles into sophisticated neuroplastic learning sequences that systematically reorganize the self-image — the internal representation of the body's structure, movement possibilities, and habitual patterns — through slow, gentle, differentiated movement exploration. Moshe Feldenkrais proposed that the self-image is the primary determinant of movement quality — not muscle strength or flexibility but the neural map of oneself in action — and that improving the self-image through learning produces generalized improvements in all movement and function. Parasitic effort — muscular activity not contributing to the intended action — is the primary obstacle to efficient movement. ATM lessons systematically explore movement variations that reduce parasitic effort and clarify the skeletal support available for each action. Functional Integration is the hands-on version of this learning — the practitioner communicates directly through touch how the student's skeleton can better support and transmit movement forces. The Feldenkrais principle of using the skeleton rather than the muscles means organizing movement so that bone-to-bone transmission carries the load rather than muscular effort. Differentiation — the development of independent movement between adjacent body parts that habitually move together — is a core learning goal enabling more refined coordination. The parasympathetic learning state — calm, curious, and exploratory — is the neurological prerequisite for the cortical plasticity that enables genuine movement learning. Spleen Yi refinement in TCM — the progressive refinement of intentional cognitive processing through careful attentive study — corresponds to the Feldenkrais learning process of refining the self-image through attentive movement exploration.

## Self-Image and Learning

- The self-image is the internal neural representation of the body's structure and movement possibilities.
- Improving the self-image through learning produces generalized improvement in movement quality and efficiency.
- Most people have significant gaps and distortions in their self-image corresponding to areas of chronic restriction.
- ATM sequences explore movement in areas of poor self-image improving neural representation and movement quality.

## Parasitic Effort

- Parasitic effort is muscular activity that is not contributing to the intended action — wasted effort.
- It arises from imprecise self-image, anxiety, and habit leading to unnecessary co-contraction.
- Reducing parasitic effort improves efficiency, reduces fatigue, and enables finer movement differentiation.
- The Feldenkrais instruction to make the movement smaller and easier targets parasitic effort reduction.

## Skeletal Support

- The skeleton transmits forces far more efficiently than muscular effort when properly aligned and used.
- Using the skeleton means organizing movement so that structural alignment carries load rather than muscle.
- Learning to rely on skeletal support reduces chronic muscular holding and enables genuine relaxation.
- Feldenkrais floor work uses gravity and the ground to provide external support revealing available skeletal function.
