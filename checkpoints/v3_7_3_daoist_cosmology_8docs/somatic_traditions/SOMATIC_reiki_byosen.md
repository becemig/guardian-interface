# Reiki Byosen Scanning

## Abstract

Byosen scanning Byosen Reikan Ho is the Reiki diagnostic technique of moving the hands slowly over the body at a distance of several inches to detect areas of Ki disturbance through the hibiki sensation — the characteristic feeling responses in the practitioner's hands that indicate underlying imbalance. Byosen means accumulated toxins or stagnant Ki — the concept that diseased, injured, or energetically disturbed areas of the body produce a field disturbance that trained practitioners can detect through hand sensitivity. Hibiki meaning reverberation or resonance is the term for the range of sensations the practitioner's hands experience over areas of byosen — ranging from subtle warmth, tingling, or pulsation at mild disturbance to heat, magnetic resistance, or numbness at more significant disturbance, to cold, pain, or repulsion at severe disturbance. The sensitivity of the practitioner's hands to these field variations is developed through regular Hatsurei Ho practice — a combination of dry brushing, breath purification Joshin Kokyu Ho, and meditation that opens and sensitizes the palm chakras and the practitioner's overall Ki sensitivity. Scanning technique in systematic Reiki practice moves from the head down the front and back of the body, pausing wherever hibiki sensations indicate disturbance, and applying extended hands-on treatment to those areas. The correspondence between byosen detection and acupuncture point sensitivity is notable — experienced Reiki practitioners often detect disturbance specifically over acupuncture points and meridian pathways, corresponding to the TCM understanding that the acupuncture point is a place of concentrated and accessible Ki.

## Hibiki Sensation Scale

- Mild hibiki: gentle warmth, tingling, or light pulsation — minor Ki disturbance or beginning of healing response.
- Moderate hibiki: stronger heat, magnetic resistance, or throbbing — significant Ki accumulation or deficiency.
- Strong hibiki: intense heat, sharp tingling, or numbness — acute or significant pathological disturbance.
- Severe hibiki: cold, pain, or repulsion at the hands — deep chronic or severe disturbance requiring extended treatment.

## Hatsurei Ho Development

- Dry brushing Kenyoku Ho clears the aura and Ki pathways preparing for practice.
- Joshin Kokyu Ho breath purification fills the tanden with Ki and activates the Ki pathways.
- Seishin Toitsu meditation unifies the mind and Ki sensitizing the practitioner to subtle field variations.
- Regular Hatsurei Ho practice progressively develops hand sensitivity and byosen detection capacity.

## Scanning and Treatment

- Systematic scanning moves from head to toe detecting areas of hibiki disturbance.
- Extended treatment time is given to areas of strong hibiki until the sensation normalizes.
- Normalization of hibiki corresponds to the resolution of Ki disturbance and return to balanced flow.
- The practitioner follows the body's response rather than applying a predetermined treatment protocol.

## TCM Correspondence

- Byosen as accumulated stagnant Ki corresponds to TCM Qi stagnation and Blood stasis patterns.
- Hibiki detection at acupuncture points corresponds to TCM point palpation finding reactive ashi points.
- Hand sensitivity to Ki field corresponds to TCM practitioner cultivation of Yi intention sensitivity.
- Treatment until hibiki normalizes corresponds to TCM treatment continuing until pulse normalization.
