# Craniosacral Therapy

## Abstract

Craniosacral therapy CST — developed from William Sutherland's discovery of cranial bone motion in the early twentieth century and systematized as a clinical therapy by John Upledger — is a gentle hands-on bodywork approach that works with the craniosacral rhythm to assess and treat restrictions in the craniosacral system consisting of the cranial bones, sacrum, dura mater, cerebrospinal fluid, and the fascial connections linking these structures throughout the body. Sutherland's primary respiratory mechanism proposes that the cranial bones undergo a subtle rhythmic motion at approximately 6-12 cycles per minute driven by the production and reabsorption of cerebrospinal fluid — a motion entirely independent of cardiovascular and pulmonary respiratory rhythms and representing a third primary body rhythm. The craniosacral rhythm can be palpated by trained practitioners anywhere in the body through the fascial connections that transmit the motion from the cranium and sacrum throughout the entire body — restrictions in fascial tension patterns alter the quality, amplitude, and symmetry of the rhythm providing diagnostic information about the location and nature of tissue dysfunction. The still point — a brief pause in the craniosacral rhythm induced by gentle compression of the occiput or sacrum — produces a profound relaxation response in the autonomic nervous system, a reduction in sympathetic tone, and a reorganization of the central nervous system that clinical practitioners report produces deep therapeutic effects for conditions ranging from headache to post-traumatic stress. Fascial unwinding is the CST technique of following the spontaneous movement impulses of the fascial system — allowing the tissue to move in the directions it seeks without imposing corrective force — producing the gradual release of long-held trauma and postural patterns stored in the fascial body. Du Mai resonance describes the CST treatment of the Governing Vessel pathway along the spine and cranium — the gentle oscillation of the Du Mai through CST techniques corresponds to Qi cultivation practices that activate the Du Mai microcosmic orbit.

## Primary Respiratory Mechanism

- Cranial bone motion at 6-12 cycles per minute independent of cardiovascular and pulmonary rhythms.
- Cerebrospinal fluid production and reabsorption drives the craniosacral rhythm.
- The rhythm transmits throughout the body via fascial connections enabling whole-body palpation.
- Restrictions alter rhythm quality, amplitude, and symmetry providing diagnostic information.

## Still Point

- Brief induced pause in the craniosacral rhythm produces profound autonomic relaxation.
- Sympathetic tone decreases and parasympathetic activity increases during and after still point.
- The nervous system reorganizes during the still point producing therapeutic reset.
- This corresponds to the TCM concept of the Shen settling and the Qi regulating during meditative stillness.

## Fascial Unwinding

- Following spontaneous fascial movement impulses without imposing corrective force.
- The tissue moves in the directions it needs to complete interrupted motion patterns from old injuries.
- Unwinding releases deeply held postural and trauma patterns without force or manipulation.
- This corresponds to the TCM principle of working with the natural direction of Qi flow rather than against it.
