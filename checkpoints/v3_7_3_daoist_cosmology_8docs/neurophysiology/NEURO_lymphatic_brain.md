# Glymphatic System and Brain Lymphatics

## Abstract

The glymphatic system discovered by Maiken Nedergaard in 2012 is a brain-wide waste clearance network that uses cerebrospinal fluid CSF flowing along periarterial spaces to flush interstitial metabolic waste through the brain parenchyma and into perivenous drainage channels. Astrocyte aquaporin-4 water channels drive the CSF-interstitial fluid exchange across the glial endfeet surrounding blood vessels. The glymphatic system is nearly 10 times more active during sleep than during wakefulness — sleep is required for efficient brain waste clearance. Amyloid-beta and tau proteins implicated in Alzheimer's disease are cleared by the glymphatic system during sleep — chronic sleep deprivation impairs their clearance and accelerates accumulation. Meningeal lymphatic vessels discovered in 2015 provide the drainage pathway from glymphatic effluent to cervical lymph nodes completing the brain lymphatic circuit. Wei Qi retreating to the interior during sleep to nourish the Yin organs and support constitutional restoration corresponds to the glymphatic clearance that is the primary biological purpose of sleep.

## Glymphatic Pathway

- CSF enters the brain along periarterial spaces driven by arterial pulsation and respiratory pressure gradients.
- Aquaporin-4 channels on astrocyte endfeet facilitate CSF exchange with interstitial fluid.
- Waste-laden interstitial fluid is cleared along perivenous spaces draining to cervical lymph nodes.
- This convective flow clears metabolic waste including amyloid-beta, tau, and other proteins.

## Sleep Dependence

- Glymphatic activity increases nearly 10-fold during slow-wave sleep compared to wakefulness.
- Sleep deprivation impairs glymphatic clearance producing next-day accumulation of metabolic waste.
- The sleeping brain expands interstitial space by approximately 60 percent facilitating convective clearance.
- Lateral sleeping position optimizes glymphatic drainage corresponding to the clinical recommendation for side sleeping.

## Alzheimer's Connection

- Amyloid-beta accumulation in Alzheimer's disease corresponds to impaired glymphatic clearance.
- Chronic sleep deprivation in midlife is a significant risk factor for later Alzheimer's disease.
- Tau spreading through the brain follows glymphatic pathways suggesting clearance failure in progression.
- Sleep interventions improving glymphatic clearance may reduce Alzheimer's risk through amyloid reduction.
