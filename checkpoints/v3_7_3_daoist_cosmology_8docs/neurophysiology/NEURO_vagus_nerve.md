# Vagus Nerve Anatomy and Function

## Abstract

The vagus nerve is the tenth cranial nerve and the primary parasympathetic outflow to thoracic and abdominal viscera. Approximately 80 percent of vagal fibers are afferent — carrying sensory information from the viscera to the brainstem — making it the primary interoceptive highway between body and brain. Vagal afferents from the heart, lungs, gut, and liver project to the nucleus tractus solitarius which relays visceral signals to the insular cortex, hypothalamus, and amygdala. The dorsal motor nucleus of the vagus provides parasympathetic efferent outflow to abdominal organs while the nucleus ambiguus provides efferent outflow to the heart and lungs. Vagal tone measured by HRV is the primary clinical index of parasympathetic health and its enhancement through practice is a key Guardian Interface telemetry target.

## Vagal Anatomy

- The vagus exits the brainstem through the jugular foramen with the glossopharyngeal and accessory nerves.
- The superior vagal ganglion contains cell bodies of somatic afferents from the ear.
- The inferior nodose ganglion contains cell bodies of visceral afferents from thorax and abdomen.
- Left and right vagal trunks differ in their distribution — right vagus primarily innervates the sinoatrial node.

## Afferent Pathways

- Cardiac afferents signal cardiac stretch, ischemia, and chemical changes through mechanoreceptors and chemoreceptors.
- Pulmonary afferents signal lung inflation through slowly adapting stretch receptors driving the Hering-Breuer reflex.
- Gut afferents signal distension, nutrient content, and inflammatory state through 80 percent of vagal fibers.
- Nucleus tractus solitarius receives all visceral afferents and projects to insular cortex, hypothalamus, and amygdala.

## Efferent Function

- Dorsal motor nucleus of vagus provides parasympathetic innervation to esophagus, stomach, intestines, and liver.
- Nucleus ambiguus provides parasympathetic innervation to heart producing vagal bradycardia and HRV.
- Vagal efferents release acetylcholine at target organs producing parasympathetic effects.
- The cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway through splenic nerve and vagal efferents suppresses systemic inflammation.
