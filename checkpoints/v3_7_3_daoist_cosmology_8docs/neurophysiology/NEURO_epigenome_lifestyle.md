# Epigenome and Lifestyle

## Abstract

The epigenome comprises the chemical modifications to DNA and histone proteins that regulate gene expression without altering the DNA sequence. DNA methylation at CpG sites typically silences gene expression while histone acetylation typically activates it. Lifestyle factors including diet, exercise, stress, sleep, and contemplative practice produce measurable epigenetic changes that alter gene expression patterns. Telomere length and telomerase activity are epigenetic aging biomarkers that respond to lifestyle interventions. Dean Ornish demonstrated that comprehensive lifestyle intervention including meditation increased telomerase activity in prostate cancer patients. The epigenome provides the molecular mechanism through which Post-Heaven Jing cultivation practices produce constitutional change corresponding to the TCM concept of acquired constitutional transformation.

## DNA Methylation

- DNA methylation adds methyl groups to cytosine bases at CpG dinucleotides typically silencing nearby genes.
- The methylation pattern across the genome constitutes the methylome which changes with aging and lifestyle.
- Epigenetic clocks using methylation patterns predict biological age independently of chronological age.
- Meditation, exercise, and diet quality all produce measurable methylome changes corresponding to reduced biological age.

## Histone Modification

- Histone acetylation loosens chromatin structure enabling transcription factor access and gene activation.
- Histone deacetylases HDACs remove acetyl groups condensing chromatin and silencing gene expression.
- Exercise produces HDAC inhibition increasing histone acetylation at neuroprotective gene promoters.
- Butyrate from gut microbiome fermentation is a potent natural HDAC inhibitor linking diet to epigenetic regulation.

## Telomeres and Aging

- Telomeres are repetitive DNA sequences protecting chromosome ends from degradation and fusion.
- Telomere shortening with each cell division is a primary molecular clock of cellular aging.
- Telomerase reverse transcriptase extends telomeres countering shortening in stem cells and activated immune cells.
- Chronic stress reduces telomerase activity accelerating telomere shortening while meditation increases it.
