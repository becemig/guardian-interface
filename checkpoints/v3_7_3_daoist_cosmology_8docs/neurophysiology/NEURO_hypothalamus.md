# Hypothalamus

## Abstract

The hypothalamus is the master regulator of homeostasis — the small diencephalic structure governing body temperature, fluid balance, energy metabolism, circadian rhythms, stress response, reproduction, and autonomic function through its control of the pituitary gland and direct neural projections. The paraventricular nucleus PVN integrates stress signals and governs the HPA axis through CRH release. The suprachiasmatic nucleus SCN is the master circadian clock. The arcuate nucleus governs energy balance through leptin and melanocortin signaling. The lateral hypothalamus governs arousal and feeding through orexin neurons. The preoptic area governs thermoregulation and sleep. The hypothalamic-pituitary axes — HPA, HPT, and HPG — govern the major endocrine systems of stress, thyroid, and reproduction. Mingmen Fire in TCM — the constitutional Yang fire housed between the two Kidneys — corresponds to the hypothalamus as the brain's constitutional regulatory center governing all homeostatic processes.

## Paraventricular Nucleus

- The PVN integrates hormonal, neural, and interoceptive signals to coordinate the stress response.
- CRH release from the PVN activates the anterior pituitary to release ACTH stimulating cortisol production.
- The PVN also contains vasopressin and oxytocin neurons governing fluid balance and social bonding.
- Chronic stress produces PVN dysregulation corresponding to HPA axis allostatic load.

## Arcuate Nucleus

- The arcuate nucleus contains AgRP neurons promoting feeding and NPY neurons suppressing metabolism.
- POMC neurons in the arcuate produce alpha-MSH suppressing appetite and increasing energy expenditure.
- Leptin from adipose tissue acts on the arcuate to signal energy sufficiency and suppress feeding.
- Ghrelin from the stomach acts on the arcuate to signal energy deficit and stimulate feeding.

## Temperature Regulation

- The preoptic area contains warm-sensitive neurons that drive heat dissipation responses.
- Fever is produced by prostaglandin E2 acting on the preoptic area to raise the temperature setpoint.
- Core body temperature follows a circadian rhythm governed by the SCN through the preoptic area.
- Sweating, shivering, vasodilation, and vasoconstriction are all governed by hypothalamic thermoregulation.
