# Language and the Brain

## Abstract

Language and the brain — the neuroscience of how the human brain processes, produces, and comprehends language through a distributed network extending far beyond the classical Broca and Wernicke areas to encompass a bilateral perisylvian language network, frontal and temporal association areas, subcortical structures including the basal ganglia and thalamus, and the cerebellum, with the dual stream model organizing language processing into a dorsal stream connecting frontal and parietal regions for syntactic and sensorimotor processing and a ventral stream connecting temporal and frontal regions for semantic and combinatorial processing, and with predictive coding frameworks proposing that language comprehension is fundamentally anticipatory, continuously generating predictions about upcoming words based on prior context. Broca and Wernicke areas — the classical lesion-based framework identifying Broca's area in the left inferior frontal gyrus as governing speech production and syntactic processing, and Wernicke's area in the left posterior superior temporal gyrus as governing speech comprehension and semantic processing, connected by the arcuate fasciculus, with this classic model now understood as an oversimplification of a much more distributed bilateral network with both areas contributing to both production and comprehension at different linguistic levels. Predictive language processing — the N400 ERP component discovered by Marta Kutas as a neural signature of semantic prediction violation, with unexpected words eliciting larger N400 amplitudes than expected words, and with the brain continuously generating probabilistic predictions about upcoming words based on sentence context, world knowledge, and conversation history, consistent with the predictive coding framework in which language comprehension is active prediction rather than passive decoding. Embodied semantics — the neuroimaging and TMS evidence that the meaning of words is grounded in sensorimotor simulations, with action words like kick and grasp activating motor cortex leg and hand representations, tool words activating the hand regions used for tool manipulation, and emotional words activating interoceptive and facial expression areas, supporting the embodied cognition view that semantic memory is a system of distributed sensorimotor simulations rather than an abstract amodal symbol system. The Heart governing speech Shen expression correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that the Heart governs the Shen and that speech is the primary expression of the Shen — the tongue being the sprout of the Heart — with the quality of speech reflecting the clarity and coherence of the Heart Shen, corresponding to the neuroscience of language as the primary behavioral output of the highest-level integrative neural systems governing consciousness and social communication.

## Dual Stream Language

- Dorsal stream: temporal to parietal to frontal — syntactic sensorimotor processing.
- Ventral stream: temporal to frontal — semantic combinatorial processing.
- Bilateral: both hemispheres contribute — left dominant for most right-handers.
- Subcortical: basal ganglia thalamus cerebellum — timing sequencing and fluency.

## Predictive Processing

- N400: ERP component to unexpected words — semantic prediction violation.
- Surprisal: unexpected words harder to process — brain predicts ahead.
- Context: sentence world knowledge conversation history — all constrain predictions.
- Production: forward model predicts sensory consequences of speech — fluency monitoring.

## Embodied Semantics

- Action words: kick activates leg motor cortex grasp activates hand motor cortex.
- Tool words: activate hand manipulation areas — grounded in use simulation.
- Emotional words: activate interoceptive and facial expression areas.
- Spatial words: above activates upward attention shift — embodied spatial simulation.
