# Sleep and Metabolism

## Abstract

The relationship between sleep and metabolism — one of the most clinically significant sleep medicine findings of the obesity epidemic era — reveals that sleep duration and quality are major determinants of metabolic health, with chronic short sleep producing a hormonal and physiological environment that promotes weight gain, insulin resistance, and metabolic syndrome through mechanisms involving appetite hormone dysregulation, insulin sensitivity reduction, and disruption of the circadian metabolic rhythm. Ghrelin-leptin dysregulation by sleep deprivation — Karine Spiegel's landmark finding published in 2004 — demonstrated that restricting sleep to 4 hours per night for 2 nights in healthy young men reduced leptin the satiety hormone by 18 percent and increased ghrelin the hunger hormone by 28 percent, producing a 24 percent increase in subjective hunger and a specific increase in appetite for calorie-dense sweet and salty foods — providing the hormonal mechanism by which sleep deprivation promotes caloric overconsumption and weight gain. Insulin sensitivity sleep dependence — demonstrated by Eve Van Cauter's research — shows that slow wave sleep disruption produces insulin resistance comparable to 8.5 years of aging: subjects whose slow wave sleep was specifically suppressed by acoustic stimulation showed a 25 percent reduction in insulin sensitivity and a 20 percent reduction in the acute insulin response to glucose, providing a direct mechanism by which sleep quality independently predicts type 2 diabetes risk. Circadian metabolic rhythm — the daily cycle of insulin sensitivity, digestive enzyme activity, gut motility, and hepatic metabolic activity governed by the peripheral circadian clocks synchronized by the SCN — establishes a metabolic window during which caloric intake produces the most favorable metabolic response: insulin sensitivity is highest in the morning and declines through the day, making early time-restricted eating more metabolically favorable than late eating regardless of total caloric intake. The Spleen restoration overnight correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that the Spleen and Stomach rest and restore their transformative and transportive capacity during sleep, with the overnight fast allowing the digestive system to complete its work and restore its constitutional function for the following day.

## Ghrelin-Leptin Dysregulation

- 4 hours sleep for 2 nights: leptin falls 18 percent, ghrelin rises 28 percent.
- 24 percent increase in subjective hunger with specific craving for calorie-dense foods.
- Mechanism: reduced leptin satiety signaling and increased ghrelin hunger drive.
- Chronic short sleep produces sustained ghrelin-leptin imbalance driving caloric overconsumption.

## Insulin Sensitivity

- Slow wave sleep suppression produces 25 percent reduction in insulin sensitivity.
- Comparable to 8.5 years of metabolic aging from one night of SWS disruption.
- Reduced acute insulin response to glucose — pancreatic beta cell function impaired.
- Chronic short sleep predicts type 2 diabetes risk independently of obesity.

## Circadian Metabolic Timing

- Insulin sensitivity highest in morning, lowest in evening — circadian metabolic variation.
- Same meal consumed in morning versus evening produces different glucose and insulin responses.
- Early time-restricted eating aligned with circadian metabolic peak produces superior metabolic outcomes.
- Late eating against circadian metabolic nadir produces higher glucose, insulin, and fat storage.
