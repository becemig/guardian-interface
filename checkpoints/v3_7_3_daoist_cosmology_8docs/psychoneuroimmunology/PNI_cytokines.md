# Cytokines and the Brain

## Abstract

Cytokines are small signaling proteins released by immune cells that coordinate inflammation and immune responses. Key pro-inflammatory cytokines — IL-1 beta, IL-6, and TNF-alpha — communicate with the brain through multiple routes to produce sickness behavior, activate the HPA axis, alter neurotransmitter metabolism, and drive neuroinflammation. The cytokine theory of depression proposes that a significant subset of depression is driven by chronic low-grade inflammation, supported by elevated CRP and IL-6 in depressed patients, depression as a side effect of cytokine immunotherapy, and anti-inflammatory interventions reducing depressive symptoms. A key mechanistic pathway is the kynurenine pathway: inflammatory cytokines activate IDO (indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase), shunting tryptophan away from serotonin synthesis toward kynurenine and its neurotoxic metabolite quinolinic acid, an NMDA agonist producing excitotoxicity and anhedonia. Neuroinflammation via microglial activation is increasingly implicated in depression, cognitive decline, PTSD, and chronic pain.

## Key Pro-Inflammatory Cytokines

- IL-1 beta: master inducer of sickness behavior - fever, fatigue, anhedonia, social withdrawal
- IL-6: acute phase protein inducer - CRP elevation - elevated in depression and CVD
- TNF-alpha: pleiotropic inflammatory cytokine - BBB disruption, synaptic pruning, apoptosis
- IFN-gamma: antiviral cytokine - activates IDO - major driver of kynurenine pathway
- IL-10: anti-inflammatory counterregulatory - limits cytokine cascade
- IL-17: promotes neuroinflammation - elevated in bipolar disorder and schizophrenia
- CRP: C-reactive protein - downstream of IL-6 - clinical biomarker of systemic inflammation

## Cytokine Routes to Brain

- Circumventricular organs: area postrema, organum vasculosum - lack BBB - cytokine entry
- Vagal afferents: hepatic and peritoneal cytokines activate vagal sensory fibers - rapid signal
- Active transport: saturable transport systems carry IL-1 and IL-6 across BBB
- BBB disruption: high TNF-alpha opens BBB - allows cytokine flood into CNS
- Perivascular macrophages: transduce peripheral signals to brain parenchyma
- Local production: brain microglia produce cytokines independently of periphery

## Kynurenine Pathway

- Normal: tryptophan converts to serotonin via TPH - supports mood and sleep
- IDO activation: IFN-gamma and IL-1 activate IDO - shunts tryptophan to kynurenine
- Tryptophan depletion: less substrate for serotonin - contributes to low mood
- Kynurenic acid: neuroprotective NMDA antagonist - produced by astrocytes
- Quinolinic acid: neurotoxic NMDA agonist - produced by microglia - excitotoxicity
- KA-QA balance: ratio determines neuroprotection vs neurodegeneration
- Anhedonia: quinolinic acid in nucleus accumbens disrupts dopamine reward circuit

## Cytokine Theory of Depression

- Evidence 1: 30-50 percent of patients on IFN-alpha develop major depression
- Evidence 2: elevated CRP and IL-6 in treatment-resistant depression
- Evidence 3: anti-inflammatory drugs (celecoxib, infliximab) reduce depression scores
- Evidence 4: LPS injection in humans produces transient depression and anxiety
- Subtype: inflammatory depression - anhedonia prominent - less responsive to SSRIs
- Biomarker: CRP above 1 mg-L predicts poor SSRI response - better response to anti-inflammatory
- TCM: Damp-Heat obstructing Heart Shen - inflammation as Dampness and Heat

## Microglial Neuroinflammation

- Microglia: resident brain immune cells - 10-15 percent of brain cells
- Surveillance state: ramified microglia survey synapses - pruning and maintenance
- Activated state: ameboid microglia release cytokines, ROS, glutamate
- Synaptic pruning: complement-mediated - excessive in depression and schizophrenia
- PET imaging: TSPO ligands detect microglial activation in living human brain
- Chronic activation: sustained neuroinflammation - neurodegeneration risk
