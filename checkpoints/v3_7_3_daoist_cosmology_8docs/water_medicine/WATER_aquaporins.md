# Aquaporins and Cellular Water Transport

## Abstract

Aquaporins — the membrane channel proteins discovered by Peter Agre, who was awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 2003 for this discovery, that facilitate the rapid and selective transport of water across cell membranes — are the molecular basis of all rapid transcellular water movement in biology, governing kidney water reabsorption, brain water homeostasis, cerebrospinal fluid production, glandular secretion, and the cellular hydration responses to osmotic stress, with aquaporin dysfunction underlying multiple pathological conditions including cerebral edema, nephrogenic diabetes insipidus, and impaired glymphatic clearance in neurodegeneration. AQP4 brain water homeostasis — the most abundant aquaporin in the brain, expressed predominantly in astrocyte end-feet surrounding blood vessels and the brain-CSF interface — is the primary molecular mediator of water movement between blood, brain parenchyma, and CSF, with AQP4 governing the glymphatic clearance of interstitial solutes including amyloid-beta during sleep and with AQP4 deletion or mislocalization impairing glymphatic function and promoting amyloid accumulation. Aquaporin tissue distribution — the 13 human aquaporin subtypes AQP0 through AQP12 with distinct tissue distributions and transport specificities — governs the water transport biology of virtually every organ: AQP1 in kidney proximal tubule mediates the majority of renal water reabsorption, AQP2 in collecting duct is the vasopressin-regulated water channel governing final urine concentration, AQP4 in astrocytes governs brain water balance, and AQP7 in adipocytes transports glycerol as well as water. Aquaporin regulation — the dynamic regulation of aquaporin expression and membrane insertion by hormones, osmolality, and cellular signaling — provides the molecular basis for the kidney's response to antidiuretic hormone vasopressin, which triggers the insertion of AQP2 from intracellular vesicles to the apical membrane of collecting duct cells to increase water reabsorption during dehydration. Glymphatic-aquaporin connection — the dependence of the brain's glymphatic waste clearance system on AQP4 in astrocyte end-feet for the bulk flow of CSF through the brain parenchyma — links aquaporin biology to neurodegeneration, with reduced AQP4 function, impaired glymphatic flow, and amyloid-beta accumulation forming a pathological cascade relevant to Alzheimer's disease prevention through sleep optimization. The Triple Burner water passage correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding of the Triple Burner as governing the transformation and passage of water through the three body cavities — a systems function corresponding to the aquaporin-mediated water transport network that governs fluid movement across all tissue compartments.

## AQP4 Brain Water

- Located in astrocyte end-feet at blood-brain barrier and glia limitans.
- Governs water movement between blood brain parenchyma and CSF compartments.
- Required for glymphatic bulk flow during sleep.
- AQP4 loss of function: impaired glymphatic clearance amyloid accumulation.

## Kidney Aquaporins

- AQP1: proximal tubule and descending loop of Henle — constitutive water reabsorption.
- AQP2: collecting duct apical membrane — vasopressin-regulated water reabsorption.
- AQP3 AQP4: collecting duct basolateral — water exit to interstitium.
- Vasopressin regulation: AQP2 vesicle insertion — molecular basis of urine concentration.

## Glymphatic Connection

- CSF enters brain along periarterial spaces — driven by arterial pulsatility.
- AQP4 in astrocyte end-feet facilitates rapid water movement across parenchyma.
- ISF exits along perivenous spaces — carries amyloid-beta tau and metabolic waste.
- Sleep dependent: glymphatic flow 60 percent greater during sleep than waking.
