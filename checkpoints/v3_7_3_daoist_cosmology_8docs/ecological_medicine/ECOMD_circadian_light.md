# Circadian Light Medicine

## Abstract

Circadian light medicine — the clinical application of light as a therapeutic agent to synchronize, restore, and optimize the circadian timing system — has emerged as one of the most evidence-based and clinically actionable domains of chronobiology, with applications spanning seasonal affective disorder, circadian rhythm sleep disorders, jet lag, shift work disorder, bipolar disorder, Alzheimer's disease, and the optimization of metabolic and immune function through circadian alignment. The discovery of the intrinsically photosensitive retinal ganglion cells ipRGCs by David Berson and their melanopsin photopigment — which responds maximally to short-wavelength blue light and projects through the retinohypothalamic tract directly to the suprachiasmatic nucleus SCN of the hypothalamus — established the molecular mechanism of circadian photoentrainment and explained why artificial blue light from screens and indoor lighting disrupts circadian rhythms with far greater potency than longer-wavelength light of equal intensity. Morning light therapy — exposure to bright outdoor light within 30-60 minutes of waking — is the most powerful circadian anchor, entraining the SCN to the solar cycle through direct melanopsin activation and producing cascading effects on cortisol, melatonin, serotonin, dopamine, thyroid function, and immune activity that last throughout the day. Seasonal affective disorder SAD — the recurrent winter depression produced by insufficient light exposure in high-latitude winters — was the condition through which light therapy was first clinically validated by Norman Rosenthal in 1984, establishing the precedent for light as a psychiatric medicine. Blue light disruption from screens and artificial lighting after sunset suppresses melatonin, delays the circadian clock, fragments sleep architecture, and produces the inflammatory and metabolic consequences of chronic circadian misalignment. The solar-constitutional rhythm correspondence describes the TCM understanding that the human constitutional rhythm is fundamentally entrained to the solar cycle — the organ clock two-hour periods, the seasonal Qi movements, and the diurnal Yin-Yang cycle — and that circadian misalignment is a form of constitutional disruption equivalent to the TCM pattern of Yang rising at the wrong time.
