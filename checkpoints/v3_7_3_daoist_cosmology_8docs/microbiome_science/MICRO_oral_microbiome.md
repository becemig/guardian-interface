# Oral Microbiome and Systemic Health

## Abstract

Oral microbiome and systemic health — the investigation of the 700 plus bacterial species inhabiting the oral cavity in biofilm communities on teeth, gingiva, tongue, and mucosal surfaces, with the oral microbiome being the second most diverse microbial community in the human body after the gut, and with oral dysbiosis in periodontal disease producing systemic consequences through the bacteremia of periodontal pathogens entering the bloodstream, the systemic dissemination of inflammatory mediators, and the downstream translocation of oral bacteria to the gut where they disrupt gut microbiome composition and induce intestinal inflammation. Oral dysbiosis and cardiovascular disease — the epidemiological association between periodontal disease and increased cardiovascular disease risk, with Porphyromonas gingivalis and other periodontal pathogens detected in atherosclerotic plaques, with P. gingivalis capable of invading endothelial cells and macrophages promoting foam cell formation, and with interventional studies showing that periodontal treatment reduces systemic inflammatory markers including CRP and IL-6 — establishing periodontal disease as a modifiable cardiovascular risk factor mediated through oral microbiome-driven systemic inflammation. Oral microbiome Alzheimer's connection — the landmark 2019 study by Dominy demonstrating P. gingivalis in the brains of Alzheimer's patients with the toxic gingipain proteases produced by this organism degrading tau protein and generating the paired helical filament tau tangles of Alzheimer's pathology, with gingipain inhibitor treatment reducing P. gingivalis brain infection and improving cognitive outcomes in preclinical models — raising the possibility that periodontal treatment may reduce Alzheimer's risk. Oral-gut microbiome axis — the underappreciated pathway through which oral dysbiosis contributes to gut dysbiosis, with approximately 140 milliliters of saliva per hour containing billions of bacteria being swallowed daily and with oral pathobionts including Fusobacterium nucleatum, Porphyromonas gingivalis, and Treponema denticola surviving gastric transit and establishing themselves in the gut where they promote intestinal inflammation and colorectal cancer development. The Lung Large Intestine oral gut axis correspondence in TCM describes the paired Yin-Yang organ relationship between the Lung and Large Intestine sharing the Metal phase, with the Lung opening to the exterior through the nose and mouth and the Large Intestine governing elimination — a classical framework corresponding to the oral-gut microbiome axis through which oral microbiome health directly influences gut microbiome composition and systemic inflammatory status.
