# Thyroid Endocrinology

## Abstract

The thyroid gland — a butterfly-shaped endocrine organ at the base of the neck governing the body's metabolic rate, thermogenesis, cardiovascular function, neurological development, and the activity of virtually every cell in the body through the thyroid hormone axis — is one of the most clinically significant endocrine systems for understanding the TCM concepts of Kidney Yang, metabolic transformation, and constitutional energetics. The hypothalamic-pituitary-thyroid HPT axis regulates thyroid hormone production through the same negative feedback architecture as the HPA axis: thyrotropin-releasing hormone TRH from the hypothalamus stimulates thyroid-stimulating hormone TSH from the anterior pituitary, which stimulates the thyroid gland to produce thyroxine T4 and triiodothyronine T3, which feed back to the hypothalamus and pituitary to reduce TRH and TSH production. The thyroid gland produces primarily T4 — the prohormone — which is converted to the active hormone T3 in peripheral tissues by deiodinase enzymes. T3 binds to nuclear thyroid hormone receptors in virtually every cell, regulating the transcription of genes governing metabolic rate, mitochondrial biogenesis, thermogenesis, cardiac function, and neurological development. Thyroid autoimmunity — Hashimoto's thyroiditis and Graves' disease — represents the most common thyroid pathology, with the immune system producing antibodies against thyroid peroxidase TPO and thyroglobulin in Hashimoto's producing progressive thyroid destruction and hypothyroidism, and antibodies against the TSH receptor in Graves' producing thyroid stimulation and hyperthyroidism. The TCM Kidney-Spleen thyroid correspondence describes the dual organ network involvement in thyroid function: the Kidney Yang as the constitutional fire that drives thyroid hormone production and metabolic rate corresponds to the HPT axis Yang activation, while the Spleen's governance of transformation and transportation corresponds to the peripheral conversion of T4 to T3 and the cellular uptake and utilization of thyroid hormone.

## HPT Axis

- TRH from hypothalamus stimulates TSH from anterior pituitary.
- TSH stimulates thyroid to produce T4 prohormone and T3 active hormone.
- T4 converted to active T3 in liver, kidney, and other peripheral tissues by deiodinase enzymes.
- T3 binds nuclear receptors regulating metabolic rate in virtually every cell.

## T3 Metabolic Effects

- Increases basal metabolic rate through mitochondrial uncoupling and ATP production stimulation.
- Regulates thermogenesis — low T3 produces cold intolerance, high T3 produces heat intolerance.
- Cardiac effects: increases heart rate and contractility — hyperthyroid produces tachycardia.
- Neurological: essential for brain development and adult cognitive function.

## Thyroid Autoimmunity

- Hashimoto's: anti-TPO and anti-thyroglobulin antibodies producing progressive thyroid destruction.
- Graves': anti-TSH receptor antibodies stimulating thyroid producing hyperthyroidism.
- Both conditions involve a failure of immune self-tolerance — corresponds to Wei Qi dysregulation.
- Stress, iodine excess, environmental toxins, and gut dysbiosis are primary triggers.
