# HPA Axis

## Abstract

The hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal HPA axis — the neuroendocrine cascade that coordinates the organism's response to psychological and physiological stress — is the central regulatory system governing the body's adaptive capacity, the primary interface between the brain's threat appraisal systems and the peripheral physiological stress response, and one of the most clinically significant endocrine systems for understanding the relationship between chronic psychological stress and physical disease. The HPA cascade initiates in the paraventricular nucleus of the hypothalamus with the release of corticotropin-releasing hormone CRH into the hypophyseal portal system, which stimulates the anterior pituitary to release adrenocorticotropic hormone ACTH into the systemic circulation, which stimulates the adrenal cortex to produce and release cortisol — the primary human glucocorticoid. Cortisol — acting through glucocorticoid receptors GR distributed throughout virtually every cell of the body — mobilizes glucose from glycogen stores, suppresses non-essential functions including immune activity and reproductive function, enhances cardiovascular performance, and prepares the organism for fight-or-flight behavioral response. The glucocorticoid receptor feedback system normally terminates the stress response: cortisol binds to GRs in the hippocampus and hypothalamus, suppressing CRH and ACTH release and returning cortisol to baseline within 60-90 minutes. Chronic stress dysregulates this feedback through GR downregulation — the reduction of GR number and sensitivity in the hippocampus — producing HPA hyperactivation, sustained elevated cortisol, and the hippocampal neurodegeneration and immune suppression associated with chronic stress pathology. Allostatic load — the cumulative physiological wear produced by repeated HPA activation and the sustained glucocorticoid exposure of chronic stress — is Bruce McEwen's framework for understanding how chronic psychological stress produces physical disease through the progressive dysregulation of the HPA axis and its downstream effects on cardiovascular, immune, metabolic, and neurological function. Kidney Yang as adrenal fire describes the TCM correspondence: the Kidney Yang — the constitutional fire stored in the Gate of Life Ming Men — corresponds anatomically and functionally to the adrenal medulla and cortex, governing the organism's energetic capacity, stress responsiveness, and constitutional warmth.

## HPA Cascade

- CRH from paraventricular nucleus of hypothalamus initiates the cascade.
- ACTH from anterior pituitary stimulates adrenal cortex cortisol production.
- Cortisol mobilizes glucose, suppresses immunity, enhances cardiovascular performance.
- GR feedback in hippocampus and hypothalamus normally terminates the response within 60-90 minutes.

## Glucocorticoid Receptor Feedback

- Hippocampal GRs are the primary negative feedback site for HPA termination.
- Chronic stress downregulates hippocampal GRs — reducing feedback sensitivity.
- GR downregulation produces HPA hyperactivation and sustained cortisol elevation.
- Hippocampal neurodegeneration from chronic glucocorticoid exposure reduces feedback further.

## Allostatic Load

- Cumulative physiological wear from repeated HPA activation and chronic cortisol exposure.
- Cardiovascular: accelerated atherosclerosis, hypertension, cardiac remodeling.
- Metabolic: insulin resistance, visceral adiposity, metabolic syndrome.
- Immune: chronic low-grade inflammation, immune suppression, increased infection susceptibility.
