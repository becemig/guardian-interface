# Circadian Metabolism

## Abstract

Circadian metabolism — the comprehensive temporal organization of metabolic processes by the circadian clock, encompassing the rhythmic regulation of glucose and lipid metabolism, mitochondrial function, NAD+ biosynthesis, insulin sensitivity, and energy expenditure that coordinates anabolic and catabolic metabolism with the predictable daily cycles of activity and rest, feeding and fasting — establishes that metabolic health is inseparable from circadian health and that the timing of metabolic processes is as important as their magnitude, with circadian misalignment producing metabolic dysfunction independently of total caloric intake or macronutrient composition. Clock-controlled metabolic enzymes — the vast transcriptional program through which CLOCK-BMAL1 directly regulates the rhythmic expression of hundreds of metabolic enzymes including rate-limiting enzymes of glycolysis, gluconeogenesis, fatty acid oxidation, lipid synthesis, and cholesterol metabolism, with BMAL1 deletion producing a metabolic phenotype of obesity, hyperlipidemia, impaired glucose homeostasis, and accelerated aging that demonstrates the essential role of the molecular clock in maintaining metabolic health. NAD+ circadian oscillation — the discovery that NAD+ levels oscillate with circadian amplitude in metabolic tissues driven by the CLOCK-BMAL1 regulation of NAMPT the rate-limiting enzyme of the NAD+ salvage pathway, creating a metabolic feedback loop in which NAD+ activates SIRT1 to deacetylate and regulate BMAL1, linking the energetic state of the cell to clock function and producing the circadian rhythmicity of SIRT1-dependent metabolic programs including mitochondrial biogenesis. Insulin sensitivity circadian rhythm — the robust diurnal variation in glucose tolerance and insulin sensitivity that is highest in the morning and progressively declines through the day, producing the clinical phenomenon of greater postprandial glucose excursions from identical meals eaten in the evening compared to the morning, and establishing morning as the metabolically optimal time for the largest meal of the day. The Yang Qi daytime metabolic fire correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that Yang Qi is most abundant and active during the day, governing the transformation and transportation of food essence and the active metabolic processes of the organism — a functional description of the daytime peak of insulin sensitivity, metabolic enzyme activity, and mitochondrial oxidative capacity governed by the circadian clock.

## Clock Metabolic Regulation

- CLOCK BMAL1: directly regulates glycolytic gluconeogenic lipid metabolic enzyme genes.
- BMAL1 knockout: obesity hyperlipidemia impaired glucose homeostasis accelerated aging.
- REV-ERB alpha: suppresses lipid synthesis genes during active phase — promotes fat oxidation.
- ROR alpha: activates lipid oxidation and mitochondrial genes — counter-regulates REV-ERB.

## NAD+ SIRT1 Loop

- NAMPT: rate-limiting NAD+ salvage enzyme — CLOCK BMAL1 regulated circadian expression.
- NAD+ peak: late active phase — drives SIRT1 SIRT3 deacetylase activity.
- SIRT1: deacetylates BMAL1 PER2 — feeds back to regulate clock amplitude.
- SIRT3: mitochondrial deacetylase — circadian regulation of mitochondrial function.

## Insulin Sensitivity Rhythm

- Morning: highest insulin sensitivity — lowest postprandial glucose for same meal.
- Evening: reduced insulin sensitivity — same meal produces higher glucose excursion.
- Chronotype interaction: late chronotypes have greater evening insulin resistance mismatch.
- Clinical implication: front-loading calories to morning improves metabolic outcomes.
