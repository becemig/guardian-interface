# Chinese Medical Nutrition: Food Energetics, Five Flavors, Seasonal Eating, and Spleen Diet

## Abstract

Chinese medical nutrition is a comprehensive dietary system based on the energetic properties of foods rather than their biochemical composition. Foods are classified by their thermal nature warming to cooling, their flavor sour sweet bitter pungent salty, and their organ tropism determining which organ systems they nourish and regulate. The five flavors correspond to the five elements and five organ systems providing a framework for dietary correction of organ imbalances. Seasonal eating aligns food choices with the energetic demands of each season supporting the organ system that is most active and vulnerable in that season. The Spleen and Stomach as the Earth element digestive center are the primary focus of TCM dietary therapy since all organ systems depend on their transformative function.

## Food Energetics

### Thermal Nature

- Warming foods increase metabolic activity, circulation, and Yang Qi supporting Cold deficiency patterns.
- Neutral foods have minimal thermal effect and are appropriate for most constitutional types and seasons.
- Cooling foods reduce heat and inflammation supporting Hot excess and Yin deficiency patterns.
- Hot foods produce the strongest warming effect while cold foods produce the strongest cooling effect.
- Cooking method modifies thermal nature — raw food is more cooling, roasting and frying add warmth.

### Warming Foods

- Ginger Sheng Jiang warms the middle Jiao, dispels cold, and stops nausea through its pungent warming nature.
- Cinnamon Rou Gui warms the Kidney Yang and channels warming the entire body from the constitutional root.
- Lamb and venison are the most warming meats supporting Kidney Yang and constitutional vitality.
- Leeks, chives, and garlic warm the middle and lower Jiao supporting Spleen and Kidney Yang.
- Walnuts He Tao Ren warm the Kidney Yang, nourish Jing, and support brain and lung function.

### Cooling Foods

- Mung beans Lu Dou clear summer heat, reduce inflammation, and detoxify corresponding to anti-inflammatory effects.
- Watermelon Xi Gua clears heat from the Heart and Stomach and promotes fluid production.
- Tofu Bean Curd cools and moistens the Lung and Stomach supporting Yin and fluid production.
- Cucumber clears heat and promotes urination supporting Damp Heat patterns in summer.
- Peppermint Bo He cools the Liver and clears head heat dispersing Wind Heat from the upper body.

## Five Flavors

### Sour — Liver and Gallbladder

- Sour flavor enters the Liver and Gallbladder meridians and has an astringent consolidating action.
- Moderate sour intake nourishes Liver Yin and Blood and consolidates leaking fluids.
- Excess sour damages the Liver and Spleen producing digestive dysfunction and sinew tension.
- Fermented foods vinegar lemon and most citrus fruits express the sour flavor.

### Bitter — Heart and Small Intestine

- Bitter flavor enters the Heart and Small Intestine and has a descending draining action.
- Moderate bitter intake clears Heart fire, drains Damp Heat, and promotes bowel function.
- Excess bitter damages the Heart and dries the Yin depleting fluids and Blood.
- Coffee dark chocolate bitter melon and many medicinal herbs express the bitter flavor.

### Sweet — Spleen and Stomach

- Sweet flavor enters the Spleen and Stomach and has a tonifying moistening action.
- Moderate sweet intake tonifies Qi and Blood, moistens dryness, and harmonizes the middle Jiao.
- Excess sweet produces Dampness, Phlegm, and Spleen Qi stagnation corresponding to metabolic syndrome.
- Rice, sweet potato, dates, honey, and most grains express the sweet flavor appropriate for Spleen tonification.

### Pungent — Lung and Large Intestine

- Pungent flavor enters the Lung and Large Intestine and has a dispersing moving action.
- Moderate pungent intake disperses Qi stagnation, promotes sweating, and opens the exterior surface.
- Excess pungent damages the Lung Qi and dries fluids depleting Wei Qi and skin moisture.
- Ginger scallion radish and aromatic herbs and spices express the pungent flavor.

### Salty — Kidney and Bladder

- Salty flavor enters the Kidney and Bladder and has a softening descending action.
- Moderate salty intake nourishes Kidney Jing, softens hardness, and supports water metabolism.
- Excess salty damages the Kidney and Heart producing hypertension and fluid retention.
- Seaweed miso millet and salt itself express the salty flavor supporting Kidney function.

## Seasonal Eating

### Spring — Liver and Wood

- Spring is the season of the Liver and Wood element governing growth, expansion, and new beginnings.
- Spring diet emphasizes sour flavor, green foods, and light dispersing foods supporting Liver Qi flow.
- Sprouted grains, young greens, and mildly pungent herbs support spring detoxification and Liver cleansing.
- Reduce heavy, greasy, and cold foods that burden the Spleen during the spring Liver activation period.

### Summer — Heart and Fire

- Summer is the season of the Heart and Fire element governing maximum Yang expansion and joy.
- Summer diet emphasizes bitter flavor, cooling foods, and light easily digestible meals.
- Watermelon, cucumber, mung bean soup, and cooling teas support Heart cooling in summer heat.
- Avoid excess cold and raw foods that shock the Spleen Yang during summer despite the external heat.

### Late Summer — Spleen and Earth

- Late summer is the unique fifth season of the Spleen and Earth element governing harvest and transformation.
- Late summer diet emphasizes sweet flavor, yellow foods, and easily digestible whole grains.
- Millet, corn, sweet potato, and pumpkin support Spleen transformation during the Earth season.
- This is the optimal season to tonify the Spleen and build digestive strength for the coming winter.

### Autumn — Lung and Metal

- Autumn is the season of the Lung and Metal element governing harvest completion and letting go.
- Autumn diet emphasizes pungent and sour flavors, white foods, and moistening foods supporting Lung Yin.
- Pears, white sesame, lily bulb, and rice porridge moisten the Lung against autumn dryness.
- Begin introducing warming foods as temperatures drop to protect Spleen Yang against cold invasion.

### Winter — Kidney and Water

- Winter is the season of the Kidney and Water element governing storage, depth, and constitutional renewal.
- Winter diet emphasizes salty and warming foods, black foods, and Kidney tonics supporting Jing storage.
- Bone broth, walnuts, black sesame, kidney beans, and lamb support Kidney Yang and Jing in winter.
- Reduce raw and cold foods entirely in winter protecting Spleen and Kidney Yang against the season cold.

## Spleen Diet Principles

- Eat warm cooked foods predominantly since raw and cold foods damage Spleen Yang.
- Eat regular meals at consistent times supporting the Spleen rhythm of transformation.
- Avoid overeating since excess quantity overwhelms Spleen transformative capacity.
- Avoid eating while emotionally upset since worry and anxiety directly impair Spleen function.
- Chew thoroughly since the mechanical breakdown of food supports Spleen digestive efficiency.
- Minimize cold drinks with meals since they extinguish the digestive fire of Spleen Yang.
