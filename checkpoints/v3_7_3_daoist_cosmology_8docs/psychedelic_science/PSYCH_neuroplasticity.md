# Psychedelic Neuroplasticity Mechanisms

## Abstract

Psychedelic neuroplasticity mechanisms — the recently discovered molecular and cellular mechanisms through which psychedelic compounds promote structural and functional neural plasticity independently of their hallucinogenic 5-HT2A receptor-mediated effects, including direct TrkB receptor binding, BDNF signaling activation, dendritic spine and synaptic density increases, and sigma-1 receptor-mediated neuroplasticity — establish psychedelics as a new class of neuroplasticity-promoting agents or psychoplastogens that may produce their therapeutic effects partly through the restoration of synaptic connectivity reduced by chronic stress, depression, and trauma. Direct TrkB binding — Moliner et al.'s 2023 discovery that psilocin, LSD, and ketamine directly bind to the BDNF receptor TrkB at a transmembrane allosteric site distinct from the BDNF binding site, with this direct TrkB binding producing BDNF-independent TrkB activation and neuroplasticity promotion, explaining the neuroplasticity effects of psychedelics at sub-hallucinogenic doses and providing the mechanism for the antidepressant effects of microdosing. Dendritic spine growth — David Olson's research demonstrating that serotonergic psychedelics including psilocin, DMT, LSD, and DOI produce rapid and sustained increases in dendritic spine density, dendrite complexity, and synaptic protein expression in cortical neurons, with psilocin-treated neurons showing 10-30 percent increases in spine density persisting for weeks after a single exposure, corresponding to the structural neuroplasticity that restores the synaptic connectivity reduced by chronic stress and depression. Psychoplastogen concept — David Olson's proposal and demonstration that the neuroplasticity-promoting properties of psychedelics can be separated from their hallucinogenic properties through structural modification of the psychedelic scaffold, producing non-hallucinogenic psychoplastogens including tabernanthalog and other compounds that promote neuroplasticity and produce antidepressant and anti-addictive effects in animal models without the subjective effects that complicate clinical use of classical psychedelics. The Shen renewal constitutional transformation correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that the deepest healing involves the renewal and transformation of the Shen spirit — not merely symptom relief but the restoration of the fundamental vitality and clarity of consciousness that constitutes constitutional health — a correspondence with psychedelic neuroplasticity as the biological substrate of the profound transformation produced by psychedelic therapy.

## Direct TrkB Binding

- Discovery: psilocin LSD ketamine bind TrkB at transmembrane allosteric site.
- Mechanism: conformational change activates TrkB signaling without BDNF.
- Significance: explains neuroplasticity at sub-hallucinogenic doses including microdosing.
- Downstream: PLCG PI3K MAPK pathways — synaptic protein synthesis neuroplasticity.

## Dendritic Spine Growth

- Psilocin: 10-30 percent dendritic spine density increase in cortical neurons.
- Duration: structural changes persist weeks after single exposure.
- Functional: increased miniature excitatory postsynaptic current frequency.
- Stress reversal: restores spine density reduced by chronic stress exposure.

## Psychoplastogens

- Tabernanthalog: ibogaine-inspired non-hallucinogenic — neuroplasticity without hallucination.
- Principle: separate neuroplasticity from hallucinogenic effects structurally.
- Potential: treat depression addiction PTSD without psychedelic session requirements.
- Research: multiple non-hallucinogenic psychoplastogens in preclinical development.
