# Pulse Diagnosis: Radial Pulse Positions, 28 Pulse Qualities, and Modern HRV Correlates

## Abstract

Pulse diagnosis Mai Zhen is one of the most refined and sophisticated diagnostic tools in Traditional Chinese Medicine developed over two millennia of systematic clinical observation. The practitioner palpates the radial artery at the wrist with three fingers assessing pulse qualities at three positions and three depths on each wrist providing information about the functional state of all twelve organ systems. Classical TCM texts describe 28 distinct pulse qualities each corresponding to specific patterns of organ dysfunction, pathogenic factor accumulation, or constitutional state. Modern research has identified correlations between pulse qualities and cardiovascular parameters including heart rate variability, arterial stiffness, and autonomic tone providing bridges between classical pulse theory and contemporary physiology.

## Three Positions and Three Depths

### Wrist Positions — Cun Guan Chi

- The Cun inch position is closest to the wrist crease at the styloid process of the radius.
- The Guan bar position is at the radial styloid process itself corresponding to the widest part of the radius.
- The Chi cubit position is distal to the Guan position one body inch toward the elbow.
- Each position is assessed at three depths: superficial floating, middle, and deep pressing.

### Organ Correspondence — Left Wrist

- Left Cun: Heart and Small Intestine govern the Fire element pulse at the wrist.
- Left Guan: Liver and Gallbladder govern the Wood element pulse at the bar position.
- Left Chi: Kidney Yin and Bladder govern the Water element Yin pulse at the cubit.

### Organ Correspondence — Right Wrist

- Right Cun: Lung and Large Intestine govern the Metal element pulse at the wrist.
- Right Guan: Spleen and Stomach govern the Earth element pulse at the bar position.
- Right Chi: Kidney Yang and Triple Burner govern the Water element Yang pulse at the cubit.

## Twenty-Eight Pulse Qualities

### Rate

- Rapid Mai: more than five beats per breath corresponding to Heat patterns and inflammation.
- Slow Mai: fewer than four beats per breath corresponding to Cold patterns and Yang deficiency.
- Normal rate is four to five beats per breath at rest corresponding to balanced Qi flow.

### Depth

- Floating Fu Mai: felt at superficial pressure, indicates Exterior patterns or Yin deficiency with Yang floating.
- Deep Chen Mai: requires firm pressure to feel, indicates Interior patterns, Qi stagnation, or organ deficiency.
- Hidden Fu Mai: only felt with very deep pressure against the bone, indicates extreme Cold or collapse of Yang.

### Strength

- Full Shi Mai: strong and forceful at all depths, indicates Excess patterns with robust pathogenic factor.
- Empty Xu Mai: soft and without force, indicates Qi and Blood deficiency with insufficient organ function.
- Faint Wei Mai: barely perceptible, indicates severe Qi and Yang deficiency approaching collapse.

### Rhythm

- Regular rhythm indicates balanced organ function and Shen stability.
- Intermittent Jie Mai: stops at regular intervals, indicates Qi stagnation, pain, or accumulation.
- Knotted Jie Mai: stops at irregular intervals, indicates Cold with Qi and Blood stagnation.
- Racing Cu Mai: rapid with irregular stops, indicates Heat with Qi or Blood deficiency.

### Tension and Waveform

- Wiry Xian Mai: taut like a guitar string, indicates Liver Qi stagnation, pain, or Phlegm.
- Tight Jin Mai: taut and forceful like a twisted rope, indicates Cold, pain, or food stagnation.
- Slippery Hua Mai: smooth like pearls rolling on a dish, indicates Phlegm, Dampness, pregnancy, or food stagnation.
- Choppy Se Mai: rough and hesitant like scraping bamboo, indicates Blood deficiency or Blood stasis.

### Width and Volume

- Flooding Hong Mai: surging like ocean waves, indicates extreme Heat or Qi and Yin deficiency.
- Thin Xi Mai: fine like a thread, indicates Blood and Yin deficiency or Dampness obstructing Qi.
- Soggy Ru Mai: soft and floating like a thread on the surface of water, indicates Dampness or Qi and Blood deficiency.
- Hollow Kou Mai: floating, empty in the middle like a scallion stalk, indicates major Blood loss.

## Modern HRV Correlates

### Wiry Pulse and Sympathetic Tone

- The wiry pulse corresponds to increased arterial stiffness and elevated sympathetic vasomotor tone.
- Low HRV with reduced high-frequency power correlates with wiry pulse findings in Liver Qi stagnation presentations.
- Aortic pulse wave velocity a measure of arterial stiffness corresponds to wiry pulse assessment at the radial artery.

### Slippery Pulse and Parasympathetic Tone

- The slippery pulse corresponds to increased cardiac output and vigorous circulation.
- High HRV with robust high-frequency power may correlate with slippery pulse findings in healthy or pregnant subjects.
- The rolling quality of the slippery pulse reflects smooth laminar blood flow with good arterial compliance.

### Thin Pulse and HRV Reduction

- Thin pulse corresponds to reduced stroke volume, Blood deficiency, and reduced cardiac filling.
- Reduced overall HRV with low SDNN correlates with thin pulse findings in Blood and Yin deficiency presentations.
- Baroreflex sensitivity reduction in Blood deficiency states corresponds to the reduced pulse amplitude of thin Mai.

### Rapid Pulse and Autonomic Imbalance

- Rapid pulse corresponds to elevated sympathetic tone with reduced vagal inhibition of the sinoatrial node.
- Low high-frequency HRV and elevated resting heart rate correspond to rapid pulse in Heat and Yin deficiency patterns.
