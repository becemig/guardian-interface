# Hippocampal Memory

## Abstract

The hippocampus is the critical structure for the formation of new declarative memories — both episodic memories of personal experience and semantic memories of facts — and for spatial navigation through its role as a cognitive map. John O'Keefe's discovery of place cells — hippocampal neurons that fire specifically when the animal occupies a particular location in the environment — established the hippocampus as the neural substrate of spatial memory and earned O'Keefe the Nobel Prize. The trisynaptic circuit of the hippocampus — entorhinal cortex to dentate gyrus, dentate gyrus to CA3, CA3 to CA1 — implements a sequence of computational operations: pattern separation in the dentate gyrus creates distinct non-overlapping representations of similar inputs reducing interference, pattern completion in CA3 allows partial cues to retrieve complete memory traces through recurrent connections, and CA1 compares current experience with stored patterns detecting novelty. Sharp wave ripples — high-frequency oscillations generated in CA3 during rest and slow wave sleep — replay the neural sequences of recent experience transmitting them to the neocortex for long-term storage. The hippocampus is critically vulnerable to stress — glucocorticoids suppress neurogenesis and damage dendritic arbors in the hippocampus corresponding to the memory impairment of chronic stress and PTSD. Hippocampal neurogenesis in the dentate gyrus — the ongoing birth of new neurons throughout adulthood — is promoted by exercise, environmental enrichment, and antidepressants and suppressed by stress, aging, and glucocorticoids. Kidney Jing recording in TCM — the Kidney's role as the repository of constitutional memory including ancestral patterns and life experience accumulated as Jing — corresponds to the hippocampus as the primary recorder of new experiential memory.

## Trisynaptic Circuit

- Entorhinal cortex provides the primary cortical input to the hippocampus carrying processed sensory information.
- Dentate gyrus performs pattern separation creating distinct sparse representations of similar inputs.
- CA3 performs pattern completion through recurrent connections allowing partial cues to retrieve full memories.
- CA1 compares current experience with CA3 predictions detecting novelty and encoding mismatches.

## Place and Grid Cells

- Place cells in CA1 and CA3 fire when the animal occupies a specific location — the place field.
- Grid cells in the entorhinal cortex fire in a hexagonal grid pattern providing the metric for spatial navigation.
- Head direction cells and border cells provide additional spatial information integrated in the hippocampal map.
- The cognitive map enables mental simulation of routes and spatial planning without physical movement.

## Memory Consolidation

- Sharp wave ripples during rest and slow wave sleep replay recent experience sequences.
- This replay transmits hippocampal memory traces to the neocortex for long-term storage — systems consolidation.
- Sleep deprivation impairs hippocampal-to-neocortical transfer producing deficits in long-term retention.
- Targeted memory reactivation — presenting learning-associated cues during slow wave sleep — enhances consolidation.
