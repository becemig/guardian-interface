# Feldenkrais Method

## Abstract

The Feldenkrais Method developed by Moshe Feldenkrais is a somatic education approach using movement to improve function, reduce pain, and expand self-awareness. Moshe Feldenkrais drew on principles from physics, neuroscience, developmental movement, and martial arts to create a system that works through the nervous system rather than muscles directly. Awareness Through Movement ATM lessons guide participants through sequences of gentle movement explorations that improve the quality and ease of movement through neurological learning. Functional Integration FI is the hands-on individual form where the practitioner uses touch to communicate directly with the student's nervous system. The Weber-Fechner law of differential sensitivity — that sensory discrimination improves as background stimulus decreases — provides the scientific principle underlying Feldenkrais's use of reduced effort to enhance proprioceptive sensitivity. The method cultivates a refined self-image through movement enabling genuine transformation of habitual neuromuscular patterns.

## Awareness Through Movement

- ATM lessons use verbal guidance to lead participants through gentle movement explorations.
- Movements are performed with minimal effort — less effort reduces proprioceptive noise and enhances sensitivity.
- Variations in direction, range, speed, and attention reveal habitual patterns and expand movement options.
- The learning occurs in the nervous system — new movement options become available through enhanced self-organization.

## Functional Integration

- FI uses the practitioner's hands to communicate directly with the student's nervous system.
- Touch is used as a language — not as manipulation or correction but as information about movement possibilities.
- The practitioner moves the student in ways that bypass habitual neuromuscular patterns.
- Students typically experience immediate changes in ease, range, and quality of movement.

## Weber-Fechner Principle

- The Weber-Fechner law states that differential sensitivity increases as background stimulus decreases.
- Using less effort in movement reduces proprioceptive background noise enabling finer sensory discrimination.
- This principle explains why Feldenkrais movements are performed gently — to enhance rather than overwhelm sensing.
- As movement ease increases the practitioner can introduce subtler and more refined distinctions.
