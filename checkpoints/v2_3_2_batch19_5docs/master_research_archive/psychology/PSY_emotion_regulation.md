# Emotion Regulation

## Abstract

Emotion regulation encompasses the processes by which individuals influence which emotions they have, when they have them, and how they experience and express them. James Gross's process model identifies five families of emotion regulation strategies targeting different points in the emotion generative process: situation selection, situation modification, attentional deployment, cognitive change, and response modulation. Cognitive reappraisal — changing the meaning of an emotional situation to alter its emotional impact — is the most adaptive strategy showing downstream benefits for well-being and relationships. Expressive suppression — inhibiting emotional expression while the emotion continues internally — shows downstream costs including increased physiological arousal and reduced social connection. Acceptance-based approaches from third-wave CBT allow emotional experience without suppression or escalation. HRV reflects emotional regulation capacity — higher resting HRV predicts better emotion regulation. The five organ spirits in TCM each governing a distinct aspect of emotional experience correspond to a comprehensive somatic model of emotion regulation.

## Process Model

- Situation selection involves approaching or avoiding situations based on their anticipated emotional impact.
- Attentional deployment redirects attention within a situation — distraction, concentration, or rumination.
- Cognitive change reappraises the meaning of a situation altering its emotional significance.
- Response modulation modifies physiological, experiential, or behavioral responses after emotion activation.

## Cognitive Reappraisal

- Reappraisal involves construing a potentially emotion-eliciting situation in a way that changes its emotional impact.
- Reappraisal reduces amygdala activity while maintaining prefrontal engagement — adaptive neural profile.
- Regular reappraisal use predicts higher well-being, better relationships, and lower depression.
- Reappraisal requires prefrontal resources — stress and cognitive load reduce reappraisal capacity.

## Acceptance

- Acceptance involves allowing emotional experience without judgment, suppression, or escalation.
- It reduces the secondary suffering of fighting against unwanted emotions while leaving primary experience unchanged.
- Mindfulness-based acceptance shows equivalent outcomes to reappraisal with different mechanism profiles.
- Both strategies are adaptive when flexibly applied to appropriate situations.
