# Bayesian Brain Hypothesis

## Abstract

Bayesian brain hypothesis — the framework proposing that the brain implements approximate Bayesian inference to combine prior beliefs about the world with likelihood functions derived from sensory evidence, computing posterior beliefs that optimally weight prior and sensory information according to their relative reliabilities, with the landmark demonstration by Ernst and Banks that human visual-haptic integration follows the statistically optimal maximum likelihood estimation rule exactly, and with Bayesian models successfully accounting for a wide range of perceptual phenomena including the ventriloquist effect, the rubber hand illusion, size-weight illusion, and the systematic distortions of perception produced by strong prior beliefs. Bayesian perception — the application of Bayes theorem to sensory processing in which the brain's posterior estimate of the state of the world combines a prior probability distribution reflecting learned statistical regularities of the environment with a likelihood function reflecting the probability of the observed sensory evidence given different world states, with the relative widths of prior and likelihood determining whether the percept is dominated by expectations or by current sensory data, and with perceptual illusions arising when strong priors overwhelm weak or ambiguous sensory evidence. Prior beliefs and likelihood — the two fundamental components of Bayesian inference in perception, with priors reflecting the brain's learned statistical model of the world including the prior probability of different object sizes, shapes, and locations, the prior expectation that light comes from above, and the prior expectation that objects move smoothly and continuously, while the likelihood reflects the sensory evidence available in the current sensory data, with the width of each distribution determining its contribution to the posterior estimate. Bayesian multisensory integration — the optimal combination of information from different sensory modalities according to their relative reliabilities, demonstrated by Ernst and Banks to follow the maximum likelihood estimation rule in which visual and haptic size estimates are combined with weights inversely proportional to their variances, producing a fused estimate more reliable than either modality alone, and with the ventriloquist effect arising when the brain infers that visual and auditory signals share a common cause and fuses them despite spatial discordance. The Wu Wei optimal prior minimal intervention correspondence in Daoist philosophy describes the principle of Wu Wei as acting in accordance with the natural flow of the Dao with minimal unnecessary intervention — a philosophical framework corresponding to the Bayesian brain's optimal prior that accurately reflects the true statistical structure of the world and therefore requires minimal updating correction.

## Bayes Theorem in Perception

- Prior: P(world state) — learned statistical model of environmental regularities.
- Likelihood: P(sensory data given world state) — current sensory evidence.
- Posterior: P(world state given sensory data) — prior times likelihood normalized.
- Optimal: posterior minimizes expected squared error — statistically optimal estimate.
