# Acupoint Theory and De Qi Sensation: Fascial Mechanoreception at Needle Sites

## Abstract

Acupoints are discrete locations on the body surface where needling produces a characteristic sensation called De Qi. De Qi encompasses feelings of heaviness, distension, aching, numbness, and propagation along the meridian pathway. Research demonstrates that acupoints correspond to fascial cleavage planes with high density of interstitial connective tissue receptors. Needling produces measurable mechanical deformation of fascial tissue, activates Piezo2 and interstitial receptors, and generates distinct neuroimaging signatures in the brain. Understanding De Qi mechanistically bridges TCM acupoint theory with contemporary fascial mechanobiology.

## Acupoint Anatomy

### Fascial Plane Correspondence

- 80 percent of acupoints correspond to sites where needles penetrate fascial cleavage planes between muscle compartments.
- These fascial planes contain dense networks of loose connective tissue with high concentrations of interstitial receptors.
- Acupoint locations cluster at sites of fascial convergence where multiple tissue planes intersect.
- This correspondence suggests meridian pathways follow fascial planes rather than discrete anatomical channels.

### Connective Tissue Winding

- Needle rotation during acupuncture produces measurable winding and mechanical deformation of collagen fibers.
- Langevin et al demonstrated that needle rotation winds collagen into a whorl pattern extending several centimeters from the needle tip.
- This mechanical deformation propagates through the fascial network activating receptors distant from the needle site.
- The propagated sensation of De Qi along the meridian pathway corresponds to this remote fascial activation.

### Receptor Activation

- Interstitial type III and IV afferents are the primary receptors activated during acupuncture needling.
- Piezo2 channels in fascial fibroblasts respond to the mechanical deformation produced by needling.
- Ruffini endings in deep fascia respond to the sustained tissue stretch produced by needle retention.
- This receptor profile explains the characteristic De Qi sensations of heaviness, distension, and dull aching.

## De Qi Sensation

### Qualitative Characteristics

- Heaviness and distension: most commonly reported, corresponds to sustained fascial mechanoreceptor activation.
- Aching and soreness: reflects activation of type III muscle afferents in periosteal and deep fascial tissue.
- Numbness: may reflect temporary C-fiber afferent saturation in densely innervated acupoint sites.
- Propagation: sensation traveling along the meridian pathway reflects fascial mechanical wave propagation.
- Warmth: local vasodilation via axon reflex and neuropeptide release at the needling site.

### Neuroimaging Findings

- De Qi sensation deactivates the default mode network and insula during needling.
- This pattern is opposite to the activation produced by non-acupoint needling or sharp pain stimulation.
- Default mode network deactivation during De Qi corresponds to a state of absorbed interoceptive awareness.
- The neuroimaging signature of De Qi overlaps with the brain state produced by mindfulness meditation.

## HRV and Autonomic Effects

- Acupuncture at points with strong vagal connections produces measurable HRV increases within minutes.
- PC-6 Neiguan needling consistently produces parasympathetic activation and HRV coherence enhancement.
- ST-36 Zusanli needling activates vagal afferents via the enteric nervous system.
- The autonomic effects of acupuncture are consistent with fascial interstitial receptor activation of vagal pathways.
