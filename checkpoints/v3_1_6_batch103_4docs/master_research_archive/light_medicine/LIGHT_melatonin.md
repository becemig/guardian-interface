# Melatonin as Systemic Hormone

## Abstract

Melatonin — the indolamine hormone produced by the pineal gland exclusively during darkness, serving as the chemical signal of night that coordinates the nocturnal physiological program of the entire organism — is one of the most evolutionarily ancient and phylogenetically conserved biological molecules, functioning as a chronobiotic hormone that communicates the duration of darkness to every tissue through MT1 and MT2 melatonin receptors and as a potent antioxidant with direct radical scavenging activity and indirect antioxidant actions through Nrf2 activation. Pineal melatonin synthesis — the light-regulated conversion of serotonin to N-acetylserotonin by arylalkylamine N-acetyltransferase AANAT and then to melatonin by hydroxyindole-O-methyltransferase HIOMT — is driven by the nocturnal increase in sympathetic input to the pineal gland from the SCN through the superior cervical ganglia, with AANAT being the rate-limiting enzyme whose activity is suppressed within minutes by light exposure through the retinohypothalamic tract. Melatonin receptor signaling — MT1 receptors mediating the acute suppression of SCN neuronal firing that produces the hypnotic and phase-shifting effects and MT2 receptors mediating the phase-shifting effects on the circadian clock — governs the downstream physiological responses to melatonin including reduction in core body temperature, induction of sleep propensity, suppression of cortisol, and the coordination of peripheral clock timing in organs throughout the body. Melatonin as chronobiotic — the use of exogenous melatonin to shift the circadian phase, treat jet lag and delayed sleep phase disorder, and improve sleep in shift workers — is based on the phase-shifting effects of melatonin on the SCN circadian clock through MT2 receptors, with melatonin most effective as a chronobiotic when taken at the appropriate phase of the circadian cycle corresponding to the rising phase of the endogenous melatonin rhythm. Melatonin antioxidant and anti-cancer actions — including direct scavenging of hydroxyl radical and peroxynitrite, induction of antioxidant enzymes through Nrf2 activation, and the anti-proliferative effects of melatonin on cancer cells through MT1-mediated suppression of growth factor signaling — provide the mechanistic basis for the epidemiological observations of increased cancer risk in shift workers with chronic melatonin suppression. The Kidney essence evening consolidation correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that the nocturnal hours are the time when the Kidney Jing is replenished and consolidated through deep sleep, corresponding to the melatonin-dependent nocturnal physiological program that governs tissue repair, immune function, and constitutional restoration.

## Pineal Synthesis Pathway

- Tryptophan: dietary precursor converted to 5-hydroxytryptophan and then serotonin.
- AANAT: rate-limiting enzyme activated by nocturnal sympathetic input from SCN.
- HIOMT: final step producing melatonin from N-acetylserotonin.
- Light suppression: photic signal from ipRGCs inhibits SCN sympathetic output within minutes.

## Melatonin Receptor Actions

- MT1: acute SCN firing suppression — hypnotic effect and amplitude reduction.
- MT2: phase-shifting of circadian clock — chronobiotic effect.
- Peripheral MT1 MT2: coordinate peripheral clock timing in heart liver kidney immune cells.
- Thermoregulation: melatonin reduces core body temperature — facilitates sleep onset.

## Antioxidant and Anti-Cancer

- Direct radical scavenger: hydroxyl radical peroxynitrite singlet oxygen.
- Nrf2 activation: induces SOD CAT GPx and glutathione synthesis.
- Anti-proliferative: MT1 suppresses growth factor receptor signaling in cancer cells.
- Immune enhancement: stimulates NK cell activity and cytotoxic T cell function.
