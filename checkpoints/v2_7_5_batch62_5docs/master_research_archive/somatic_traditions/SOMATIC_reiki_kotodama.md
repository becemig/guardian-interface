# Reiki Kotodama

## Abstract

Kotodama meaning word spirit or soul of language is the Japanese concept that spoken sounds carry spiritual power and can produce physical and energetic effects corresponding to their meaning and vibrational quality. In the original Usui Reiki Ryoho system the three primary symbols were used not primarily as visual symbols to draw or visualize but as kotodama — sacred sounds to be toned, chanted, or whispered in specific ways that activate their energetic qualities through vibrational resonance. The three primary Reiki kotodama correspond to the three main Reiki symbols: Choku Rei CKR is the power symbol — its kotodama activates concentrated Ki focus and grounding; Hon Sha Ze Sho Nen HSZSN is the distance symbol — its kotodama activates the connection across space, time, and the levels of being; Sei He Ki SHK is the mental-emotional symbol — its kotodama activates the clearing of mental and emotional patterns and the healing of the mind-body connection. Jumon is the Japanese term for the verbal incantations used in Reiki — the spoken activation phrases that accompany the symbols and kotodama in Reiki practice. The vibrational healing dimension of Reiki through kotodama connects it to the broader spectrum of sound healing traditions: Tibetan Buddhist mantra, Sanskrit Vedic chanting, Taoist sacred sound cultivation, and indigenous vocal healing practices all use the vibrational quality of specific sounds to produce healing effects on the physical, energetic, and spiritual dimensions. The neurophysiological mechanisms of sound healing include vagal nerve stimulation through vocalization, resonant frequency entrainment of body tissues, and the psychoacoustic effects of specific frequencies on brainwave states.

## Three Primary Kotodama

- Choku Rei CKR: power and focus kotodama — activates concentrated Ki and grounding presence.
- Sei He Ki SHK: mental-emotional kotodama — clears mental-emotional patterns and opens the mind-body connection.
- Hon Sha Ze Sho Nen HSZSN: distance and connection kotodama — activates connection across space, time, and being levels.
- Each kotodama is toned with specific breath, intention, and vocal quality to activate its corresponding quality.

## Vibrational Mechanisms

- Vocalization directly stimulates the vagus nerve through laryngeal vibration producing parasympathetic response.
- Specific frequencies entrain body tissue resonance — bones, organs, and water-containing tissues respond to sound.
- Chanting produces brainwave entrainment toward theta and alpha states associated with healing and receptivity.
- The intention embodied in sacred sound activates the practitioner's own Ki through psychophysiological coherence.

## Jumon Practice

- Jumon phrases are spoken or whispered as activation statements accompanying symbol work.
- The combination of visual symbol and spoken jumon creates a multisensory activation of the corresponding Reiki quality.
- Traditional Reiki practice used kotodama as the primary activation method — symbols were secondary.
- Restoring kotodama practice recovers the vibrational dimension that Western Reiki largely replaced with visual symbol work.
