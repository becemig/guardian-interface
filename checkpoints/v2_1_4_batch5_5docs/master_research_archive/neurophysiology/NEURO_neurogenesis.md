# Neurogenesis and Brain Plasticity

## Abstract

Adult neurogenesis — the birth of new neurons in the adult brain — occurs primarily in the hippocampal dentate gyrus and olfactory bulb. Exercise is the most potent known stimulator of adult hippocampal neurogenesis through BDNF brain-derived neurotrophic factor upregulation. New neurons in the hippocampus contribute to pattern separation, episodic memory, and mood regulation. Depression is associated with reduced hippocampal neurogenesis while antidepressants and exercise restore it. Kidney Marrow in TCM provides the constitutional substrate for brain renewal corresponding to the neurogenic niche and its constitutional requirements.

## Adult Hippocampal Neurogenesis

- New neurons are born in the subgranular zone of the hippocampal dentate gyrus throughout adult life.
- Approximately 700 new neurons are added to each human hippocampus daily under normal conditions.
- New neurons mature over 4 to 6 weeks becoming functionally integrated into hippocampal circuits.
- New neurons contribute specifically to pattern separation — distinguishing similar experiences and memories.

## BDNF and Exercise

- BDNF is the primary growth factor supporting neuronal survival, differentiation, and synaptic plasticity.
- Aerobic exercise produces the largest acute and chronic increases in hippocampal BDNF of any intervention.
- Running produces a 2 to 3 fold increase in hippocampal BDNF within days of initiating regular exercise.
- Tai Chi and Qigong produce BDNF increases through their aerobic, coordinative, and mind-body components.

## Neurogenesis and Mood

- Depression is associated with reduced hippocampal volume and impaired neurogenesis.
- Antidepressants require intact neurogenesis to produce their behavioral effects in animal models.
- Exercise-induced neurogenesis produces antidepressant effects through new neuron contribution to mood circuits.
- Qigong and Tai Chi demonstrate antidepressant effects consistent with neurogenesis-mediated mood improvement.
