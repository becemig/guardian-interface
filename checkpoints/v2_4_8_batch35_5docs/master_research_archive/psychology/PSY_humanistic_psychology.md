# Humanistic Psychology

## Abstract

Humanistic psychology emerged in the 1950s and 1960s as the third force in psychology alongside psychoanalysis and behaviorism, emphasizing human potential, meaning, freedom, and the primacy of subjective experience. Abraham Maslow's hierarchy of needs proposes that basic physiological and safety needs must be met before higher needs for belonging, esteem, and ultimately self-actualization can be pursued. Self-actualization — the realization of one's full human potential — is characterized by peak experiences, B-cognition being-cognition of the world as it is, creativity, and the full expression of individual uniqueness. Carl Rogers developed person-centered therapy based on three core conditions: unconditional positive regard — complete acceptance of the client without conditions, empathic understanding — accurate sensing of the client's inner world, and congruence — the therapist's genuine presence and authenticity. Rogers proposed that these three conditions are both necessary and sufficient for therapeutic growth. The actualizing tendency — the inherent drive of all living organisms toward growth and the full expression of their potential — is the foundational concept of person-centered therapy. Conditions of worth — the internalized standards that must be met to deserve love and acceptance — are the primary source of psychological distortion. Shen flourishing in TCM — the progressive actualization of the Heart-Mind's full luminous potential through cultivation and authentic expression — corresponds to the humanistic psychological project of self-actualization.

## Maslow's Hierarchy

- Physiological needs — food, water, shelter, sleep — must be met before higher needs become motivating.
- Safety needs — security, stability, freedom from fear — constitute the second tier of the hierarchy.
- Belonging and love needs — connection, intimacy, community — emerge when physiological and safety needs are met.
- Esteem needs — achievement, competence, recognition — precede the emergence of self-actualization motivation.

## Person-Centered Therapy

- Unconditional positive regard accepts the client completely without conditions or judgment.
- Empathic understanding accurately senses and reflects the client's inner world without interpretation.
- Congruence means the therapist is genuine and present — not hiding behind a professional role.
- These three conditions create the relational climate in which the actualizing tendency can operate freely.

## Actualizing Tendency

- The actualizing tendency is the inherent drive of all living organisms toward growth and full expression.
- It is blocked by conditions of worth — internalized standards that produce conditional self-acceptance.
- Person-centered therapy removes the blocks to the actualizing tendency rather than providing direction.
- The fully functioning person lives openly, trusts their organismic valuing process, and embraces experience.
