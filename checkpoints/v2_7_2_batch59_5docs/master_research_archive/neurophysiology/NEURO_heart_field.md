# Heart Electromagnetic Field

## Abstract

The heart generates the strongest electromagnetic field in the body — approximately 100 times stronger electrically and 5000 times stronger magnetically than the brain's field — producing a torus-shaped electromagnetic field that extends several feet beyond the body and can be detected by sensitive magnetometers. Rollin McCraty and the HeartMath Institute have systematically documented the physiological and psychological effects of this heart field and demonstrated that the coherence or incoherence of the heart's rhythm pattern — heart rate variability — reflects and affects the state of the entire physiological system. Psychophysiological coherence is the HeartMath concept for the state in which the heart rhythm pattern becomes highly ordered and sinusoidal, producing synchronized oscillations in the heart, brain, immune, and hormonal systems. Coherent heart rhythm states correlate with positive emotions — appreciation, compassion, love — and with improved cognitive performance, reduced stress, and enhanced immune function. The intrinsic cardiac nervous system — first comprehensively mapped by J. Andrew Armour — contains approximately 40,000 neurons organized into a complex processing network that operates independently of the brain, processes sensory information, and makes local decisions about cardiac function. This has been called the heart brain — a neural network capable of learning, memory, and independent decision-making. The heart communicates with the brain through four pathways: neurological through vagal and sympathetic afferents, biochemical through hormones and neurotransmitters including ANF, biophysical through pulse wave pressure and mechanical forces, and electromagnetic through the heart's field interacting with the brain's field.
