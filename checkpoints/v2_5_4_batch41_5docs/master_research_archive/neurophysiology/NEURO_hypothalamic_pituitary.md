# Hypothalamic-Pituitary Axis

## Abstract

The hypothalamic-pituitary axis is the master neuroendocrine control system integrating neural signals from the brain with hormonal signals from peripheral endocrine glands through a hierarchical cascade of releasing hormones, pituitary tropic hormones, and peripheral effector hormones regulated by negative feedback. The hypothalamus sits at the apex of this hierarchy — receiving inputs from the limbic system, brainstem, circadian clock, and metabolic sensors — integrating these signals into releasing hormone pulses secreted into the hypothalamic-pituitary portal system. The three primary axes are: the HPA hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis governing stress response through CRH-ACTH-cortisol, the HPT hypothalamic-pituitary-thyroid axis governing metabolism through TRH-TSH-thyroid hormone, and the HPG hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis governing reproduction through GnRH-LH/FSH-sex steroids. Negative feedback at multiple levels — rapid membrane-mediated glucocorticoid feedback at the hypothalamus and pituitary, and slow genomic feedback at all three levels — precisely regulates each axis. The suprachiasmatic nucleus drives circadian modulation of all three axes — cortisol peaks in the early morning, TSH peaks at night, and gonadotropins show complex pulsatile and circadian patterns. Disruption of axis regulation produces the clinical syndromes of Cushing's disease, hypothyroidism, hypogonadism, and related conditions. Kidney Yang root in TCM — the constitutional Yang fire of Mingmen that warms and activates all organ network functions — corresponds to the hypothalamic-pituitary axis as the neuroendocrine implementation of the Kidney's governing function over all physiological regulation.

## HPA Axis

- CRH from the paraventricular nucleus drives ACTH from the anterior pituitary driving cortisol from the adrenal cortex.
- Cortisol exerts negative feedback at hypothalamus, pituitary, and hippocampus regulating its own production.
- Chronic stress impairs hippocampal feedback producing hypercortisolemia and HPA dysregulation.
- Circadian cortisol peak in early morning corresponds to the Yang rising of morning in TCM temporal theory.

## HPT Axis

- TRH from the hypothalamus drives TSH from the anterior pituitary driving thyroid hormone from the thyroid gland.
- Thyroid hormone regulates metabolic rate, thermogenesis, cardiac function, and neurological development.
- Hypothyroidism with cold sensitivity, fatigue, and weight gain corresponds to Kidney Yang deficiency in TCM.
- The HPT axis responds to cold exposure, caloric restriction, and chronic illness with adaptive TSH changes.

## HPG Axis

- GnRH pulses from the hypothalamus drive LH and FSH from the anterior pituitary driving gonadal sex steroids.
- GnRH pulse frequency and amplitude determine the LH-to-FSH ratio and the pattern of gonadal stimulation.
- Reproductive suppression by chronic stress operates through CRH inhibition of GnRH pulse generator.
- The HPG axis governs sexual development, reproductive cycling, and the sex steroid effects on brain and behavior.
