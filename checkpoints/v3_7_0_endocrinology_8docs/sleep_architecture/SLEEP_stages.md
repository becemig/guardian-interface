# Sleep Stage Architecture

## Abstract

Human sleep is organized into repeating 90-minute ultradian cycles composed of distinct stages with unique electrophysiological signatures, neurochemical profiles, and functional roles. NREM sleep comprises three stages progressing from light sleep (N1) through sleep spindle consolidation (N2) to deep slow-wave sleep (N3/SWS). REM sleep is characterized by rapid eye movements, near-complete skeletal muscle atonia, vivid dreaming, and EEG patterns resembling waking. A typical night contains 4-6 complete cycles, with early cycles dominated by SWS and later cycles increasingly dominated by REM. The precise architecture of these stages is not random - it reflects the orchestrated execution of memory consolidation, glymphatic clearance, hormonal secretion, and emotional processing programs that require specific physiological conditions to run.

## NREM Stage N1 - Light Sleep

- Duration: 1-7 minutes per cycle
- EEG: theta waves (4-8 Hz) replacing alpha - slowing of cortical activity
- Hypnic jerks: sudden muscle contractions common at N1 onset - normal
- Hypnagogia: hallucinations at sleep onset - fragmentary images and sensations
- Easily awakened: noise or light returns to waking immediately
- Function: transition state - prepares thalamic gating for deeper sleep

## NREM Stage N2 - Sleep Spindles and K-Complexes

- Duration: 10-25 minutes per cycle - largest proportion of total sleep
- EEG: sleep spindles (12-15 Hz bursts, 0.5-3 seconds) and K-complexes
- Sleep spindles: generated by thalamic reticular nucleus - gate sensory input
- Spindle function: protect sleep continuity and consolidate motor and declarative memory
- K-complexes: large amplitude biphasic waves - response to environmental stimuli
- Heart rate and respiration slow and stabilize
- Temperature: core body temperature continues to fall

## NREM Stage N3 - Slow Wave Sleep (SWS)

- Duration: 20-40 minutes in early cycles, diminishes in later cycles
- EEG: delta waves (0.5-4 Hz) high amplitude slow oscillations
- Depth: hardest stage to awaken from - disorientation if awakened
- Glymphatic activity: highest during SWS - cerebrospinal fluid flushes interstitial waste
- Growth hormone: primary GH pulse occurs during first SWS episode
- Memory: hippocampal-neocortical memory transfer occurs during SWS
- Immune: cytokine production and immune restoration peak during SWS

## REM Sleep - Rapid Eye Movement

- Duration: 10-20 minutes early cycles, 30-60 minutes in final cycles
- EEG: low amplitude mixed frequency - resembles waking EEG (paradoxical sleep)
- Eye movements: rapid conjugate saccades - PGO waves from pons
- Atonia: skeletal muscle paralysis via brainstem glycinergic inhibition - prevents acting out dreams
- Dreaming: vivid narrative dreaming primarily in REM - emotional and associative content
- Norepinephrine absent: LC noradrenergic neurons silent during REM - emotional memory reprocessing
- Emotional processing: traumatic memories replayed without stress neurochemistry - desensitization

## Ultradian 90-Minute Cycle

- Cycle structure: N1 - N2 - N3 - N2 - REM - repeat
- Cycle count: 4-6 complete cycles per 8-hour night
- Early night bias: cycles 1-2 dominated by SWS - physical restoration priority
- Late night bias: cycles 4-6 dominated by REM - psychological restoration priority
- Sleep truncation: losing final 2 hours eliminates 60-70 percent of total REM
- TCM parallel: ultradian cycle maps to Wei Qi 25-cycle diurnal rhythm through organ clock
