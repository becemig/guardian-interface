# Qigong Theory

## Abstract

Qigong the cultivation of Qi is the foundational practice system of Chinese medicine and Taoist self-cultivation — a comprehensive discipline of body, breath, and mind training that refines and develops the three treasures San Bao of Jing essence, Qi vital force, and Shen spirit through sustained systematic practice. The three regulations San Tiao are the universal structural principles of all Qigong practice: regulating the body Tiao Shen through specific postures, alignments, and movements, regulating the breath Tiao Xi through specific breathing patterns, and regulating the mind Tiao Xin through specific mental states and focal attention. The Dan Tian cinnabar fields are the three primary energy centers of the body: the lower Dan Tian two inches below the navel as the seat of Jing and the foundation of physical vitality, the middle Dan Tian at the heart center as the seat of Qi and emotional integration, and the upper Dan Tian at the third eye as the seat of Shen and spiritual awareness. Nei Gong internal work is the deeper dimension of Qigong practice that works directly with internal Qi cultivation rather than external movement — developing the capacity to feel, move, and refine Qi through directed awareness. Wai Gong external work emphasizes the external form and movement sequences. The refinement sequence Jing hua wei Qi, Qi hua wei Shen, Shen hua wei Xu describes the progressive sublimation of essence into increasingly subtle and refined energy. Modern research documents Qigong effects on HRV, cortisol, immune function, and inflammatory markers.

## Three Regulations

- Regulating the body establishes correct structural alignment enabling unobstructed Qi flow through the meridians.
- Regulating the breath synchronizes the respiratory rhythm with Qi movement through specific breath patterns.
- Regulating the mind establishes the mental state of Yi — focused yet relaxed intention that guides Qi movement.
- The three regulations must be integrated simultaneously for genuine Qigong effect rather than mere exercise.

## Dan Tian Cultivation

- Lower Dan Tian below the navel is the root center storing Jing and providing the foundation of physical vitality.
- Middle Dan Tian at the heart is the integrating center processing emotional experience as Qi.
- Upper Dan Tian at the third eye is the crown center where refined Shen awareness becomes luminous.
- Cultivation progresses from establishing the lower Dan Tian foundation upward through the three centers.

## Jing-Qi-Shen Refinement

- Jing essence as the densest treasure provides the material basis for Qi generation through cultivation.
- Qi as the vital force mediates between the physical Jing and the spiritual Shen in the refinement process.
- Shen as the spirit is the most refined treasure — the luminous awareness that is the fruit of cultivation.
- The refinement sequence progressively sublimates denser energy into subtler form through sustained practice.

## TCM Correspondence

- Dan Tian centers correspond to the three burning spaces of the Triple Burner as energetic processing zones.
- Jing-Qi-Shen refinement corresponds to the progressive cultivation of constitutional essence toward spiritual awareness.
- The three regulations correspond to the TCM principle that body, breath, and mind must align for Qi to flow freely.
- Governing and Conception Vessels as the microcosmic orbit are the primary channels activated in Qigong cultivation.
