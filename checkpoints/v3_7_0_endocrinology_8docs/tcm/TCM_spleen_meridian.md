# Spleen Meridian: Earth Element, Transformation, and Muscle Governance

## Abstract

The Spleen organ system in Traditional Chinese Medicine is the central axis of postnatal Qi production, governing the transformation and transportation of food and fluids into usable Qi and Blood. The Spleen houses the Yi intellect and intention, governs the muscles and flesh, and controls the Blood within the vessels. The Spleen meridian ascends from the great toe along the inner leg and abdomen to the chest. Spleen function underpins the physical capacity for sustained practice, muscular endurance, and the quality of Yi intention that drives internal movement.

## Spleen Functions in TCM

### Transformation and Transportation

- The Spleen transforms food and drink into Gu Qi grain essence which combines with air Qi in the Lung to form postnatal Qi.
- The Spleen transports nutrients and fluids to all organ systems and tissues throughout the body.
- Strong Spleen function produces abundant Qi and Blood supporting vigorous physical and mental activity.
- Weak Spleen function produces fatigue, poor appetite, loose stools, and accumulation of Dampness.

### Yi Intellect and Intention

- The Yi is the intellect housed in the Spleen governing thought, concentration, memory, and directed intention.
- In internal arts practice Yi is the directing intelligence that leads Qi through the body.
- The principle Yi leads Qi, Qi leads Blood expresses the Spleen-mediated relationship between intention and physical movement.
- Overthinking and excessive mental activity consume Spleen Qi and deplete the Yi capacity for clear intention.

### Muscle and Flesh Governance

- The Spleen governs the muscles and flesh throughout the body via Qi and Blood nourishment.
- Strong Spleen Qi produces well-developed, responsive muscles with good tone and endurance.
- Deficient Spleen Qi produces muscle weakness, easy fatigue, and poor recovery from exertion.
- The practical capacity for sustained Tai Chi practice depends fundamentally on Spleen Qi sufficiency.

### Blood Containment

- The Spleen holds Blood within the vessels preventing abnormal bleeding.
- Spleen Qi deficiency can produce easy bruising, heavy menstrual bleeding, and subcutaneous hemorrhage.
- This function complements Heart Blood governance in maintaining circulatory integrity.

## Meridian Pathway

- Originates at SP-1 Yinbai on the medial great toe.
- Ascends along the medial foot and inner ankle.
- Continues up the inner leg and thigh parallel to the Liver meridian but anterior to it.
- Enters the abdomen and ascends to the chest terminating at SP-21 Dabao on the lateral chest.
- Internal branch connects to Spleen, Stomach, Heart, and tongue.

## Key Acupoints

- SP-1 Yinbai: Jing-well point, great toe, uterine bleeding, nightmares, mental disorders.
- SP-3 Taibai: Source point, medial foot, digestive disorders, heavy limbs, Spleen deficiency.
- SP-6 Sanyinjiao: Meeting of three Yin meridians, above ankle, digestive, gynecological, and sleep disorders.
- SP-9 Yinlingquan: He-sea point, inner knee, edema, urinary disorders, Dampness accumulation.
- SP-10 Xuehai: Sea of Blood, above knee, Blood disorders, skin conditions, menstrual irregularity.
- SP-21 Dabao: Great Luo of Spleen, lateral chest, whole body pain, weakness of limbs.

## Convergence with Internal Arts

- Yi intention cultivated in Tai Chi and Qigong directly strengthens Spleen function through engaged directed awareness.
- The emphasis on relaxed muscle tone in Tai Chi avoids Spleen Qi depletion from excessive muscular effort.
- Post-practice fatigue indicates Spleen Qi depletion and signals need for rest and nourishment.
- SP-6 Sanyinjiao is among the most clinically significant acupoints linking Spleen, Liver, and Kidney Yin systems.
