# Liver Qi Stagnation

## Abstract

Liver Qi stagnation is the most commonly diagnosed pattern in contemporary clinical TCM practice reflecting the epidemic of emotional constraint, chronic stress, and sedentary lifestyle in modern populations. The Liver governs the free and smooth flow of Qi throughout the body and is particularly sensitive to emotional repression, frustration, and unresolved anger. Liver Qi stagnation produces a characteristic clinical presentation of hypochondriac distension, emotional volatility, sighing, premenstrual tension, and digestive irregularity. Prolonged Liver Qi stagnation generates Heat producing Liver Fire while impeding Spleen function producing Dampness and Phlegm. Treatment emphasizes coursing the Liver and rectifying Qi through movement, emotional expression, and Shu Gan regulating herbs.

## Pathomechanism

- Emotional constraint particularly suppressed anger, frustration, and resentment is the primary cause.
- Sedentary lifestyle impairs the natural movement of Qi through the sinew channels governed by the Liver.
- Irregular eating, overwork, and constitutional Liver excess contribute to Qi stagnation.
- The Liver Qi stagnation pattern is self-reinforcing — stagnation impairs emotional processing which produces more stagnation.

## Clinical Presentation

- Hypochondriac distension or pain along the costal margins where the Liver and Gallbladder meridians run.
- Emotional volatility with irritability, frustration, and tendency to anger or depression.
- Sighing as a spontaneous attempt to move stuck Liver Qi through the diaphragm.
- Premenstrual tension, breast distension, and menstrual irregularity from Liver Blood and Qi impairment.
- Digestive irregularity including bloating, belching, and alternating bowel habits from Liver invading Spleen.

## Transmission Patterns

- Liver Qi stagnation generating Heat produces Liver Fire with severe irritability, headache, and red eyes.
- Liver Qi stagnation impeding Spleen produces Dampness and Phlegm with fatigue and digestive dysfunction.
- Liver Qi stagnation impeding Blood produces Blood stasis with fixed pain and menstrual clots.
- Liver Qi stagnation disturbing Heart produces insomnia, palpitations, and anxiety.

## Treatment Principles

- Course the Liver and rectify Qi — Shu Gan Li Qi is the primary treatment principle.
- Vigorous aerobic movement is the most effective self-treatment for Liver Qi stagnation.
- Tai Chi and Qigong specifically address Liver Qi stagnation through whole-body Qi movement and emotional release.
- Chai Hu Shu Gan San and Xiao Yao San are the classical herbal formulas for Liver Qi stagnation.
