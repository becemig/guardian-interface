# TCM Herbs and Microbiome

## Abstract

The gut microbiome is the primary site of action for many TCM herbs — particularly the tonic and digestive herbs — whose bioactive compounds are produced not directly from the herb's constituents but from the microbial transformation of those constituents in the gut. This microbiome-mediated transformation explains why the same herb can produce different effects in different individuals depending on their microbiome composition, and why traditional fermentation processes and preparation methods dramatically alter herb activity. Astragalus Huang Qi polysaccharides are among the most thoroughly studied prebiotic TCM compounds — they selectively promote Bifidobacterium and Lactobacillus species, increase short-chain fatty acid SCFA production, strengthen intestinal barrier function, and modulate immune activation. These effects on the microbiome correspond precisely to the TCM Huang Qi action of tonifying Spleen Qi, consolidating Wei Qi, and strengthening the body's defensive capacity. Licorice Gan Cao glycyrrhizin and glycyrrhizinic acid are metabolized by gut bacteria to 18-beta-glycyrrhetinic acid — the primary active form — and modulate the microbiome through anti-inflammatory and prebiotic effects. Short-chain fatty acids butyrate, propionate, acetate produced by microbial fermentation of herbal polysaccharides are the primary signaling molecules of the gut-brain axis — they activate vagal afferents, modulate enteric serotonin production, regulate blood-brain barrier permeability, and directly affect BDNF expression in the hippocampus. pH balance is intimately connected to microbiome composition — dysbiosis produces acid-generating bacterial overgrowth while a healthy microbiome maintains the slightly alkaline intestinal environment corresponding to the TCM Spleen-Stomach harmony pattern. The gut-brain axis through vagal afferents connects microbiome health directly to HPA axis regulation, mood, cognition, and immune function.

## Prebiotic Polysaccharides

- Huang Qi polysaccharides selectively promote Bifidobacterium and Lactobacillus increasing SCFA production.
- Dang Shen Codonopsis polysaccharides restore microbiome diversity after dysbiosis corresponding to Spleen Qi restoration.
- Ling Zhi Reishi beta-glucans modulate gut immune Peyer's patches and strengthen intestinal barrier function.
- Fu Ling Poria polysaccharides promote beneficial bacterial species while inhibiting pathogenic overgrowth.

## SCFAs and Gut-Brain Axis

- Butyrate is the primary SCFA fuel source for colonocytes and regulates epigenetic histone acetylation throughout the body.
- Propionate and acetate reach the brain through vagal afferents and portal circulation affecting mood and cognition.
- SCFA production from herbal polysaccharides directly upregulates BDNF in the hippocampus through epigenetic mechanisms.
- The gut serotonin system produces 95 percent of the body's serotonin — microbiome health directly determines serotonergic tone.

## pH Balance

- Healthy Spleen-Stomach harmony in TCM corresponds to maintenance of slightly alkaline intestinal pH.
- Damp-heat patterns in TCM correspond to acid-generating dysbiotic overgrowth altering intestinal pH downward.
- Bitter cold herbs Huang Lian, Huang Bai with berberine correct dysbiosis restoring healthy pH through antimicrobial action.
- Apple cider vinegar, fermented foods, and sour TCM herbs regulate gastric acid and intestinal pH through organic acid buffering.

## TCM Correspondence

- Spleen governing transformation and transportation corresponds to the microbiome performing the actual biochemical transformation of nutrients and herbs.
- Damp-phlegm accumulation pattern corresponds to dysbiosis with increased intestinal permeability and inflammatory LPS translocation.
- Spleen Qi deficiency corresponds to reduced microbiome diversity and SCFA production impairing gut-brain axis signaling.
- Large Intestine governing elimination corresponds to the microbiome's role in determining what is absorbed versus eliminated.
