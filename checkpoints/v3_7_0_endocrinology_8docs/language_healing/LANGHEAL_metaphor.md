# Metaphor and Medicine

## Abstract

Metaphor and medicine — the recognition following George Lakoff and Mark Johnson's conceptual metaphor theory that metaphor is not merely a literary device but the fundamental cognitive mechanism through which abstract domains including health, illness, and the body are understood in terms of more concrete experiential domains, with medical practice saturated with metaphors including the body as machine, illness as enemy, the immune system as army, the heart as pump, and the brain as computer — metaphors that are not neutral descriptions but active framings that shape clinical reasoning, patient experience, treatment choices, and the emotional response to illness. Conceptual metaphor theory — Lakoff and Johnson's demonstration that abstract reasoning in domains including time, causation, mind, and morality is systematically structured by conceptual metaphors mapping the structure of concrete source domains onto abstract target domains, with metaphors like argument is war, time is money, and ideas are food not merely decorative but structuring the way people reason about and act within those domains, with the embodied grounding of metaphor in sensorimotor experience providing the cognitive basis for abstract thought. Medical metaphors and treatment — the experimental evidence that the metaphors used to describe illness influence patient and clinician behavior, with cancer framed as an enemy to be fought producing more aggressive treatment preferences and greater psychological distress than cancer framed as a journey to be navigated, with the battle metaphor for cancer associated with guilt at disease progression and a sense of personal failure, and with chronic pain framed as a signal to be respected producing different coping responses than pain framed as a battle to be won. Embodied metaphor physiology — the neuroimaging evidence that processing metaphors activates the sensorimotor regions corresponding to their source domains, with texture metaphors activating somatosensory cortex, temperature metaphors activating insula temperature areas, and action metaphors activating motor cortex, suggesting that metaphor comprehension partially reinstates the embodied sensorimotor states that ground the metaphor, providing a neural basis for the physiological effects of healing metaphors and therapeutic imagery. The Classical Medical Image Resonance correspondence in TCM describes the rich system of natural metaphors embedded in classical Chinese medical theory including Qi as flowing water, the meridians as rivers and channels, the organs as officials in a kingdom, and Wind as the pathogenic force of rapid change — a system of embodied metaphors that shapes clinical perception, diagnostic reasoning, and the therapeutic imagination of both practitioner and patient.

## Conceptual Metaphor Examples

- Argument is war: attacks positions defends claims shoots down ideas.
- Time is money: spend save waste invest borrow time.
- Mind is machine: mental breakdown processing power shutting down.
- Body as machine: broken down needs repair worn out parts replacement.

## Medical Metaphor Effects

- Battle metaphor: aggressive treatment preference psychological distress guilt at progression.
- Journey metaphor: acceptance adaptive coping narrative meaning.
- War on cancer: failure framing when disease progresses — personal defeat.
- Alternative frames: siege negotiation coexistence — different clinical implications.

## Embodied Metaphor Neural Basis

- Texture metaphors: activate somatosensory cortex S1.
- Temperature metaphors: activate insula temperature processing areas.
- Action metaphors: activate premotor and motor cortex.
- Grounding: metaphor comprehension reinstates source domain sensorimotor states.
