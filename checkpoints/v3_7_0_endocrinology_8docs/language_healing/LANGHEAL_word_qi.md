# Word as Qi Transmission

## Abstract

Word as Qi transmission — the investigation of how spoken and written language does not merely describe reality but actively creates, transforms, and transmits states between people, with J.L. Austin's speech act theory establishing the distinction between constative utterances that describe states of affairs and performative utterances that enact what they state, with the extreme case of Walter Cannon's voodoo death demonstrating that the social pronouncement of a death curse can produce actual physiological death through the nocebo mechanism of profound autonomic collapse, and with the healing traditions of blessing, prayer, and therapeutic intention representing the positive counterpart of this capacity of language to transmit states with physiological consequences. Speech acts and social reality — J.L. Austin's classification of speech acts into locutionary acts of saying something, illocutionary acts that perform social functions including promising, declaring, warning, and blessing through the act of saying, and perlocutionary acts that produce effects in the listener beyond the speech act itself, with John Searle extending this to the construction of social reality through institutional facts that exist only because communities of speakers collectively accept their existence — including money, marriage, property, and medical diagnosis. Performative language — the category of speech acts that do not describe but enact — I now pronounce you married, I hereby declare, I promise, I forgive — that produce social realities through the socially authorized act of saying, with medical diagnosis as a performative act that transforms a person's social identity and self-understanding through the authoritative speech act of naming their condition, with profound consequences for illness experience and trajectory. Voodoo death and nocebo — Walter Cannon's 1942 documentation of voodoo death in which individuals pronounced dead by socially authorized curse-makers in traditional cultures die within days through profound parasympathetic collapse and dehydration, representing the most extreme nocebo effect demonstrating that socially transmitted language can produce fatal physiological effects through the nervous system's transduction of social meaning into autonomic and endocrine responses. The Ming Naming Power Actualization correspondence in TCM describes the classical Chinese understanding that names have power — that the proper naming of a condition is already a step toward its treatment, that the naming of Qi stagnation in the Liver already implies the therapeutic direction — a correspondence with the speech act theory understanding that medical diagnosis is a performative act that partially constitutes the illness it names.

## Speech Act Theory

- Locutionary: the act of saying something with meaning and reference.
- Illocutionary: performing a social act through saying — promising warning blessing.
- Perlocutionary: producing effects in listener beyond the speech act itself.
- Institutional reality: social facts constituted by collective acceptance of declarations.

## Medical Diagnosis as Performance

- Diagnosis: performative act transforming person into patient with named condition.
- Identity change: diagnostic label changes self-understanding and social role.
- Illness behavior: diagnosis activates culturally scripted illness behavior patterns.
- Naming power: correct diagnosis brings relief — the name reduces uncertainty anxiety.

## Voodoo Death Evidence

- Cannon documentation: death within days of curse pronouncement in multiple cultures.
- Mechanism: profound parasympathetic activation leading to hypotension and circulatory failure.
- Modern parallels: nocebo deaths documented in clinical settings.
- Social mediation: requires belief in curse authority and community witness.

- Naming the pattern as therapeutic act corresponds to the performative power of correct diagnosis to initiate healing.
