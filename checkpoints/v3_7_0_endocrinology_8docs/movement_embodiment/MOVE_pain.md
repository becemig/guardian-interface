# Pain Neuroscience and Somatic Experience

## Abstract

Pain is not a faithful readout of tissue damage but a protective output of the brain based on its assessment of threat to body integrity, integrating nociceptive signals with contextual, emotional, cognitive, and social information. Gate control theory established that pain transmission is modulated at the spinal dorsal horn by descending and peripheral inputs. Central sensitization describes the amplification of pain processing in the CNS through synaptic potentiation, reduced inhibition, and glial activation, explaining chronic pain that persists beyond tissue healing. Diffuse noxious inhibitory controls (DNIC) reveal endogenous pain inhibition systems that are impaired in chronic pain conditions. Trigger points are hyperirritable foci within taut fascial bands that produce referred pain in predictable patterns. TCM Bi syndrome provides a sophisticated differential diagnosis framework for pain based on Wind Cold Damp and Heat invasion patterns.

## Gate Control Theory

- Melzack and Wall 1965: spinal dorsal horn gates modulate pain transmission
- Large fiber activation: A-beta mechanoreceptor activity closes gate - reduces pain
- Small fiber activation: C and A-delta nociceptor activity opens gate - increases pain
- Descending control: brain sends signals down to open or close gate
- Clinical applications: TENS, massage, vibration all activate large fibers - close gate
- Acupuncture: needle stimulation activates A-delta and A-beta fibers - gate modulation

## Central Sensitization

- Definition: amplification of neural signaling in CNS producing hypersensitivity to pain
- Wind-up: repeated C fiber stimulation progressively amplifies dorsal horn neuron response
- LTP: long-term potentiation at dorsal horn synapses - NMDA receptor dependent
- Allodynia: non-painful stimulus produces pain - central amplification
- Hyperalgesia: painful stimulus produces exaggerated pain response
- Glial activation: microglia and astrocytes release pro-inflammatory cytokines amplifying pain
- Fibromyalgia: paradigmatic central sensitization syndrome - widespread pain without tissue damage
- Treatment: graded exposure, pain neuroscience education, sleep, exercise, stress reduction

## Descending Modulation

- PAG: periaqueductal gray - primary descending pain control center
- RVM: rostral ventromedial medulla - relay for PAG to spinal cord
- Endorphins: PAG stimulation releases endogenous opioids - analgesia
- Serotonin and NE: descending pathways use 5-HT and NE to inhibit dorsal horn
- SNRIs: duloxetine and venlafaxine enhance descending inhibition - effective for chronic pain
- Emotion-pain interaction: fear and depression reduce descending inhibition - amplify pain
- Acupuncture: activates PAG-RVM descending inhibition via endorphin release

## DNIC - Diffuse Noxious Inhibitory Controls

- Mechanism: painful stimulus in one body region inhibits pain in another - pain inhibits pain
- Pathway: spinal cord to brainstem to spinal cord - supraspinal loop
- Clinical test: conditioned pain modulation (CPM) - cold pressor test reduces test pain
- Impairment: CPM deficits found in fibromyalgia, IBS, chronic low back pain
- Acupuncture: distal point needling activates DNIC - explains remote analgesic effects

## Trigger Points and Referred Pain

- Trigger point: hyperirritable spot in taut band of skeletal muscle
- Active trigger point: spontaneously refers pain to distant site
- Latent trigger point: pain only on palpation - reduces range of motion
- Mechanism: motor end plate dysfunction - sustained ACh release - local ischemia
- Referred pain: convergence of afferent inputs at dorsal horn - brain misattributes source
- Trapezius trigger point: refers to temporal headache and jaw
- Acupuncture Ashi points: correspond closely to myofascial trigger point locations

## Pain Catastrophizing and Interoception

- Catastrophizing: magnification, rumination, helplessness - strongest psychological predictor of chronic pain
- PFC regulation: prefrontal cortex dampens amygdala pain response - reduced in catastrophizers
- Interoception: accurate body awareness reduces pain catastrophizing
- Nocebo: negative expectation increases pain - physiological mechanism via CCK
- Placebo: positive expectation reduces pain - endogenous opioid and cannabinoid release
- Pain neuroscience education: reconceptualizing pain as protective reduces threat - reduces pain

## TCM Bi Syndrome

- Bi: obstruction - pathogenic factors obstruct meridian flow causing pain
- Wind Bi: moving pain - location changes - Wind predominant
- Cold Bi: severe fixed pain - worse with cold - Cold predominant - vascular spasm
- Damp Bi: heavy fixed pain with swelling - worse in damp weather - inflammatory
- Heat Bi: hot red swollen joints - Heat predominant - acute inflammatory arthritis
- Bone Bi: chronic deep pain - Kidney Jing deficiency - degenerative joint disease
- Treatment: dispel pathogenic factor and restore meridian flow - point selection by type
