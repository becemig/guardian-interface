# Acupuncture Pain Mechanisms

## Abstract

Acupuncture pain mechanisms — the investigation of the biological processes through which acupuncture needle insertion at specific acupuncture points produces analgesia, including the local release of adenosine at needle sites activating A1 receptors on sensory afferents, the activation of A-delta afferents by needle manipulation producing segmental inhibition of nociceptive C fiber input through gate control, the activation of descending pain inhibitory pathways including the PAG-RVM endorphin system through the De Qi needle sensation, and the central modulation of brain pain processing networks including deactivation of the default mode network and activation of the descending inhibitory system demonstrated by fMRI neuroimaging. De Qi sensation neuroscience — the classical acupuncture needle sensation of heaviness, distension, soreness, and tingling that accompanies correct needle insertion and manipulation, associated with the activation of deep tissue mechanoreceptors in the connective tissue fascia and the winding of connective tissue fibers around the rotating needle demonstrated by Langevin's research, with De Qi associated with better clinical outcomes and with the A-delta afferent activation produced by De Qi being the likely mechanism of segmental and supraspinal analgesia. Acupuncture endorphin release — Jiesheng Han's systematic research demonstrating that different frequencies of electroacupuncture stimulation release different endogenous opioid peptides, with 2 Hz stimulation preferentially releasing enkephalin and beta-endorphin in supraspinal sites, 100 Hz stimulation preferentially releasing dynorphin in the spinal cord, and 2/100 Hz alternating stimulation releasing all three providing the broadest analgesic coverage, with the analgesic effects of electroacupuncture blocked by naloxone confirming opioid mediation. fMRI acupuncture brain effects — Vitaly Napadow's neuroimaging studies demonstrating that acupuncture at real points produces deactivation of the default mode network and limbic structures associated with pain affect while sham acupuncture at non-acupuncture points produces activation of these structures, suggesting that real acupuncture specifically engages the descending inhibitory system while sham acupuncture engages pain-related affective circuits. The Needle Qi Arrival channel opening correspondence in TCM describes the classical requirement for De Qi needle sensation indicating that Qi has arrived at the needle and the channel has been opened — a clinical indicator of correct needling corresponding to the modern evidence that De Qi sensation predicts better analgesic outcomes through A-delta afferent activation and connective tissue mechanoreceptor engagement.

## De Qi Mechanism

- Connective tissue: needle rotation winds fascia fibers — mechanoreceptor activation.
- A-delta afferents: De Qi sensation carried by A-delta and C fibers.
- Gate control: A-delta activation inhibits C fiber nociception at dorsal horn.
- Outcome prediction: De Qi sensation predicts better analgesic outcomes.

## Endorphin Frequencies

- 2 Hz: enkephalin beta-endorphin — supraspinal — mu delta receptors.
- 100 Hz: dynorphin — spinal — kappa receptors.
- 2/100 Hz alternating: all three peptides — broadest analgesic coverage.
- Naloxone reversal: confirms opioid mediation of electroacupuncture analgesia.

## fMRI Brain Effects

- Real acupuncture: deactivates DMN and limbic pain affect areas.
- Sham acupuncture: activates limbic pain affect areas — different mechanism.
- Descending inhibitory: real acupuncture engages PAG-RVM inhibitory system.
- Specific points: ST36 LI4 PC6 show distinct but overlapping brain activation patterns.

## TCM Correspondence

- Needle Qi arrival De Qi corresponds to A-delta afferent activation and connective tissue mechanoreceptor engagement predicting analgesic outcome.
- Opening channels with needling corresponds to gate control segmental inhibition of nociceptive C fiber input by A-delta activation.
- Point specificity in TCM corresponds to fMRI evidence of point-specific brain activation patterns.
- Tonifying and dispersing needle technique corresponds to different electroacupuncture frequencies releasing different endogenous opioid peptides.
