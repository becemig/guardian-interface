# Default Mode Network Disruption

## Abstract

Default mode network disruption — the primary neuroimaging finding of psychedelic neuroscience research demonstrating that psilocybin, LSD, DMT, and other serotonergic psychedelics powerfully suppress activity and disrupt the functional connectivity of the default mode network, the midline cortical network governing self-referential processing, autobiographical memory, and the narrative self, with the degree of DMN disruption correlating with the intensity of ego dissolution and the therapeutic outcome in depression treatment — provides the neural mechanism for understanding how psychedelics produce their profound alterations of self-experience and their therapeutic effects through temporary dissolution of the rigid self-referential processing patterns that characterize depression, addiction, and PTSD. Default mode network and self-referential processing — the resting-state brain network comprising the medial prefrontal cortex, posterior cingulate cortex, angular gyrus, and hippocampus that is active during self-referential thinking, mind-wandering, autobiographical memory retrieval, and social cognition, and whose hyperactivity is the most consistent neuroimaging finding in major depression, with rumination as a form of excessive self-referential DMN processing. REBUS theory — Robin Carhart-Harris and Karl Friston's Relaxed Beliefs Under Psychedelics model proposing that psychedelics work by flattening the precision-weighting of high-level priors in the Bayesian predictive coding hierarchy, allowing sensory information and low-level neural signals to have greater influence on perception and cognition than usual, producing the loosening of fixed belief structures, habitual thought patterns, and pathologically entrenched self-models that constitutes the therapeutic mechanism. Entropy brain model — Carhart-Harris' earlier proposal that psychedelics increase the entropy of brain activity, measured by increases in functional connectivity complexity, signal diversity, and the repertoire of brain states accessed, with the primary consciousness of the psychedelic state corresponding to a higher entropy mode that underlies ordinary waking consciousness, and with the low entropy of depressed rumination and addiction cravings being disrupted by the high-entropy psychedelic state. The ego dissolution Wu Wei correspondence in TCM describes the classical Daoist understanding of Wu Wei non-self action as the state in which the rigid ego's constant effortful doing dissolves into the spontaneous action of the Dao — a philosophical correspondence with the ego dissolution produced by psychedelic DMN suppression that allows the individual to experience identity beyond the constructed narrative self.

## DMN Suppression

- Posterior cingulate cortex: most consistently suppressed region — hub of self-referential processing.
- Medial prefrontal cortex: suppressed — self-relevant evaluation and rumination.
- Functional connectivity: within-DMN connectivity reduced — network disintegration.
- Dose-response: DMN suppression correlates with subjective ego dissolution intensity.

## REBUS Model

- Predictive coding: brain as prediction machine — high-level priors govern perception.
- Psychedelic effect: flattens prior precision — sensory signals gain relative influence.
- Therapeutic: rigid depressive priors loosen — new belief formation enabled.
- Mystical state: complete flattening of self-model priors — pure experience without interpreter.

## Entropy Brain

- Normal consciousness: moderate entropy — structured but flexible processing.
- Depression: low entropy — rigid attractor states rumination.
- Psychedelic state: high entropy — maximum repertoire of brain states.
- Therapeutic window: temporarily high entropy disrupts low-entropy pathological attractors.
