# Basal Ganglia Motor Circuits

## Abstract

The basal ganglia are a collection of subcortical nuclei — striatum, globus pallidus, subthalamic nucleus, and substantia nigra — that form recurrent circuits with the cortex and thalamus governing action selection, habit formation, and the suppression of competing motor programs. John Mink proposed the focused selection model — the basal ganglia select desired motor programs by releasing the thalamic brake on cortex while simultaneously inhibiting competing programs. The direct pathway through the striatum facilitates desired actions while the indirect pathway suppresses competing actions. Dopamine from the substantia nigra pars compacta modulates striatal output — loss of nigral dopamine in Parkinson's disease produces hypokinesia, rigidity, and tremor by disrupting the action selection balance. Ann Graybiel demonstrated that the basal ganglia support habit chunking — packaging sequences of actions into automated units retrieved as single items. Yi decisiveness — the Spleen spirit's capacity to commit to a course of action and execute it without vacillation — corresponds to the basal ganglia action selection function enabling decisive motor commitment.

## Direct and Indirect Pathways

- The direct pathway from striatum to globus pallidus interna to thalamus facilitates desired actions.
- The indirect pathway through globus pallidus externa and subthalamic nucleus suppresses competing programs.
- Balanced direct and indirect pathway activity enables precise action selection with suppression of alternatives.
- Dopamine potentiates the direct pathway and suppresses the indirect pathway biasing toward action.

## Habit Formation

- The striatum supports the chunking of action sequences into automated habits through dopamine-mediated learning.
- Habit learning transfers control from prefrontal cortex to the striatum enabling automatic execution.
- Once habitual the action sequence is executed without ongoing conscious monitoring or intention.
- Habit disruption requires reinstatement of prefrontal control — difficult when habits are strongly consolidated.

## Parkinson's Disease

- Loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta disrupts striatal action selection.
- Reduced direct pathway facilitation and excess indirect pathway suppression produces hypokinesia.
- Rigidity, tremor, and postural instability reflect disrupted basal ganglia-thalamo-cortical circuit function.
- Dopamine replacement with L-DOPA restores the action selection balance improving motor function.
