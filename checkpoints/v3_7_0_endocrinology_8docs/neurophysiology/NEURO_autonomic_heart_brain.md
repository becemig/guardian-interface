# Autonomic Heart-Brain Axis

## Abstract

The autonomic heart-brain axis describes the bidirectional communication system between the heart and brain through vagal afferent and efferent pathways, cardiac sympathetic innervation, and circulating hormonal signals — a system in which the heart is both a target of brain regulation and a significant sender of neural information to the brain. The heart contains approximately 40,000 neurons in the intrinsic cardiac nervous system — more than the spinal cord — enabling local cardiac information processing independent of the central nervous system. Approximately 80-90% of vagal fibers are afferent — carrying information from the heart to the brainstem — meaning the heart sends more information to the brain than it receives. These cardiac afferents project to the NTS, thalamus, and cortex including the insula and anterior cingulate, influencing emotional processing, cognitive function, and pain perception. Julian Thayer and Richard Lane's neurovisceral integration model proposes that vagal tone — reflected in HRV — is the primary index of the brain's capacity to regulate the body and the emotions. Higher HRV corresponds to greater prefrontal inhibitory control over subcortical emotional circuits enabling flexible adaptive responding. The HeartMath Institute's research on coherence documented that positive emotional states produce a distinctive HRV pattern — smooth, regular, sine-wave-like rhythm — that corresponds to enhanced cognitive performance, emotional stability, and autonomic efficiency. Heart Shen coherence in TCM — the Heart Shen's state of integrated clarity, emotional stability, and vital resonance — corresponds to the HRV coherence state as its physiological signature and primary telemetry measure.

## Cardiac Afferents

- The heart contains 40,000 intrinsic neurons processing cardiac information locally.
- 80-90% of vagal fibers carry information from heart to brain rather than the reverse.
- Cardiac afferents project to NTS, thalamus, insula, and ACC influencing cognition and emotion.
- Cardiac interoception — awareness of one's own heartbeat — correlates with emotional intensity and body awareness.

## HRV and Vagal Tone

- HRV reflects the dynamic balance of sympathetic and parasympathetic modulation of heart rate.
- Higher HRV reflects greater vagal tone and greater prefrontal inhibitory control over emotional reactivity.
- RSA respiratory sinus arrhythmia is the HRV component reflecting vagal modulation with breathing.
- Low HRV predicts cardiovascular disease, depression, anxiety, and reduced cognitive flexibility.

- It reflects efficient, ordered communication between heart, brain, and autonomic nervous system.
- Coherence techniques use controlled breathing and positive emotion activation to induce this state.
- Coherence state produces enhanced cognitive performance, emotional stability, and immune function.
