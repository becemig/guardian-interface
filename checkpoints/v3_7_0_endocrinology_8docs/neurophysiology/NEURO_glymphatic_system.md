# Glymphatic System

## Abstract

The glymphatic system — discovered by Maiken Nedergaard and colleagues in 2012 — is a brain-wide waste clearance system that uses the perivascular spaces surrounding cerebral blood vessels as channels for cerebrospinal fluid CSF to flow through the brain parenchyma, exchanging with interstitial fluid ISF and flushing metabolic waste products including amyloid-beta and tau proteins from the brain interstitium. The system is named glymphatic because it depends on glial cells — specifically the aquaporin-4 AQP4 water channels expressed on astrocyte endfeet that form the perivascular sheath — and functions analogously to the peripheral lymphatic system. Jeffrey Iliff's landmark 2012 paper documented the paravascular pathway through which CSF influx drives ISF efflux clearing solutes from the brain parenchyma. Lulu Xie's 2013 paper demonstrated that glymphatic clearance is dramatically increased during sleep — approximately 60% greater than during waking — corresponding to a 60% expansion of the interstitial space during sleep. This finding provides a mechanistic explanation for the restorative function of sleep — the brain literally cleans itself during sleep. Impaired glymphatic function is implicated in Alzheimer's disease — amyloid and tau accumulation represents a failure of nocturnal glymphatic clearance. Lateral sleeping position has been found to be most efficient for glymphatic drainage. Kidney water governance in TCM — the Kidney's role as the master of water metabolism and the root of all fluid transformation in the body — corresponds to the glymphatic system as the brain's specialized fluid clearance mechanism under Kidney governance.

## Perivascular Pathway

- CSF from the subarachnoid space enters the brain along the periarterial spaces surrounding penetrating arteries.
- AQP4 water channels on astrocyte endfeet drive CSF through the parenchyma exchanging with ISF.
- ISF and its solute burden exit along perivenous spaces toward cervical lymphatics and dural sinuses.
- Arterial pulsation drives the convective flow of CSF through the perivascular channels.

## Sleep-Dependent Clearance

- Glymphatic flow increases approximately 60% during NREM sleep compared to waking.
- The interstitial space expands 60% during sleep enabling more efficient bulk flow through the parenchyma.
- Amyloid-beta clearance is highest during slow wave sleep corresponding to the highest glymphatic activity.
- Chronic sleep deprivation impairs glymphatic clearance and accelerates amyloid accumulation.

## Alzheimer and Neurodegeneration

- Amyloid-beta and tau — the protein aggregates of Alzheimer's disease — are primary glymphatic substrates.
- Impaired glymphatic clearance is proposed as a key mechanism in Alzheimer's disease progression.
- AQP4 mislocalization away from perivascular endfeet reduces glymphatic efficiency in aging and disease.
- Restoring glymphatic function is a therapeutic target for Alzheimer's disease prevention.
