# Gut-Brain Axis Mechanisms

## Abstract

Gut-brain axis mechanisms — the bidirectional communication network connecting the enteric nervous system of the gut with the central nervous system through multiple parallel channels including the vagus nerve carrying afferent signals from gut to brain, the enteric nervous system with its 500 million neurons operating semi-autonomously, the neuroendocrine system with gut-derived hormones including serotonin, GLP-1, PYY, and ghrelin acting on brain circuits, the immune system with gut-educated immune cells patrolling and signaling to the brain, and the circulatory system carrying microbiome-derived metabolites including short chain fatty acids, tryptophan metabolites, and secondary bile acids to influence brain function. Vagus nerve microbiome signaling — the primary neural highway of the gut-brain axis, with 80-90 percent of vagal fibers being afferent carrying information from the gut to the brainstem nucleus tractus solitarius, with vagal afferents expressing receptors for gut hormones, bacterial metabolites, and immune signals, and with vagotomy studies demonstrating that many behavioral effects of gut bacteria including the anxiolytic effects of Lactobacillus rhamnosus are abolished by vagal transection — establishing the vagus nerve as the primary route through which gut bacteria influence brain function and behavior. Microbiome neurotransmitter production — the gut microbiome's capacity to produce or regulate the production of neurotransmitters and their precursors, with gut bacteria producing approximately 90 percent of the body's serotonin through their influence on enterochromaffin cell tryptophan hydroxylase expression, producing GABA through Lactobacillus species, producing dopamine precursors, and influencing tryptophan availability for both serotonin and kynurenine pathway production — making the gut microbiome a major regulator of neurotransmitter availability with profound implications for mood, anxiety, and cognition. Intestinal permeability and neuroinflammation — the leaky gut brain axis mechanism in which dysbiosis and intestinal barrier dysfunction allow bacterial lipopolysaccharide and other inflammatory molecules to enter the circulation and reach the brain through the circumventricular organs and disrupted blood-brain barrier, activating microglia and producing the neuroinflammatory state associated with depression, chronic fatigue, and neurodegenerative disease. The Spleen Heart axis gut brain correspondence in TCM describes the classical Five Phase relationship between the Earth element Spleen and the Fire element Heart, with the Spleen's capacity to generate Blood directly nourishing the Heart Shen, and with digestive dysfunction producing Heart Shen disturbance through Blood and Qi deficiency — a classical framework corresponding to the gut-brain axis through which gut microbiome dysfunction produces neuroinflammation, neurotransmitter dysregulation, and mental health disturbances.

## Vagal Communication

- Afferent dominance: 80-90 percent of vagal fibers carry gut to brain signals.
- NTS: nucleus tractus solitarius — primary brainstem relay for gut signals.
- Bacterial metabolites: SCFAs bile acids — activate vagal afferent receptors.
- Vagotomy: abolishes many behavioral effects of probiotic bacteria.

## Neurotransmitter Production

- Serotonin: 90 percent produced in gut — enterochromaffin cell TPH1.
- GABA: Lactobacillus species produce GABA — anxiolytic signaling.
- Tryptophan: microbiome competes for tryptophan — serotonin vs kynurenine.
- Dopamine: gut bacteria produce dopamine and DOPA — precursor availability.

## Leaky Gut Brain

- Dysbiosis: reduces tight junction protein expression — permeability increases.
- LPS: lipopolysaccharide enters circulation — systemic inflammation.
- Blood-brain barrier: LPS activates endothelial TLR4 — BBB disruption.
- Microglia: activated by circulating LPS — neuroinflammation depression.
