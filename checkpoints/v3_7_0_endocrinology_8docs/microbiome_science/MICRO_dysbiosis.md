# Dysbiosis and Disease

## Abstract

Dysbiosis and disease — the disruption of the healthy gut microbiome composition, diversity, and function producing the dysbiotic states associated with a growing list of diseases including inflammatory bowel disease, obesity, metabolic syndrome, type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, colorectal cancer, depression, anxiety, autism spectrum disorder, Parkinson's disease, and Alzheimer's disease, with the mechanisms of dysbiosis-driven disease including increased intestinal permeability allowing bacterial products to reach systemic circulation, reduced production of protective short chain fatty acids, altered bile acid metabolism, increased production of pro-inflammatory bacterial metabolites, and disruption of the immune calibration that depends on the healthy gut microbiome. IBD microbiome — the consistent finding of reduced microbial diversity in Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis, with specific depletion of Faecalibacterium prausnitzii the most abundant butyrate-producing bacterium in the healthy gut and a potent anti-inflammatory organism, reduced Roseburia and other butyrate producers, and expansion of potentially pathogenic Proteobacteria including adherent-invasive Escherichia coli that invades the intestinal mucosa and activates innate immune responses — making microbiome restoration a therapeutic target in IBD through probiotics, prebiotics, diet, and fecal microbiota transplantation. Microbiome obesity metabolism — the landmark study by Turnbaugh demonstrating that transferring the gut microbiome from obese mice to germ-free recipient mice produces greater fat gain than transferring lean microbiome, with the obese microbiome showing increased capacity for energy harvest from dietary polysaccharides, establishing that the gut microbiome is a causal contributor to obesity and metabolic syndrome through its effects on caloric extraction, adipose tissue metabolism, insulin sensitivity, and the gut-adipose-brain axis. Antibiotic disruption — the profound and partially irreversible disruption of gut microbiome diversity produced by broad-spectrum antibiotics, with a single course of antibiotics producing measurable reductions in diversity and altered composition persisting for months to years, with early-life antibiotic exposure associated with increased risk of obesity, asthma, allergies, and inflammatory conditions through the disruption of critical windows of immune programming and microbiome-dependent development. The Spleen Damp accumulation dysbiosis correspondence in TCM describes the classical pathological pattern in which Spleen dysfunction fails to transform and transport moisture, producing the accumulation of pathological Dampness that obstructs Qi flow, impairs digestion, produces heaviness and fatigue, and creates the fertile ground for Phlegm — a classical pattern corresponding to the dysbiotic state in which reduced butyrate production and increased intestinal permeability produce the systemic low-grade inflammation, metabolic impairment, and cognitive fog of dysbiosis-related disease.

## IBD Microbiome

- Reduced diversity: hallmark of Crohn's and ulcerative colitis.
- F. prausnitzii depletion: loss of primary butyrate producer and anti-inflammatory.
- Proteobacteria expansion: adherent-invasive E. coli mucosal invasion.
- Restoration: FMT probiotics prebiotics diet — therapeutic targets.

## Obesity Microbiome

- Energy harvest: obese microbiome extracts more calories from same diet.
- FMT transfer: obese microbiome transferred to germ-free causes greater fat gain.
- Mechanisms: SCFA signaling adipose tissue bile acid metabolism insulin sensitivity.
- Intervention: diet prebiotic fiber shifts microbiome improves metabolic markers.

## Antibiotic Disruption

- Single course: diversity reduction persisting months to years.
- Early life: critical window — antibiotic exposure increases obesity asthma allergy risk.
- C. difficile: antibiotic disruption enables C. diff overgrowth.
- FMT: 90 percent cure rate for recurrent C. diff — microbiome restoration.
