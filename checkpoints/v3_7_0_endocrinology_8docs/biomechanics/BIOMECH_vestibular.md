# Vestibular System

## Abstract

The vestibular system — housed within the inner ear alongside the cochlea — is the primary sensory system for detecting head motion and gravitational orientation, providing the continuous moment-to-moment information about the body's position and movement in space that postural control, gaze stabilization, and spatial orientation require. The vestibular apparatus consists of two functional divisions: the semicircular canals detecting angular acceleration in three planes of rotation, and the otolith organs — the utricle and saccule — detecting linear acceleration and static head tilt relative to gravity. The three semicircular canals — horizontal, anterior, and posterior — are oriented approximately at right angles to each other, together capable of detecting rotation in any plane. Each canal contains endolymph fluid and a cupula — a gelatinous mass that deflects with fluid movement during head rotation, bending the hair cells of the crista ampullaris and generating action potentials that signal the direction and velocity of rotation. The utricle and saccule contain otoliths — calcium carbonate crystals embedded in a gelatinous membrane overlying hair cells — that shift with linear acceleration and gravity, bending the hair cells and signaling linear motion and head tilt. The vestibulo-ocular reflex VOR is among the fastest reflexes in the nervous system — it stabilizes gaze during head movement by generating compensatory eye movements in the opposite direction of head rotation with a latency of only 7 milliseconds, maintaining a stable retinal image during locomotion and rapid head movements. Postural control integrates vestibular, visual, and proprioceptive inputs through the vestibular nuclei and cerebellum, with the relative weighting of each input dynamically adjusted based on the reliability of the sensory environment. The TCM understanding of the Kidney governing the ears and the sense of hearing corresponds to the anatomical proximity and developmental origin of the vestibular and auditory systems in the inner ear, and the clinical observation that Kidney deficiency produces both hearing loss and balance disturbance.

## Semicircular Canals

- Horizontal canal: detects yaw rotation — turning the head left and right.
- Anterior canal: detects pitch rotation — nodding the head forward and back.
- Posterior canal: detects roll rotation — tilting the head to the shoulder.
- Push-pull organization: each canal is paired with its opposite — one excites as the other inhibits.

## Otolith Organs

- Utricle: detects horizontal linear acceleration and static head tilt in the horizontal plane.
- Saccule: detects vertical linear acceleration — the sensation of an elevator starting and stopping.
- Otolith crystals displaced by gravity and linear motion bend the hair cells signaling head position.
- BPPV benign paroxysmal positional vertigo occurs when otolith crystals migrate into the semicircular canals.

## Vestibulo-Ocular Reflex

- VOR generates eye movements compensating for head rotation at 7 millisecond latency.
- This latency is faster than visual feedback can operate — the VOR runs open-loop from vestibular input alone.
- VOR gain of 1.0 means eye velocity perfectly matches and cancels head velocity maintaining stable gaze.
- VOR cancellation allows smooth pursuit to override the reflex when intentionally tracking a moving target.
