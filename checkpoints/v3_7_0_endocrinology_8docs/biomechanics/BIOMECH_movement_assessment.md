# Movement Assessment

## Abstract

Movement assessment is the systematic evaluation of movement quality — examining posture, mobility, stability, coordination, and compensation patterns to identify the movement dysfunctions that underlie musculoskeletal pain, injury risk, and reduced performance. Gray Cook's Functional Movement Screen FMS provides a standardized seven-test battery that evaluates fundamental movement patterns — deep squat, hurdle step, inline lunge, shoulder mobility, active straight leg raise, trunk stability push-up, and rotary stability — scoring each on a 0-3 scale to identify mobility limitations, stability deficits, and asymmetries that predict injury risk. The regional interdependence model proposes that restrictions and dysfunctions in one body region inevitably produce compensatory adaptations in adjacent and distant regions — a stiff thoracic spine produces compensatory lumbar hypermobility and cervical tension; a tight hip flexor produces compensatory lumbar extension and anterior pelvic tilt. Compensation patterns are the body's intelligent adaptive responses to restriction — the nervous system finds alternative movement strategies that accomplish the functional goal while working around the limitation, but at the cost of overloading compensatory structures. Postural analysis reveals the chronic compensation patterns held in the body's resting alignment — the anterior pelvic tilt of shortened hip flexors, the rounded shoulders of tight pectorals, the forward head posture of weak deep cervical flexors. TCM constitutional movement diagnosis extends movement assessment into the energetic domain — observing the quality of movement as a diagnostic indicator of organ network health: the halting tentative movement of Kidney deficiency, the explosive rigid movement of Liver excess, the heavy slow movement of Spleen Dampness, the shallow restricted movement of Lung Qi deficiency.

## FMS Seven Tests

- Deep squat: assesses bilateral hip, knee, ankle mobility and thoracic extension — Kidney and Spleen channel mobility.
- Hurdle step: assesses single-leg stability and contralateral hip mobility — Gallbladder and Kidney channel balance.
- Inline lunge: assesses hip mobility, knee stability, and ankle dorsiflexion — Stomach and Spleen channel integrity.
- Shoulder mobility: assesses shoulder internal-external rotation and scapular mobility — Lung and Heart channel opening.
- Active straight leg raise: assesses hamstring and calf flexibility with contralateral stability — Bladder channel length.
- Trunk stability push-up: assesses anterior core reflexive stability — Conception Vessel and abdominal integrity.
- Rotary stability: assesses unilateral proprioception and core stability — Extraordinary Vessel coordination.
