# Psychodynamic Defense Mechanisms

## Abstract

Defense mechanisms are unconscious psychological processes that protect the ego from anxiety arising from unacceptable impulses, affects, and external threats. Anna Freud systematized the defense mechanisms in her 1936 work The Ego and the Mechanisms of Defense identifying repression, projection, reaction formation, isolation, undoing, introjection, regression, sublimation, and turning against the self. George Vaillant's longitudinal research on adaptation classified defenses on a maturity hierarchy from psychotic defenses through immature, neurotic, and mature defenses. Repression is the primary defense mechanism — the exclusion of threatening material from conscious awareness. Projection attributes one's own unacceptable impulses or qualities to another person. Reaction formation transforms an unacceptable impulse into its opposite. Rationalization provides plausible but false explanations for unacceptable behavior. Sublimation — the most mature defense — redirects unacceptable impulses into socially valued creative or prosocial activity. Splitting divides people and situations into all-good or all-bad without integration of ambivalence. Projective identification induces in another the feelings one cannot tolerate in oneself. Hun shadow in TCM — the shadow dimension of the Liver Hun that contains the unlived, repressed, and projected aspects of the psyche — corresponds to the psychodynamic understanding of unconscious defense and the shadow of personality.

## Repression

- Repression excludes threatening memories, impulses, and affects from conscious awareness.
- Unlike suppression which is conscious, repression operates below the threshold of awareness.
- Repressed material continues to exert influence through symptoms, dreams, and behavioral patterns.
- The goal of psychodynamic therapy is the recovery and integration of repressed material into consciousness.

## Projection and Projective Identification

- Projection attributes one's own unacceptable qualities to another person reducing internal discomfort.
- Projective identification induces the projected quality in the other through subtle interpersonal pressure.
- Projection underlies prejudice, scapegoating, and relationship patterns of blaming and victimization.
- Working through projection requires recognizing the projected quality as belonging to the self.

## Maturity Hierarchy

- Psychotic defenses include denial, distortion, and delusional projection — primitive reality distortion.
- Immature defenses include projection, passive aggression, and acting out — producing interpersonal problems.
- Neurotic defenses include repression, reaction formation, and rationalization — producing internal suffering.
- Mature defenses include sublimation, humor, altruism, and suppression — adaptive transformation of impulse.
