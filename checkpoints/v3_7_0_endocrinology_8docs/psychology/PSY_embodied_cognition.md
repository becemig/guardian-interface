# Embodied Cognition

## Abstract

Embodied cognition is the theoretical framework proposing that cognitive processes are not confined to the brain but are fundamentally shaped by and dependent on the body's structure, sensorimotor capabilities, and its dynamic interaction with the environment. This represents a fundamental challenge to the computational view of mind as an abstract information processing system that could in principle run on any substrate — embodied cognition insists that the specific body we have, with its particular sensorimotor capacities and interoceptive landscape, constitutes rather than merely implements our cognitive processes. Maurice Merleau-Ponty's phenomenological concept of the body schema is the philosophical foundation — the body schema is the pre-reflective, implicit knowledge of where the body is and how it can move that underlies all skilled action and perception, and that is constantly updated through proprioceptive feedback and motor experience. Varela, Thompson, and Rosch's enactivism proposes that cognition is the enactment of a world through sensorimotor coupling — the organism does not passively receive information from a pre-given world but actively brings forth a world through its patterns of sensorimotor engagement. Antonio Damasio's somatic marker hypothesis proposes that the body's emotional responses — the somatic markers — provide the rapid intuitive guidance that orients decision-making before deliberate reasoning can operate. Andy Clark's extended mind thesis proposes that cognitive processes can extend beyond the skull to include external tools, symbols, and environments that are functionally integrated with brain processes. Yi as embodied intelligence in TCM — the Spleen spirit that governs intention, thinking, and the capacity to orient toward goals — corresponds to the embodied cognition understanding that intelligence is fundamentally a body-environment coupling rather than a brain-internal computation.

## Body Schema

- The body schema is the implicit pre-reflective knowledge of body position and movement capacity.
- It is continuously updated through proprioceptive, vestibular, and interoceptive feedback.
- Skilled practitioners develop body schemas that include their tools — the musician includes the instrument.
- TCM cultivation practices develop expanded body schemas that include the energetic body and the environment.

## Somatic Markers

- Somatic markers are body-based emotional signals — gut feelings, chest tightness, postural changes — that orient decision-making.
- Damage to the ventromedial prefrontal cortex disconnects somatic markers from decision-making producing poor choices.
- The body's emotional wisdom — somatic markers — often contains more relevant information than deliberate analysis.
- This corresponds to the TCM concept of Yi intention arising from Spleen as centered grounded knowing.

## Enactivism

- Cognition is not computation on internal representations but the dynamic coupling of organism and environment.
- Perception is not passive reception but active exploration — we perceive through moving, touching, looking.
- Disrupting the body disrupts cognition — this explains why somatic trauma produces cognitive symptoms.
- TCM understanding of Yi-Qi-Li as the sequence of embodied action corresponds to the enactivist account.
