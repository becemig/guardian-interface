# Emotion Regulation

## Abstract

Emotion regulation refers to the processes by which individuals influence which emotions they have, when they have them, and how they experience and express these emotions. James Gross's process model is the dominant framework — organizing emotion regulation strategies according to when in the emotion-generative process they intervene. Antecedent-focused strategies intervene before the emotional response is fully generated: situation selection choosing which situations to enter or avoid, situation modification changing features of the situation, attentional deployment directing attention toward or away from emotional aspects, and cognitive change reappraising the meaning of the situation. Response-focused strategies intervene after the emotional response has been generated: expressive suppression inhibiting the outward expression of emotion. Cognitive reappraisal — changing the meaning of a situation to alter its emotional impact — is the most widely studied antecedent-focused strategy. It is effective, does not consume cognitive resources in the long term, produces genuine emotional change rather than masked expression, and is associated with positive outcomes including wellbeing, relationship quality, and physical health. Expressive suppression — inhibiting emotional expression — is less effective, consumes cognitive resources, fails to reduce subjective experience, and is associated with negative outcomes including reduced wellbeing, poorer relationships, and worse health. Individual differences in habitual use of reappraisal versus suppression predict many aspects of psychological wellbeing. Mindfulness and acceptance-based strategies represent a third approach — changing one's relationship to the emotion rather than changing the emotion or suppressing it. Heart-Liver Qi harmony in TCM — the smooth coordination of Heart Shen clarity and Liver Qi free flow enabling appropriate emotional expression and regulation — corresponds to effective emotion regulation as its physiological substrate.

## Process Model

- Situation selection intervenes earliest by choosing which emotion-eliciting situations to enter or avoid.
- Attentional deployment directs attention toward or away from emotionally significant aspects of the situation.
- Cognitive reappraisal changes the meaning of the situation to alter the emotional response before it fully develops.
- Expressive suppression intervenes after the emotional response has been generated inhibiting its expression.

## Reappraisal

- Cognitive reappraisal produces genuine change in subjective emotional experience not just masked expression.
- It is associated with positive affect, life satisfaction, wellbeing, and positive social relationships.
- Reappraisal does not consume significant cognitive resources once the strategy is retrieved and applied.
- Neural correlates include increased prefrontal activation and reduced amygdala activation.

## Suppression

- Expressive suppression inhibits outward emotional expression without reducing subjective emotional experience.
- It consumes significant cognitive resources reducing cognitive performance on concurrent tasks.
- Social partners of suppressors report less positive interactions corresponding to the inauthenticity of suppression.
- Habitual suppression is associated with reduced wellbeing, poorer relationships, and worse physical health.
