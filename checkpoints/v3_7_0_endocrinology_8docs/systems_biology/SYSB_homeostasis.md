# Homeostasis and Allostasis

## Abstract

Homeostasis, introduced by Walter Cannon, describes the maintenance of stable internal physiological parameters through error-correcting negative feedback — body temperature, blood glucose, pH, osmolarity all maintained near fixed setpoints. Allostasis, developed by Bruce McEwen and Peter Sterling, extends this framework to account for the brain-mediated anticipatory regulation of physiological parameters in response to predicted demands, with setpoints that shift dynamically rather than remaining fixed. Sterling refined this into predictive regulation — the brain continuously models expected demands and pre-emptively adjusts physiology, making error-correction feedback only the last resort. Allostatic load describes the cumulative physiological cost of repeated allostatic adjustments, producing organ-level wear that eventually manifests as disease. Biological redundancy and degeneracy provide robustness to regulatory system damage.

## Cannon Homeostasis

- Definition: maintenance of stable internal milieu through negative feedback error correction
- Core principle: deviations from setpoint trigger corrective responses restoring the setpoint
- Examples: thermoregulation, blood glucose, blood pressure, pH, osmolarity
- Mechanism: sensors detect deviation, control center computes correction, effectors respond
- Limitation: purely reactive - cannot anticipate demands or adjust setpoints
- TCM parallel: Zang-Fu mutual regulation maintaining organ balance as homeostatic system

## McEwen Allostasis

- Definition: achieving stability through change - setpoints shift to meet anticipated demands
- Brain-mediated: CNS anticipates demands and pre-adjusts physiology before error occurs
- Adaptive: allostatic shifts are appropriate and beneficial when time-limited
- Allostatic load: cumulative wear from repeated or prolonged allostatic activation
- Four types of allostatic load:
  1. Frequent stress: repeated hits with insufficient recovery
  2. Failure to habituate: response does not diminish with repeated exposure
  3. Failure to shut off: response activated but not terminated
  4. Inadequate response: insufficient allostatic response requires other systems to compensate

## Sterling Predictive Regulation

- Core insight: the brain does not merely react to deviations - it predicts and pre-empts them
- Predictive model: brain maintains internal model of body state and anticipated demands
- Setpoint is variable: blood pressure, heart rate, glucose all optimized to predicted context
- Efficiency principle: predictive regulation minimizes metabolic cost of reactive correction
- Chronic stress mechanism: when predictions are chronically wrong, regulation becomes expensive
- Interoception: accurate interoceptive signaling is required for good predictive models
- TCM parallel: Heart Shen governing anticipatory regulation of body state

## Allostatic Load Biomarkers

- Primary: cortisol AUC, epinephrine, norepinephrine, DHEA-S
- Secondary: blood pressure, waist-hip ratio, HDL cholesterol, glycated hemoglobin
- Tertiary: atherosclerosis, diabetes diagnosis, cognitive decline markers
- HRV: reduced HRV is a robust allostatic load marker - reflects ANS regulatory capacity
- Inflammatory: IL-6, CRP, fibrinogen reflect cumulative inflammatory burden
- Guardian Interface: HRV and skin conductance provide real-time allostatic load proxies

## Redundancy and Degeneracy

- Redundancy: multiple identical components performing the same function
- Degeneracy: multiple structurally distinct components capable of performing the same function
- Edelman distinction: degeneracy is more common and more powerful in biological systems
- Neural degeneracy: different neural circuits can produce identical outputs
- Metabolic redundancy: multiple pathways to same metabolic product
- Robustness: degeneracy allows system to maintain function despite component failure
- TCM parallel: multiple treatment strategies reaching same therapeutic endpoint reflects biological degeneracy
