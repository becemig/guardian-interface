# Neural Oscillations and Synchrony

## Abstract

Neural oscillations and synchrony — the rhythmic fluctuations in neural population activity spanning frequencies from less than 1 Hz infraslow oscillations through delta 1-4 Hz, theta 4-8 Hz, alpha 8-12 Hz, beta 12-30 Hz, gamma 30-100 Hz, and high-frequency oscillations above 100 Hz, each with distinct functional roles in cognition, memory, attention, and sensorimotor processing, and with cross-frequency coupling between different oscillatory frequencies providing a mechanism for organizing neural computation across multiple timescales simultaneously — represent the fundamental temporal structure of neural computation through which the brain coordinates activity across spatially distributed neural populations. Gamma oscillations and binding — Wolf Singer's binding by synchrony hypothesis proposing that the brain solves the binding problem of integrating features processed in different brain regions into unified perceptual objects by synchronizing the gamma oscillations of neural populations processing related features, with experimental support including the demonstration that neurons responding to the same moving bar synchronize their gamma oscillations while neurons responding to different bars do not, and with gamma oscillations generated by the interplay between fast-spiking parvalbumin interneurons and pyramidal cells in cortical microcircuits. Theta rhythms and memory — the 4-8 Hz theta rhythm of the hippocampus as the temporal framework organizing memory encoding and retrieval, with theta oscillations generated by the medial septum acting as a pacemaker, theta phase organizing the firing of place cells to encode spatial position and temporal sequences during navigation, and the theta-gamma coupling mechanism proposed by John Lisman in which each theta cycle contains multiple gamma cycles each encoding a different memory item, providing the computational architecture for short-term memory and working memory. Alpha suppression — the 8-12 Hz alpha rhythm as an active inhibitory mechanism for routing information through cortical networks, with alpha power increasing in task-irrelevant cortical regions to suppress their processing and decreasing in task-relevant regions to facilitate information flow, implementing a gating mechanism for attention through rhythmic inhibition rather than passive absence of activation. The Qi pulsation rhythmic neural field correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding of Qi as fundamentally rhythmic and pulsatile — manifesting in the pulse, the breath, and the rhythmic fluctuations of vital energy — a correspondence with the rhythmic oscillatory structure of neural computation that organizes all brain function through precisely timed pulsatile neural activity.

## Gamma and Binding

- Frequency: 30-100 Hz — generated by PV interneuron pyramidal cell circuits.
- Binding: synchrony of gamma across areas codes relatedness — binding hypothesis.
- GABA: fast inhibitory interneurons essential for gamma generation.
- Attention: gamma power increases in attended stimulus representations.

## Theta and Memory

- Hippocampal theta: 4-8 Hz — medial septum pacemaker.
- Place cells: fire at specific theta phases encoding position — phase precession.
- Theta-gamma coupling: each theta cycle contains gamma cycles — each codes one item.
- Memory encoding: theta phase at encoding predicts subsequent recall.

## Alpha Gating

- Task-irrelevant: alpha increases — active suppression of distractors.
- Task-relevant: alpha decreases — release of inhibition facilitates processing.
- Attention: alpha lateralizes — ipsilateral to attended side increases.
- Thalamic: thalamic alpha gates sensory relay — top-down attentional control.
