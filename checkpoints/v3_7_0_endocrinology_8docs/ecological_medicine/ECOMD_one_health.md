# One Health Framework

## Abstract

The One Health framework — the recognition that the health of humans, domestic and wild animals, and ecosystems are inextricably linked and that durable solutions to health challenges require addressing all three simultaneously — represents the most systems-level approach to health currently operating within mainstream medicine and public health, and the framework that most closely corresponds to the TCM and indigenous healing traditions' understanding of the human as embedded within and dependent upon the living systems of the Earth. The COVID-19 pandemic — a zoonotic spillover from bat coronaviruses facilitated by habitat destruction, wildlife trade, and human encroachment on wild animal populations — provided the most dramatic recent demonstration of the One Health principle: the destruction of natural habitats that maintained ecological barriers between wildlife viral reservoirs and human populations created the conditions for pandemic spillover with global consequences. Zoonotic disease ecology — the study of how pathogens move between animal reservoirs and human populations — demonstrates that the majority of emerging infectious diseases are zoonotic, and that the primary drivers of zoonotic spillover are anthropogenic: deforestation, agricultural expansion, wildlife trade, and climate change that brings humans into contact with animal viral reservoirs they would not historically have encountered. Antibiotic resistance as ecological crisis reframes the antimicrobial resistance crisis from a pharmaceutical challenge to an ecological catastrophe: the release of antibiotic compounds into agricultural soils and water systems selects for resistant organisms throughout the environmental microbiome, creating a global commons tragedy in which the antibiotic drugs that enabled modern medicine are being rendered ineffective by agricultural overuse. Ecosystem services — the benefits that functioning natural ecosystems provide to human populations including clean water, clean air, climate regulation, pollination, flood control, and disease regulation — have been valued in the trillions of dollars annually, making their loss through ecosystem degradation a massive public health and economic crisis. Traditional ecological knowledge TEK — the accumulated understanding of local ecosystems, their species, their seasonal dynamics, and their health relationships held by indigenous communities through thousands of years of observation — represents the most detailed and place-specific ecological health knowledge available and is increasingly recognized as an essential complement to scientific ecology for conservation and health.

## Zoonotic Disease Ecology

- Over 70 percent of emerging infectious diseases are zoonotic — originating in animal populations.
- Primary drivers: deforestation, agricultural expansion, wildlife trade, and climate change.
- Habitat destruction removes ecological barriers between wildlife viral reservoirs and humans.
- Corresponds to the TCM understanding that disrupting the natural order creates conditions for epidemic pathogenic Qi.

## Antibiotic Resistance Ecological Crisis

- Agricultural antibiotic use selects for resistant organisms throughout environmental microbiome.
- Resistant genes spread from agricultural soils to human pathogens through horizontal gene transfer.
- Global commons tragedy: individual benefit of agricultural antibiotic use imposes collective cost of resistance.
- Corresponds to the TCM materia medica principle that overuse of any therapeutic agent disrupts natural balance.

## Traditional Ecological Knowledge

- Indigenous communities hold thousands of years of detailed ecological health knowledge.
- TEK documents medicinal plant distribution, seasonal availability, and sustainable harvesting.
- TEK identifies ecological health indicators that Western science is only beginning to measure.
- Corresponds to the TCM naturalist tradition of the physician as ecological observer and environmental medicine practitioner.
