# Baguazhang Circle Walking

## Abstract

Circle walking is the foundational practice of Baguazhang — the eight trigram palm art — and distinguishes it from all other internal martial arts through its continuous circular locomotion as both training method and combat strategy. Unlike Taijichuan which primarily trains through slow sequential posture transitions, Baguazhang trains through sustained walking in a circle while maintaining the internal cultivation requirements of the art — this continuous movement develops the unique Baguazhang qualities of agility, evasion, and multi-directional force generation that are its martial signature. Mud stepping Ni Bu is the characteristic Baguazhang footwork — lifting the foot as if stepping out of deep mud, keeping the foot close to the ground, and placing it with precision and rootedness. Mud stepping trains the specific muscular patterns and fascial tensions of the Baguazhang leg structure that generate spiral force from the ground upward through the body. The circle itself is typically 6-12 feet in diameter — large enough to develop momentum and internal force, small enough to maintain precise postural alignment throughout the arc. Walking the circle continuously in one direction for extended periods develops the body's capacity to generate and sustain spiral force — the characteristic Baguazhang internal power that combines horizontal rotational force with vertical axial force to produce the twisting penetrating power of the art. The eight directions of the pakua are trained by periodically changing direction and palm at specific points on the circle — each direction corresponding to a trigram, an organ, and a quality of force. Governing Vessel cultivation through sustained circle walking corresponds to the activation of the spinal Yang channel through the continuous spiral movement of the Baguazhang body.

## Mud Stepping

- Mud stepping lifts the foot horizontally as if pulling it from deep mud — training hip flexor and root control.
- The foot remains close to the ground throughout the step — no high lifting that compromises stability.
- Placement is precise and grounded — no heel strike, full foot contact from outside edge to full sole.
- Sustained mud stepping builds the distinctive Baguazhang leg structure and ground-connection quality.

## Spiral Force

- Circle walking generates spiral force — the combination of horizontal rotation and vertical axis force.
- The body turns continuously while the spine maintains its vertical axis — creating a living spiral.
- Spiral force penetrates through straight force — it twists through defenses rather than meeting them directly.
- The Baguazhang spiral corresponds to the double helix of DNA as the fundamental structure of living force.

## Eight Directions

- The eight trigram directions are trained by changing palm and direction at specific circle positions.
- Each direction change trains a different quality of force corresponding to the trigram's elemental nature.
- Heaven Qian — maximum Yang — trains full expansion and structural completeness.
- Earth Kun — maximum Yin — trains full yielding and receptive gathering.
