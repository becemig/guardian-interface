# Taijichuan Neijin

## Abstract

Neijin meaning internal force or internal strength is the defining quality that distinguishes Taijichuan from external martial arts — the ability to generate and express tremendous force from a state of deep relaxation Song rather than through muscular tension. The classical teaching states that Neijin is rooted in the feet, developed by the legs, controlled by the waist, and expressed through the fingers — describing the biomechanical chain through which whole-body ground force is transmitted as a unified wave from root to expression. Silk reeling Chan Si Jin is the fundamental movement quality of Chen-style Taijichuan and the training methodology for developing Neijin — spiraling movements that wind and unwind through the body in the manner of silk being drawn from a cocoon without breaking. The silk reeling quality corresponds to the fascial tensegrity model where force applied anywhere in the tensegrity system is transmitted throughout — silk reeling trains the practitioner to maintain and transmit this whole-body tensegrity connection through all movement. Song the Chinese concept of relaxed and open without being collapsed or limp is the foundational quality required for Neijin development — muscular tension blocks the transmission of force through the kinetic chain while deep relaxation allows force to flow unimpeded from root to expression. The paradox of Taijichuan is that maximum force expression requires maximum relaxation — a principle that directly contradicts the intuitive approach of most people to generating force and that requires years of dedicated training to embody. Peng Jin is the primary Neijin quality — the springy expansive ward-off energy that maintains the structural integrity of the sphere while yielding at the surface. All other jin energies are expressions or variations of Peng Jin.

## Root-Waist-Hand Chain

- Force originates in the ground reaction force at the feet — the root.
- The legs transmit and amplify ground force through optimal structural alignment.
- The waist Kua hip-crease is the master controller — every direction change initiates from the waist.
- The force arrives at the hands as the final expression of the whole-body unified movement.

## Silk Reeling

- Chan Si Jin silk reeling trains continuous spiraling movement through the entire body.
- The spiral quality maintains the fascial tensegrity connection through all phases of movement.
- Outer spiral and inner spiral correspond to the yin and yang rotation quality of the silk thread.
- Silk reeling practice is the primary method for developing neijin in the Chen family tradition.

## Song Relaxation

- Song means open, relaxed, and available — not collapsed, not tense.
- Muscular tension creates local resistance that blocks force transmission through the kinetic chain.
- Deep Song allows force to flow like water through the body from root to expression.
- Developing Song requires the systematic release of habitual holding patterns throughout the body.

## TCM Correspondence

- Neijin force transmission through the whole-body kinetic chain corresponds to the TCM concept of Zong Qi governing whole-body movement.
- Silk reeling spiraling movement corresponds to the TCM spiral Qi circulation through the extraordinary vessels.
- Song relaxation as the prerequisite for force corresponds to the TCM concept of smooth Qi flow requiring relaxed open channels.
- Root in Kidney corresponds to the TCM Kidney governing the bones and constitutional structural foundation.
