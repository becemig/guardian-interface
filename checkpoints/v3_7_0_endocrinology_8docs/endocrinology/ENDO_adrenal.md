# Adrenal Physiology

## Abstract

The adrenal glands — paired pyramid-shaped endocrine organs sitting atop each kidney — are the constitutional stress response organs par excellence, producing the catecholamines epinephrine and norepinephrine from the medulla for immediate fight-or-flight response and the corticosteroids cortisol, aldosterone, and adrenal androgens from the cortex for sustained stress adaptation, metabolic regulation, electrolyte balance, and secondary sex hormone production. The adrenal cortex is organized into three concentric zones each producing distinct steroid hormones from cholesterol: the zona glomerulosa produces aldosterone the primary mineralocorticoid governing sodium retention and blood pressure, the zona fasciculata produces cortisol the primary glucocorticoid governing metabolic stress response, and the zona reticularis produces adrenal androgens DHEA and androstenedione serving as precursors for peripheral sex hormone synthesis. The adrenal medulla — embryologically derived from neural crest cells and functionally a modified sympathetic ganglion — produces epinephrine adrenaline and norepinephrine noradrenaline through the catecholamine synthesis pathway from tyrosine, releasing these into the systemic circulation within seconds of sympathetic nervous system activation to produce the immediate cardiovascular, metabolic, and behavioral components of the acute stress response. Aldosterone and the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system RAAS governs blood pressure and electrolyte homeostasis: renin released from juxtaglomerular cells in response to reduced renal perfusion cleaves angiotensinogen to angiotensin I, converted by ACE to angiotensin II, which stimulates adrenal zona glomerulosa aldosterone production, causing renal sodium and water retention and blood pressure restoration. The Kidney-adrenal Ming Men correspondence is the most anatomically precise TCM-biomedical correspondence in the entire endocrine system: the Ming Men Gate of Life — the constitutional fire described in TCM as residing between the two Kidneys — corresponds anatomically and functionally to the adrenal glands sitting atop the kidneys, governing the organism's constitutional energy, stress responsiveness, reproductive vitality, and the Yang fire that drives all physiological processes.

## Adrenal Cortex Zones

- Zona glomerulosa: aldosterone production governed by RAAS and potassium — blood pressure regulation.
- Zona fasciculata: cortisol production governed by ACTH — stress response and metabolic regulation.
- Zona reticularis: DHEA and androstenedione production — peripheral sex hormone precursors.
- All three zones synthesize steroids from cholesterol through the steroidogenesis pathway.

## Adrenal Medulla Catecholamines

- Chromaffin cells synthesize epinephrine 80 percent and norepinephrine 20 percent.
- Catecholamine synthesis: tyrosine to DOPA to dopamine to norepinephrine to epinephrine.
- Release within seconds of sympathetic activation — immediate fight-or-flight physiology.
- Epinephrine: cardiac acceleration, bronchodilation, glycogenolysis, lipolysis.

## RAAS Aldosterone Axis

- Renin from kidney juxtaglomerular cells cleaves angiotensinogen to angiotensin I.
- ACE converts angiotensin I to angiotensin II in pulmonary vasculature.
- Angiotensin II stimulates adrenal aldosterone and vasoconstriction — blood pressure restoration.
- Aldosterone causes renal sodium retention and potassium excretion.
