# Yijing

## Abstract

The Yijing — the Book of Changes — is the oldest and most foundational of the Chinese classics, a cosmological text and divination system that models the universe as a field of continuously changing patterns generated by the dynamic interaction of Yin and Yang. The Yijing's binary structure — built from the two fundamental symbols of the unbroken Yang line and the broken Yin line — was recognized by Gottfried Leibniz in the 17th century as isomorphic with his binary arithmetic, anticipating the computational logic that underlies all modern digital technology. The eight trigrams Bagua — combinations of three Yin and Yang lines — represent the eight fundamental archetypal forces or processes that emerge from the interaction of Yin and Yang: Qian heaven, Kun earth, Zhen thunder, Xun wind, Kan water, Li fire, Gen mountain, and Dui lake. Each trigram has a family of correspondences — a season, direction, organ, body part, family member, animal, and archetypal quality — that form the basis of the five element system's predecessor and complement. The 64 hexagrams formed by combining any two trigrams provide the complete system of 64 archetypal situations or processes that, according to Yijing cosmology, encompass the full range of possible human situations and their natural resolution trajectories. Divination through the Yijing is not fortune-telling but a sophisticated pattern recognition practice: the random process of generating a hexagram — through yarrow stalks or coins — is understood to synchronistically reflect the pattern of the moment, providing a mirror in which the questioner can recognize the archetypal pattern underlying their situation and its natural trajectory. The ten wings — the ten commentary texts attributed to Confucius — systematize the cosmological and philosophical implications of the Yijing, establishing it as the foundational text of all subsequent Chinese cosmological thought.

## Eight Trigrams

- Qian three unbroken lines: pure Yang, heaven, father, creative force, head, northwest.
- Kun three broken lines: pure Yin, earth, mother, receptive force, abdomen, southwest.
- The six remaining trigrams represent different combinations of Yin and Yang in dynamic relationship.
- The trigrams map onto body regions, organs, and constitutional types in Chinese medical cosmology.

## 64 Hexagrams

- Each hexagram consists of two trigrams — a lower inner trigram and an upper outer trigram.
- The 64 hexagrams represent the complete range of archetypal situations in the human experience.
- Each hexagram has a name, a judgment, an image, and line texts describing the trajectory of change.
- Changing lines indicate the dynamic movement within the current pattern toward the next pattern.

## Binary Foundation

- The Yijing binary system predates and independently discovers the mathematical logic of binary arithmetic.
- Leibniz recognized the Yijing as confirming his binary arithmetic — the same logical structure across cultures.
- The binary structure of the Yijing corresponds to the digital logic underlying all computational systems.
- This correspondence suggests that the Yijing models something fundamental about information and pattern.
