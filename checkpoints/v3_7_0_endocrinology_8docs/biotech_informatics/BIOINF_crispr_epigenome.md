# CRISPR Epigenome Editing

## Abstract

CRISPR-Cas9 — clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats and CRISPR-associated protein 9 — is the revolutionary genome editing technology that enables precise, programmable modification of DNA sequences in living cells, making targeted changes to the genome that would previously have required years of painstaking molecular biology possible in days. The Nobel Prize awarded to Jennifer Doudna and Emmanuelle Charpentier in 2020 recognized CRISPR as one of the most transformative scientific discoveries of the century. Beyond the original CRISPR-Cas9 double-strand break system, David Liu's laboratory has developed base editing — which enables the direct chemical conversion of one DNA base to another without double-strand breaks, dramatically reducing off-target effects — and prime editing — which uses a reverse transcriptase to write new genetic information directly into a specific genomic location, providing a molecular search-and-replace capability for the genome. CRISPRa and CRISPRi — CRISPR activation and interference — use a catalytically dead Cas9 fused to transcriptional activators or repressors to increase or decrease the expression of specific genes without altering the DNA sequence itself, providing programmable control of gene expression that corresponds to epigenetic regulation. Epigenome editing extends CRISPR tools to the epigenetic layer — using dCas9 fused to DNA methyltransferases, demethylases, histone acetyltransferases, or deacetylases to add or remove specific epigenetic marks at targeted genomic locations, enabling the rewriting of epigenetic programs that determine cell identity and function. The molecular basis of constitutional change — the mechanism by which lifestyle practices, environmental exposures, and therapeutic interventions produce lasting changes in health — operates substantially through epigenetic reprogramming, and CRISPR epigenome editing tools provide the molecular instruments to both study and potentially intervene in these epigenetic mechanisms.

## Base Editing

- Converts one DNA base to another without double-strand breaks — cytosine to thymine or adenine to guanine.
- Dramatically reduced off-target effects compared to original CRISPR-Cas9 cutting.
- Enables correction of point mutations responsible for thousands of genetic diseases.
- Corresponds to the TCM principle of minimal intervention achieving maximal constitutional correction.

## Prime Editing

- Molecular search-and-replace: writes new genetic sequences into specific genomic locations.
- Uses pegRNA programmable guide RNA plus reverse transcriptase to insert new sequence.
- Can correct all 12 types of point mutations plus small insertions and deletions.
- Represents the most precise genome editing capability yet developed.

## CRISPRa and CRISPRi

- CRISPRa activates gene expression without altering DNA sequence — programmable epigenetic activation.
- CRISPRi represses gene expression without altering DNA sequence — programmable epigenetic silencing.
- Enables systematic gain-of-function and loss-of-function screens across the entire genome.
- Corresponds to the TCM tonic and sedation treatment principles — activating deficient and reducing excess.
