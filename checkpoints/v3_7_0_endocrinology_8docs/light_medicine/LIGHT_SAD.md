# Seasonal Affective Disorder and Light Therapy

## Abstract

Seasonal affective disorder SAD — the recurrent pattern of major depressive episodes occurring in autumn and winter with spontaneous remission in spring, first formally described by Norman Rosenthal and colleagues in 1984 — represents the most dramatic demonstration of light's effect on mood and mental health, establishing the fundamental role of photoperiod in human psychological functioning and validating the development of bright light therapy as a first-line treatment for seasonal depression that is comparable in efficacy to antidepressant medication. Photoperiod and mood — the physiological mechanism by which the shortening days of autumn produce the depressive, hypersomnic, carbohydrate-craving, low-energy syndrome of SAD — involves multiple interacting systems: the phase delay of the circadian clock produced by reduced morning light exposure, the increased duration of melatonin secretion in longer nights, the seasonal reduction in serotonin synthesis and increased serotonin transporter expression reducing synaptic serotonin, and the reduction in vitamin D synthesis from reduced solar UVB exposure. Light box therapy mechanisms — the treatment of SAD with daily 30-60 minute exposure to 10,000 lux bright white light in the morning, which is approximately 20 times the intensity of typical indoor lighting — produces antidepressant effects through the phase-advancing of the circadian clock, the suppression of the extended melatonin signal, the stimulation of serotonin synthesis and release, and the activation of the alerting arousal pathways mediated by the SCN. Serotonin seasonal variation — the PET imaging studies demonstrating that serotonin transporter binding is higher in winter than summer in human brain, reducing synaptic serotonin availability during the season of SAD — provides the neurochemical mechanism linking reduced winter light exposure to depressed mood and corresponding to the known efficacy of both light therapy and serotonergic antidepressants for SAD. Dawn simulation therapy — the gradual brightening of bedroom lighting over 30-90 minutes before waking to simulate the natural progression of dawn, using a programmable bedside light — has been demonstrated to be as effective as bright light box therapy for SAD and to improve mood, energy, and cognitive function in winter through its phase-advancing circadian effects occurring during the sensitive early morning phase of the phase response curve. The seasonal Yang Qi waxing and waning correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that Yang Qi is abundant and expansive in summer and contracted and conserved in winter, with cultivation practice adapting to the seasonal rhythm by rising later and conserving energy in winter — a framework that corresponds to the modern understanding of seasonal circadian and neurochemical variation driving SAD.

## SAD Pathophysiology

- Phase delay hypothesis: reduced morning light in winter delays circadian clock.
- Melatonin duration: longer winter nights extend melatonin secretion duration.
- Serotonin deficit: reduced synthesis increased transporter expression in winter.
- Vitamin D reduction: reduced solar UVB in winter reduces vitamin D and mood.

## Light Box Therapy

- 10000 lux white light for 30-60 minutes within one hour of waking.
- Phase-advances circadian clock through morning light phase response curve.
- Suppresses extended winter melatonin signal.
- Efficacy comparable to SSRIs for SAD in randomized controlled trials.

## Dawn Simulation

- Programmable bedside light brightens from 0 to 250 lux over 30-90 minutes before alarm.
- Activates phase advance through early morning circadian sensitive period during sleep.
- Comparable to 10000 lux light box in SAD randomized trial.
- Additional benefit: improved waking quality and morning mood versus abrupt alarm.
