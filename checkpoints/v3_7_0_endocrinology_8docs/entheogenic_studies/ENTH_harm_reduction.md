# Psychedelic Harm Reduction

## Abstract

Psychedelic harm reduction — the evidence-based set of practices, information, and interventions designed to minimize the risks associated with psychedelic use while respecting individual autonomy and avoiding the harms of criminalization — is an essential component of responsible engagement with psychedelic substances whether in clinical, ceremonial, or personal contexts. The risks of psychedelic use are real but substantially manageable through informed preparation, appropriate screening, skilled facilitation, and the avoidance of known dangerous combinations — making harm reduction information both ethically necessary and practically effective. Drug checking — the laboratory analysis of substances to verify identity and purity before ingestion — is the most fundamental harm reduction intervention, addressing the significant risk of substances being misrepresented or adulterated in unregulated markets. Fentanyl contamination of the unregulated drug supply represents the most acute contemporary harm reduction crisis, with fentanyl test strips providing a simple and effective frontline harm reduction tool. Contraindications for psychedelic use include personal or family history of psychosis or bipolar disorder type I, current lithium use which dramatically increases seizure risk with classic psychedelics, current MAOI use which creates potentially fatal serotonin syndrome risk particularly with MDMA and tryptamine psychedelics, severe cardiovascular disease for stimulant-type substances, and pregnancy. Challenging experience navigation — the skills and principles for supporting a person through a difficult psychedelic experience — include the foundational harm reduction principle of being with rather than trying to end the experience, the use of grounding techniques including physical touch, breath guidance, and environmental anchoring, and the judicious use of benzodiazepines as a pharmacological rescue in situations where the experience cannot be safely managed through non-pharmacological means. The ethical container describes the principles governing responsible facilitation: informed consent, confidentiality, clear role boundaries, no sexual contact, and the primacy of the participant's wellbeing over any other consideration.

## Key Contraindications

- Personal or family history of psychosis or bipolar disorder type I: high risk of precipitation or exacerbation.
- Current lithium use: dramatically increases seizure risk with classic psychedelics — absolute contraindication.
- Current MAOI use: potentially fatal serotonin syndrome with MDMA and tryptamines — absolute contraindication.
- Severe cardiovascular disease: stimulant-type substances including MDMA increase cardiac demand.

## Challenging Experience Navigation

- Being with the experience rather than trying to end it — resistance amplifies difficulty.
- Grounding techniques: physical touch, breath guidance, environmental anchoring to the present moment.
- Reassurance that the experience is temporary and the person is safe and supported.
- Benzodiazepines as pharmacological rescue when non-pharmacological support is insufficient.

## Drug Checking

- Laboratory analysis verifies substance identity and purity before ingestion.
- Fentanyl test strips detect fentanyl contamination — essential frontline harm reduction tool.
- Reagent testing provides presumptive identification of common psychedelics in unregulated markets.
- Drug checking services normalize safety-seeking behavior and reduce stigma around psychedelic use.
