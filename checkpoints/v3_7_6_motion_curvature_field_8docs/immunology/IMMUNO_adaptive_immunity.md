# Adaptive Immunity T and B Cells

## Abstract

Adaptive immunity T and B cells — the antigen-specific arm of the immune system that provides targeted defense against specific pathogens through the clonal expansion of lymphocytes bearing receptors precisely complementary to pathogen-derived antigens, developing over days to weeks following pathogen encounter but generating immunological memory cells that enable rapid amplified responses upon re-exposure — representing the basis of vaccination and the acquired immunity that protects against reinfection. Clonal selection and lymphocyte activation — Frank Macfarlane Burnet's clonal selection theory proposing that the immune repertoire contains pre-existing lymphocytes bearing randomly generated antigen receptors covering essentially all possible antigens, with antigen binding selecting and driving the clonal expansion of specific lymphocytes bearing complementary receptors while deleting self-reactive clones during central tolerance induction in the thymus and bone marrow. CD4 T helper subsets — the functional diversity of CD4 T helper cells differentiated from naive precursors by the cytokine environment of the secondary lymphoid organ, with Th1 cells producing IFN-gamma and TNF directing cellular immunity against intracellular pathogens, Th2 cells producing IL-4 IL-5 and IL-13 directing humoral immunity against parasites and driving allergic responses, Th17 cells producing IL-17 and IL-22 directing neutrophil-mediated immunity against extracellular bacteria and fungi, and regulatory T cells producing TGF-beta and IL-10 suppressing immune responses and maintaining self-tolerance. B cell antibody responses — the maturation of antigen-specific B cells in germinal centers of secondary lymphoid organs through the processes of somatic hypermutation introducing random mutations in antibody variable regions and affinity maturation selecting mutants with higher antigen affinity, producing high-affinity antibodies of multiple isotypes including IgG for systemic opsonization and neutralization, IgA for mucosal defense, and IgE for antiparasitic immunity and allergic sensitization, with long-lived plasma cells and memory B cells providing durable humoral immunity. The Zheng Qi adaptive defense correspondence in TCM describes the classical concept of Zheng Qi as the body's true vital Qi representing its overall constitutional strength and adaptive capacity to resist and overcome pathogenic factors — a classical framework corresponding to the adaptive immune system's capacity to learn from pathogen encounter, mount targeted responses, and generate lasting immunological memory.

## Clonal Selection

- Receptor diversity: VDJ recombination — greater than 10 to the 15th possible TCR specificities.
- Central tolerance: thymic negative selection deletes self-reactive T cells.
- Peripheral tolerance: regulatory T cells and anergy prevent autoimmunity.
- Clonal expansion: antigen-selected lymphocytes expand 1000-fold or more.

## T Helper Subsets

- Th1: IFN-gamma TNF — intracellular pathogens viruses mycobacteria.
- Th2: IL-4 IL-5 IL-13 — parasites allergic responses IgE switching.
- Th17: IL-17 IL-22 — extracellular bacteria fungi — neutrophil recruitment.
- Treg: TGF-beta IL-10 FOXP3 — immune suppression self-tolerance.

## B Cell Responses

- Germinal center: somatic hypermutation and affinity maturation — high affinity.
- Isotype switching: IgM to IgG IgA IgE — effector function diversification.
- Long-lived plasma cells: bone marrow — decades of antibody production.
- Memory B cells: rapid re-activation upon re-exposure — basis of vaccination.
