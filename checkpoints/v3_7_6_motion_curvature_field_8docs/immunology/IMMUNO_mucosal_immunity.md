# Mucosal Immunity and Barrier Defense

## Abstract

Mucosal immunity and barrier defense — the specialized immune system of the mucosal surfaces lining the gastrointestinal, respiratory, genitourinary, and mammary gland epithelia, constituting the largest immune compartment in the body with over 80 percent of activated B cells being mucosal and with the mucosal immune system facing the unique challenge of maintaining tolerance to the trillions of commensal microorganisms and dietary antigens while mounting protective responses against pathogens — a challenge solved through the layered defense of epithelial barrier, mucus layer, secretory IgA, and lamina propria immune cells coordinating tolerance and defense simultaneously. GALT and Peyers patches — the gut-associated lymphoid tissue comprising the Peyers patches organized lymphoid structures in the small intestinal submucosa, isolated lymphoid follicles, and the mesenteric lymph nodes, with the Peyers patches sampling luminal antigens through specialized M cells that transcytose particulate antigens to subepithelial dendritic cells for presentation to T and B cells, initiating the mucosal immune responses that drive local secretory IgA production and mucosal T cell responses while simultaneously inducing regulatory T cell responses to harmless dietary antigens. Secretory IgA — the predominant antibody class of mucosal surfaces produced at approximately 3-5 grams per day exceeding the combined production of all other immunoglobulin isotypes, transported through the epithelium by the polymeric immunoglobulin receptor and released with the secretory component that protects it from proteolytic degradation, coating bacteria and toxins in the lumen to prevent epithelial adhesion and invasion — the primary mechanism of immune exclusion that maintains the separation between the luminal microbial world and the sterile submucosa. Mucosal tolerance and oral tolerance — the active immunological suppression of systemic immune responses to dietary antigens and commensal bacteria through the induction of regulatory T cells in the mesenteric lymph nodes by tolerogenic dendritic cells presenting antigen in the presence of TGF-beta and retinoic acid, with oral tolerance failure contributing to food allergy development and with the commensal microbiome being required for normal mucosal tolerance induction. The Lung Spleen mucosal Wei Qi boundary correspondence in TCM describes the classical relationship between the Lung governing the exterior and skin, the Spleen governing the digestive interior, and the Triple Burner governing the spaces between — together defining the boundaries of self and providing the defensive capacity of all mucosal surfaces — a classical framework corresponding to the mucosal immune system as the primary interface between the internal body and the external world of microorganisms and food antigens.

## GALT Structure

- Peyers patches: organized lymphoid structures — small intestinal submucosa.
- M cells: specialized epithelial cells — antigen sampling from lumen.
- Dendritic cells: subepithelial — antigen presentation to naive T and B cells.
- Mesenteric lymph nodes: primary site of mucosal T cell response induction.

## Secretory IgA

- Production: 3-5 grams per day — largest immunoglobulin class by mass.
- Transport: polymeric Ig receptor — epithelial transcytosis to lumen.
- Secretory component: protects from protease degradation in lumen.
- Function: immune exclusion — prevents bacterial epithelial adhesion.

## Intestinal Barrier

- Tight junctions: ZO-1 claudin occludin — paracellular permeability control.
- Zonulin: tight junction regulator — increased in celiac disease and IBD.
- Mucus layer: inner sterile layer outer colonized — physical exclusion.
- Antimicrobial peptides: defensins RegIII — epithelial innate defense.
