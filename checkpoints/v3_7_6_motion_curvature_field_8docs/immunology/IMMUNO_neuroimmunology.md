# Neuroimmunology and Brain Immune Interactions

## Abstract

Neuroimmunology and brain immune interactions — the bidirectional communication between the nervous system and immune system through neural, endocrine, and humoral pathways that collectively determine the brain's response to peripheral inflammation, the immune system's response to neural signals, and the pathophysiology of conditions at the interface including depression, schizophrenia, Alzheimer's disease, multiple sclerosis, and the post-COVID neurological syndrome. Microglia as brain immune cells — the resident macrophages of the brain constituting 10-15 percent of brain cells, derived from yolk sac progenitors and maintained by local proliferation, continuously surveilling the brain parenchyma with highly motile processes, responding to injury and infection with rapid activation and cytokine production, participating in normal brain development and plasticity through synaptic pruning using the complement pathway, and becoming chronically activated in neurodegenerative disease contributing to progressive neuronal loss through oxidative stress and pro-inflammatory cytokine production. Neuroinflammation and psychiatric disease — the evidence that peripheral inflammatory cytokines including IL-6, TNF-alpha, and IL-1beta can produce the full spectrum of depressive symptoms through the mechanisms of tryptophan catabolism to kynurenine reducing serotonin availability, induction of indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase increasing quinolinic acid production with NMDA receptor agonist activity, activation of microglia and increased brain cytokine production, and disruption of glutamate reuptake increasing excitotoxic tone — providing the biological basis for the inflammatory subtype of depression. Cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway — Kevin Tracey's discovery that efferent vagal nerve stimulation suppresses peripheral macrophage TNF-alpha production through the splenic nerve and splenic macrophage alpha7 nicotinic acetylcholine receptors — establishing a direct neural control of peripheral inflammation through which the brain actively suppresses immune activation, with implications for vagal nerve stimulation as an anti-inflammatory therapy in rheumatoid arthritis, sepsis, and inflammatory bowel disease. The Heart Shen neuroimmune axis correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that the Heart governing Shen and consciousness is intimately connected with the immune defensive function of the body, with Shen disturbance both causing and resulting from pathological inflammatory states — a correspondence with the neuroimmune axis through which depression and stress dysregulate immune function and inflammation dysregulates mood and cognition.
