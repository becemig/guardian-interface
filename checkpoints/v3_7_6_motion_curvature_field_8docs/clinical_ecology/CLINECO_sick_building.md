# Sick Building Syndrome

## Abstract

Sick building syndrome — the cluster of nonspecific symptoms including headache, eye and throat irritation, fatigue, difficulty concentrating, and skin irritation experienced by building occupants that are temporally related to time spent in a specific building and resolve when occupants leave, without identifiable specific illness causation, affecting an estimated 30 percent of new and remodeled buildings according to WHO estimates and representing a major source of occupational morbidity and reduced productivity — arises from the complex interaction of inadequate ventilation, indoor chemical emissions from building materials and furnishings, microbial contamination of HVAC systems, and individual susceptibility factors. Indoor air quality science — the investigation of indoor air as a complex mixture of outdoor air pollutants entering through ventilation, indoor chemical emissions from thousands of building products including volatile organic compounds from paints adhesives carpets furniture and cleaning products, particulate matter from occupant activities and outdoor infiltration, biological contaminants including mold bacteria dust mites and pet dander, and combustion products from gas appliances and attached garages, with indoor air in modern tightly sealed energy-efficient buildings frequently exceeding outdoor air in concentrations of many pollutants. VOC and particulate indoor exposure — the hundreds of volatile organic compounds measured in indoor air including formaldehyde from pressed wood products and insulation at concentrations associated with eye and respiratory irritation, benzene from tobacco smoke and stored fuels as a leukemogen, trichloroethylene from dry cleaning and degreasing agents as a probable carcinogen, and the mixture effects of multiple simultaneous VOC exposures producing symptoms at concentrations below individual threshold values. HVAC contamination — the role of heating, ventilation, and air conditioning systems as both potential sources of biological and chemical contamination through dirty coils, stagnant drain pans, and contaminated ductwork, and as determinants of ventilation adequacy measured by CO2 concentration as a proxy for fresh air delivery, with inadequate ventilation increasing CO2, VOCs, and bioaerosol concentrations and directly impairing cognitive performance at CO2 concentrations common in classrooms and offices. The Environmental Qi Poison habitat correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that the quality of the living and working environment profoundly affects health, with environmental Qi poisons including foul air, damp environments, and toxic vapors recognized as pathogenic factors capable of producing the multisystem complaints of sick building syndrome.

## Indoor Air Chemistry

- VOC sources: paints adhesives carpets furniture pressed wood cleaning products.
- Formaldehyde: pressed wood insulation — eye respiratory irritant probable carcinogen.
- Benzene: tobacco smoke stored fuels — leukemogen no safe threshold.
- Mixture effects: VOC combinations produce symptoms below individual thresholds.

## Ventilation Adequacy

- CO2 proxy: indoor CO2 above 1000 ppm indicates inadequate fresh air.
- Cognitive impairment: CO2 above 1000 ppm reduces decision-making performance.
- ASHRAE standard: 15-20 cfm per person minimum ventilation.
- Energy efficiency tradeoff: tight buildings reduce ventilation increase pollutant concentration.

## HVAC Contamination

- Drain pans: stagnant water supports mold and Legionella growth.
- Cooling coils: organic matter accumulation supports biofilm.
- Ductwork: dust accumulation supports mold and bacterial growth.
- Remediation: HVAC cleaning filtration upgrade ventilation increase.
