# Micronutrients and Cofactors

## Abstract

Micronutrients — vitamins, minerals, and trace elements — are required in small quantities but are essential cofactors for hundreds of enzymatic reactions that underlie energy metabolism, DNA synthesis and repair, immune function, neurotransmitter production, and antioxidant defense. Bruce Ames' triage theory proposes that when micronutrients are scarce, the body prioritizes short-term survival functions over long-term health maintenance — explaining how subclinical deficiencies can accelerate aging without producing overt clinical disease. Vitamin D functions as a hormone, with receptors in nearly every tissue, and deficiency is extraordinarily prevalent in modern populations. Magnesium is required as a cofactor for over 300 enzymes including ATP synthase, DNA polymerase, and all kinases. B vitamins are central to the methylation cycle, which regulates gene expression, neurotransmitter synthesis, and detoxification. Zinc is required for over 300 enzymes and is critical for immune function and DNA repair. Nutrigenomics reveals that genetic variants like MTHFR polymorphisms alter individual micronutrient requirements, supporting personalized nutrition approaches. In TCM, micronutrient sufficiency maps to the quality of Gu Qi (food essence) and the Spleen's capacity to extract refined substance from food.

## Fat-Soluble Vitamins

- Vitamin A (retinol): vision, immune function, epithelial integrity, gene expression via RAR
- Vitamin D (calcitriol): calcium absorption, immune modulation, gene expression - 1000+ genes
- Vitamin D deficiency: >1 billion people worldwide - linked to cancer, autoimmunity, depression
- Vitamin E (tocopherol): lipid-soluble antioxidant - protects cell membranes from peroxidation
- Vitamin K2 (menaquinone): activates osteocalcin and MGP - bone and vascular calcification
- Storage: stored in adipose and liver - fat-soluble vitamins accumulate - toxicity possible

## B Vitamins and Methylation

- B1 (thiamine): pyruvate dehydrogenase cofactor - carbohydrate metabolism - deficiency causes beriberi
- B2 (riboflavin): FAD and FMN - electron carriers in ETC - antioxidant recycling
- B3 (niacin): NAD+ and NADP+ precursor - central to all redox metabolism
- B5 (pantothenic acid): CoA precursor - fatty acid synthesis and beta-oxidation
- B6 (pyridoxal phosphate): transamination, neurotransmitter synthesis, heme synthesis
- B9 (folate): one-carbon metabolism - DNA synthesis - neural tube development
- B12 (cobalamin): methylmalonyl-CoA mutase and methionine synthase - methylation and myelin
- MTHFR: methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase - 677C>T variant reduces enzyme activity 70 percent
- Methylation cycle: folate-B12-methionine-SAM - donates methyl groups to DNA, neurotransmitters

## Key Minerals

- Magnesium: 300+ enzyme cofactor - ATP must be bound to Mg2+ to be active - sleep, muscle
- Magnesium deficiency: very common - depleted by stress, alcohol, sugar, medications
- Zinc: 300+ enzymes - immune, wound healing, taste, smell, testosterone, DNA repair
- Iron: hemoglobin, myoglobin, cytochromes - oxygen transport and ETC
- Calcium: bone mineral, muscle contraction, neurotransmitter release, signaling
- Selenium: selenoproteins - glutathione peroxidase antioxidant - thyroid hormone conversion
- Iodine: thyroid hormone synthesis - T3 and T4 - metabolic rate

## Ames Triage Theory

- Premise: evolution prioritized short-term survival over long-term health maintenance
- Mechanism: scarce micronutrients allocated to immediate survival enzymes first
- Long-term cost: DNA repair, antioxidant defense, and aging-prevention functions sacrificed
- Subclinical deficiency: no overt disease but accelerated aging and disease accumulation
- Optimization: getting all micronutrients above triage threshold is fundamental longevity strategy
- TCM: full extraction of Gu Qi requires healthy Spleen function - weak Spleen means deficiency
