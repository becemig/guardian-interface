# Planetary Health and Constitutional Medicine

## Abstract

Planetary health — the interdisciplinary field studying the relationships between human health and the natural systems of Earth — proposes that human health and the health of Earth's natural systems are inseparable: the degradation of ecosystems, the loss of biodiversity, the disruption of biogeochemical cycles, and the destabilization of the climate system are simultaneously environmental crises and public health crises, producing cascading effects on human physical and mental health that cannot be adequately addressed through conventional disease-by-disease medicine. The Lancet Planetary Health commission articulated the framework that humans are embedded in and dependent upon the natural systems of the Earth, and that the Anthropocene disruption of those systems constitutes the greatest threat to human health in the history of medicine. The exposome — the totality of environmental exposures an individual experiences across the lifespan, from air and water quality to green space access, microbiome diversity, chemical exposures, and climate — is the environmental complement to the genome, and the interaction of the exposome with the genome determines the phenotypic health of the individual. Graham Rook's old friends hypothesis proposes that the immune system co-evolved with a specific community of organisms — parasites, commensal bacteria, and environmental microorganisms encountered through contact with soil, water, and animals — and that the modern loss of these old friends through urbanization, antibiotics, and environmental degradation produces the immune dysregulation underlying the epidemic of allergic, autoimmune, and inflammatory diseases. Biophilia — E.O. Wilson's hypothesis that humans have an innate affinity for the natural world derived from millions of years of evolution in natural environments — predicts that contact with natural environments produces measurable physiological and psychological benefits, a prediction confirmed by the extensive research literature on nature exposure, forest bathing, and green space health effects. Earth as patient frames the planetary health perspective in clinical terms: the Earth's ecosystems are the patient, the disruption of their homeostatic regulatory systems is the disease, and the task of planetary health medicine is to diagnose and treat the planetary condition.
