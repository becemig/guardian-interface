# Psychoneuroimmunology Overview

## Abstract

Psychoneuroimmunology (PNI) is the scientific field studying the bidirectional communication between the nervous system, endocrine system, and immune system. Founded by Robert Ader and Nicholas Cohen in 1975 when they demonstrated that immune responses could be classically conditioned — meaning the brain could modulate immunity through learned associations — PNI overturned the dogma of immune system autonomy. The nervous system communicates with the immune system via the HPA axis (glucocorticoids), the sympathetic nervous system (catecholamines), and the vagus nerve (acetylcholine). The immune system communicates back to the brain via cytokines, which cross the blood-brain barrier or signal via vagal afferents, producing sickness behavior, mood changes, cognitive alterations, and HPA activation. This bidirectional circuit means that psychological states directly regulate immune function and that immune activation directly produces psychological and neurological changes. Guardian Interface models this axis as one of the core integrating systems linking somatic, emotional, and cognitive node clusters.

## Founding Discoveries

- Ader and Cohen 1975: conditioned taste aversion with cyclophosphamide - conditioned immunosuppression
- Implication: brain can learn to suppress immunity - nervous and immune systems connected
- Besedovsky 1977: immune activation changes hypothalamic firing rates - immune-to-brain signal confirmed
- Felten 1985: sympathetic nerve fibers found in lymphoid organs - direct neural innervation of immune tissue
- Cytokine receptors in brain: IL-1 receptors found in hippocampus and hypothalamus
- Paradigm shift: immune system is not autonomous - it is a sensory organ for the brain

## Brain-to-Immune Pathways

- HPA axis: cortisol is primary immunosuppressive glucocorticoid - suppresses NF-kB and cytokines
- Acute cortisol: redistributes immune cells - enhances innate, suppresses adaptive temporarily
- Chronic cortisol: global immunosuppression - increased infection and cancer risk
- Sympathetic nervous system: norepinephrine binds adrenergic receptors on lymphocytes
- SNS-immune: acute stress mobilizes NK cells and neutrophils - prepares for wound infection
- Vagus nerve efferent: acetylcholine suppresses macrophage TNF-alpha - cholinergic anti-inflammatory
- Neuropeptides: substance P, VIP, NPY all modulate immune cell function

## Immune-to-Brain Pathways

- Cytokines: IL-1, IL-6, TNF-alpha signal to brain via multiple routes
- BBB crossing: small cytokines cross BBB at circumventricular organs
- Vagal afferents: peripheral cytokines activate vagal sensory neurons - rapid brain signal
- Perivascular macrophages: brain border cells transduce peripheral inflammation to CNS
- Prostaglandins: peripheral IL-1 induces brain prostaglandin E2 - mediates fever and sickness
- Speed: vagal route is fastest - minutes - BBB crossing is slower - hours

## Sickness Behavior

- Definition: behavioral syndrome induced by peripheral immune activation
- Components: fatigue, social withdrawal, anhedonia, reduced appetite, increased pain, cognitive slowing
- Adaptive function: conserves energy for immune response - reduces pathogen spread
- Mediator: IL-1 beta in brain is primary mediator - blocked by IL-1 receptor antagonist
- Depression overlap: sickness behavior and depression share symptoms - cytokine theory of depression
- TCM: invasion of pathogenic factors - Zheng Qi retreats inward - Wei Qi depleted

## Conditioned Immune Responses

- Classical conditioning: neutral stimulus paired with immunosuppressant - later produces immune suppression alone
- Clinical implications: placebo immune effects, white coat hypertension immune component
- Conditioned enhancement: positive conditioning can enhance NK cell activity
- Stress conditioning: learned helplessness reduces NK cell activity
- Therapeutic potential: conditioned responses may reduce drug doses needed for immunosuppression
