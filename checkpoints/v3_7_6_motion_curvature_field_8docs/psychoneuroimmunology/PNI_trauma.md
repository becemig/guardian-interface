# Trauma and Immune Dysregulation

## Abstract

Adverse childhood experiences (ACEs) and psychological trauma produce lasting dysregulation of the immune system through epigenetic programming of immune cell gene expression, HPA axis sensitization, and autonomic nervous system remodeling. The original ACE study (Felitti 1998) demonstrated dose-response relationships between childhood adversity and adult rates of heart disease, cancer, autoimmune conditions, and early death. Subsequent research has shown that childhood maltreatment elevates inflammatory biomarkers decades later, predicts glucocorticoid resistance in adulthood, and shortens telomere length — a cellular marker of biological aging. PTSD is associated with a distinctive immune profile including elevated IL-6, CRP, and TNF-alpha alongside paradoxical hypocortisolism. Trauma immune dysregulation can be transmitted intergenerationally through epigenetic mechanisms affecting offspring immune reactivity before birth. ## ACE Study Findings

- ACE score: counts 10 categories of childhood adversity - abuse, neglect, household dysfunction
- Dose-response: each additional ACE increases adult disease risk by 20-30 percent
- ACE 4 plus: 460 percent increased risk of depression, 240 percent increased risk of STI
- Mechanisms: chronic stress programming of HPA, ANS, immune, and metabolic systems
- Prevalence: 64 percent of US adults have at least one ACE - 17 percent have 4 or more
- Reversibility: ACE effects modifiable by protective relationships and therapeutic intervention

## Epigenetic Immune Programming

- DNA methylation: early adversity alters methylation of immune regulatory genes
- GR gene: NR3C1 methylation reduces glucocorticoid receptor expression - impairs HPA feedback
- FKBP5: stress-sensitive gene regulating GR sensitivity - early trauma programs hypomethylation
- IL-6 promoter: childhood maltreatment hypomethylates IL-6 promoter - lifelong elevated IL-6
- Persistence: epigenetic changes established in childhood persist into adulthood
- Reversibility: some epigenetic marks reversible with psychotherapy and environmental change
- TCM: Jing-level imprinting - constitutional patterns established early in life

## PTSD Immune Profile

- Hypocortisolism: low basal cortisol with enhanced negative feedback - opposite of melancholic depression
- GC hypersensitivity: immune cells hypersensitive to cortisol - despite low cortisol
- Pro-inflammatory: elevated IL-6, CRP, TNF-alpha despite low cortisol
- NK cell reduction: reduced NK cytotoxicity - impaired tumor and viral surveillance
- NF-kB: elevated NF-kB activation in PTSD - drives pro-inflammatory gene expression
- Paradox: low cortisol yet high inflammation - GC resistance at genomic level despite sensitivity at HPA
- Treatment: EMDR and somatic therapies normalize HPA and inflammatory markers

## Telomere Shortening

- Telomeres: protective caps on chromosomes - shorten with each cell division
- Stress acceleration: chronic psychological stress accelerates telomere shortening
- Childhood adversity: ACEs predict shorter telomere length in adults
- Inflammation: oxidative stress from chronic inflammation damages telomeres
- Biological age: short telomeres indicate accelerated biological aging
- Meditation: MBSR increases telomerase activity - protective against shortening
- TCM: Jing depletion as accelerated aging - premature exhaustion of constitutional reserve

## Intergenerational Immune Transmission

- Maternal stress: prenatal stress alters fetal HPA and immune programming via glucocorticoids
- Epigenetic inheritance: some stress-induced epigenetic marks transmitted via sperm and egg
- Holocaust survivor offspring: altered cortisol and FKBP5 methylation in children of survivors
- Microbiome transmission: maternal dysbiosis transmitted to infant at birth and via breastfeeding
- Protective: maternal security and attunement buffers stress immune programming in offspring
- TCM: prenatal Jing quality determined by ancestral health and maternal state during gestation
