# Endocannabinoid Synthesis and Degradation

## Abstract

Endocannabinoid synthesis and degradation — the unique on-demand biosynthesis of lipid signaling molecules from membrane phospholipid precursors in response to neuronal activity or receptor stimulation, with the two primary endocannabinoids being N-arachidonoylethanolamide anandamide and 2-arachidonoylglycerol 2-AG, synthesized by distinct enzymatic pathways, acting on CB1 and CB2 receptors, and rapidly degraded by specific hydrolytic enzymes that terminate their signaling — distinguishing endocannabinoids from classical neurotransmitters by their lipophilicity, membrane synthesis, retrograde signaling direction, and the absence of vesicular storage. Anandamide biosynthesis — the synthesis of anandamide from N-arachidonoyl phosphatidylethanolamine by the phospholipase D enzyme NAPE-PLD in response to calcium influx and Gq-coupled receptor activation in postsynaptic neurons, with anandamide acting as a partial agonist at CB1 with lower efficacy than 2-AG, also activating TRPV1 channels contributing to its complex pain-modulating pharmacology, and being degraded primarily by fatty acid amide hydrolase FAAH in postsynaptic neurons and astrocytes — with FAAH inhibition producing anxiolytic and analgesic effects. 2-Arachidonoylglycerol biosynthesis — the synthesis of 2-AG from diacylglycerol by diacylglycerol lipase alpha DAGLalpha in response to postsynaptic calcium influx and metabotropic glutamate receptor activation, serving as a full agonist at CB1 and CB2 at concentrations 100-1000 fold higher than anandamide, being the primary endocannabinoid mediating retrograde synaptic suppression at most brain synapses, and being degraded primarily by monoacylglycerol lipase MAGL in presynaptic terminals. Clinical endocannabinoid deficiency — Ethan Russo's hypothesis that conditions including migraine, fibromyalgia, and irritable bowel syndrome share the pathophysiological basis of insufficient endocannabinoid tone, with each condition characterized by reduced pain threshold, visceral hypersensitivity, and autonomic dysregulation that would be explained by insufficient endocannabinoid signaling — with clinical support from reduced CSF anandamide in migraine patients and the therapeutic response of these conditions to cannabis. The Jing Essence on-demand mobilization correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that Kidney Jing provides the constitutional reserve of vital essence that is mobilized on demand during times of stress, illness, or intense activity — a classical framework corresponding to the on-demand biosynthesis of endocannabinoids from membrane precursors in response to physiological demand, providing immediate regulatory signaling drawn from a reservoir embedded in the cell membrane.

## Anandamide Pathway

- Precursor: N-arachidonoyl phosphatidylethanolamine — membrane phospholipid.
- Synthesis enzyme: NAPE-PLD — calcium and Gq activation dependent.
- Receptor: partial CB1 agonist full TRPV1 agonist — dual pharmacology.
- Degradation: FAAH fatty acid amide hydrolase — postsynaptic astrocytic.

## 2-AG Pathway

- Precursor: diacylglycerol from phosphatidylinositol — phospholipase C cleaves.
- Synthesis enzyme: DAGLalpha — calcium and mGluR5 activation dependent.
- Receptor: full CB1 CB2 agonist — higher efficacy than anandamide.
- Degradation: MAGL monoacylglycerol lipase — presynaptic terminal.

## Endocannabinoid Deficiency

- Migraine: reduced CSF anandamide — low endocannabinoid tone hypothesis.
- Fibromyalgia: central sensitization overlap — ECS regulation of pain threshold.
- IBS: visceral hypersensitivity — ECS regulation of gut motility and pain.
- Treatment: FAAH MAGL inhibition — raising endocannabinoid tone therapeutically.
