# Cognitive Behavioral Therapy

## Abstract

Cognitive behavioral therapy CBT developed by Aaron Beck is the most extensively researched form of psychotherapy with robust evidence across depression, anxiety disorders, OCD, PTSD, eating disorders, and chronic pain. Beck's cognitive model proposes that psychological distress arises from distorted patterns of thinking — the cognitive triad in depression comprising negative views of self, world, and future. Automatic thoughts are rapid unconscious cognitive appraisals that trigger emotional responses — CBT identifies, examines, and restructures maladaptive automatic thoughts. Core schemas — deeply held beliefs about self and world formed in early experience — organize automatic thought patterns. Cognitive distortions include all-or-nothing thinking, catastrophizing, mind reading, emotional reasoning, and personalization. Behavioral activation is a key component of depression treatment — scheduling rewarding activities counteracts the withdrawal and inertia of depression through behavioral change preceding cognitive change. Exposure therapy for anxiety disorders systematically confronts feared situations to enable extinction learning. The evidence base for CBT is extensive with meta-analyses demonstrating superiority to control conditions across most anxiety and depressive disorders. Yi restructuring in TCM — the Spleen spirit's capacity for clear discernment and accurate cognitive processing — corresponds to the CBT process of identifying and correcting distorted cognitive patterns.

## Cognitive Triad

- Negative view of self — I am defective, inadequate, or worthless — is the first leg of the depression triad.
- Negative view of the world — the world is unfair, demanding, and full of obstacles — is the second leg.
- Negative view of the future — nothing will improve and the future holds only suffering — is the third leg.
- All three legs mutually reinforce creating the self-sustaining cognitive architecture of clinical depression.

## Automatic Thoughts

- Automatic thoughts arise spontaneously in response to situations without deliberate conscious processing.
- They are often brief, habitual, and accepted as true without examination.
- Socratic questioning examines the evidence for and against automatic thoughts revealing their distorted nature.
- Thought records systematically document situations, automatic thoughts, emotions, and alternative responses.

## Schema Work

- Core schemas are deep organizing beliefs about self and world formed through early developmental experience.
- They filter information selectively attending to schema-consistent evidence and dismissing inconsistent evidence.
- Schema change requires sustained work through behavioral experiments, imagery rescripting, and therapeutic relationship.
- Young's schema therapy extends CBT schema work to address personality disorder level pathology.
