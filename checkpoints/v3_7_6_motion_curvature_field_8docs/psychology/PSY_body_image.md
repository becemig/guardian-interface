# Body Image and Embodiment

## Abstract

Body image is the multidimensional psychological experience of one's own body including perceptual, cognitive, affective, and behavioral components. Body schema is the implicit sensorimotor map of the body used for action and movement distinct from the explicit perceptual body image. Interoceptive accuracy — the precision of sensing internal body signals — is a key determinant of embodiment and body image accuracy. The rubber hand illusion demonstrates that body ownership extends to artificial limbs through multisensory integration suggesting that the felt sense of body is constructed rather than directly perceived. Body dysmorphic disorder involves severe distortion of body image perception unrelated to actual appearance. Eating disorders involve complex distortions of body image, interoceptive awareness, and the meaning of bodily experience. Shen-body integration in TCM — the Heart-Mind inhabiting and governing the body with clarity — corresponds to the optimal embodiment state where self-perception accurately reflects the body's actual state.

## Body Schema vs Body Image

- Body schema is the implicit sensorimotor body map used for action, balance, and movement coordination.
- Body image is the explicit perceptual and conceptual representation of the body available to conscious reflection.
- Body schema operates automatically below awareness while body image is consciously accessible.
- Dissociation between body schema and body image produces coordination difficulties and perceptual distortions.

## Interoceptive Accuracy

- Interoceptive accuracy is measured by heartbeat detection tasks — sensing one's heartbeat without tactile cues.
- Higher interoceptive accuracy is associated with more accurate body image and reduced body dissatisfaction.
- Anorexia nervosa involves impaired interoceptive accuracy contributing to distorted body perception.
- Somatic practices including Tai Chi and yoga improve interoceptive accuracy and body image satisfaction.

## Rubber Hand Illusion

- Synchronous stroking of a rubber hand and hidden real hand produces ownership of the rubber hand.
- This illusion demonstrates that body ownership depends on multisensory integration not anatomical fact.
- The felt sense of body boundary is constructed from visual, tactile, and proprioceptive integration.
- Meditation practice alters the rubber hand illusion suggesting that embodiment is trainable.
