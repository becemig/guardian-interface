# Meaning Making

## Abstract

Meaning making is the psychological process of constructing purpose, coherence, and significance from experience, particularly in the face of adversity, loss, and suffering. Viktor Frankl's logotherapy proposes that the primary human motivation is the will to meaning — the drive to find purpose in existence — and that meaning can be found in suffering itself. Dan McAdams' narrative identity theory proposes that the self is fundamentally a story — a narrative constructed from life experiences that provides coherence and continuity across time. Post-traumatic growth describes the positive psychological changes that can emerge from the struggle with highly challenging life circumstances. Aaron Antonovsky's salutogenesis model proposes that sense of coherence — comprehensibility, manageability, and meaningfulness — is the primary determinant of health in the face of adversity. Zhi — the Kidney will and purpose — provides the deep constitutional ground of meaning and directed life purpose.

## Frankl and Logotherapy

- The will to meaning is the primary human motivation preceding and transcending the drives for pleasure and power.
- Meaning can be found in three ways: creating work, experiencing beauty or love, and choosing one's attitude toward suffering.
- Even in unavoidable suffering the human capacity to choose one's response preserves dignity and meaning.
- The existential vacuum — meaninglessness — is the primary cause of neurosis in modern affluent society.

## Narrative Identity

- The self is a narrative — a story constructed from life experiences providing coherence and continuity.
- Narrative identity integrates past, present, and future into a coherent autobiographical account.
- Redemption narratives — finding growth or meaning from adversity — are associated with well-being and maturity.
- Contamination narratives — good experiences turning bad — are associated with depression and stagnation.

## Post-Traumatic Growth

- Post-traumatic growth describes positive change emerging from the struggle with highly challenging circumstances.
- Growth domains include personal strength, new possibilities, relating to others, appreciation of life, and spiritual change.
- Growth does not mean absence of suffering but the co-existence of distress with positive transformation.
- The narrative reconstruction of the traumatic experience is central to the growth process.
