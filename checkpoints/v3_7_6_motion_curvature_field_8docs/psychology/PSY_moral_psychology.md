# Moral Psychology

## Abstract

Moral psychology examines the psychological processes underlying moral judgment, moral development, and moral behavior — including the roles of intuition versus reasoning, emotion versus cognition, and individual versus social factors in shaping how people distinguish right from wrong and how they act on those distinctions. Jonathan Haidt's moral foundations theory proposes that human moral psychology is organized around six innate foundational modules — care-harm, fairness-cheating, loyalty-betrayal, authority-subversion, sanctity-degradation, and liberty-oppression — that evolved to solve different adaptive problems of social living. Haidt's research demonstrated that moral judgments are primarily intuitive rather than reasoned — people experience immediate gut-level moral reactions and then construct post-hoc rationalizations for those intuitions. Lawrence Kohlberg's stage model of moral development proposes that individuals develop through six stages of moral reasoning from pre-conventional self-interest, through conventional social conformity, to post-conventional principled ethics. Carol Gilligan's critique of Kohlberg identified a care ethics perspective that emphasizes relationships and context rather than abstract universal principles — proposing that the relational, contextual orientation of care ethics represents a distinct moral voice rather than a developmental deficit. Virtue ethics — the Aristotelian tradition emphasizing the development of virtuous character traits — resonates strongly with both the TCM cultivation tradition and contemporary positive psychology research on character strengths. Zhi as moral will in TCM — the Kidney spirit governing the capacity for sustained commitment, integrity, and the follow-through that translates values into action — corresponds to the virtue ethics concept of will power as the executive capacity that enables virtuous character to actually produce virtuous action.

## Moral Intuition

- Moral judgments are primarily fast intuitive responses rather than deliberate reasoning.
- The reasoning that follows moral intuition is largely post-hoc rationalization.
- Moral emotions — disgust, compassion, admiration, indignation — are the primary drivers of moral judgment.
- This corresponds to the TCM understanding that the Five Shen govern moral orientation before Yi deliberate thinking engages.

## Moral Foundations

- Care-harm: the foundation of compassion and aversion to suffering — Heart Shen compassion.
- Fairness-cheating: the foundation of reciprocity and justice — Liver Hun equitable judgment.
- Loyalty-betrayal: the foundation of group cohesion — Spleen Yi relational belonging.
- Authority-subversion: the foundation of hierarchy and tradition — Kidney Zhi respect for elders.
- Sanctity-degradation: the foundation of purity and pollution — Lung Po body respect.

## Virtue Ethics

- Virtue ethics proposes that the goal of moral development is the cultivation of virtuous character.
- Virtues are stable dispositions to perceive, feel, and act well in morally relevant situations.
- Character strengths research identifies 24 universal virtues across cultures and traditions.
- This corresponds directly to the TCM five virtue cultivation system: benevolence, righteousness, propriety, wisdom, integrity.
