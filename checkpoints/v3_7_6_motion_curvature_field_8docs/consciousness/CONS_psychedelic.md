# Psychedelic Neuroscience

## Abstract

Classical psychedelics — psilocybin, LSD, DMT, mescaline — produce their profound alterations of consciousness primarily through agonism at the 5-HT2A serotonin receptor, particularly in layer V pyramidal neurons of the prefrontal cortex. The REBUS model (Relaxed Beliefs Under Psychedelics) proposes that psychedelics flatten the predictive hierarchy by reducing top-down precision weighting, allowing bottom-up sensory and associative signals to dominate experience. The entropic brain hypothesis frames psychedelic states as high-entropy brain states with increased neural signal diversity, reduced modularity, and dissolution of the default mode network. This controlled dissolution of habitual self-referential processing is proposed as the core therapeutic mechanism — enabling flexible updating of rigid beliefs, trauma memories, and depressive self-schemas. Psychedelics also trigger a neuroplasticity window by increasing BDNF, promoting dendritic spine growth, and enhancing synaptic plasticity for days to weeks post-dose. MDMA and ketamine have distinct mechanisms but overlapping therapeutic applications.

## 5-HT2A Mechanism

- Primary target: 5-HT2A receptor - Gq-coupled - layer V pyramidal neurons
- Location: prefrontal cortex, claustrum, thalamic reticular nucleus
- Effect: increases glutamate release - excites thalamocortical circuits
- Thalamic gating: normal thalamus filters sensory input - 5-HT2A disrupts filtering
- Head-twitch response: animal model marker of 5-HT2A activation
- Tolerance: 5-HT2A rapidly downregulates - cross-tolerance between classical psychedelics
- No addiction: 5-HT2A agonism does not activate mesolimbic dopamine - no dependence

## REBUS Model

- Relaxed Beliefs Under Psychedelics: psychedelics reduce precision of high-level priors
- Predictive hierarchy: normally top-down predictions dominate sensory processing
- Psychedelic effect: flattens hierarchy - bottom-up signals gain influence
- Result: habitual perceptual and self-models destabilized - new associations possible
- Therapeutic implication: rigid depressive, trauma, or addictive schemas can be revised
- Ego dissolution: extreme flattening - no stable self-model remains - mystical experience

## Entropic Brain Hypothesis

- Neural entropy: diversity of neural signal patterns - measure of brain state complexity
- Normal waking: intermediate entropy - structured but flexible
- Deep sleep: low entropy - highly synchronized slow waves
- Psychedelic state: high entropy - increased signal diversity, reduced modularity
- DMN dissolution: default mode network loses coherence - self-narrative suspended
- Cross-network communication: psychedelics increase between-network connectivity
- fMRI: psychedelics produce more complex, less predictable BOLD signal patterns

## Neuroplasticity Window

- BDNF: psilocybin and LSD increase BDNF in prefrontal cortex and hippocampus
- Dendritic spines: psilocybin increases spine density and head size in mouse PFC
- TrkB: psychedelics directly bind TrkB receptor - independent of 5-HT2A - promotes plasticity
- Window duration: neuroplasticity elevated for days to weeks post-dose
- Integration: behavioral change most likely when therapy coincides with plasticity window
- Microdosing: sub-perceptual doses may sustain neuroplasticity without acute experience

## Therapeutic Mechanisms

- Psilocybin: treatment-resistant depression, end-of-life anxiety, addiction - 2 doses
- MDMA: 3,4-methylenedioxymethamphetamine - releases serotonin, dopamine, norepinephrine, oxytocin
- MDMA-PTSD: phase 3 trials - 67 percent no longer meet PTSD criteria after 3 sessions
- Ketamine: NMDA antagonist - rapid antidepressant - hours not weeks - distinct from classic psychedelics
- Mystical experience: intensity of mystical experience predicts therapeutic outcome
- Set and setting: mindset and environment are primary determinants of experience quality
