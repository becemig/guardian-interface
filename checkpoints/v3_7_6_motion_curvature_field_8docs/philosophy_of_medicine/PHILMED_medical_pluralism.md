# Medical Pluralism and Integrative Medicine

## Abstract

Medical pluralism and integrative medicine — the recognition that biomedicine coexists with diverse traditional and complementary medical systems that serve the majority of the world's population, and the emerging integrative medicine movement that seeks to combine the best evidence from all healing traditions into a patient-centered comprehensive care model — raises fundamental questions about epistemological pluralism, the standards of evidence appropriate for evaluating heterogeneous healing traditions, the ethics of medical pluralism in global health contexts, and the philosophical possibility of genuine integration versus superficial coexistence of incommensurable medical paradigms. Traditional medicine global burden — the WHO estimate that 80 percent of the world's population relies primarily on traditional medicine for primary health care, with traditional Chinese medicine, Ayurveda, Unani, and indigenous healing systems collectively serving more patients than biomedicine globally, and with the global traditional medicine use representing an enormous repository of empirical clinical observation accumulated over millennia that biomedical research has barely begun to systematically evaluate. WHO Traditional Medicine Strategy 2019-2025 — the WHO framework acknowledging traditional medicine as a crucial component of global health systems and calling for its integration into national health systems, standardization of traditional medicine education and practice, and systematic research evaluation of traditional medicine safety and efficacy, representing the official global health recognition of medical pluralism as a policy reality rather than a marginal alternative. Integrative medicine evidence challenges — the fundamental methodological difficulties in evaluating traditional medical systems using biomedical research methods, including the impossibility of blinding complex multicomponent individualized interventions, the inapplicability of disease-specific endpoints to system-level interventions, the cultural and contextual dependency of healing effects, and the fundamental ontological incommensurability between biomedical disease categories and traditional diagnostic categories such as TCM pattern diagnosis. The universal healing wisdom convergence describes the philosophical possibility and empirical evidence that diverse traditional medical systems independently arrived at overlapping insights about human health through different conceptual languages — with the convergence in understanding of breathwork, movement, diet, community, meaning, and relationship as foundations of health suggesting a universal empirical basis for healing wisdom that transcends any single paradigm.

## WHO Strategy Framework

- Safety: pharmacovigilance for herbal medicines traditional treatments.
- Quality: standardization of traditional medicine education and practice.
- Efficacy: systematic research evaluation using appropriate methods.
- Integration: national health system integration of traditional medicine.

## Integrative Medicine Framework

- Weil model: evidence-based selection from all healing traditions.
- Patient-centered: treatment plan integrating biomedical and traditional approaches.
- Evidence hierarchy problem: RCT hierarchy disadvantages traditional systems.
- Whole systems research: new methodologies for evaluating complex interventions.
