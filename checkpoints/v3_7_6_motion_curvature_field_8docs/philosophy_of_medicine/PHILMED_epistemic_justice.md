# Epistemic Justice in Medicine

## Abstract

Epistemic justice in medicine — the application of Miranda Fricker's epistemic justice framework to healthcare, examining how structural inequalities in credibility attribution, interpretive resources, and knowledge validation systematically disadvantage patients from marginalized groups in clinical encounters and in the broader production of biomedical knowledge — reveals that medical injustice is not only a matter of unequal access to care but also an epistemic injustice in which patients' illness testimonies are systematically disbelieved, their symptoms are misdiagnosed through the imposition of inappropriate interpretive frameworks, and the healing knowledge of their communities is excluded from legitimate medical knowledge production. Testimonial injustice in clinical encounters — the systematic underestimation of the credibility of patients from marginalized groups including women, Black patients, elderly patients, and patients with mental illness histories, producing the well-documented phenomena of undertreated pain in Black patients due to false beliefs about biological differences, delayed diagnosis of endometriosis and autoimmune conditions in women whose pain is attributed to psychological factors, and the medical gaslighting of patients whose medically unexplained symptoms are dismissed as psychosomatic. Hermeneutical injustice in diagnosis — the epistemic harm produced when a patient's illness experience falls outside the existing interpretive frameworks of medicine because those frameworks were developed without adequate input from affected populations, with examples including the absence of fibromyalgia, chronic fatigue syndrome, and complex PTSD from diagnostic categories until patient advocacy forced their recognition, and the systematic pathologization of normal human variation in gender, sexuality, and neurodevelopment by diagnostic frameworks reflecting majority culture norms. Structural racism in medicine — Harriet Washington's documentation of the centuries-long history of medical experimentation on Black bodies without consent, the exclusion of Black patients from clinical trials that then produce guidelines applied to them, the persistent myths about racial biological differences that distort clinical decision-making, and the structural underfunding of diseases disproportionately affecting marginalized communities — establishes that biomedical knowledge itself bears the marks of the structural inequalities within which it was produced. The universal Qi no hierarchy correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that Qi is the universal animating force present in all people regardless of social position, with the physician's ethical duty to serve all patients equally — a principle of universal healing without hierarchy that corresponds to the epistemic justice demand for equal credibility and equal care across social differences.

## Testimonial Injustice

- Credibility deficit: marginalized patients granted less credibility for illness reports.
- Pain undertreated: Black patients undertreated for pain due to false biological beliefs.
- Endometriosis: average 7-10 year diagnostic delay due to dismissal of women's pain.
- Medical gaslighting: patient symptoms attributed to psychological causes without evaluation.

## Hermeneutical Injustice

- Missing concepts: patient experience has no name in medical vocabulary.
- Fibromyalgia CFS: absent from diagnostic categories until patient advocacy.
- Pathologization: normal variation labeled disorder due to majority norms.
- Remedy: patient-led knowledge production diverse researcher representation.

## Decolonizing Medical Knowledge

- Colonial medicine: suppressed indigenous healing systems in colonial territories.
- Bioprospecting: extracted traditional plant knowledge without community benefit.
- Validation asymmetry: traditional knowledge requires RCT validation while biomedical knowledge does not.
- Decolonization: equal epistemic status for traditional knowledge systems.

## TCM Correspondence

- Universal Qi animating all persons equally corresponds to epistemic justice demand for equal credibility.
- TCM as non-Western healing knowledge system corresponds to traditional knowledge requiring epistemic justice recognition.
- TCM empirical clinical tradition as legitimate knowledge corresponds to decolonizing medicine including traditional systems.
- Physician ethical duty to all corresponds to structural justice demand for equal quality care.
