# Quantum Coherence as Qi Conduction Mechanism

## Abstract

The proposal that quantum coherence provides the physical mechanism for Qi conduction through meridian pathways — a hypothesis at the speculative frontier of quantum biology and TCM research — draws on converging lines of evidence from biophoton research, quantum electrodynamics of water, liquid crystal biology, and the molecular meridian research reviewed elsewhere in this archive to construct a physically grounded model of how coherent biological fields could support the rapid, coordinated, and informationally rich signaling attributed to the meridian system in classical TCM. Fritz-Albert Popp's biophoton research has demonstrated that living cells emit ultra-weak coherent light in the visible and near-UV range — far below the thermal emission level and with coherence properties indicating a laser-like biological light source rather than random thermal emission. Biophoton emission is highest from metabolically active cells, is reduced by anesthesia, correlates with health status, and has been measured to be significantly higher along acupuncture meridian pathways than along control lines — suggesting that the meridian system is a preferential channel for biological photon conduction through optically transparent connective tissue structures. Mae-Wan Ho's quantum electrodynamics QED model of the organism proposes that the living system is maintained as a quantum coherent whole through the quantum electrodynamic coherence domains of biological water — regions of liquid water in which all molecules oscillate in phase with an electromagnetic field trapped within the coherence domain, storing and releasing energy with quantum efficiency. Coherence domains in biological water extend from nanometers to millimeters and could in principle form a continuous coherent medium through the interstitial fluid spaces of the meridian pathways — providing a quantum electrodynamic basis for the rapid and coherent signal propagation attributed to meridian conduction. The liquid crystalline organization of biological macromolecules — proteins, DNA, collagen fibers, cell membranes — provides a second candidate substrate for coherent quantum signaling: liquid crystals support collective quantum excitations that propagate rapidly through the ordered molecular lattice, and the fascial connective tissue planes corresponding to meridian pathways are organized as liquid crystalline arrays of collagen fibers.
