# Dissipative Structures

## Abstract

Dissipative structures — the self-organized ordered patterns that arise in open thermodynamic systems far from equilibrium through the continuous dissipation of energy — were discovered and theorized by Nobel laureate Ilya Prigogine, whose work demonstrated that the second law of thermodynamics — the tendency of closed systems toward maximum entropy and disorder — does not prevent but actually drives the spontaneous emergence of order in open systems that exchange energy and matter with their environment. Erwin Schrodinger's 1944 book What is Life? posed the question that motivated much of subsequent biophysics: how do living organisms maintain their ordered, low-entropy structure against the thermodynamic tendency toward disorder? His answer — that organisms maintain themselves by importing negentropy negative entropy from their environment, feeding on ordered energy sources food, sunlight and exporting entropy as heat and metabolic waste — established the thermodynamic framework for understanding life as a far-from-equilibrium dissipative process. Prigogine's theory of dissipative structures formalized Schrodinger's insight: open systems maintained far from thermodynamic equilibrium by a continuous throughput of energy can spontaneously organize into ordered structures — dissipative structures — that are maintained by the continuous dissipation of energy and that would immediately collapse to disorder if the energy supply were interrupted. Bifurcations — the critical points where a dissipative system can spontaneously jump to a new organized state when the energy throughput exceeds a threshold — correspond to phase transitions in biological systems: the spontaneous onset of coordinated rhythmic activity in the heart, the self-organization of the immune response, and the phase transitions of neural dynamics between different brain states. Order through fluctuations — the mechanism by which small random fluctuations at a bifurcation point are amplified by positive feedback to produce the new organized state — provides a thermodynamic explanation for the sensitivity of biological systems to small perturbations at critical moments. Jing as thermodynamic reserve describes the TCM understanding of constitutional Jing — the inherited vital substance stored in the Kidney — as the thermodynamic reserve that sustains the organism's far-from-equilibrium dissipative structure through the lifespan.
