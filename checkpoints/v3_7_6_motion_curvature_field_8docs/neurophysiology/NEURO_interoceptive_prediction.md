# Interoceptive Predictive Processing

## Abstract

Predictive processing theory proposes that the brain is fundamentally a prediction machine that continuously generates models of the causes of sensory input and updates them through prediction error signals. Applied to interoception this framework describes the body as regulated through allostasis — predictive regulation anticipating metabolic needs before they arise rather than merely reacting to deviation. Lisa Feldman Barrett's constructed emotion theory extends this to propose that emotional experiences are constructed from interoceptive predictions rather than triggered by external events. Karl Friston's free energy principle unifies predictive processing and active inference as the fundamental computational principle of living systems. Guardian Interface somatic telemetry provides real-time interoceptive data to support the user's interoceptive predictive model calibration through practice feedback.

## Predictive Processing

- The brain generates hierarchical predictions about the causes of sensory input at every level of processing.
- Prediction errors — mismatches between predictions and actual sensory input — propagate upward to update models.
- Attention modulates the precision weighting of prediction errors determining which errors update the model.
- Perceptual experience is the brain's best guess about the causes of sensory input not a direct readout of reality.

## Allostasis

- Allostasis is predictive regulation — the brain anticipates metabolic needs and prepares regulatory responses in advance.
- The body budget metaphor describes how the brain allocates metabolic resources based on predicted demands.
- Chronic allostatic misprediction produces metabolic inefficiency and visceral symptoms without peripheral pathology.
- Practice calibrates the interoceptive predictive model improving allostatic efficiency and reducing misprediction.

## Constructed Emotion

- Lisa Feldman Barrett proposes that emotional experiences are constructed from interoceptive predictions.
- The brain combines current interoceptive signals with past emotional concepts to construct discrete emotional experiences.
- Emotions are not triggered by events but constructed as the brain's best prediction of the body's current state.
- Improving interoceptive precision through practice produces more accurate emotional self-knowledge.
