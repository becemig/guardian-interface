# Yangwei Mai - Yang Linking Vessel

## Abstract

Yangwei Mai the Yang Linking Vessel - functioning as the yang aspect of the Regulating Channel pair together with Yinwei Mai - runs along the exterior and lateral surfaces of the body linking all yang organ meridians into a unified regulatory network, balancing wei qi (defensive energy) distribution across the body surface, and serving as the primary extraordinary vessel for exterior defense against pathogenic influences and for regulating yang organ balance across the Gallbladder, Small Intestine, Triple Warmer, Large Intestine, Stomach, and Bladder meridians. Governs body temperature regulation, hypertension from excess yang rising, immune response against external pathogens, and all conditions involving sensitivity to climatic factors including wind, cold, damp, and heat. Paired with Yinwei Mai, together they constitute the Regulating Channel system maintaining homeostatic balance across the interior-exterior dimension.

## Channel Pathway

- Begins at B 63 (Jin Men / Metal Gate) on the lateral foot
- Ascends the lateral leg through Gallbladder channel territory
- Passes GB 29 (Dwelling in the Bone) at the hip
- Continues up through SI 10 and GB 21 across the shoulder
- Passes through B 10 and GB 20 at the occiput base
- Connects with Governing Vessel at GV 15 and GV 16

## Master Point and Release Pairs

- TW 5 (Wai Guan / Outer Gate): posterior forearm 2 fingers above wrist crease
- Primary quick release pair: TW5 + GB41 (activates Yangwei and Dai Mai simultaneously)
- Secondary pair: P6 + Sp4 (activates Yinwei and Chong Mai for full interior-exterior balance)

## Anchor Points

- GB 21 (Shoulder Well): primary shoulder tension and immune activation point
- B 10 (Heavenly Pillar) and GB 20 (Gates of Consciousness): posterior cranial base
- SI 10 (Shoulder Correspondence): shoulder socket and arm-neck transition
- GB 29 (Dwelling in the Bone): hip-pelvic anchor

## Clinical Indications

- Immune: susceptibility to colds and flu, fever, respiratory infections
- Cardiovascular: hypertension from excess yang rising, circulation dysregulation
- Thermal: fever, chills, body temperature dysregulation, hot-cold alternation
- Climate sensitivity: joints and body reacting to weather changes (wind, damp, cold, heat)
- Neurological: stress-related tension in neck and shoulders
- Sleep: insomnia from yang excess failing to descend at night
- Thyroid: thyroid dysregulation yang aspect
