# Taiji Principles

## Abstract

Taijiquan the supreme ultimate fist is the most widely practiced internal martial art and cultivation system in the world — a moving meditation that embodies Taoist cosmological principles in physical form while developing the internal martial qualities of Peng ward-off, Lu roll-back, Ji press, An push, Cai pluck, Lie split, Zhou elbow, and Kao shoulder. The foundational principle of Taiji is the dynamic interplay of Yin and Yang — every movement contains within it the seed of its opposite, fullness yields to emptiness, substantial yields to insubstantial. Song — often translated as relaxation but more precisely referring to the release of unnecessary tension while maintaining structural integrity — is the prerequisite for all Taiji development. Root is the quality of stable connection to the ground through relaxed structural alignment enabling the receipt and redirection of force. Chan Si Jin silk reeling energy is the spiral force quality characteristic of Chen style Taiji — a continuous spiraling movement originating from the Dan Tian and expressed through the limbs like silk being reeled from a cocoon. Wuji standing post practice cultivates the foundational qualities of song and root that enable Taiji movement quality. Peng Jin ward-off energy is the fundamental expansive springy quality underlying all eight Taiji energies — the quality of a fully inflated ball that rebounds force without collapse. Jing Jin transmission — the transmission of refined internal force through physical contact — is the highest expression of Taiji martial development. Modern research documents Taiji practice effects on balance, fall prevention, HRV, cortisol, and immune function.

## Song and Root

- Song is the release of unnecessary muscular tension while maintaining structural alignment and readiness.
- It cannot be achieved through effort — it requires the release of habitual holding patterns through patient practice.
- Root is the quality of stable ground connection enabling receipt and redirection of force without collapse.
- Song and root together enable the Taiji practitioner to remain centered and grounded under pressure.

## Silk Reeling

- Chan Si Jin silk reeling is the continuous spiral force quality originating from the Dan Tian.
- The spiral movement follows the myofascial spiral lines of the body connecting ground force to limb expression.
- Silk reeling practice trains the nervous system to coordinate the whole body as a unified spiral structure.
- The continuous quality of silk reeling prevents the breaks and gaps that create martial vulnerability.

## Eight Energies

- Peng ward-off is the foundational expansive springy quality underlying all eight energies.
- Lu roll-back receives and redirects incoming force by yielding and leading it to emptiness.
- Ji press transmits force through a smaller surface area concentrating Peng energy.
- An push releases stored potential energy through a wave-like whole-body extension.

## TCM Correspondence

- Kidney providing root and constitutional groundedness corresponds to the Taiji quality of root through the lower Dan Tian.
- Liver governing the sinews and their elastic recoil corresponds to the Peng Jin springy ward-off energy.
- Song releasing unnecessary tension corresponds to the TCM principle of free Qi flow through relaxed tissue.
- Silk reeling following the myofascial spiral lines corresponds to the sinew channel spiral pathways of TCM.
