# Chronotype and Social Jet Lag

## Abstract

Chronotype and social jet lag — the individual differences in the preferred timing of the sleep-wake cycle that reflect constitutional differences in the circadian clock period and phase, and the chronic circadian misalignment produced when social and occupational schedules force early waketimes that conflict with late chronotypes' biological sleep timing — represent a major and underrecognized public health problem in which the mismatch between biological clock timing and social clock demands produces chronic sleep deprivation, metabolic dysregulation, mood disorders, and cognitive impairment in the estimated 30 percent of the population with late chronotypes. Morning-evening chronotype genetics — the substantial heritability of chronotype estimated at 50 percent from twin studies, with genome-wide association studies identifying over 350 genetic loci associated with chronotype including variants in core clock genes Per1, Per2, Per3, and RORA, and with the PER3 VNTR polymorphism producing a 4-repeat late chronotype and a 5-repeat early chronotype with different sleep architecture and cognitive performance characteristics. Dim-light melatonin onset DLMO as chronotype marker — the gold standard objective measure of circadian phase in which saliva or plasma melatonin is sampled hourly in dim light conditions to determine the time of melatonin onset, with DLMO occurring approximately 2 hours before habitual sleep onset and varying by up to 6 hours between extreme morning and evening chronotypes, providing the objective phase marker that quantifies the circadian misalignment of social jet lag. Social jet lag health consequences — Till Roenneberg's epidemiological research demonstrating that each hour of social jet lag is associated with a 33 percent increased risk of obesity, with additional associations with depression, cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, and cancer, producing an estimated population-level health burden comparable to moderate alcohol consumption. The constitutional temporal nature correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that individuals have constitutional differences in their Yin Yang balance and Five Element composition that influence their optimal daily rhythms — a clinical tradition of recognizing individual temporal constitution that corresponds to the modern genetics of chronotype.

## Chronotype Distribution

- Population: normal distribution from extreme morning to extreme evening types.
- Epidemiology: 25 percent morning types 25 percent evening types 50 percent intermediate.
- Age trajectory: children early peak adolescence extreme delay aging returns to earlier.
- Sex differences: men later than women in reproductive years — converge after menopause.

## DLMO Assessment

- Protocol: hourly saliva melatonin collection in dim light less than 10 lux for 6 hours.
- DLMO definition: first sample exceeding 3 pg/mL threshold — objective phase marker.
- Chronotype correlation: DLMO and habitual sleep midpoint strongly correlated.
- Clinical use: guides light therapy timing and melatonin administration for phase shifting.

## Social Jet Lag

- Definition: difference between social and biological sleep midpoint on work vs free days.
- Prevalence: average 1 hour in population — 2 plus hours in late chronotypes.
- Mechanisms: chronic partial sleep deprivation circadian misalignment disrupted feeding rhythm.
- Remedy: flexible work schedules chronotype-appropriate school start times light therapy.
