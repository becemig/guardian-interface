# Dynamical Systems Neuroscience

## Abstract

Dynamical systems neuroscience — the application of mathematical dynamical systems theory including attractor landscapes, phase plane analysis, bifurcation theory, and chaos theory to understanding how neural circuits generate their characteristic dynamics including oscillations, bistable switches, chaos, and the transitions between neural states that underlie cognitive operations including working memory, decision-making, and the sleep-wake cycle — provides a geometric and mathematical language for understanding neural computation that transcends the details of individual neuron properties to reveal the emergent computational properties of neural populations. Attractor dynamics — the concept that neural circuit activity tends toward stable states called attractors in the high-dimensional state space of neural activity, with fixed point attractors corresponding to persistent activity states such as the sustained firing maintaining working memory contents in prefrontal cortex, limit cycle attractors corresponding to oscillatory states such as the rhythmic activity of central pattern generators, and chaotic attractors corresponding to the complex irregular dynamics that may provide the variability necessary for flexible neural computation. Phase plane analysis — the geometric approach to understanding the dynamics of two-variable neural models by plotting the trajectory of the system in the phase plane of membrane voltage versus recovery variable, with the nullclines marking where each variable has zero rate of change intersecting at fixed points that determine the system's equilibria, and with the geometry of the phase plane revealing the qualitative dynamics including excitability, oscillation, and bistability without requiring numerical integration. Bifurcations and neural transitions — the qualitative changes in the dynamical behavior of neural systems that occur at critical parameter values called bifurcation points, with the saddle-node bifurcation underlying the abrupt threshold for action potential generation, the Hopf bifurcation underlying the transition from quiescence to oscillation in central pattern generators, and the cusp catastrophe underlying bistable neural switching in working memory and decision-making. The Taiji dynamic balance point correspondence in Daoist cosmology describes the Taiji as the dynamic balance point between Yin and Yang — the point of perfect dynamic equilibrium from which all motion and transformation arise — a correspondence with the bifurcation points and critical transitions of dynamical systems neuroscience where small perturbations can shift the system between qualitatively different dynamical regimes.

## Attractor Types

- Fixed point: stable equilibrium — persistent activity working memory.
- Limit cycle: stable oscillation — CPG rhythms neural oscillations.
- Chaotic attractor: deterministic chaos — complex irregular variability.
- Landscape: energy landscape metaphor — valleys as attractors hills as separatrices.

## Phase Plane

- Nullclines: V-nullcline and w-nullcline — zero rate of change curves.
- Fixed points: nullcline intersections — equilibria of the system.
- Stability: eigenvalues of Jacobian at fixed point — stable vs unstable.
- Trajectories: flow in phase plane reveals qualitative dynamics geometrically.

## Bifurcations

- Saddle-node: action potential threshold — abrupt all-or-none transition.
- Hopf: quiescence to oscillation — CPG and thalamic oscillation onset.
- Cusp catastrophe: bistable switch — hysteresis in working memory and decisions.
- Critical slowing: near bifurcation system slows — early warning of transition.

## TCM Correspondence

- Taiji dynamic balance point corresponds to the bifurcation point between qualitatively different dynamical neural regimes.
- Yin Yang dynamic equilibrium corresponds to the balanced excitation-inhibition maintaining neural systems at optimal operating point.
- Pathological excess of Yin or Yang corresponds to pathological attractors in psychiatric and neurological disorders.
- Cultivation moving system toward healthy attractor corresponds to therapeutic interventions shifting neural dynamics toward healthy regimes.
