# Tai Chi Neijin Internal Power

## Abstract

Tai Chi Neijin internal power — the cultivation of integrated whole-body force transmission through relaxed structural alignment, fascial tensegrity, and the development of the four primary energies of Peng ward-off, Lu rollback, Ji press, and An push that together constitute the fundamental vocabulary of Tai Chi push hands and the expression of internal martial arts power — represents the highest technical achievement of Tai Chi practice, distinguishing internal martial arts from external styles through the emphasis on yielding, redirection, and the use of ground reaction force transmitted through a relaxed connected structure rather than through muscular tension and brute force. Peng Lu Ji An four primary energies — the four cardinal energies of Tai Chi corresponding to the four cardinal directions and the four primary strategic responses in push hands combat, with Peng ward-off energy providing the structural integrity that maintains spherical expansion against incoming force, Lu rollback energy receiving and redirecting incoming force through yielding rotation, Ji press energy projecting force forward in a compressed unified emission, and An push energy pressing downward and forward using the opponent's own structure against them. Song relaxation science — the foundational principle of Tai Chi practice requiring the progressive release of habitual muscular tension while maintaining structural integrity through fascial and skeletal alignment, with Song representing not limpness but a quality of active release in which unnecessary tension is dissolved while the deep postural musculature maintains form, enabling sensitivity to incoming force, efficient force transmission, and the heightened proprioceptive awareness that underlies Ting Jin listening force skill. Chan Si Jin silk reeling force — the characteristic spiraling force transmission pattern of Chen style Tai Chi in which force travels through the body in helical paths corresponding to the myofascial spiral lines of anatomy trains, with the silk reeling metaphor capturing the quality of continuous smooth connected force transmission without breaks or collapses in the kinetic chain. The yielding overcomes hardness biomechanical correspondence in Daoist philosophy describes the Dao De Jing teaching that water overcomes rock, soft overcomes hard, and yielding overcomes force — a philosophical principle given precise biomechanical expression in Tai Chi's use of yielding and redirection to neutralize and redirect incoming force rather than meeting it with opposing muscular tension.

## Four Primary Energies

- Peng: ward-off — spherical structural expansion maintaining integrity against compression.
- Lu: rollback — yielding rotation receiving and redirecting incoming force.
- Ji: press — unified forward force emission along connected kinetic chain.
- An: push — downward forward press using structural transmission not muscular push.

## Song Relaxation

- Progressive release: habitual tension dissolved while structural alignment maintained.
- Deep postural: intrinsic postural muscles active superficial muscles released.
- Sensitivity: Song enables Ting Jin proprioceptive sensitivity to incoming force.
- Efficiency: relaxed structure transmits ground force more efficiently than tense muscle.

## Silk Reeling Biomechanics

- Helical paths: force travels in spirals through body — myofascial spiral lines.
- Continuous: no breaks in kinetic chain — force transmission uninterrupted.
- Dantian center: all silk reeling originates from and returns to lower Dantian.
- Whole body: every joint participates in silk reeling — no isolated segment.

## TCM Correspondence

- Yielding overcomes hardness corresponds to the Daoist principle underlying Tai Chi strategic biomechanics.
- Kidney governing root and lower body foundation corresponds to the rooting and ground reaction force of Tai Chi internal power.
- Liver governing smooth free flow corresponds to the Song relaxation enabling smooth connected force transmission.
- Du Mai Governing Vessel as central axis corresponds to the spinal integrity central to all Tai Chi internal power expression.
