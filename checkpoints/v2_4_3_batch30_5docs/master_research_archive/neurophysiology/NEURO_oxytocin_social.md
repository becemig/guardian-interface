# Oxytocin and Social Bonding

## Abstract

Oxytocin is a nine amino acid neuropeptide synthesized in the paraventricular nucleus PVN and supraoptic nucleus SON of the hypothalamus that functions both as a peripheral hormone governing uterine contraction and milk ejection and as a central neuromodulator governing social bonding, trust, maternal behavior, and affiliation. Thomas Insel and Larry Young's research on prairie voles demonstrated that pair bonding is mediated by oxytocin and vasopressin acting on species-specific receptor distributions in the reward circuitry. Michael Kosfeld's intranasal oxytocin study demonstrated that oxytocin increases economic trust in humans — one of the most cited social neuroscience findings. Oxytocin reduces amygdala reactivity to threatening social stimuli enabling approach rather than avoidance in social contexts. Oxytocin release is triggered by social touch, eye contact, synchronous movement, and positive social interaction — creating a positive feedback loop between social engagement and oxytocin release. Deficient oxytocin signaling is implicated in autism spectrum disorder through reduced social salience and reward. Heart-Pericardium connection in TCM — the Heart governing love and relationship while the Pericardium protects appropriate emotional boundaries — corresponds to the oxytocin system governing the neurobiological substrate of social love and trust.

## Social Bonding

- Prairie vole pair bonding requires oxytocin receptor activation in the nucleus accumbens during mating.
- Oxytocin-dopamine interaction in the reward system creates the motivational salience of social bonding.
- Human pair bond strength correlates with peripheral oxytocin levels and receptor gene polymorphisms.
- Synchronous movement, music, and ritual produce oxytocin release supporting group cohesion.

## Trust and Prosociality

- Intranasal oxytocin increases trust, generosity, and cooperation in economic games.
- Oxytocin reduces social threat perception enabling approach to unfamiliar social situations.
- The pro-social effects of oxytocin are context-dependent — oxytocin increases in-group favoritism and can increase out-group hostility.
- Therapeutic touch, massage, and safe physical contact produce oxytocin release supporting therapeutic alliance.

## Maternal Behavior

- Parturition triggers massive oxytocin release establishing maternal bonding and caregiving behavior.
- Oxytocin from infant suckling during nursing reinforces maternal attachment through reward circuit activation.
- Maternal oxytocin levels predict the quality and sensitivity of maternal caregiving behavior.
- Cross-species, oxytocin is the conserved neurochemical substrate of mammalian caregiving and attachment.
