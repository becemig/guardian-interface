# Circadian Sleep Architecture

## Abstract

The circadian regulation of sleep — governed by the interaction of the suprachiasmatic nucleus SCN master circadian clock and the homeostatic sleep pressure system — determines when we sleep, how long we sleep, and the quality and architecture of sleep across the night, with disruption of either system producing the characteristic insomnia, daytime fatigue, and cognitive impairment of circadian sleep disorders. Alexander Borbely's two-process model of sleep regulation — proposed in 1982 and remaining the dominant framework for understanding sleep-wake regulation — identifies two interacting processes: Process C the circadian process driven by the SCN that produces a daily rhythm of sleep propensity regardless of prior sleep history, and Process S the homeostatic sleep pressure process driven by adenosine accumulation during waking that produces increasing sleep drive proportional to time awake and dissipates during sleep. Adenosine — the metabolic byproduct of neural activity that accumulates in the extracellular space of the brain during waking — is the primary molecular substrate of homeostatic sleep pressure: adenosine activates inhibitory A1 and A2A receptors on wake-promoting neurons in the basal forebrain and brainstem, progressively suppressing wakefulness as adenosine levels rise, and this adenosine accumulation is precisely what caffeine blocks through competitive antagonism of adenosine receptors. Melatonin — the pineal hormone produced exclusively during darkness and suppressed by light through the retinohypothalamic tract to the SCN — provides the circadian darkness signal that initiates the biological night, with dim light melatonin onset DLMO occurring approximately 2 hours before habitual sleep onset and serving as the most reliable biomarker of circadian phase. Chronotypes — the biologically determined individual variation in circadian phase preference from extreme morning types larks to extreme evening types owls — are governed by genetic polymorphisms in core clock genes including PER3 CLOCK and CRY and shift systematically across the lifespan with childhood early chronotype shifting to adolescent delayed chronotype and returning to earlier phase in older adulthood. The Wei Qi circadian sleep-wake cycle in TCM describes the classical understanding that Wei Qi circulates through the Yang surface of the body during waking and retreats into the Yin interior organs during sleep, with the 50 circulations of Wei Qi through the body each day governing the sleep-wake cycle.

## Two-Process Model

- Process C circadian: SCN-driven daily rhythm of sleep propensity — independent of prior sleep.
- Process S homeostatic: adenosine accumulation during waking — proportional to time awake.
- Sleep occurs when Process S pressure exceeds the circadian wake-promoting signal.
- Interaction of C and S produces the characteristic timing and architecture of normal sleep.

## Adenosine Sleep Pressure

- Neural activity produces adenosine as metabolic byproduct — accumulates during waking.
- Adenosine activates A1 and A2A receptors suppressing wake-promoting neurons.
- Caffeine competitively blocks adenosine receptors — delays but does not eliminate sleep pressure.
- Slow wave sleep produces the most efficient adenosine clearance — resetting sleep pressure.

## Melatonin Circadian Signal

- Produced exclusively during darkness by pineal gland.
- Light suppresses melatonin through retinohypothalamic tract to SCN to pineal.
- DLMO approximately 2 hours before habitual sleep onset — most reliable circadian biomarker.
- Blue light 460-480 nm most potent melatonin suppressor — evening screen use delays circadian phase.
