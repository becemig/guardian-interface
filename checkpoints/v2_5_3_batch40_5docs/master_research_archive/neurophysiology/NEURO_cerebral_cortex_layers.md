# Cerebral Cortex Layers

## Abstract

The neocortex is organized into six horizontal layers with distinct cellular compositions, connectivity patterns, and functional roles — a laminar architecture conserved across all mammalian species that implements the fundamental computations of cortical processing. Vernon Mountcastle's discovery of the cortical column demonstrated that the cortex is organized vertically as well as horizontally — columns of approximately 100 neurons extending through all six layers constitute the basic functional unit of cortical processing, with all neurons in a column sharing similar response properties. Layer I the molecular layer contains mostly dendrites and axons with few cell bodies — it receives modulatory input from higher areas. Layer II-III contain pyramidal neurons that project to other cortical areas enabling intracortical communication. Layer IV the granular layer is the primary recipient of thalamic sensory input — it is thick in primary sensory cortices and absent in primary motor cortex. Layer V contains large pyramidal neurons including the Betz cells of motor cortex — the primary output layer projecting to subcortical targets including the spinal cord. Layer VI contains pyramidal neurons that provide the primary corticothalamic feedback. Daniel Felleman and David Van Essen described the hierarchical organization of visual cortical areas — feedforward connections from lower to higher areas terminate in layer IV while feedback connections from higher to lower areas terminate in layers I and VI. Shen stratification in TCM — the multi-level organization of the Heart-Mind from surface Shen expression to deep constitutional Jing-Shen — corresponds to the laminar hierarchy of cortical processing from primary sensory to highest association areas.

## Six-Layer Architecture

- Layer I molecular layer contains dendrites, axons, and Cajal-Retzius cells receiving modulatory top-down input.
- Layers II-III external pyramidal layers contain pyramidal neurons projecting to other cortical areas horizontally.
- Layer IV internal granular layer receives primary thalamic sensory input and is absent in motor cortex.
- Layer V internal pyramidal layer contains the largest pyramidal neurons projecting to subcortical motor targets.
- Layer VI multiform layer provides corticothalamic feedback completing the thalamocortical loop.

## Columnar Organization

- Cortical columns of approximately 100 neurons extending through all six layers constitute the processing unit.
- Neurons within a column share similar tuning properties — the same orientation preference in visual cortex.
- Ocular dominance columns alternate left and right eye dominance across the visual cortex surface.
- The cortical column is the basic unit that is replicated across the cortical surface in modular fashion.

## Cortical Hierarchy

- Feedforward connections from lower to higher areas terminate in layer IV carrying specific sensory information.
- Feedback connections from higher to lower areas terminate in layers I and VI carrying predictive signals.
- The cortical hierarchy processes information from concrete sensory features at lower levels to abstract concepts at higher levels.
- Prefrontal cortex at the top of the hierarchy integrates information across all modalities and time scales.
