# Multi-Omics Integration

## Abstract

Multi-omics — the integrated analysis of multiple layers of molecular information including genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, and metabolomics — represents the systems biology approach to understanding health and disease at the molecular level, providing the most comprehensive view of the molecular basis of constitutional variation and the molecular mechanisms by which lifestyle, environment, and therapeutic interventions produce health changes. Genomics — the complete sequencing and analysis of an individual's DNA — reveals the constitutional blueprint: the inherited variants in gene sequence that predispose to specific disease risks, drug responses, and constitutional characteristics. Genome-wide association studies GWAS have identified thousands of common genetic variants associated with disease risk, though each individual variant typically explains only a small fraction of the total heritable risk. Transcriptomics — the analysis of the complete set of RNA transcripts expressed by the genome at a given moment — reveals which genes are actually active in which tissues under which conditions, providing a dynamic picture of gene expression that changes with health state, treatment, and lifestyle. RNA sequencing RNA-seq is the primary transcriptomics technology, capable of quantifying the expression level of every gene simultaneously with single-base resolution. Proteomics — the large-scale study of the complete set of proteins expressed by a cell, tissue, or organism — reveals the functional molecules that actually carry out cellular processes, providing a closer view of biological function than genomics or transcriptomics alone. Mass spectrometry is the primary proteomics technology, capable of identifying and quantifying thousands of proteins simultaneously. Metabolomics — the comprehensive analysis of small molecule metabolites in a biological sample — provides the most functionally proximate molecular picture of health state, reflecting the integrated output of all genomic, transcriptomic, and proteomic activity as well as the direct influence of diet, microbiome, and environment. The integration of all four omics layers through systems biology computational methods produces a molecular portrait of the individual that corresponds to the TCM constitutional assessment — revealing the unique molecular signature of each person's constitutional nature.
