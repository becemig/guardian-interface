# Evolutionary Mismatch Theory

## Abstract

Evolutionary mismatch theory — the framework proposing that the chronic diseases of modern civilization arise primarily from the mismatch between the environments and lifestyles for which human biology was shaped by evolution and the novel environments and lifestyles of modern industrial civilization — is the foundational organizing principle of evolutionary medicine and provides the most coherent explanatory framework for understanding the epidemic of chronic non-communicable disease in contemporary populations. The Paleolithic baseline — the environmental conditions, diet, activity patterns, sleep patterns, social structures, and stress exposures under which Homo sapiens evolved over the past 300,000 years — establishes the reference standard against which modern environments can be assessed for mismatch: humans evolved as hunter-gatherers moving 5-15 km per day, sleeping in alignment with the solar cycle, eating a diverse whole-food diet with high fiber and low glycemic load, experiencing acute rather than chronic stress, living in small cohesive social groups, and spending the majority of waking hours outdoors in natural environments. Boyd Eaton and Melvin Konner's landmark 1985 New England Journal of Medicine paper establishing the discordance hypothesis — that the human genome is adapted to Paleolithic conditions and that modern diets and lifestyles produce pathology through discordance between genetic expectations and environmental reality — launched the field of evolutionary medicine and established the intellectual framework for understanding obesity, type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, depression, anxiety, autoimmune disease, and numerous other modern epidemics as diseases of mismatch. Chronic disease as mismatch describes the specific mechanisms by which Paleolithic adaptations produce pathology in modern environments: the fat-storage thrifty genotype that enabled survival through food scarcity produces obesity and metabolic syndrome in the food-abundant environment; the acute stress response that enabled survival of predator encounters produces cardiovascular disease and immune dysregulation when chronically activated by psychological stressors; the inflammatory immune response that protected against parasites and infections produces autoimmune disease and chronic inflammation in the hygienic modern environment. The ancestral Qi cultivation correspondence describes the TCM alignment with evolutionary medicine: the TCM cultivation traditions of appropriate movement, seasonal diet, natural sleep rhythms, social connection, and nature exposure correspond to the evolutionary medicine prescription for reducing mismatch and restoring alignment with the Paleolithic baseline.

## Paleolithic Baseline

- Movement: 5-15 km per day walking plus varied physical activity — no sedentary baseline.
- Diet: diverse whole foods, high fiber 100g per day, low glycemic, high omega-3 to omega-6 ratio.
- Sleep: aligned with solar cycle, 7-9 hours, no artificial light disruption.
- Stress: acute predator and resource stressors — not chronic psychological stressors.
- Social: small cohesive bands of 20-150, dense social connection, no social isolation.

## Discordance Mechanisms

- Thrifty genotype: fat storage adaptation produces obesity in food-abundant environment.
- Acute stress response: fight-or-flight produces cardiovascular disease when chronically activated.
- Inflammatory immunity: parasite defense produces autoimmune disease in hygienic environment.
- Sweet-fat seeking: caloric density preference produces metabolic disease with processed food.

## Modern Mismatch Diseases

- Metabolic: obesity, type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome — dietary and activity mismatch.
- Cardiovascular: atherosclerosis, hypertension — stress, diet, and activity mismatch.
- Immune: autoimmune disease, allergy, asthma — hygiene hypothesis and inflammatory mismatch.
- Mental: depression, anxiety, loneliness — social, sleep, and nature exposure mismatch.
