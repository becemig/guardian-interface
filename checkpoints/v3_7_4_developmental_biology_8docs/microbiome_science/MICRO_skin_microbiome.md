# Skin Microbiome and Dermatological Health

## Abstract

Skin microbiome and dermatological health — the diverse community of bacteria, fungi, viruses, and mites inhabiting the 1.8 square meters of human skin surface in distinct ecological niches defined by moisture, sebum production, and temperature, with the dominant genera being Staphylococcus and Cutibacterium in sebaceous areas, Corynebacterium and Staphylococcus in moist areas, and beta-Proteobacteria and Flavobacteriales in dry areas, with the skin microbiome serving as a critical component of the skin barrier defense through competitive exclusion of pathogens, production of antimicrobial peptides, and calibration of skin immune responses. Skin dysbiosis and atopic dermatitis — the well-characterized expansion of Staphylococcus aureus in the lesional and non-lesional skin of atopic dermatitis patients, with S. aureus producing virulence factors including staphylococcal enterotoxins that act as superantigens activating T cells non-specifically, V8 protease degrading the skin barrier protein filaggrin, and delta-toxin promoting mast cell degranulation, with the reduction of healthy commensal diversity allowing S. aureus overgrowth, and with topical probiotic application of Roseomonas mucosa demonstrating improvement in atopic dermatitis severity in early clinical trials. Cutibacterium acnes and acne — the gram-positive anaerobe formerly known as Propionibacterium acnes that colonizes sebaceous follicles and produces lipases cleaving sebum triglycerides to release free fatty acids that disrupt the follicular microenvironment and activate toll-like receptor 2 on keratinocytes and monocytes to trigger the innate immune response underlying acne inflammation, with acne associated not with overall C. acnes burden but with specific C. acnes ribotypes and with reduced skin microbiome diversity enabling pathogenic ribotype dominance. Skin-gut microbiome axis — the bidirectional relationship between gut and skin microbiomes with gut dysbiosis associated with skin conditions including psoriasis, rosacea, and atopic dermatitis through the leaky gut mechanism of increased intestinal permeability allowing bacterial products to reach the skin through the circulation, and with probiotic treatment improving both gut and skin microbiome composition in inflammatory skin conditions. The Lung Skin Wei Qi barrier correspondence in TCM describes the classical relationship between the Lung organ system governing both respiration and the skin surface, with the Lung distributing Wei Qi to the skin to maintain the defensive barrier against external pathogenic factors — a classical framework corresponding to the skin microbiome as a living component of the skin barrier defense system calibrated by immune signals from the Lung-associated mucosal immune system.

## Site-Specific Composition

- Sebaceous areas: face scalp chest — Cutibacterium Staphylococcus dominant.
- Moist areas: axilla groin toe webs — Corynebacterium Staphylococcus.
- Dry areas: forearm palm — diverse beta-Proteobacteria Flavobacteriales.
- Stable: skin microbiome more stable over time than gut microbiome.

## Atopic Dermatitis

- S. aureus expansion: lesional and non-lesional AD skin.
- Filaggrin degradation: V8 protease disrupts skin barrier — increased permeability.
- Superantigens: staphylococcal enterotoxins — non-specific T cell activation.
- Roseomonas mucosa: commensal — topical application improves AD severity.

## Skin-Gut Axis

- Gut dysbiosis: associated with psoriasis rosacea atopic dermatitis.
- Leaky gut: LPS and bacterial products reach skin via circulation.
- Probiotics: oral probiotic treatment improves skin inflammatory conditions.
- Bidirectional: skin microbiome alterations also influence gut composition.
