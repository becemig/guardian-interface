# Rhythm Entrainment and Neural Synchrony

## Abstract

Rhythm entrainment — the spontaneous synchronization of biological oscillators to external rhythmic stimuli — is one of the most fundamental and pervasive phenomena in biology and neuroscience, governing the synchronization of neural oscillations to auditory rhythms, the interpersonal synchronization of movement, physiology, and social behavior between individuals, and the therapeutic applications of rhythmic stimulation in neurological rehabilitation, pain management, and social bonding. Beat induction — the automatic neural process by which the auditory system extracts a regular pulse from complex rhythmic patterns — activates the basal ganglia and supplementary motor area even in the absence of movement, demonstrating the hardwired coupling between auditory rhythm processing and the motor system that underlies the irresistible urge to move to music. Meter perception — the hierarchical organization of beats into measures and phrases — involves a network of neural oscillators that synchronize to the metric hierarchy of the musical rhythm, with EEG delta oscillations tracking the measure level, theta oscillations tracking the beat level, and gamma oscillations tracking the subdivision level in a nested oscillatory architecture that mirrors the hierarchical structure of musical meter. Groove — the quality of rhythmic music that most strongly induces the desire to move — is produced by specific rhythmic features including moderate rhythmic complexity, syncopation that creates a tension between the implied beat and the surface rhythm, and the presence of a strong bass frequency pulse that engages the subcortical auditory-motor circuits most directly. Interpersonal entrainment — the synchronization of movement, physiology, and neural activity between interacting individuals — produces measurable increases in prosocial behavior, cooperation, and social bonding: synchronized movement produces increases in pain threshold, charitable giving, and feelings of social connection through the mechanisms of shared motor representation and oxytocin release. The pulse-Qi rhythmic correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding of pulse diagnosis as the reading of the rhythmic Qi flow pattern — the fundamental rhythmic vitality of the organism expressed through the arterial pulse — and the therapeutic use of rhythmic percussion, drumming, and movement to regulate the constitutional Qi rhythm.
