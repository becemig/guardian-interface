# I Ching Divination as Somatic Practice

## Abstract

I Ching divination as somatic practice reclaims the consultation process as a full-body meditative engagement rather than a mechanical fortune-telling procedure — the quality of presence, intention, and somatic awareness brought to the consultation process determines the depth of the response received from this ancient mirror of change. The yarrow stalk method is the traditional and most meditative consultation method — using fifty yarrow stalks in a ritual counting process that takes fifteen to twenty minutes, creating sustained meditative focus and allowing time for the question to settle fully into the body before the hexagram emerges. Carl Jung's concept of synchronicity — the acausal connecting principle that links meaningful coincidences — provides the Western psychological framework for understanding how a randomly generated hexagram can speak meaningfully to a specific life situation: the hexagram does not cause the situation but reflects it, arising from the same moment as the question through the acausal connecting principle of meaningful coincidence. Felt sense consultation uses Eugene Gendlin's felt sense methodology to prepare the question — allowing the full bodily knowing of the situation to form before casting, and receiving the hexagram response through the felt sense rather than through purely intellectual interpretation. Yi as mirror — the I Ching as a mirror of the present moment — reframes consultation from prediction to reflection: the hexagram reflects the pattern of the moment, helping the consultant see their situation with new clarity rather than telling them what will happen. The ritual dimension of I Ching consultation — the preparation of the space, the settling of the mind, the careful handling of the stalks or coins — is itself a somatic practice cultivating the quality of presence from which meaningful consultation emerges.

## Yarrow Stalk Method

- Fifty yarrow stalks are used — one set aside, forty-nine divided and counted in a ritual process.
- The counting process takes fifteen to twenty minutes creating sustained meditative focus.
- Each line of the hexagram requires three separate counting operations producing old or young Yin or Yang.
- The slow ritual pace allows the question to settle fully into the body before the hexagram is complete.

## Synchronicity

- Jung proposed synchronicity as the acausal connecting principle linking meaningful coincidences.
- The randomly generated hexagram reflects the pattern of the moment through synchronicity.
- The I Ching does not predict the future but mirrors the present — the pattern of what is happening now.
- Synchronicity requires the abandonment of linear causality — the willingness to find meaning in apparent chance.

## Felt Sense Consultation

- Before casting, allow the full bodily felt sense of the situation to form — not just the intellectual question.
- The question emerges from the body's knowing of the situation — more complete than the mind's version.
- Receive the hexagram response through the felt sense — noticing what resonates in the body as true.
- The bodily resonance of specific lines and images is the guidance — not the intellectual interpretation alone.
