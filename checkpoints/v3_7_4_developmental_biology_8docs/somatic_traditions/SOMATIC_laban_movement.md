# Laban Movement Analysis

## Abstract

Laban Movement Analysis LMA is a comprehensive framework for describing, interpreting, and documenting human movement developed by Rudolf Laban and elaborated by Irmgard Bartenieff. LMA organizes movement observation into four major categories: Body describing what parts of the body are moving and how they are organized, Effort describing the qualitative dynamics of movement, Shape describing how the body changes form in space, and Space describing the spatial pathways and orientations of movement. The Effort category describes four motion factors — Weight (strong/light), Space (direct/indirect), Time (sudden/sustained), and Flow (bound/free) — as continua between two polarities. These effort qualities correspond directly to the qualities of Qi movement described in TCM and Qigong — strong weight effort corresponds to heavy sinking Qi while light weight effort corresponds to rising Yang Qi.

## Effort Qualities

- Weight effort ranges from strong (firm, powerful, heavy) to light (delicate, fine-touch, weightless).
- Space effort ranges from direct (single-focused, linear) to indirect (multi-focused, flexible, meandering).
- Time effort ranges from sudden (urgent, quick, accent) to sustained (leisurely, lingering, ongoing).
- Flow effort ranges from bound (controlled, restrained, careful) to free (abandoned, fluent, released).

## Shape

- Shape Flow describes the growing and shrinking of the body in relation to itself — breathing in and out.
- Directional Shape describes the spoke-like or arc-like movements reaching toward destinations in space.
- Shaping describes the three-dimensional molding of the body that adapts to and creates the environment.
- Shape qualities correspond to the expanding and condensing dynamics of Qi in different practice states.

## Kinesphere

- The kinesphere is the personal space surrounding the body reachable without locomotion.
- Movement can be near reach, mid reach, or far reach within the kinesphere.
- Dimensional, planar, and diagonal spatial orientations correspond to different qualitative movement experiences.
- Kinesphere awareness in Tai Chi corresponds to Laban's spatial organization of movement.

## TCM Correspondence

- Strong weight effort corresponds to heavy sinking Earth and Water Qi qualities.
- Light weight effort corresponds to rising Yang Fire and Wood Qi qualities.
- Sustained time effort corresponds to the Yin, receptive, Water quality of deep practice.
- Sudden time effort corresponds to the Yang, assertive, Fire quality of explosive martial application.
