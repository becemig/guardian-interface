# Myofascial Release

## Abstract

Myofascial Release MFR as developed by John Barnes is a manual therapy approach addressing the fascial system — the continuous three-dimensional web of collagen and elastic fibers embedded in ground substance that permeates and connects every structure in the body. Barnes proposes that fascial restrictions arising from trauma, inflammation, and poor posture create tensile forces throughout the fascial web that can produce pain and dysfunction far from the restriction site through the biotensegrity architecture of the fascial system. MFR uses sustained low-load pressure held for 90-120 seconds or longer — far longer than most manual therapy techniques — to access the viscoelastic and piezoelectric properties of fascia enabling tissue elongation and reorganization. Robert Schleip's research on fascial plasticity documented that fascia contains smooth muscle-like contractile cells myofibroblasts that actively regulate tissue tension and respond to manual therapy through mechanoreceptor pathways. The thixotropic property of fascial ground substance — becoming more fluid with sustained mechanical input — provides the physical basis for MFR tissue change. Fascial unwinding is the spontaneous three-dimensional movement of a body region following the natural unwinding pattern of fascial restriction — the tissue leads the movement rather than the practitioner directing it. Sinew channel release in TCM — the release of accumulated tension in the myofascial pathways of the twelve sinew channels — corresponds to MFR as its Western anatomical equivalent.

## Sustained Pressure

- MFR uses sustained low-load pressure maintained for 90-120 seconds minimum to access fascial change.
- This duration exceeds the elastic response of fascia reaching the viscoelastic and piezoelectric response ranges.
- The piezoelectric effect in collagen fibers converts mechanical pressure into electrical signals altering ground substance viscosity.
- Shorter duration techniques only address the elastic recoil of fascia without reaching the plastic change range.

## Thixotropy and Ground Substance

- Ground substance is the gel-like matrix surrounding collagen fibers that regulates tissue viscosity.
- Thixotropy is the property of becoming more fluid with sustained mechanical input — like stirring honey.
- Sustained MFR pressure increases ground substance fluidity enabling collagen fiber reorganization.
- Dehydrated or fibrotic ground substance increases tissue stiffness and reduces fascial mobility.

## Fascial Unwinding

- Fascial unwinding involves following the tissue's spontaneous movement rather than directing it.
- The practitioner provides a light floating support as the tissue follows its natural unwinding pattern.
- Unwinding sequences may retrace previous positions held during injury retracing tissue memory.
- Emotional release often accompanies fascial unwinding as stored somatic content surfaces during processing.
