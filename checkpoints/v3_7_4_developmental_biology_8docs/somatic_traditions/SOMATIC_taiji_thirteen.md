# Taijichuan Thirteen Postures

## Abstract

The thirteen postures Shi San Shi are the complete movement vocabulary of Taijichuan — the eight jing energies and five stepping directions from which all Taijichuan forms and techniques are composed. Zhang Sanfeng's classical treatise on Taijichuan states that the thirteen postures arise from Wuji — the primordial undifferentiated state — and return to Wuji through practice, making the thirteen postures not merely a technical catalog but a cosmological framework for understanding movement as the expression of Taoist principles. The eight energies Bajin correspond to the eight trigrams of the I Ching: Peng ward-off corresponds to Qian heaven, Lu roll-back corresponds to Kun earth, Ji press corresponds to Kan water, An push corresponds to Li fire, Cai pluck corresponds to Sun wind, Lie split corresponds to Zhen thunder, Zhou elbow corresponds to Dui lake, Kao shoulder corresponds to Gen mountain. The five steppings correspond to the five elements and five directions: advance Jin corresponds to Metal west, retreat Tui corresponds to Wood east, look left Gu Zuo corresponds to Water north, gaze right Pan You corresponds to Fire south, central equilibrium Zhong Ding corresponds to Earth center. The correspondence of the eight energies to the eight trigrams and the five steppings to the five elements places Taijichuan within the same cosmological framework as the I Ching and TCM five element theory — making it a somatic expression of these same organizing principles. Wuji practice is the foundation posture — standing in apparent stillness that contains the seeds of all movement — from which all thirteen postures arise and to which they return.

## Eight Energies

- Peng ward-off: expanding outward spherical energy corresponding to Qian heaven and Lung Wei Qi.
- Lu roll-back: yielding redirecting energy corresponding to Kun earth and Spleen receiving.
- Ji press: concentrated forward penetrating energy corresponding to Kan water and Kidney depth.
- An push: downward settling energy corresponding to Li fire and Heart clarifying.
- Cai pluck: sudden downward uprooting energy corresponding to Sun wind and Liver Hun precision.
- Lie split: spiraling separating energy corresponding to Zhen thunder and Gallbladder decisive action.
- Zhou elbow: close-range striking energy corresponding to Dui lake and joy in directness.
- Kao shoulder: whole-body contact energy corresponding to Gen mountain and groundedness.

## Five Steppings

- Advance Jin: Metal quality forward movement — decisive direct engagement.
- Retreat Tui: Wood quality backward yielding — drawing the opponent into emptiness.
- Look left Gu Zuo: Water quality leftward shifting — following the natural flow.
- Gaze right Pan You: Fire quality rightward turning — pivoting to redirect.
- Central equilibrium Zhong Ding: Earth quality central stillness — the root of all movement.

## TCM Correspondence

- Eight energies corresponding to eight trigrams connects Taijichuan to the same cosmological framework as TCM eight principle diagnosis.
- Five steppings corresponding to five elements connects movement directions to organ network qualities.
- Wuji as the origin and return of all movement corresponds to the TCM concept of Yuan Qi as the undifferentiated source.
- The thirteen postures as a complete cosmological movement vocabulary corresponds to TCM theory as a complete mapping of all possible Qi states.
