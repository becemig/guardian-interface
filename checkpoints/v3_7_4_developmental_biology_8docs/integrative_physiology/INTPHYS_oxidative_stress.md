# Systemic Oxidative Stress and Redox Signaling

## Abstract

Reactive oxygen species and redox signaling — the field that has evolved from understanding ROS as purely damaging oxidants to recognizing them as essential second messengers governing cellular signaling, adaptation, and survival — has transformed the understanding of oxidative stress from a simple damage model to a nuanced redox biology in which the same molecules that cause pathology at high concentrations govern essential physiological processes at physiological concentrations, corresponding to the TCM understanding of Fire as essential Yang warmth at physiological levels and pathological Yin-deficiency Fire blazing at excess levels. ROS as signaling molecules — hydrogen peroxide H2O2 acting as a second messenger through the reversible oxidation of cysteine residues in regulatory proteins — govern growth factor receptor signaling, immune activation, hypoxic response, and metabolic adaptation through redox-sensitive protein switches that are activated by localized H2O2 production from NADPH oxidases and are reversed by glutathione and thioredoxin systems, demonstrating that ROS are not simply damaging byproducts but essential components of cellular information processing. Nrf2 antioxidant response — the master transcription factor that senses cellular oxidative stress through its redox-sensitive regulator Keap1 and activates the coordinated transcription of hundreds of antioxidant, detoxification, and cytoprotective genes — is the primary cellular defense against oxidative damage and the molecular target of hormetic interventions including exercise, caloric restriction, phytochemicals, and heat shock that activate Nrf2 at sub-toxic intensities to produce adaptive upregulation of antioxidant capacity. Mitochondrial ROS — produced primarily by Complex I and Complex III of the electron transport chain as a byproduct of oxidative phosphorylation — serve as signals of mitochondrial metabolic state, activating retrograde signaling from mitochondria to the nucleus governing mitochondrial biogenesis, autophagy, and the mitochondrial unfolded protein response, with excessive mitochondrial ROS production in obesity, aging, and metabolic disease driving the oxidative damage underlying metabolic dysfunction. Hormesis — the biological phenomenon in which low doses of stressors including ROS, heat, exercise, and phytochemical toxins produce adaptive beneficial responses through Nrf2 and other stress response pathways while high doses produce damage — provides the biological framework for understanding why exercise, fasting, cold exposure, and phytomedicinal plants produce health benefits through the activation of cellular stress response pathways. The Yin deficiency Fire blazing correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding of the pathological state in which the cooling moistening Yin substances are depleted, allowing the Yang Fire to blaze unchecked — a pattern that corresponds to the oxidative stress state in which antioxidant capacity is insufficient to buffer excess ROS production.

## ROS as Second Messengers

- H2O2: stable enough to diffuse and oxidize distant cysteine targets — primary redox signal.
- NADPH oxidases: regulated ROS production for signaling purposes in immune and vascular cells.
- Protein tyrosine phosphatases: inhibited by H2O2 — amplifies growth factor receptor signaling.
- Redox-sensitive cysteine switches: rapid reversible oxidation governs protein activity.

## Nrf2 Antioxidant Response

- Keap1: cytoplasmic Nrf2 inhibitor that senses oxidative stress through reactive cysteines.
- Nrf2 activation: Keap1 oxidation releases Nrf2 for nuclear translocation and ARE activation.
- ARE target genes: SOD CAT GPx HO-1 NQO1 glutathione synthesis thioredoxin.
- Hormetic activators: sulforaphane curcumin resveratrol exercise heat cold all activate Nrf2.

## Mitochondrial ROS

- Complex I and III: primary sites of electron leak producing superoxide.
- Superoxide dismutase: converts superoxide to H2O2 for signaling or detoxification.
- Mitohormesis: low mitochondrial ROS activates adaptive responses extending healthspan.
- Mitochondrial dysfunction: excess ROS production in obesity and aging driving metabolic disease.
