# Default Mode Network

## Abstract

The default mode network DMN is a set of brain regions including the medial prefrontal cortex, posterior cingulate cortex, angular gyrus, and hippocampal formation that show increased activity during rest and decreased activity during externally focused tasks. Marcus Raichle identified the DMN as a default mode of brain function active during undirected thought, self-referential processing, and mind-wandering. The DMN supports narrative self-construction, mental time travel — imagining past and future — social cognition and theory of mind, and creative incubation. Excessive DMN activity is associated with depression, anxiety, and rumination while meditative states modulate DMN activity toward non-self-referential open awareness. Shen rumination — the Heart-Mind caught in repetitive self-referential thought loops — corresponds to maladaptive DMN hyperactivity.

## DMN Regions

- Medial prefrontal cortex governs self-referential processing and evaluation of social relevance.
- Posterior cingulate cortex integrates self-referential information and autobiographical memory retrieval.
- Angular gyrus integrates multimodal information contributing to narrative and semantic self-representation.
- Hippocampal formation contributes autobiographical memory and future simulation to DMN processing.

## Functions of the DMN

- Self-referential processing — evaluating information relative to oneself and constructing the narrative self.
- Mental time travel — simulating past experiences and imagining future scenarios for planning and rehearsal.
- Social cognition and theory of mind — inferring others' mental states through perspective-taking.
- Creative incubation — the unconscious associative processing during mind-wandering that produces insight.

## Meditation and DMN

- Focused attention meditation reduces DMN activity through sustained external focus suppressing mind-wandering.
- Open monitoring meditation modulates DMN toward non-self-referential open awareness without suppression.
- Long-term meditators show reduced posterior cingulate activity and altered DMN functional connectivity.
- Non-dual awareness meditation produces a distinctive DMN state where self-referential processing is present but not grasped.
