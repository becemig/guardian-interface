# Vestibular System

## Abstract

The vestibular system is the sensory system governing balance, spatial orientation, and the coordination of head and eye movements. The peripheral vestibular apparatus in the inner ear contains the otolith organs — utricle and saccule — detecting linear acceleration and gravity, and the three semicircular canals detecting rotational acceleration in three planes. The vestibulo-ocular reflex VOR stabilizes gaze during head movement by producing equal and opposite eye movements. Central vestibular processing in the vestibular nuclei integrates vestibular, visual, and proprioceptive input to construct a coherent sense of spatial orientation and body position. Vestibular dysfunction produces vertigo, dizziness, oscillopsia, and postural instability. Tai Chi and balance training improve vestibular function and reduce fall risk in older adults. Governing Vessel governing the central axis of the body and Kidney governing the brain through marrow correspond to the vestibular system's central role in maintaining the body's relationship to gravity and space.

## Otolith Organs

- The utricle detects horizontal linear acceleration and head tilt relative to gravity.
- The saccule detects vertical linear acceleration and is sensitive to vibration.
- Hair cells in the otolithic membrane transduce mechanical displacement into neural signals.
- Otolith signals contribute to postural reflexes, spatial orientation, and the subjective vertical.

## Semicircular Canals

- Three semicircular canals arranged in mutually perpendicular planes detect rotational acceleration.
- Endolymph inertia during head rotation deflects the cupula activating hair cells in the ampulla.
- The horizontal canal pair detects yaw while anterior and posterior canal pairs detect pitch and roll.
- Canal signals drive the VOR producing compensatory eye movements that stabilize gaze during head movement.

## Vestibulo-Ocular Reflex

- The VOR produces eye movements equal and opposite to head movements stabilizing gaze on a fixed target.
- VOR gain of 1.0 produces perfect gaze stabilization — any deviation produces oscillopsia or blurred vision.
- VOR adaptation to new optical demands demonstrates vestibular neuroplasticity in the cerebellum.
- Gaze stabilization exercises used in vestibular rehabilitation exploit VOR adaptation mechanisms.
