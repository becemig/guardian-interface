# Postural Stability and Balance

## Abstract

Postural stability is the capacity to maintain the body center of mass within the base of support under static and dynamic conditions. The nervous system integrates vestibular, proprioceptive, and visual signals to generate anticipatory and reactive postural adjustments that prevent falls. Anticipatory postural adjustments are feedforward corrections that precede voluntary movement while reactive adjustments are feedback corrections following perturbation. The Kidney root in TCM governs the foundational stability of the body corresponding to postural stability as a constitutional function. Tai Chi produces the strongest evidence of any exercise modality for fall prevention through its specific training of postural stability mechanisms.

## Center of Mass and Base of Support

- The center of mass is the point where the body mass is equally distributed in all directions.
- The base of support is the area enclosed by the contact points between body and ground.
- Postural stability requires maintaining center of mass projection within the base of support.
- Expanding the base of support through wide stance or assistive devices increases stability margin.

## Anticipatory Postural Adjustments

- Anticipatory postural adjustments APAs are feedforward muscle activations preceding voluntary limb movement.
- APAs counteract the predictable destabilizing effects of limb movement before they occur.
- The supplementary motor area and cerebellum generate APAs based on internal forward models.
- Tai Chi single-leg stance training refines APA timing and magnitude for stepping and weight transfer.

## Reactive Balance

- Reactive balance involves rapid corrective responses to unexpected perturbations.
- The long-loop transcortical response at 120 to 180ms supplements the short-loop spinal reflex at 40 to 80ms.
- Tai Chi improves reactive balance response amplitude and latency in older adults.
- Hip strategy and step strategy are the primary reactive balance recovery mechanisms.
