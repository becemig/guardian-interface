# Neuroinflammation Advanced

## Abstract

Neuroinflammation — inflammation within the central nervous system involving microglial activation, cytokine signaling, and disruption of the blood-brain barrier — has emerged as a major pathophysiological mechanism underlying depression, neurodegenerative disease, and cognitive decline. Robert Dantzer's sickness behavior research established that peripheral immune activation through cytokines IL-1beta, IL-6, and TNF-alpha produces a coordinated behavioral response — reduced activity, social withdrawal, increased sleep, reduced appetite, and low mood — that is adaptive during acute infection but pathological when chronically activated. The IDO kynurenine pathway is a key mechanism linking inflammation to depression — inflammatory cytokines activate indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase IDO diverting tryptophan from serotonin synthesis toward the kynurenine pathway, reducing serotonin availability while producing neuroactive kynurenine metabolites including quinolinic acid NMDA agonist and neurotoxin. Andrew Miller and Charles Raison proposed that treatment-resistant depression is substantially driven by chronic low-grade neuroinflammation — CRP and IL-6 elevations predict antidepressant non-response and anti-inflammatory treatments produce antidepressant effects. Microglial activation states range from homeostatic surveillance through inflammatory M1-like activation producing cytokines and reactive oxygen species to alternative M2-like activation supporting repair and neuroprotection. Chronic stress, obesity, gut dysbiosis, sleep deprivation, and social isolation all promote microglial M1 activation and neuroinflammation. Damp-Heat in the brain in TCM — Dampness obstructing clear Yang ascent to the brain combined with Heat producing inflammatory disturbance of Shen — corresponds to neuroinflammation as its pathophysiological equivalent.

## Sickness Behavior

- Peripheral cytokines IL-1beta, IL-6, and TNF-alpha signal the brain via vagal afferents and circumventricular organs.
- This produces coordinated sickness behavior — reduced activity, withdrawal, increased sleep, reduced appetite.
- Sickness behavior is adaptive during acute infection conserving energy for immune response.
- Chronic cytokine elevation from non-infectious sources produces pathological depression-like sickness behavior.

## IDO-Kynurenine Pathway

- Inflammatory cytokines activate IDO diverting tryptophan from serotonin synthesis to the kynurenine pathway.
- Kynurenine metabolites include kynurenic acid neuroprotective and quinolinic acid NMDA agonist neurotoxic.
- Elevated quinolinic acid produces glutamate excitotoxicity in the hippocampus corresponding to depression-related volume loss.
- IDO inhibition and anti-inflammatory treatments reduce kynurenine pathway activation and improve depression.

## Microglial Activation

- Homeostatic microglia survey the brain continuously for damage signals and pathogens.
- M1-like activated microglia produce pro-inflammatory cytokines, ROS, and neurotoxic factors.
- M2-like activated microglia produce anti-inflammatory cytokines and support synaptic pruning and repair.
- Chronic stress shifts microglia toward M1 activation through glucocorticoid and ATP-P2X7 signaling.
