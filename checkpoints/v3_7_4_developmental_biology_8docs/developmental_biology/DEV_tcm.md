# TCM Developmental Theory

## Abstract

Traditional Chinese Medicine articulates a comprehensive theory of human development, aging, and longevity organized around Kidney Jing as the constitutional substrate of all growth, maturation, and decline. The Huang Di Nei Jing Su Wen Chapter 1 presents the foundational model of human development through seven-year cycles in women and eight-year cycles in men, tracing the waxing and waning of Kidney Qi and Tian Gui (heavenly water — the hormonal and reproductive substrate) from childhood through old age. Prenatal Jing — inherited from both parents and shaped by the quality of their Jing and the conditions of conception and gestation — establishes the constitutional foundation that cannot be replenished but can be conserved or squandered. Postnatal Jing — refined from food, air, and rest — supplements prenatal Jing and provides the ongoing resource for daily vitality. Yang Sheng (nourishing life) is the comprehensive Daoist-medical tradition of longevity cultivation that aims to conserve Jing, circulate Qi harmoniously, clarify Shen, and align with seasonal and cosmological rhythms to maximize both healthspan and lifespan. Modern geroscience validates this framework through epigenetic clocks, telomere biology, and longevity pathway research.

## Prenatal and Postnatal Jing

- Prenatal Jing: inherited from parents - fixed at conception - cannot be replenished
- Quality determined by: ancestral health, parental Jing quality, conditions of conception
- Maternal influence: mother's health during gestation shapes prenatal Jing quality
- Storage: stored in Kidneys - the constitutional reserve
- Postnatal Jing: refined from food by Spleen-Stomach - supplements prenatal
- Daily renewal: good diet and lifestyle continuously refine postnatal Jing
- Conservation: primary longevity strategy - spend postnatal before prenatal
- Depletion: excessive work, sex, fear, stimulants all consume prenatal Jing prematurely

## Seven and Eight Year Cycles

- Female cycle 1 (7): Kidney Qi flourishes - hair grows long and strong - teeth change
- Female cycle 2 (14): Tian Gui arrives - Ren and Chong Mai flourish - menarche
- Female cycle 3 (21): Kidney Qi balanced - wisdom teeth emerge - full growth
- Female cycle 4 (28): sinews and bones strongest - hair and body at peak
- Female cycle 5 (35): Yangming declines - face begins to wither - hair begins to fall
- Female cycle 6 (42): three yang decline - face withers - hair turns white
- Female cycle 7 (49): Ren Mai empty - Chong Mai depleted - Tian Gui exhausted - menopause
- Male cycles: same pattern on eight-year intervals - peak at 32, decline from 40, Jing sparse at 64

## Tian Gui

- Definition: heavenly water - the substance enabling reproduction and development
- Arrival: at puberty - Kidney Qi sufficient to overflow into reproductive capacity
- Departure: at menopause and andropause - Kidney Jing insufficient to maintain Tian Gui
- Modern parallel: sex hormones - estrogen, progesterone, testosterone, FSH, LH
- Not identical: Tian Gui encompasses more than hormones - reproductive vitality broadly
- GH parallel: growth hormone and IGF-1 trajectory mirrors Tian Gui waxing and waning
- Cultivation: Nei Dan practices aim to conserve and recycle Tian Gui

## Kidney as Root of Development

- Kidney governs growth: Kidney Jing drives all developmental milestones
- Kidney governs bone-marrow-brain: constitutional substrate for skeletal and neural development
- Kidney governs reproduction: Tian Gui and gonads under Kidney governance
- Kidney governs hearing: ear is opening of Kidney - hearing decline maps to Kidney decline
- Kidney governs hair: head hair reflects Kidney Jing status - graying and loss signal depletion
- Kidney governs fear: excessive fear depletes Kidney Qi - chronic fear accelerates aging
- Root of yin and yang: Kidney Yin and Yang are the foundation of all organ yin and yang

## Yang Sheng - Nourishing Life

- Definition: comprehensive Daoist-medical tradition of longevity cultivation
- Seasonal alignment: adapt diet, sleep, activity, and emotion to seasonal Qi
- Spring: expand, rise early, express, eat lightly - nourish Liver
- Summer: full activity, joy, connection, cool foods - nourish Heart
- Autumn: contract, let go, harvest, warm foods - nourish Lung
- Winter: conserve, sleep more, quiet cultivation, warming foods - nourish Kidney
- Daily practices: Qigong, Tai Chi, meditation, dietary discipline, adequate sleep
- Modern validation: Blue Zones lifestyle mirrors Yang Sheng principles
