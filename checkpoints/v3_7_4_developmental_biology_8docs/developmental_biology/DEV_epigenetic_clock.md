# Epigenetic Aging Clocks

## Abstract

Epigenetic aging clocks are algorithmic tools that predict biological age from patterns of DNA methylation at specific CpG sites across the genome. Steve Horvath's 2013 pan-tissue clock demonstrated that methylation at 353 CpG sites could predict chronological age with remarkable precision across all tissues and cell types — a finding that fundamentally transformed aging research. Biological age as measured by epigenetic clocks is meaningfully different from chronological age and predicts mortality, disease risk, and functional capacity independently of calendar age. Subsequent clocks — PhenoAge, GrimAge, and DunedinPACE — improved predictive power by incorporating clinical biomarkers and measuring the pace of aging rather than just a point estimate. Critically, epigenetic age can be accelerated by chronic stress, smoking, obesity, and social isolation — and decelerated or reversed by exercise, dietary restriction, meditation, and partial cellular reprogramming with Yamanaka factors. The TRIIM trial (Fahy 2019) provided the first human evidence of epigenetic age reversal — 2.5 years of reversal using a combination of GH, DHEA, and metformin. In TCM, epigenetic age maps directly to the rate of Kidney Jing depletion.

## Horvath Clock

- Published: 2013 - analyzed 8000 samples across 51 tissue types
- Sites: 353 CpG sites - some gain methylation with age - some lose
- Precision: median absolute deviation of 3.6 years from chronological age
- Pan-tissue: works across blood, brain, liver, kidney, breast - same clock
- Intrinsic clock: reflects cell-intrinsic aging process - not just accumulated cell turnover
- Reprogramming: Yamanaka factor induction resets Horvath clock to embryonic levels
- Paradox: cancer tissue is epigenetically old yet proliferates - dissociation of clocks

## Second Generation Clocks

- PhenoAge: trained on clinical biomarkers of biological age - better mortality predictor
- GrimAge: trained on smoking pack-years and mortality - strongest predictor of lifespan
- GrimAge components: DNAm proxies for plasminogen activator inhibitor-1, GDF-15, leptin
- DunedinPACE: measures pace of aging - how fast the clock is ticking right now
- DunedinPACE validation: predicts cognitive and physical decline in 45-year-olds
- Blood-based: most clocks use blood DNA - accessible for clinical use
- Commercial testing: biological age tests available - Elysium Health, TruDiagnostic

## Clock Acceleration Factors

- Smoking: accelerates epigenetic age 1-2 years per decade of smoking
- Obesity: BMI strongly associated with accelerated GrimAge
- Chronic stress: psychological distress accelerates DunedinPACE
- Childhood adversity: ACEs predict accelerated epigenetic age in adults
- Social isolation: loneliness accelerates epigenetic aging
- Alcohol: heavy alcohol use accelerates clock - light use neutral or slightly protective
- Shift work: disrupted circadian rhythms accelerate epigenetic aging

## Clock Deceleration Factors

- Exercise: regular aerobic exercise decelerates DunedinPACE and PhenoAge
- Mediterranean diet: adherence predicts younger epigenetic age
- Caloric restriction: CALERIE trial - 2 years of 12 percent CR slows DunedinPACE
- Sleep: adequate sleep quality predicts younger epigenetic age
- Meditation: intensive retreat reduces GrimAge acceleration
- Social connection: strong social ties predict younger biological age
- Omega-3: higher omega-3 index predicts younger PhenoAge

## Age Reversal Evidence

- TRIIM trial: GH plus DHEA plus metformin - 2.5 years of Horvath clock reversal
- Partial reprogramming: OSK factors (Oct4, Sox2, Klf4) reverse epigenetic age in mice
- Vision restoration: OSK gene therapy restores vision in aged mice via clock reversal
- Plasma dilution: replacing old mouse plasma with saline reverses epigenetic aging
- Fasting: prolonged fasting followed by refeeding may produce transient clock reversal
- Significance: clock reversibility demonstrates aging is not one-way - has become modifiable
