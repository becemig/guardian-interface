# Neurodevelopment

## Abstract

The development of the human nervous system is the most elaborate biological construction process known, spanning from neural tube formation in the third week of gestation through prefrontal cortex maturation in the mid-twenties. The process proceeds through sequential stages — neurulation, neurogenesis, migration, differentiation, synaptogenesis, myelination, and activity-dependent pruning — each with characteristic timing, molecular mechanisms, and vulnerability windows. The brain produces a massive excess of neurons and synapses early in development, then sculpts the final architecture through experience-dependent pruning guided by activity and BDNF signaling. Critical periods are windows of heightened plasticity when specific experiences are necessary for normal circuit development — the visual critical period, language acquisition window, and attachment period are the most studied. Adolescence brings a second major reorganization — pruning of prefrontal connections, myelination of association cortex, and remodeling of reward and social circuits — extending vulnerability into the mid-twenties. Disruptions at any stage underlie neurodevelopmental conditions including ASD, ADHD, and schizophrenia.

## Neural Tube Formation

- Week 3: neural plate forms from ectoderm - induced by notochord
- Neurulation: neural plate folds and fuses to form neural tube
- Neural tube defects: failure of fusion - spina bifida, anencephaly - prevented by folate
- Patterning: Shh from notochord patterns ventral tube - BMP patterns dorsal
- Neural crest: cells emigrate from neural tube - peripheral nervous system, melanocytes, craniofacial
- Vesicles: three primary brain vesicles form - prosencephalon, mesencephalon, rhombencephalon

## Neurogenesis and Migration

- Peak neurogenesis: weeks 10-20 of gestation - 250,000 neurons per minute
- Ventricular zone: proliferative zone lining ventricles - progenitor cells
- Inside-out: cortical layers form inside-out - deep layers first, superficial last
- Radial glia: scaffolding for neuronal migration - neurons climb radial glia to final position
- Tangential migration: interneurons migrate tangentially from ganglionic eminence
- Reelin: signaling protein guiding migration - mutations cause lissencephaly
- Adult neurogenesis: hippocampal dentate gyrus and olfactory bulb - exercise and BDNF stimulate

## Synaptogenesis

- Timing: begins prenatally - peaks in first two years - continues through adolescence
- Overproduction: synaptic density peaks at 150 percent of adult level in visual cortex
- Use it or lose it: active synapses strengthened - inactive synapses pruned
- BDNF: brain-derived neurotrophic factor - promotes synapse formation and survival
- Hebbian: neurons that fire together wire together - activity drives circuit consolidation
- Complement: C1q and C3 tag weak synapses for microglial elimination
- ASD: excess synaptic density in some ASD subtypes - insufficient pruning

## Critical Periods

- Definition: windows of heightened plasticity when specific experience is necessary
- Visual: ocular dominance columns - monocular deprivation during critical period causes permanent loss
- Language: phonemic discrimination - L1 critical period closes around age 7
- Attachment: social bonding circuits - early caregiver relationship programs stress reactivity
- Opening: critical periods opened by increases in BDNF and reduction of inhibitory brake
- Closing: PV interneuron maturation and perineuronal net formation close critical periods
- Reopening: psychedelics, ketamine, fluoxetine can reopen critical period plasticity in adults

## Myelination

- Myelin: oligodendrocyte-produced lipid sheath - increases conduction velocity 100-fold
- Timing: proceeds from posterior to anterior - sensory before motor before prefrontal
- Prefrontal: PFC myelination continues into mid-twenties - last region to mature
- White matter: myelinated axon bundles - DTI measures white matter integrity
- Experience: social and cognitive experience drives myelination of relevant circuits
- Schizophrenia: white matter abnormalities - disrupted myelination of prefrontal circuits

## Adolescent Brain Reorganization

- Second wave: major pruning and myelination of prefrontal and association cortex
- Reward: dopamine system peaks in adolescence - heightened reward sensitivity and risk-taking
- Social: intense sensitivity to peer evaluation - social pain as potent as physical pain
- PFC lag: prefrontal control lags behind subcortical drive - imbalance drives adolescent behavior
- Sleep: circadian phase delay in adolescence - biologically driven later sleep onset
- Vulnerability: adolescence is peak onset period for schizophrenia, bipolar, eating disorders
- TCM: Wood phase - Liver Qi surging upward - Yang rising without full Kidney root
