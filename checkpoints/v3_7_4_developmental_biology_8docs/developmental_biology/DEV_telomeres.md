# Telomeres and Cellular Aging

## Abstract

Telomeres are repetitive DNA sequences (TTAGGG in humans) capping chromosome ends that protect genomic integrity and serve as a molecular clock counting cell divisions. Each replication shortens telomeres by 50-200 base pairs due to the end-replication problem — the inability of DNA polymerase to fully replicate linear chromosome ends. When telomeres reach a critical minimum length, cells enter permanent senescence or apoptosis — the Hayflick limit. Telomerase, an RNA-dependent DNA polymerase discovered by Blackburn, Greider, and Szostak (Nobel Prize 2009), can extend telomeres and is expressed in germ cells, stem cells, and most cancers. Leukocyte telomere length (LTL) has emerged as a biomarker of biological aging and cumulative life stress. Elissa Epel's landmark 2004 study demonstrated that chronic psychological stress — particularly caregiver stress — was associated with shorter telomeres and lower telomerase activity equivalent to 9-17 years of additional aging. Lifestyle interventions including exercise, meditation, stress reduction, and omega-3 supplementation protect and can increase telomere length. TCM maps telomere biology to Kidney Jing conservation — the constitutional reserve that determines lifespan.

## Telomere Structure

- Sequence: TTAGGG repeated 1000-2000 times - 10-15 kb total in young humans
- G-overhang: single-stranded 3 prime overhang - threads back into duplex forming t-loop
- T-loop: protective structure - prevents telomere being recognized as double-strand break
- Shelterin: six-protein complex (TRF1, TRF2, POT1, TIN2, TPP1, RAP1) - protects telomere
- TRF2: prevents ATM kinase activation - keeps telomere from triggering DNA damage response
- Uncapping: critically short telomeres lose shelterin protection - DNA damage response activated

## Hayflick Limit and Replicative Senescence

- Hayflick 1961: human fibroblasts divide approximately 50 times then permanently arrest
- Mechanism: telomere shortening is the molecular basis of Hayflick limit
- Senescence trigger: critically short telomere activates p53 and p21 - G1 arrest
- Irreversible: senescent cells do not re-enter cell cycle even with growth factors
- In vivo: tissues with high turnover (gut, blood) most affected by replicative senescence
- Cancer bypass: most cancers reactivate telomerase or use ALT to bypass senescence

## Telomerase

- Components: TERT (catalytic reverse transcriptase) and TERC (RNA template)
- Mechanism: TERC provides TTAGGG template - TERT extends 3 prime overhang
- Expression: high in germ cells, stem cells, activated lymphocytes - low in somatic cells
- Cancer: 85-90 percent of cancers reactivate telomerase - enables immortal proliferation
- Regulation: hTERT promoter mutations most common non-coding mutations in cancer
- Therapeutic: telomerase activators (TA-65) under investigation for anti-aging applications

## Psychological Stress and Telomere Attrition

- Epel 2004: mothers caring for chronically ill children - shorter telomeres than controls
- Magnitude: highest stress group showed equivalent of 9-17 years additional aging
- Mechanism: cortisol reduces telomerase activity - oxidative stress damages telomere directly
- ACEs: childhood adversity predicts shorter adult telomere length
- PTSD: PTSD associated with accelerated telomere shortening vs trauma-exposed controls
- Depression: MDD associated with shorter telomeres - antidepressant treatment partially protective

## Lifestyle Factors

- Exercise: aerobic exercise increases telomerase activity in leukocytes - 3-4 sessions per week
- Meditation: intensive meditation retreat increases telomerase activity 30 percent
- Omega-3: higher omega-3 index predicts slower telomere attrition over 5 years
- Sleep: short sleep duration associated with shorter telomeres
- Obesity: visceral adiposity associated with shorter telomeres and lower telomerase
- Smoking: smoking accelerates telomere shortening by 4-5 base pairs per year
- TCM: Jing conservation practices - adequate sleep, sexual moderation, stress reduction
