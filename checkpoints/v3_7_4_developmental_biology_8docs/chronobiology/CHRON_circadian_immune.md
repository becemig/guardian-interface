# Circadian Immune Regulation

## Abstract

Circadian immune regulation — the profound temporal organization of virtually all aspects of innate and adaptive immune function by the circadian clock, including the rhythmic trafficking of immune cells between blood, lymphoid tissues, and peripheral tissues, the circadian peaks and troughs of pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory cytokine production, the clock gene regulation of macrophage and dendritic cell activation thresholds, and the circadian gating of vaccine responses and infectious susceptibility — establishes the immune system as one of the most thoroughly circadian-regulated physiological systems, with clinical implications for optimal vaccination timing, the morning peaks of autoimmune and cardiovascular symptoms, and the immune consequences of circadian disruption in shift workers and jet-lagged travelers. Immune cell trafficking rhythms — the CXCL12-CXCR4 and CXCL5-mediated circadian rhythmicity of neutrophil, monocyte, NK cell, and T cell egress from bone marrow and lymphoid organs into blood during the active phase and tissue infiltration during the rest phase, governed by adrenergic signaling from SCN-controlled sympathetic output that drives the rhythmic expression of adhesion molecules and chemokine receptors on immune cells and endothelial cells. Cytokine circadian peaks — the early morning peaks of pro-inflammatory cytokines including TNF-alpha, IL-6, IL-1beta, and IL-12 driven by the circadian rhythmicity of NF-kB activation in macrophages and monocytes, producing the morning symptom peaks of rheumatoid arthritis, asthma, and cardiovascular events that are the clinical manifestation of circadian immune regulation, and the anti-inflammatory IL-10 peak in the afternoon providing the daily counter-regulatory phase. Clock genes in immune cells — the cell-autonomous circadian clocks in macrophages, dendritic cells, T cells, and B cells that govern their inflammatory thresholds, cytokine production capacity, and antigen presentation function through clock gene regulation of NF-kB, IRF3, and NLRP3 inflammasome activity, with BMAL1 deletion in macrophages producing constitutively elevated inflammation and sepsis susceptibility. The Wei Qi circadian defense cycling correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that Wei Qi defensive energy is most active at the body's surface during the day and retreats inward during sleep, cycling between exterior defense and interior nourishment — a functional description of the circadian immune cell trafficking between peripheral tissues during the active phase and lymphoid organ return during sleep.

## Immune Cell Trafficking

- Neutrophils: peak blood levels early morning — tissue infiltration during active phase.
- Monocytes: circadian egress from bone marrow — adrenergic CXCL12 regulation.
- NK cells: peak blood levels at night — tissue surveillance during sleep.
- T cells: homing to lymph nodes during rest phase — effector deployment during activity.

## Cytokine Peaks

- TNF-alpha: peaks 6-8 AM — drives morning RA joint stiffness and swelling.
- IL-6: peaks early morning — morning CRP elevation and acute phase response.
- IL-1beta: early morning peak — cardiovascular event morning clustering.
- IL-10: afternoon peak — anti-inflammatory counter-regulation.

## Clock Genes in Immunity

- BMAL1 macrophage: governs NF-kB and NLRP3 activation threshold.
- BMAL1 knockout: constitutive inflammation — elevated TNF IL-6 at all times.
- REV-ERB alpha: suppresses NLRP3 inflammasome — circadian anti-inflammatory.
- Cry1 Cry2: suppress NF-kB — loss produces hyperinflammatory macrophages.

- Lung governing Wei Qi surface defense corresponds to circadian regulation of mucosal and skin immune surveillance.
