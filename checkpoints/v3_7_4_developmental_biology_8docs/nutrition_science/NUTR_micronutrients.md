# Micronutrient Cofactors and Enzyme Systems

## Abstract

Micronutrient cofactors and enzyme systems — the essential roles of vitamins and minerals as cofactors and coenzymes in enzymatic reactions across virtually every metabolic pathway in the body, with deficiencies in even a single micronutrient capable of impairing dozens of dependent enzymatic reactions and producing widespread metabolic dysfunction — establish micronutrient sufficiency as a foundational requirement for optimal physiological function that is increasingly compromised by the nutrient depletion of modern food systems and the high micronutrient demands of contemporary stress, toxin exposure, and inflammation. Zinc cofactor functions — the structural and catalytic roles of zinc in over 300 enzyme systems including zinc metalloenzymes governing DNA synthesis, RNA transcription, protein metabolism, immune function, wound healing, and antioxidant defense through copper-zinc superoxide dismutase, with zinc finger protein domains providing the structural scaffold for hundreds of transcription factors and nuclear hormone receptors — establish zinc as one of the most functionally critical trace minerals with deficiency producing impaired immunity, impaired wound healing, reduced taste and smell, infertility, and cognitive dysfunction. Magnesium ATP dependency — the requirement for magnesium as a cofactor in all ATP-utilizing enzymatic reactions, since the actual biological substrate of ATP-dependent enzymes is the magnesium-ATP complex MgATP rather than free ATP, making magnesium essential for glycolysis, oxidative phosphorylation, DNA and RNA synthesis, protein synthesis, muscle contraction, and nerve conduction, with widespread magnesium deficiency in Western populations due to soil depletion and processing losses producing subclinical impairment of energy metabolism, cardiovascular function, and neurological function. Vitamin D genomic signaling — the nuclear hormone receptor function of the active vitamin D metabolite 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D3 calcitriol as a ligand for the vitamin D receptor VDR, a nuclear transcription factor that regulates the expression of over 1000 genes in virtually every tissue including immune regulation, calcium metabolism, cell proliferation, cardiovascular function, and neuroprotection, with widespread vitamin D deficiency in high-latitude populations driving epidemic rates of autoimmune disease, cardiovascular disease, and cancer. The mineral Jing treasure correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding of minerals and salts as concentrated Earth essence that nourishes the Kidney Jing and supports the most fundamental physiological functions — a correspondence with the modern understanding of essential minerals as irreplaceable cofactors for the enzymatic machinery of life.

## Zinc Functions

- Structural: zinc finger domains in 2500 transcription factors and nuclear receptors.
- Catalytic: metalloenzyme active site in carbonic anhydrase carboxypeptidase alcohol dehydrogenase.
- Antioxidant: copper-zinc SOD primary cytoplasmic antioxidant enzyme.
- Immune: thymulin zinc-dependent hormone governing T-cell maturation.

## Magnesium MgATP

- All kinases: require MgATP as substrate — phosphorylation reactions.
- Glycolysis: 7 of 10 glycolytic enzymes require magnesium.
- ATP synthase: magnesium required for F1-ATPase rotary catalysis.
- Deficiency prevalence: estimated 50-80 percent of Western populations suboptimal.

## Vitamin D VDR Signaling

- VDR: nuclear receptor — binds 1,25-OH2-D3 to regulate gene transcription.
- 1000 plus genes: regulated by vitamin D across virtually all tissues.
- Immune regulation: innate and adaptive immune system modulation.
- Optimal level: 40-60 ng/mL 25-OH-D3 for genomic signaling optimization.
