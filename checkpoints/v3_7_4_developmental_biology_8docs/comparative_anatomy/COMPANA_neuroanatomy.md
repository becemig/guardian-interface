# Comparative Neuroanatomy

## Abstract

Comparative neuroanatomy — the study of nervous system evolution across vertebrate and invertebrate lineages to understand the origins, homologies, and adaptive significance of neural structures, with the human brain revealing through comparative analysis its deep evolutionary heritage in the brainstem and limbic structures shared with all vertebrates, the cerebellar expansion shared with active-locomoting vertebrates, and the neocortical expansion uniquely elaborated in mammals and reaching its extreme in humans and cetaceans — provides the evolutionary context for understanding why the human brain has the structure it does and why understanding its phylogenetic history illuminates its functional organization. Triune brain model and critique — Paul MacLean's influential but now critiqued framework proposing that the human brain contains three evolutionary layers including the reptilian complex of basal ganglia governing instinctual behaviors, the paleomammalian limbic system governing emotion, and the neocortex governing rational cognition, with the critique by Lisa Feldman Barrett and others demonstrating that the triune model oversimplifies the true evolutionary history, that reptiles have homologs of all mammalian brain regions, that the limbic system is not a coherent anatomical or functional unit, and that cognition and emotion are not localized to separate brain regions but arise from the same distributed cortical networks. Pallial evolution — the evolutionary history of the pallium, the dorsal region of the telencephalon that gives rise to the cerebral cortex in vertebrates, with the three-layered paleocortex of olfactory processing and the three-layered archicortex of hippocampal formation shared across vertebrates, the six-layered neocortex appearing in mammals and reaching its greatest elaboration in primates and cetaceans, with gyrification of the neocortex enabling greater surface area within a constrained skull volume. Polyvagal phylogeny — Stephen Porges' proposal that the autonomic nervous system evolved in three phylogenetic stages, with the most ancient dorsal vagal parasympathetic system governing metabolic conservation and freeze responses shared with all vertebrates, the sympathetic system governing active mobilization appearing in bony fish, and the most evolutionarily recent ventral vagal system governing social engagement behaviors appearing in mammals with myelinated fibers enabling rapid communication. The Three Treasures hierarchical brain correspondence in TCM describes the classical framework of Jing Qi Shen as three hierarchical levels of vitality, with Jing as the constitutional material foundation, Qi as the dynamic functional energy, and Shen as the luminous awareness — corresponding to the phylogenetically oldest brainstem governing vital functions, the intermediate limbic system governing drives and emotions, and the most recently evolved prefrontal cortex governing conscious awareness and higher-order cognition.

## Triune Brain Critique

- MacLean model: reptilian complex limbic system neocortex as three layers.
- Critique: reptiles have dorsal cortex homologs of mammalian neocortex.
- Limbic system: not coherent anatomical or functional unit — outdated concept.
- Reality: distributed networks integrating old and new brain regions for all functions.

## Pallial Evolution

- Paleocortex: three-layer olfactory cortex — all vertebrates.
- Archicortex: three-layer hippocampal formation — all vertebrates.
- Neocortex: six-layer isocortex — mammals only — sensory motor association areas.
- Gyrification: folding increases surface area — humans have most complex gyrification.

## Polyvagal Phylogeny

- Dorsal vagal: ancient unmyelinated — metabolic conservation freeze immobilization.
- Sympathetic: bony fish — active mobilization fight flight.
- Ventral vagal: mammals — myelinated social engagement facial vocal cardiac.
- Hierarchy: ventral vagal first sympathetic second dorsal vagal last resort.
