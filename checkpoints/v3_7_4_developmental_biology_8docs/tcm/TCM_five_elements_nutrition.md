# TCM Five Elements Nutrition

## Abstract

TCM nutritional theory applies the same five element and eight pattern diagnostic framework to food that it applies to herbs — every food has a nature hot warm neutral cool cold, a flavor, and a channel affinity that determines its therapeutic effects on specific organ networks. This constitutes a comprehensive dietary medicine system that addresses not just macronutrient composition but the energetic and informational quality of food in relation to the individual's constitutional pattern. The five flavors of food correspond to the five organ networks: sour foods nourish and stimulate the Liver, bitter foods stimulate the Heart and clear heat, sweet foods tonify the Spleen, pungent foods activate the Lung and promote Qi circulation, and salty foods soften hardness and tonify the Kidney. The five colors similarly correspond: green foods nourish the Liver, red foods the Heart, yellow-orange foods the Spleen, white foods the Lung, and black-dark foods the Kidney. Seasonal eating aligns food choices with the organ network and elemental quality of each season — spring is Liver-Wood requiring sour and upward-moving foods, summer is Heart-Fire requiring bitter cooling foods, late summer is Spleen-Earth requiring sweet warming foods, autumn is Lung-Metal requiring pungent consolidating foods, and winter is Kidney-Water requiring salty warming foods. Acid-alkaline balance in TCM corresponds approximately to the damp-heat versus cold-damp continuum — excess acid-forming foods meat, dairy, refined sugar, alcohol create damp-heat conditions while alkaline-forming foods vegetables, most fruits, miso, green tea support Spleen-Stomach harmony. Constitutional eating tailors food choices to the individual's dominant pattern deficiency or excess, hot or cold, Yin or Yang — the same food is therapeutic for one constitutional pattern and harmful for another.

## Five Flavors and Organ Feeding

- Sour foods astringent organic acids: lemons, vinegar, fermented foods — nourish Liver, astringe Qi and fluids.
- Bitter foods alkaloids and polyphenols: dark greens, coffee, dark chocolate — stimulate Heart, clear heat, dry damp.
- Sweet foods polysaccharides and sugars: root vegetables, grains, legumes — tonify Spleen, build Qi and Blood.
- Pungent foods volatile oils and sulfur compounds: onions, ginger, garlic — activate Lung, move Qi, disperse cold.
- Salty foods minerals and sea vegetables: seaweed, miso, sea salt — tonify Kidney, soften hardness, promote fluid metabolism.

## Seasonal Eating

- Spring Liver-Wood: emphasize sour, light, upward-moving foods — sprouts, leafy greens, young vegetables.
- Summer Heart-Fire: emphasize bitter cooling foods — bitter melon, dandelion greens, cucumber, mint.
- Late summer Spleen-Earth: emphasize sweet warming foods — squash, sweet potato, millet, rice, well-cooked vegetables.
- Autumn Lung-Metal: emphasize pungent white foods — pears, daikon, white mushrooms, ginger.
- Winter Kidney-Water: emphasize salty warming foods — black beans, walnuts, bone broth, dark sea vegetables.

## Acid-Alkaline and Pattern Diagnosis

- Damp-heat pattern corresponds to excess acid-forming diet: meat, dairy, refined carbohydrates, alcohol, coffee.
- Spleen Qi deficiency corresponds to irregular eating, cold raw foods, and excess sweet foods impairing digestive fire.
- Kidney Yang deficiency corresponds to excess cold foods and fluids depleting the constitutional metabolic fire.
- Liver Qi stagnation corresponds to excess pungent stimulants and alcohol creating heat in the Liver channel.

## TCM Correspondence

- Five element nutrition directly applies TCM diagnostic categories to dietary therapy.
- Constitutional eating as precision nutrition corresponds to pharmacogenomics tailoring intervention to the individual pattern.
- Seasonal eating corresponds to the TCM principle of aligning lifestyle with the Qi quality of each season.
- Acid-alkaline balance corresponds to the TCM Spleen-Stomach harmony as the foundation of digestive health.
