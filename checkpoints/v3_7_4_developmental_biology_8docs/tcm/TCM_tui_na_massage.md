# Tui Na

## Abstract

Tui Na push-grasp is the system of Chinese medical manual therapy that applies systematic hand techniques to acupuncture points, meridians, and body regions to regulate Qi and Blood, release sinew channel tension, and treat musculoskeletal and internal disorders. As one of the five branches of TCM alongside acupuncture, herbal medicine, Qigong, and dietary therapy, Tui Na has a continuous documented history spanning over two thousand years. The eight fundamental Tui Na techniques are: Gun Fa rolling, Yi Zhi Chan Tui Fa one-finger pushing, An Fa pressing, Mo Fa circular rubbing, Rou Fa kneading, Ca Fa scrubbing, Dou Fa shaking, and Na Fa grasping. Each technique has distinct mechanical effects on tissue — Gun Fa produces deep fascial mobilization through rolling pressure, Yi Zhi Chan Tui Fa stimulates acupoints through focused oscillating pressure, and Ca Fa produces friction heat that warms and activates local circulation. Sinew channel treatment applies Tui Na specifically to the myofascial pathways of the twelve sinew channels releasing tension patterns that restrict meridian Qi flow. Paediatric Tui Na is a specialized system with unique point locations on the hand and forearm used to treat children without needles. Modern research documents Tui Na effects on muscle tension, HRV, inflammatory markers, and joint mobility.

## Eight Fundamental Techniques

- Gun Fa rolling applies deep rolling pressure through the dorsum and ulnar border of the hand mobilizing deep fascia.
- Yi Zhi Chan Tui Fa applies focused oscillating pressure through the thumb tip stimulating acupoints.
- An Fa applies sustained vertical pressure to points and regions producing Qi arrival sensation.
- Mo Fa applies light circular rubbing to the surface producing superficial warming and calming effects.

## Sinew Channel Treatment

- Tui Na follows the twelve sinew channel pathways releasing myofascial tension in meridian territory.
- Gun Fa along the Bladder sinew channel releases paraspinal tension from occiput to sacrum.
- Na Fa grasping releases the Gallbladder sinew channel tension along the lateral neck and shoulder.
- Ca Fa scrubbing along the Governing Vessel warms the spinal Yang and releases superficial Wei Qi stagnation.

## Paediatric Tui Na

- Paediatric Tui Na uses unique point locations on the child's hand and forearm not used in adult treatment.
- The Spleen point on the thumb tip is the primary digestive tonification point in paediatric practice.
- Treatment is gentler and faster than adult Tui Na using thousands of repetitions of light rapid strokes.
- Paediatric Tui Na treats fever, diarrhea, constipation, cough, and poor appetite without needles.

## TCM Correspondence

- Tui Na treating sinew channels directly addresses the myofascial substrate of meridian Qi flow.
- Bladder meridian Tui Na along the spine stimulates the Back Shu points through manual pressure.
- The Tui Na principle of moving stagnant Qi and Blood corresponds to the mechanical mobilization of fascial tissue.
- Gun Fa deep rolling corresponding to fascial manipulation corresponds to modern myofascial release techniques.
