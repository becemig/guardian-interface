# Stress, Cortisol, and HPA Axis Regulation in Somatic Health

## Abstract

The hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis is the primary neuroendocrine system governing the physiological stress response. Cortisol is the primary glucocorticoid released by the adrenal cortex in response to HPA axis activation. Chronic HPA axis dysregulation produces allostatic load, a cumulative burden of physiological wear resulting from repeated or sustained stress activation. Internal movement arts including Tai Chi and Qigong consistently demonstrate cortisol reduction and HPA axis normalization effects, positioning them as potent interventions for stress-related somatic disorders.

## HPA Axis Physiology

### Stress Response Cascade

- The hypothalamus releases corticotropin-releasing hormone in response to perceived threat or physiological stressor.
- CRH stimulates the anterior pituitary to release adrenocorticotropic hormone into circulation.
- ACTH stimulates the adrenal cortex to synthesize and release cortisol within 15 to 30 minutes.
- Cortisol exerts negative feedback on the hypothalamus and pituitary completing the regulatory loop.

### Cortisol Functions

- Cortisol mobilizes glucose from liver glycogen stores providing energy for fight-or-flight response.
- Cortisol suppresses immune function, digestion, reproduction, and growth during acute stress.
- Cortisol enhances memory consolidation for emotionally significant events via amygdala potentiation.
- Diurnal cortisol rhythm peaks within 30 minutes of waking and declines through the day.

### Sympathetic-HPA Coordination

- The sympathetic nervous system activates within seconds via catecholamine release producing immediate stress response.
- The HPA axis activates over minutes to hours sustaining the stress response through hormonal signaling.
- Both systems are regulated by the prefrontal cortex, amygdala, and hippocampus via descending pathways.
- HRV reflects real-time sympathetic-parasympathetic balance and serves as a proxy for HPA axis tone.

## Allostatic Load

- Allostatic load is the cumulative physiological cost of chronic stress adaptation across multiple organ systems.
- Primary mediators include cortisol, catecholamines, DHEA, and inflammatory cytokines.
- Secondary outcomes include elevated blood pressure, impaired glucose regulation, and immune dysregulation.
- Tertiary outcomes include cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, cognitive decline, and depression.
- Allostatic load accumulates across the lifespan and reflects the biological cost of chronic psychosocial stress.

## Practice Effects

- Tai Chi practice reduces morning cortisol levels in older adults after 12 weeks of training.
- Qigong reduces salivary cortisol and improves diurnal cortisol rhythm in stressed populations.
- HRV increases produced by internal arts practice reflect enhanced vagal tone and reduced HPA axis reactivity.
- Mindfulness-based stress reduction produces measurable reductions in cortisol, inflammatory markers, and psychological stress.
