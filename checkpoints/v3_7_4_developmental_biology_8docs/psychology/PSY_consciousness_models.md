# Consciousness Models

## Abstract

The scientific study of consciousness — what it is, how it arises from physical processes, and why there is subjective experience at all — remains the hardest problem in science, with multiple competing theoretical frameworks each capturing important aspects of the phenomenon without providing a complete account. Bernard Baars' Global Workspace Theory GWT proposes that consciousness corresponds to the broadcast of information to a global workspace — a distributed set of cortical areas that when activated together make information widely available to multiple specialized cognitive processes simultaneously. This global broadcast is what distinguishes conscious from unconscious processing — unconscious processing occurs in isolated modules while conscious processing recruits the global workspace making information available for deliberate attention, verbal report, and voluntary action. Giulio Tononi's Integrated Information Theory IIT proposes that consciousness corresponds to integrated information phi — the degree to which a system generates more information as a whole than the sum of its parts. IIT makes specific predictions about which physical systems can be conscious based on their information integration capacity and proposes that consciousness is a fundamental property of certain physical organizations. Higher Order Theory HOT proposes that a mental state is conscious when it is accompanied by a higher order representation of that state — consciousness requires meta-cognition, the mind's awareness of its own states. The hard problem of consciousness — David Chalmers' identification of the explanatory gap between physical brain processes and subjective experience — remains unresolved by any current theory. Shen as witness awareness in the Taoist and Buddhist traditions corresponds to the non-conceptual background awareness within which all experience arises — not a theory of consciousness but a direct description of the witness quality of awareness that meditation cultivates.

## Global Workspace Theory

- Consciousness corresponds to global broadcast making information available to multiple cognitive systems.
- The prefrontal-parietal network is the primary global workspace substrate.
- Unconscious processing occurs in isolated modules; conscious processing recruits the global workspace.
- Anesthesia and sleep reduce global workspace broadcasting corresponding to reduced Shen presence.

## Integrated Information Theory

- Phi measures the degree to which a system generates more information as a whole than its parts.
- High phi systems are conscious; low phi systems are not — regardless of their substrate.
- IIT implies that consciousness is graded and widespread — not an all-or-nothing biological property.
- This corresponds to the Buddhist understanding that awareness pervades all phenomena to varying degrees.

## Shen as Witness Awareness

- The Taoist Shen cultivation tradition points to a non-conceptual background awareness within which all experience arises.
- This witness awareness is not a content of experience but the luminous space in which experience occurs.
- Meditation cultivates the capacity to rest in witness awareness rather than being captured by its contents.
- This corresponds to the Heart Shen as the seat of awareness that is present whether or not it is noticed.

## TCM Correspondence

- Heart as the seat of Shen and consciousness corresponds to the Heart's role as the master of the global workspace.
- Shen clarity corresponding to high phi integrated information — the organized coherent activity of a healthy nervous system.
- Witness awareness cultivation corresponding to the development of metacognitive capacity through meditation practice.
- Shen disturbance in pathology corresponding to reduced global workspace integration and disrupted consciousness.
