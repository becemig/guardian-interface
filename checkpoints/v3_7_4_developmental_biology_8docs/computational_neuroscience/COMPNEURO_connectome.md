# Connectome and Graph Theory

## Abstract

Connectome and graph theory — the application of network science and graph theoretical analysis to the structural and functional connectivity of the brain, revealing that the human connectome is organized as a small-world network with high clustering coefficient and short path length enabling efficient local and global information integration, with a rich club of highly interconnected hub regions including the precuneus, posterior cingulate, medial prefrontal cortex, and superior frontal gyrus forming the structural backbone of the default mode network and providing the most densely connected nodes through which information flows between brain regions. Human Connectome Project — the large-scale neuroscience initiative using high-resolution diffusion tensor imaging and resting-state fMRI to map the structural and functional connectivity of the human brain at unprecedented resolution, revealing the white matter tract architecture connecting cortical and subcortical regions and the functional network organization of resting-state networks including the default mode, salience, executive control, sensorimotor, and visual networks. Small world network properties — the graph theoretical property of the brain network characterized by high local clustering enabling specialized local processing within modules combined with short global path length enabling rapid integration across the whole network, implemented through the combination of abundant short-range local connections providing clustering and sparse long-range connections providing shortcuts that reduce the average path length between any two nodes. Rich club organization — the tendency of high-degree hub nodes in the brain connectome to be more densely interconnected with each other than would be expected by chance, forming a rich club of brain regions that are mutually highly connected and that serve as the primary integration hubs through which information from different specialized processing modules is combined, with the rich club comprising bilateral precuneus, superior frontal gyrus, superior parietal cortex, hippocampus, putamen, and thalamus. The Meridian Network Graph Topology correspondence in TCM describes the classical meridian system as a network of channels connecting all parts of the body through which Qi and Blood circulate — a correspondence with the connectome as a network of white matter tracts and functional connections through which neural signals are transmitted across the brain.

## Small World Properties

- Clustering coefficient: high — local specialized processing within modules.
- Path length: short — global integration across modules.
- Small world index: brain exceeds random network clustering with similar path length.
- Efficiency: local efficiency from clustering global efficiency from short paths.

## Rich Club

- Hub nodes: precuneus posterior cingulate medial prefrontal superior frontal.
- Rich club: hubs more interconnected with each other than expected by chance.
- Integration: rich club nodes integrate information across specialized modules.
- Vulnerability: hub damage produces disproportionate functional disruption.

## Structural Functional Coupling

- DTI: diffusion tensor imaging maps white matter tract structural connectivity.
- rsfMRI: resting state fMRI maps functional connectivity — correlated BOLD fluctuations.
- Coupling: structural connectivity constrains but does not determine functional connectivity.
- Hubs: structural hubs tend to be functional hubs — default mode network core.
