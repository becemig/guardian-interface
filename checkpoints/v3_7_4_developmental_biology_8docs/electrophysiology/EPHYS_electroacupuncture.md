# Electroacupuncture Mechanisms

## Abstract

Electroacupuncture mechanisms — the neurophysiological and neurochemical basis of the therapeutic effects of electroacupuncture, in which electrical stimulation is applied between acupuncture needles to produce controlled frequency-dependent activation of peripheral nerves and the release of specific endogenous opioid neuropeptides — represent the most extensively studied and scientifically validated aspect of acupuncture science, with Ji-Sheng Han's landmark research demonstrating that different stimulation frequencies selectively release different neuropeptides that produce distinct analgesic and therapeutic effects, providing the molecular mechanism for the classical TCM understanding of Qi activation through needling. Frequency-dependent neuropeptide release — Han's central finding that low-frequency electroacupuncture at 2 Hz selectively releases met-enkephalin and beta-endorphin in the spinal cord and brain acting on mu and delta opioid receptors, while high-frequency electroacupuncture at 100 Hz selectively releases dynorphin acting on kappa opioid receptors, and that combined alternating frequency at 2-100 Hz releases all three peptides for maximal analgesia — provides the molecular basis for the clinical selection of electroacupuncture frequency protocols based on desired therapeutic outcomes. De Qi electrical correlates — the characteristic sensation of soreness, numbness, distension, and heaviness produced by acupuncture needle insertion corresponding to the activation of polymodal nociceptors and mechanoreceptors by needle manipulation — has been correlated with measurable electrical changes at the needle site, with De Qi corresponding to the activation of Adelta and C fiber afferents that initiate the spinal and supraspinal analgesic cascades. Electroacupuncture analgesia pathways — the ascending nociceptive inhibition produced by EA through activation of the descending pain inhibitory system from the PAG through the NRM to the dorsal horn, release of endogenous opioids at spinal and supraspinal levels, and the anti-inflammatory effects of opioid and adenosine receptor activation at the needle site — provide the neurophysiological framework for understanding how peripheral needle stimulation produces widespread analgesic effects. The Qi activation electrical stimulation correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding of electroacupuncture as a modern technological implementation of the fundamental TCM principle of activating and directing Qi flow through the meridian system — with the electrical stimulation frequency and parameters corresponding to the manual needle technique parameters of tonification and dispersion.

## Frequency-Dependent Release

- 2 Hz low frequency: met-enkephalin beta-endorphin release — mu delta opioid receptors.
- 100 Hz high frequency: dynorphin release — kappa opioid receptor analgesia.
- 2-100 Hz alternating: all three peptides — maximal and sustained analgesia.
- Tolerance: high frequency tolerance develops faster than low frequency.

## De Qi Correlates

- Needle manipulation activates Adelta and C polymodal nociceptors and mechanoreceptors.
- Adelta activation: sharp well-localized sensation — fast pain and initial De Qi.
- C fiber activation: dull aching diffuse sensation — sustained De Qi component.
- Connective tissue rotation: needle grasping produces tissue displacement activating mechanoreceptors.

## Analgesia Pathways

- Spinal: enkephalin interneurons inhibit dorsal horn pain transmission neurons.
- Supraspinal: PAG-RVM descending inhibition enhanced by EA-released beta-endorphin.
- Anti-inflammatory: adenosine released at needle site activates A1 receptors reducing local inflammation.
- Autonomic: EA modulates HRV and sympathovagal balance through brainstem pathways.

## TCM Correspondence

- Low frequency 2 Hz corresponds to tonification technique activating and building Qi.
- High frequency 100 Hz corresponds to dispersion technique moving and clearing Qi stagnation.
- Frequency selection corresponds to TCM differentiation of tonification versus dispersion indication.
- De Qi as prerequisite for therapeutic effect corresponds to afferent activation requirement for neuropeptide release.
