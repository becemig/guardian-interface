# Craniosacral Therapy

## Abstract

Craniosacral therapy is a light-touch bodywork approach developed by osteopath John Upledger from William Sutherland's cranial osteopathy tradition. The craniosacral system comprises the brain and spinal cord surrounded by the cerebrospinal fluid within the dural membrane tube extending from the cranium to the sacrum. The cranial rhythmic impulse CRI is a subtle rhythmic motion at 6 to 12 cycles per minute palpable at the cranium and sacrum reflecting CSF pressure fluctuations and meningeal motility. Still points are periods of spontaneous cessation of the CRI during which the nervous system reorganizes and resets. The Governing Vessel Du Mai traversing the spinal cord and brain directly corresponds to the craniosacral axis as the primary central regulatory channel.

## Craniosacral System

- The dural tube is the meningeal sleeve enclosing the brain and spinal cord containing cerebrospinal fluid.
- The dural tube attaches firmly at the foramen magnum, C2, C3, and the sacrum creating a functional unit.
- CSF produced by choroid plexus and reabsorbed by arachnoid granulations creates rhythmic pressure fluctuations.
- These pressure fluctuations produce the CRI — a subtle tissue motion palpable throughout the body.

## Cranial Rhythmic Impulse

- The CRI oscillates at 6 to 12 cycles per minute distinct from respiratory and cardiac rhythms.
- Palpation at the cranium, sacrum, feet, and other sites reveals this rhythmic motion in skilled practitioners.
- Asymmetry, restriction, or cessation of CRI indicates dysfunction in the craniosacral system.
- Treatment uses light touch to release dural restrictions and support natural CRI expression.

## Still Point

- A still point is a spontaneous pause in CRI motion lasting seconds to minutes.
- During still points the nervous system reorganizes releasing chronic tension patterns and autonomic dysregulation.
- Practitioners can induce therapeutic still points through specific compression of the occipital base.
- Still points produce deep relaxation and parasympathetic dominance corresponding to vagal activation.

## TCM Correspondence

- Governing Vessel Du Mai traversing the spinal cord and brain directly corresponds to the craniosacral central axis.
- The rhythmic pulsation of CSF corresponds to the Wei Qi circulation through the Governing Vessel pathway.
- The sacrum as the lower anchor of the dural tube corresponds to the lower Dan Tian and Kidney constitutional root.
- Still point induction corresponds to the Taoist principle of Wu Wei allowing natural reorganization through non-intervention.
