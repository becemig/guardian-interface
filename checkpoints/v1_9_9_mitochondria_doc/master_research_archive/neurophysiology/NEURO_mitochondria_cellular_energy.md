# Mitochondria and Cellular Energy: ATP, Oxidative Phosphorylation, and Jing as Cellular Vitality

## Abstract

Mitochondria are the primary energy-producing organelles of eukaryotic cells generating adenosine triphosphate ATP through oxidative phosphorylation. They are the evolutionary descendants of proteobacteria that entered into endosymbiosis with ancestral eukaryotic cells approximately 1.5 billion years ago. Mitochondrial function governs cellular energy availability, reactive oxygen species production, calcium signaling, and the initiation of apoptotic cell death. The progressive decline in mitochondrial number, efficiency, and quality control capacity with aging corresponds closely to the TCM concept of Kidney Jing depletion as the fundamental substrate of vitality loss over the lifespan. Internal movement arts enhance mitochondrial biogenesis, improve oxidative capacity, and reduce pathological reactive oxygen species production.

## Mitochondrial Structure and Function

### Electron Transport Chain

- The electron transport chain is embedded in the inner mitochondrial membrane comprising four protein complexes.
- NADH and FADH2 from the citric acid cycle donate electrons to Complex I and Complex II respectively.
- Electrons pass through Complexes III and IV driving proton pumping across the inner membrane.
- The resulting proton gradient drives ATP synthase Complex V to produce ATP from ADP and inorganic phosphate.
- Approximately 36 to 38 ATP molecules are produced per glucose molecule through complete oxidative phosphorylation.

### Reactive Oxygen Species

- Electron leakage from the electron transport chain produces superoxide radicals as a normal byproduct of respiration.
- Superoxide is converted to hydrogen peroxide by superoxide dismutase and further neutralized by catalase and glutathione peroxidase.
- When antioxidant capacity is overwhelmed reactive oxygen species accumulate producing oxidative stress and cellular damage.
- Mitochondrial DNA is particularly vulnerable to oxidative damage due to its proximity to the electron transport chain.

### Mitochondrial Dynamics

- Mitochondria continuously undergo fission dividing into smaller units and fusion merging into extended networks.
- Fission isolates damaged mitochondrial segments for selective degradation through mitophagy.
- Fusion allows complementation between mitochondria sharing functional components and diluting damaged elements.
- Disrupted mitochondrial dynamics underlie neurodegenerative diseases and accelerated aging phenotypes.

## Mitochondrial Biogenesis

### PGC-1 Alpha

- PGC-1 alpha peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma coactivator 1 alpha is the master regulator of mitochondrial biogenesis.
- Exercise activates PGC-1 alpha through AMPK and calcium signaling pathways driving new mitochondrial production.
- Cold exposure, caloric restriction, and certain phytochemicals also activate PGC-1 alpha.
- Regular aerobic exercise produces measurable increases in skeletal muscle mitochondrial density and oxidative capacity.

### NAD Metabolism

- NAD nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide is the primary electron carrier in cellular respiration and a substrate for sirtuin deacetylases.
- NAD levels decline with aging reducing mitochondrial efficiency and sirtuin-mediated cellular maintenance.
- NAD precursors including NMN and NR are emerging as interventions to restore mitochondrial function in aging.
- Caloric restriction and exercise both maintain higher NAD levels supporting mitochondrial health.

## Aging and Jing Depletion

### Mitochondrial Theory of Aging

- Accumulated mitochondrial DNA mutations reduce electron transport chain efficiency over decades.
- Declining mitochondrial quality control through reduced mitophagy allows damaged mitochondria to persist.
- Progressive reduction in mitochondrial number and function produces the energy deficit characteristic of aging.
- This mitochondrial aging trajectory corresponds precisely to the TCM concept of progressive Kidney Jing depletion.
