# Macronutrients and Metabolism

## Abstract

Macronutrient metabolism describes how the body extracts, converts, and stores energy from carbohydrates, fats, and proteins through interconnected biochemical pathways coordinated by hormonal signals and cellular energy sensors. Carbohydrates are metabolized via glycolysis to pyruvate, then either to acetyl-CoA for the Krebs cycle and oxidative phosphorylation, or to lactate under anaerobic conditions. Fats undergo beta-oxidation to acetyl-CoA, generating more ATP per gram than carbohydrates. Proteins are deaminated and their carbon skeletons enter metabolic pathways at multiple points. The Krebs cycle and electron transport chain in mitochondria convert acetyl-CoA to ATP with high efficiency. Metabolic flexibility — the capacity to shift fluidly between carbohydrate and fat oxidation based on availability and demand — is a key marker of metabolic health and athletic performance. Insulin and glucagon coordinate substrate availability and storage. In TCM, macronutrient metabolism maps to the Spleen-Stomach transforming and transporting function — the central axis of postnatal Qi production.

## Carbohydrate Metabolism

- Glycolysis: glucose to pyruvate - 10 enzymatic steps - net 2 ATP per glucose
- Aerobic: pyruvate to acetyl-CoA via pyruvate dehydrogenase - enters Krebs cycle
- Anaerobic: pyruvate to lactate - regenerates NAD+ - allows continued glycolysis
- Glycogen: glucose stored as glycogen in liver (100g) and muscle (400g)
- Gluconeogenesis: liver synthesizes glucose from lactate, glycerol, alanine during fasting
- Pentose phosphate: alternative glucose pathway - generates NADPH and ribose-5-phosphate
- Insulin: promotes glucose uptake, glycolysis, glycogen synthesis, fat storage

## Fat Metabolism

- Beta-oxidation: fatty acids cleaved into acetyl-CoA units - 2 carbons per cycle
- ATP yield: palmitate (16C) yields 129 ATP vs 36 ATP for glucose - more energy dense
- Ketogenesis: excess acetyl-CoA converted to ketones in liver during fasting or low carb
- Ketones: beta-hydroxybutyrate and acetoacetate - brain and muscle fuel during fasting
- Lipolysis: triglyceride breakdown - stimulated by glucagon, epinephrine - inhibited by insulin
- Essential fatty acids: linoleic acid (omega-6) and alpha-linolenic acid (omega-3) - must be consumed
- Eicosanoids: arachidonic acid and EPA precursors to inflammatory and anti-inflammatory mediators

## Protein Metabolism

- Digestion: proteases break proteins to amino acids - absorbed in small intestine
- Transamination: amino group transferred to alpha-ketoglutarate - forms glutamate
- Urea cycle: amino groups excreted as urea - liver - detoxification
- Glucogenic: most amino acids can be converted to glucose via gluconeogenesis
- Ketogenic: leucine and lysine exclusively ketogenic - feed Krebs via acetyl-CoA
- mTOR: branched-chain amino acids (leucine) activate mTOR - stimulate muscle protein synthesis
- Protein turnover: 300g protein synthesized and degraded daily - highly dynamic

## Krebs Cycle and Oxidative Phosphorylation

- Krebs cycle: 8 reactions - acetyl-CoA to CO2 - generates NADH and FADH2
- ETC: NADH and FADH2 donate electrons - proton gradient drives ATP synthase
- Yield: up to 36-38 ATP per glucose via full oxidative phosphorylation
- Mitochondria: power plants of the cell - highly dynamic - fuse and fission
- ROS: 1-2 percent of electrons leak and form superoxide - primary source of aging damage
- Uncoupling: UCP1 in brown fat uncouples ETC - generates heat not ATP - thermogenesis

## Metabolic Flexibility

- Definition: capacity to shift substrate use based on availability and metabolic state
- Fed state: primarily glucose oxidation - RQ near 1.0
- Fasted state: shift to fat oxidation and ketone production - RQ near 0.7
- Metabolic inflexibility: hallmark of insulin resistance and metabolic syndrome
- Training: endurance training increases fat oxidation capacity at any given intensity
- TCM: flexible Qi transformation - Spleen Qi adapting to seasonal and daily demands
