# Allostasis and Allostatic Load

## Abstract

Allostasis — achieving stability through change — was proposed by Peter Sterling and Joseph Eyer as an alternative to homeostasis emphasizing that the body maintains stability by actively changing its set points in response to anticipated demands rather than returning to fixed set points. Bruce McEwen and Eliot Stellar introduced allostatic load as the cumulative wear-and-tear on the body and brain from chronic stress and repeated cycles of allostatic response. Allostatic load is measured through multi-system biomarker composites including HPA axis markers cortisol and DHEA-S, sympathetic markers norepinephrine and epinephrine, cardiovascular markers blood pressure and HRV, metabolic markers waist-hip ratio and blood glucose, and inflammatory markers CRP and IL-6. High allostatic load predicts accelerated aging, cardiovascular disease, cognitive decline, immune dysfunction, and mortality — making it one of the most comprehensive biological measures of chronic stress burden. Allostatic overload occurs when allostatic demands exceed the organism's regulatory capacity — corresponding to the collapse of homeostatic regulation. Low HRV is among the most sensitive allostatic load markers reflecting reduced cardiovascular adaptability. Jing depletion in TCM — the exhaustion of constitutional essence through chronic overexertion, illness, and excessive demand — corresponds precisely to allostatic load as the accumulated constitutional cost of chronic adaptive demand.

## Allostasis vs Homeostasis

- Homeostasis maintains fixed set points — body temperature, blood pH — through feedback regulation.
- Allostasis maintains stability through anticipatory change in set points based on predicted demands.
- The cardiovascular system is allostatic — heart rate and blood pressure vary continuously with anticipated demands.
- Anticipatory allostatic responses prepare the body for demands before they occur reducing reactive strain.

## Allostatic Load Measurement

- Primary mediators include cortisol, DHEA-S, norepinephrine, epinephrine — the direct stress hormones.
- Secondary outcomes include blood pressure, HRV, waist-hip ratio, fasting glucose, and cholesterol.
- Tertiary outcomes include clinical disease endpoints — atherosclerosis, cognitive impairment, and immune dysfunction.
- HRV is among the most sensitive allostatic load indicators — low HRV reflects reduced cardiovascular adaptability.

## Allostatic Overload

- Allostatic overload type 1 occurs when demands exceed available energy resources requiring emergency responses.
- Allostatic overload type 2 occurs when there is sufficient energy but lifestyle and social conditions maintain chronically elevated allostatic state.
- Chronic overload produces structural changes — hippocampal atrophy, arterial stiffening, and immune dysregulation.
- Recovery from allostatic overload requires reducing load and actively building allostatic reserve.
