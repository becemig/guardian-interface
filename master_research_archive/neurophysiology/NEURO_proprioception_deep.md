# Proprioception and Deep Somatic Sensing

## Abstract

Proprioception is the sense of body position, movement, and force arising from mechanoreceptors in muscles, tendons, joints, and skin. Charles Sherrington coined the term proprioception to distinguish this sense of self-movement and position from exteroception of the external world and interoception of the internal visceral body. Muscle spindles are the primary proprioceptors detecting muscle length and rate of change through Ia and II afferent fibers. Golgi tendon organs detect muscle force through Ib afferent fibers providing the safety reflex for excessive tension. Joint mechanoreceptors including Ruffini endings, Pacinian corpuscles, and free nerve endings detect joint position, movement, and pain. The gamma motor system independently controls muscle spindle sensitivity enabling the CNS to adjust proprioceptive gain during movement. Sinew channels in TCM governing the tendons and ligaments of each meridian pathway correspond to the proprioceptive and myofascial signaling network of the musculoskeletal system.

## Muscle Spindles

- Muscle spindles are encapsulated intrafusal muscle fibers running parallel to extrafusal contractile fibers.
- Ia afferents from the primary annulospiral endings detect both muscle length and velocity of stretch.
- II afferents from the secondary flower-spray endings detect static muscle length.
- The gamma motor system controls intrafusal fiber contraction adjusting spindle sensitivity during movement.

## Golgi Tendon Organs

- GTOs are located at the muscle-tendon junction detecting the force developed in the tendon.
- Ib afferents from GTOs produce inhibitory postsynaptic potentials on alpha motor neurons providing autogenic inhibition.
- GTO activation during excessive force prevents muscle and tendon damage through the inverse stretch reflex.
- Proprioceptive neuromuscular facilitation PNF exploits GTO inhibition to produce muscle lengthening.

## Joint Receptors

- Ruffini endings respond to joint position and sustained pressure providing slow-adapting position signals.
- Pacinian corpuscles respond to rapid joint movement and vibration providing fast-adapting velocity signals.
- Free nerve endings in joint capsules detect pain, temperature, and extreme mechanical stress.
- Joint receptor signals integrate with muscle spindle and GTO input to construct the body schema.

## TCM Correspondence

- Sinew channels governing tendons and ligaments correspond to the proprioceptive and myofascial signaling network.
