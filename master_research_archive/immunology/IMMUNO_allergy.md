# Allergy and Hypersensitivity

## Abstract

Allergy and hypersensitivity — the inappropriate immune responses to normally harmless environmental substances including pollen, food proteins, animal dander, and drugs that produce the spectrum of allergic diseases including allergic rhinitis, asthma, atopic dermatitis, food allergy, and anaphylaxis, affecting over 30 percent of the population in industrialized countries and representing the immune system's misdirected application of the antiparasitic Th2 immune response against harmless environmental proteins in the context of reduced regulatory tone and old friend exposure. IgE mast cell basophil axis — the cellular and molecular basis of immediate hypersensitivity reactions, with initial allergen exposure driving Th2-polarized immune responses and IL-4 and IL-13 signaling to B cells to undergo class switch recombination to IgE production, with allergen-specific IgE binding to the high-affinity IgE receptor FcepsilonRI on mast cells and basophils sensitizing these cells for immediate degranulation upon subsequent allergen cross-linking — releasing histamine, tryptase, leukotrienes, and prostaglandins producing the vasodilatation, bronchospasm, and mucus secretion of acute allergic reactions. Th2 immune deviation — the immunological basis of allergic sensitization through the preferential differentiation of allergen-specific CD4 T cells toward the Th2 phenotype producing IL-4, IL-5, IL-9, and IL-13, driven by the innate cytokine TSLP produced by epithelial cells in response to allergens and barrier disruption, with Th2 cytokines promoting IgE switching, eosinophil recruitment, mast cell differentiation, and goblet cell hyperplasia — and with the Th1 regulatory balance and old friend-driven Treg responses being required to prevent Th2 skewing. Allergen immunotherapy mechanisms — the only disease-modifying treatment for IgE-mediated allergy, administering escalating doses of allergen subcutaneously or sublingually to induce tolerance through the generation of allergen-specific regulatory T cells producing IL-10 and TGF-beta, the induction of blocking IgG4 antibodies competing with IgE for allergen binding, and the progressive reduction in mast cell and basophil reactivity — producing durable tolerance that persists after treatment cessation. The Wind Cold invasion allergic inflammatory response correspondence in TCM describes the classical pathology of Wind Cold or Wind Heat invading the Lung through the exterior to produce sneezing, nasal congestion, watery discharge, and respiratory symptoms — a classical syndrome corresponding to the immediate IgE-mediated mast cell degranulation of allergic rhinitis producing histamine-driven vasodilation, mucus secretion, and sneezing.

## IgE Mechanism

- Sensitization: Th2 response drives IL-4 IL-13 signaling to B cells — IgE production.
- FcepsilonRI: high affinity IgE receptor on mast cells basophils — sensitization.
- Cross-linking: allergen bridges two IgE molecules — immediate degranulation.
- Mediators: histamine tryptase leukotrienes PGD2 — acute allergic symptoms.

## Th2 Deviation

- TSLP: epithelial cytokine from allergen and barrier disruption — Th2 driver.
- IL-4: Th2 differentiation and IgE class switching.
- IL-5: eosinophil survival and recruitment — tissue eosinophilia.
- IL-13: goblet cell hyperplasia mucus airway hyperreactivity.

## Immunotherapy

- Mechanism: allergen-specific Treg IL-10 TGF-beta induction.
- Blocking IgG4: competes with IgE for allergen binding.
- Mast cell desensitization: reduced FcepsilonRI expression and reactivity.
- Durability: tolerance persists after treatment cessation — disease modification.
