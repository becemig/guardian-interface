# Epigenetic Inheritance

## Abstract

Epigenetic inheritance — the transmission of epigenetic information including DNA methylation patterns, histone modifications, and non-coding RNA populations across cell generations and in some cases across organismal generations through the germline — challenges the central dogma of genetics by demonstrating that acquired characteristics of the soma can under specific circumstances influence the epigenome of subsequent generations, with documented examples of transgenerational epigenetic inheritance in response to famine, toxin exposure, trauma, and exercise providing a molecular basis for the inheritance of acquired characteristics beyond DNA sequence. Transgenerational epigenetic inheritance — the transmission of environmentally induced epigenetic changes through the germline to affect the phenotype of F2 and F3 generations that were never directly exposed to the original environmental trigger, with documented examples including the Dutch Hunger Winter cohort showing increased metabolic disease risk in grandchildren of famine-exposed grandmothers, Michael Skinner's demonstration that endocrine disruptor exposure produces transgenerational reproductive abnormalities through germline epigenetic transmission, and the Swedish Overkalix cohort showing transgenerational effects of paternal grandfather nutrition on grandson cardiovascular mortality. Germline epigenetic reprogramming — the two waves of global DNA demethylation and remethylation that normally erase parental epigenetic marks during gametogenesis and early embryogenesis to reset the epigenome to a pluripotent state, with imprinted genes and some repeat elements escaping this reprogramming providing windows through which environmental epigenetic information can be transmitted transgenerationally. Epigenetic clocks — Steve Horvath's discovery that DNA methylation at specific CpG sites across the genome tracks biological age with remarkable precision across tissues, with the epigenetic clock measuring biological age independently of chronological age and predicting health outcomes, mortality risk, and the effectiveness of anti-aging interventions. The ancestral Jing lineage transmission correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that Kidney Jing carries the accumulated constitutional essence of the ancestral lineage, with each generation's experiences and health influencing the Jing transmitted to subsequent generations — a philosophical framework that corresponds to the modern science of transgenerational epigenetic inheritance.

## Transgenerational Evidence

- Dutch Hunger Winter: grandchildren of famine-exposed grandmothers — increased metabolic disease.
- Overkalix cohort: paternal grandfather nutrition affects grandson cardiovascular mortality.
- Skinner endocrine disruptors: F3 generation reproductive abnormalities from F0 vinclozolin exposure.
- Exercise: paternal exercise improves offspring metabolic health through sperm epigenome.

## Germline Reprogramming Windows

- First wave: primordial germ cell migration — global demethylation to near zero.
- Remethylation: sex-specific methylation patterns established during gametogenesis.
- Imprinting escape: IGF2 H19 DMRs resist demethylation — parent-of-origin marks preserved.
- Repeat elements: some retrotransposons escape reprogramming — transmission window.

## Epigenetic Clock

- Acceleration: epigenetic age acceleration predicts mortality independent of chronological age.
- Interventions: caloric restriction exercise reduce epigenetic age.
- Reprogramming: partial Yamanaka factor expression reduces epigenetic age in vivo.
