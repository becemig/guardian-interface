# MDMA-Assisted Therapy for PTSD

## Abstract

MDMA-assisted therapy for PTSD — the MAPS-sponsored clinical development program that has produced Phase 3 trial evidence demonstrating that MDMA-assisted psychotherapy produces large and durable reductions in PTSD symptom severity that significantly exceed those of psychotherapy alone, with 67 percent of participants no longer meeting PTSD diagnostic criteria after three sessions of MDMA-assisted therapy in the pivotal Phase 3 trial — represents a transformative breakthrough for treatment-resistant PTSD and has generated significant regulatory momentum toward FDA approval, with the therapeutic mechanism involving MDMA's unique neurochemical profile that simultaneously increases prosocial connection and reduces defensive fear responding to enable processing of traumatic memories. MDMA neurochemistry — the mechanism of action of 3,4-methylenedioxymethamphetamine as a substrate-type releaser of serotonin, dopamine, and norepinephrine that produces massive vesicular monoamine release, with the therapeutic effects attributable primarily to the extraordinary serotonin release producing oxytocin secretion, increased feelings of trust and social connection, and reduced amygdala fear responding while maintaining conscious processing of emotional memories. Phase 3 trial evidence — the MAPS MAPP1 and MAPP2 trials demonstrating that three sessions of MDMA-assisted therapy over 18 weeks produced mean reductions in CAPS-5 PTSD symptom scores of 24 points compared to 14 points for placebo-controlled therapy, with 67 percent versus 32 percent no longer meeting PTSD diagnostic criteria and 71 versus 48 percent achieving functional improvement, establishing MDMA-assisted therapy as the most effective treatment for PTSD yet demonstrated in controlled trials. Therapeutic mechanism — the unique psychological state produced by MDMA described as a therapeutic window in which the patient is simultaneously emotionally open, connected, and trusting while also able to access traumatic memories with reduced overwhelming fear, enabling the trauma processing that conventional therapy cannot achieve due to the hyperarousal and avoidance that prevent engagement with traumatic material.
