# EEG Brain States and Neural Oscillations

## Abstract

EEG brain states and neural oscillations — the frequency-specific patterns of synchronized neural activity measurable through electroencephalography that reflect the functional state of large-scale neural networks and correlate with specific cognitive, emotional, and physiological states — provide the primary window into the electrical life of the brain accessible without invasive techniques, with the five canonical frequency bands delta, theta, alpha, beta, and gamma each corresponding to distinct functional states and clinical applications that overlap with the TCM five-Shen model of distributed consciousness across the organ networks. Delta oscillations 0.5-4 Hz — the large-amplitude slow oscillations predominant during deep slow-wave sleep that reflect the synchronized downstates of widespread cortical neurons and the clearance of metabolic waste by the glymphatic system — are also generated by thalamocortical circuits during certain types of deep meditation and correlate with reduced metabolic activity, cellular restoration, and the unconscious processing associated with deep sleep. Theta oscillations 4-8 Hz — generated primarily by the hippocampus during spatial navigation and memory encoding and by the medial prefrontal cortex during error monitoring and emotional regulation, and increased during drowsiness, meditation, and certain creative states — provide the oscillatory scaffold for episodic memory formation through the theta-gamma coupling mechanism in which gamma-frequency bursts encoding individual memory items are nested within theta cycles that organize their temporal sequence. Alpha oscillations 8-13 Hz — the dominant resting rhythm of the relaxed, eyes-closed adult brain arising from thalamocortical circuits — reflect an idling or inhibitory state in posterior cortices that reduces information processing in unattended areas while facilitating processing in attended areas, with alpha power inversely correlating with cortical excitability and directly correlating with relaxed attentiveness. Gamma oscillations 30-100 Hz — the high-frequency oscillations generated by interneuron networks through fast GABA inhibition that produce precise synchrony within and between cortical areas — are associated with conscious perception, feature binding, focused attention, and working memory, with 40 Hz gamma in particular proposed as the neural correlate of conscious awareness by Francis Crick and Christof Koch and demonstrated to be enhanced in experienced meditators. The Five Shen brain state correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding of the five Shen as the five aspects of consciousness distributed across the five organ networks — the Shen Heart governing waking awareness, the Zhi Kidney governing will and deep constitutional intelligence, the Hun Liver governing dreaming and imagination, the Po Lung governing instinct and somatic wisdom, and the Yi Spleen governing focused thought — with each Shen corresponding to characteristic EEG brain state profiles.

## Frequency Band Functions

- Delta 0.5-4 Hz: deep sleep glymphatic clearance cellular restoration.
- Theta 4-8 Hz: memory encoding spatial navigation meditation creative insight.
- Alpha 8-13 Hz: relaxed attentiveness cortical inhibition idling rhythm.
- Beta 13-30 Hz: active thinking focused attention motor control.
- Gamma 30-100 Hz: conscious perception feature binding working memory focused awareness.

## Meditation EEG

- Mindfulness: increased frontal theta and posterior alpha — relaxed attentive state.
- Loving-kindness: increased gamma synchrony in experienced meditators.
- Transcendental meditation: increased alpha coherence across cortical regions.
- Advanced meditators: high-amplitude gamma self-induction during mental practice.

- Gamma training: cognitive enhancement in aging and neurodegeneration.
