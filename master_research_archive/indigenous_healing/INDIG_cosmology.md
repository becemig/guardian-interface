# Indigenous Cosmologies and Sacred Geography

## Abstract

Every indigenous healing tradition is embedded in a cosmology — a comprehensive account of the nature of reality, the structure of the universe, and the place of human beings within it. These cosmologies are not primitive attempts at physics: they are sophisticated relational ontologies that encode thousands of years of observation about the patterns of life, health, and healing. The single most consistent feature across indigenous cosmologies worldwide is animism — in Graham Harvey's contemporary formulation, not the naive belief that rocks have souls, but the recognition that the world is populated by persons of many kinds, only some of whom are human, and that appropriate relationship with these persons is the foundation of health and flourishing. The world tree or axis mundi — documented from Siberian shamanism to Norse mythology to Mayan cosmology — encodes the vertical structure of reality: below the ground, the roots and ancestors; on the surface, the ordinary world of human relationships; above, the sky world of elevated powers and cosmic order. Sacred geography transforms landscape into a living body: mountains as elders, rivers as arteries, specific sites as acupoints of the land body where the membrane between worlds is thin and healing power is concentrated. The Aboriginal Dreaming is perhaps the most sophisticated cosmological framework: a dimension of reality outside ordinary time in which the ancestral creative acts that brought the world into being are still occurring, accessible through ceremony, song, and dream. Health in this framework is not merely biological: it is cosmological alignment — the person, community, land, and cosmos in right relationship, each tier of the living universe supporting the others. Guardian Interface, integrating Daoist cosmology, TCM channel theory, and now indigenous cosmologies, is building a multi-tradition map of the living universe and its healing geometries.
