# Neural Port-Hamiltonian Systems

## Abstract

The nervous system is, at every level of organization from ion channel to connectome, an energy processing network — and its energy dynamics can be formulated with remarkable precision within the port-Hamiltonian framework. The neuron is a port-Hamiltonian system: the cell membrane is a capacitor storing charge energy, the ion channels are conductance elements dissipating energy, the sodium-potassium pump is an effort source maintaining the electrochemical gradient, and synaptic inputs are external ports through which energy is injected or extracted. The Hodgkin-Huxley model of action potential generation, the most successful quantitative model in neuroscience, is naturally a port-Hamiltonian system with gated conductance elements whose dynamics are driven by voltage-dependent port opening and closing. At the network level, John Hopfield's attractor neural network has an explicit energy function — the Hopfield energy — whose minima correspond to stored memories, and whose gradient descent dynamics describe the memory retrieval process. Karl Friston's free energy principle extends this framework to the entire brain: the brain is a hierarchical generative model that minimizes variational free energy — a bound on surprise — through predictive coding, and all perception, action, and learning are manifestations of this single free energy minimization imperative. In TCM terms, Shen — the mental-spiritual aspect of Heart energy — is the neural Hamiltonian: the energy function whose landscape determines the quality of consciousness, the clarity of perception, and the coherence of mental function. Disturbed Shen is a perturbed neural Hamiltonian — its landscape deformed by trauma, heat, phlegm, or Qi stagnation — and treatment restores the Shen landscape toward its healthy minimum-energy configuration.
