# Resonance Frequency Breathing

## Abstract

Resonance frequency breathing — breathing at the precise rate that maximally amplifies heart rate variability through baroreflex resonance — is the most physiologically grounded and clinically validated therapeutic breathwork technique, providing a measurable, trainable, and immediately effective method for increasing vagal tone, improving emotional regulation, and optimizing autonomic nervous system function. The baroreflex — the homeostatic reflex that maintains blood pressure within narrow limits through continuous heart rate and vascular tone adjustments — oscillates naturally at approximately 0.1 Hz, the low-frequency band of HRV representing a 10-second cycle of heart rate increase and decrease driven by the baroreflex feedback loop. When breathing rate is synchronized to this natural baroreflex oscillation frequency — approximately 5-6 breaths per minute or 0.083-0.1 Hz — respiratory sinus arrhythmia RSA and baroreflex oscillations enter resonance, producing maximum HRV amplitude: the heart rate rises and falls in the largest possible swings synchronized with each breath. This resonance condition is the most efficient state for vagal training because it maximally engages the parasympathetic cardiac control system, providing the strongest possible training signal for increasing resting vagal tone through repeated practice. The individual resonance frequency varies slightly between people — typically between 4.5 and 7 breaths per minute — and can be identified precisely through HRV biofeedback equipment that identifies the breathing rate producing the largest HRV amplitude. Coherent breathing — Stephen Elliott's formulation of resonance frequency breathing at exactly 5 breaths per minute for adults — represents a simplified fixed-rate protocol that approximates the resonance frequency for most adults without requiring individual biofeedback assessment. Twenty minutes of resonance frequency breathing daily for 4-8 weeks produces lasting increases in resting HRV, reduced anxiety, improved emotional regulation, and improved cardiovascular and respiratory function through the mechanisms of vagal tone training and baroreflex gain improvement.
