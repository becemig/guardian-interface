# Apnea and Freediving Physiology

## Abstract

Apnea physiology — the physiological adaptations that enable breath-hold diving and extended respiratory arrest — reveals one of the most remarkable constitutional training responses in human biology: the mammalian dive response, an evolutionarily ancient reflex complex shared with all diving mammals that produces profound cardiovascular and metabolic changes optimizing oxygen conservation during breath-hold immersion. The mammalian dive response is triggered by facial cold water immersion combined with breath-hold and consists of three primary components: bradycardia — a dramatic slowing of heart rate to 10-50 percent of resting rate driven by vagal activation — peripheral vasoconstriction — a massive reduction of blood flow to the muscles and viscera preserving oxygen delivery to the heart and brain — and blood shift — the redistribution of blood from the periphery into the thoracic vasculature to prevent lung compression at depth. The spleen — recognized in TCM as the organ governing Blood storage and transformation — plays a crucial role in apnea physiology: splenic contraction during breath-hold releases stored red blood cells into circulation, increasing hematocrit and oxygen-carrying capacity by 6-9 percent within minutes of breath-hold initiation. Erika Schagatay's research at Mid Sweden University has demonstrated that splenic contraction volume is trainable: regular breath-hold training produces spleen hypertrophy and increased splenic red blood cell storage capacity, with elite freedivers showing spleens significantly larger than non-divers. Hypoxic tolerance — the capacity to function and maintain consciousness at arterial oxygen saturations that would produce syncope in untrained individuals — is similarly trainable: regular breath-hold practice shifts the hypoxic threshold through adaptations in cerebrovascular autoregulation, hemoglobin oxygen affinity, and central pattern generator tolerance for hypoxic conditions. Breath-hold as constitutional training in the TCM framework corresponds to the practice of kumbhaka retention in pranayama — the suspension of breath to consolidate and intensify the Qi retained within the body.

## Mammalian Dive Response

- Facial cold water immersion plus breath-hold triggers the complete dive reflex complex.
- Bradycardia: heart rate falls to 10-50 percent of resting — profound vagal activation.
- Peripheral vasoconstriction: blood flow redirected from muscles to heart and brain.
- Blood shift: peripheral blood redistributed into thoracic vasculature at depth.

## Spleen Contraction

- Spleen contracts within seconds of breath-hold initiation releasing stored red blood cells.
- Hematocrit increases 6-9 percent — significant increase in oxygen-carrying capacity.
- Elite freedivers show spleen hypertrophy from training — larger spleen stores more red blood cells.
- Corresponds to the TCM Spleen governing Blood storage and its mobilization for constitutional demand.

## Hypoxic Tolerance Training

- Regular breath-hold training shifts the syncope threshold to lower oxygen saturations.
- Adaptations include cerebrovascular autoregulation, hemoglobin oxygen affinity changes.
- Elite freedivers maintain consciousness at SpO2 levels of 50 percent that would cause syncope in untrained individuals.
