# Taijichuan Martial Application

## Abstract

The martial application of Taijichuan demonstrates the most counter-intuitive principle in all of martial arts: that yielding defeats force, that four ounces can move a thousand pounds, and that the practitioner who is most relaxed, most sensitive, and most present will defeat the practitioner who is strongest, fastest, and most aggressive. This principle — expressed in the classical saying that if the opponent does not move I do not move, if the opponent moves slightly I move first — describes the combat strategy of listening and following the opponent's force to the point of emptiness where it can be redirected, uprooted, and discharged. Vital point strategy Dim Mak Dian Xue applies the TCM acupuncture point system to martial application — striking specific acupuncture points along channels to disrupt Qi flow, cause sudden pain, or produce neurological effects. The classical Taijichuan masters were also TCM practitioners who understood the channel system deeply and incorporated this knowledge into their martial application. Specific strikes correspond to specific organ network disruption: striking Neiguan PC-6 disrupts Heart Pericardium Qi causing cardiac arrhythmia response; striking Zusanli ST-36 disrupts Stomach Qi causing sudden nausea and weakness; striking Taichong LV-3 disrupts Liver Qi causing sudden anger and destabilization. Combat Neijin is the application of the internally cultivated Neijin force to martial exchange — the sudden Fa Jin discharge that emerges from Song relaxation through the whole-body kinetic chain to a specific point. The paradox of Taijichuan combat effectiveness is that it requires the complete absence of aggression — aggression creates tension that blocks Neijin transmission and listening sensitivity simultaneously.

## Four Ounces Moving Thousand Pounds

- The principle demonstrates that redirecting force requires far less energy than opposing it.
- The practitioner who listens accurately can redirect the opponent's force using their own momentum.
- This requires the practitioner to move at the precise moment and in the precise direction — no earlier, no later.
- The timing comes from Ting Jin listening sensitivity not from anticipation or muscular preparation.

## Vital Point Strategy

- PC-6 Neiguan: disrupts Heart-Pericardium Qi producing cardiac response and psychological destabilization.
- ST-36 Zusanli: disrupts Stomach Qi producing sudden weakness and grounding loss.
- LV-3 Taichong: disrupts Liver Qi producing sudden anger, loss of focus, and muscular cramping.
- GB-21 Jianjing: disrupts Gallbladder Qi producing shoulder collapse and arm weakness.

## Combat Neijin

- Fa Jin discharge emerges from complete Song relaxation through the whole-body kinetic chain.
- The discharge is instantaneous — from complete stillness to complete expression in a fraction of a second.
- Aggression, tension, or anticipation blocks Fa Jin by creating resistance in the transmission chain.
- The most effective combat expression of Taijichuan requires the practitioner to be completely non-aggressive.

## TCM Correspondence

- Vital point strategy directly applies TCM channel and point theory to martial disruption.
- Fa Jin as Song-to-force corresponds to the TCM concept of Qi gathering in stillness and expressing in movement.
- Combat without aggression corresponds to the Taoist Wu Wei principle of responding to what is without adding force.
- Yielding defeating force corresponds to the TCM Kidney Water quality — the deepest and most powerful through its yielding.
