# Anatomy Trains Superficial Back Line and Posterior Chain Organization

## Abstract

The Superficial Back Line is the longest myofascial meridian in Thomas Myers Anatomy Trains framework. It runs from the plantar surface of the foot up the entire posterior aspect of the body to the forehead, connecting all posterior structures into a continuous tensile chain. The Superficial Back Line maintains full body extension against gravity, protects the vulnerable posterior neural structures, and closely parallels the Bladder meridian pathway in Traditional Chinese Medicine. Understanding the Superficial Back Line provides structural context for posterior chain tension patterns, hamstring tightness, and the spinal elongation principles central to Tai Chi posture.

## Structural Pathway

### Foot and Lower Leg

- Originates at the plantar surface of the toes via the short toe flexors and plantar fascia.
- The plantar fascia is the foundational anchor of the entire Superficial Back Line.
- Ascends via the Achilles tendon and gastrocnemius-soleus complex up the posterior lower leg.
- Connects to the posterior knee capsule and popliteal fascia at the knee.

### Thigh and Pelvis

- Continues via the hamstring muscle group from the posterior knee to the ischial tuberosity.
- The hamstrings are the primary posterior thigh segment of the Superficial Back Line.
- Crosses the sacrotuberous ligament connecting the ischium to the sacrum.
- This ligament-muscle transition is a critical force transmission node in the posterior chain.

### Spine and Skull

- Continues via the sacrolumbar fascia and erector spinae muscles up the entire length of the spine.
- The thoracolumbar fascia is the broadest and thickest fascial structure in the Superficial Back Line.
- Continues via the epicranial fascia and galea aponeurotica over the skull.
- Terminates at the supraorbital ridge of the frontal bone above the eyebrows.

## Postural Extension Function

- The Superficial Back Line maintains upright extension of the body against gravitational flexion forces.
- Chronic shortening of the Superficial Back Line produces the forward flexed posture common in aging.
- Hamstring tightness reflects Superficial Back Line tension extending from the plantar fascia to the lumbar spine.
- Plantar fasciitis often reflects global Superficial Back Line tension rather than isolated foot pathology.

## TCM Convergence

- The Superficial Back Line pathway closely parallels the Bladder meridian ascending from the foot to the head.
- The Bladder meridian is the longest meridian in the body mirroring the Superficial Back Line as the longest myofascial chain.
- Back shu transport points of the Bladder meridian lie along the erector spinae corresponding to the spinal segment of the Superficial Back Line.
- The Governing Vessel Du Mai ascending along the spinous processes runs parallel and deep to the Superficial Back Line erector segment.

## Relevance to Internal Movement Arts

- Spinal elongation and axial extension in Tai Chi requires coordinated lengthening of the Superficial Back Line.
- The principle of an invisible thread lifting the crown of the head corresponds to gentle Superficial Back Line traction producing spinal decompression.
- Hamstring flexibility developed through Qigong and stretching practice reduces Superficial Back Line restriction across the entire posterior chain.
- Plantar grounding in Tai Chi activates the plantar fascia origin of the Superficial Back Line establishing root through the posterior chain.
