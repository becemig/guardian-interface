# Tantric Somatic Traditions

## Abstract

Tantric somatic traditions — encompassing Hatha Yoga, Kundalini Yoga, and the broader Shaiva and Shakta Tantric traditions of India — provide a comprehensive psychophysiological map of the subtle body that parallels and corresponds to the Chinese meridian system in significant ways, representing an independent convergent discovery of the same underlying energetic-somatic reality. The nadi system describes a network of subtle energy channels — some traditions describe 72,000 nadis — through which prana vital force flows. The three primary nadis are: Sushumna the central channel running along the spinal axis, Ida the lunar cooling left channel, and Pingala the solar warming right channel. These three primary nadis correspond precisely to the Chinese Governing Vessel Sushumna, Conception Vessel, and the paired Yin-Yang axes of the meridian system. The seven chakras are energy centers located along the Sushumna at the spinal levels corresponding to the endocrine plexuses — Muladhara root at the pelvic floor, Svadhisthana sacral, Manipura solar plexus, Anahata heart, Vishuddha throat, Ajna third eye, and Sahasrara crown. Kundalini is the coiled dormant evolutionary energy at the base of the spine that rises through the chakras when awakened through spiritual practice. The five prana vayus describe the directional qualities of prana in the body — Prana upward moving, Apana downward moving, Samana equalizing, Udana upward-outward moving, and Vyana pervading. Bandhas — yogic locks — compress specific body regions to redirect prana. Chong Mai correspondence — the Penetrating Vessel as the central axis channel — corresponds precisely to the Sushumna as the primary channel of Kundalini ascent.

## Nadi System

- Sushumna is the central channel along the spinal axis — the pathway of kundalini ascent and liberation.
- Ida is the lunar cooling left channel governing parasympathetic function and feminine principle.
- Pingala is the solar warming right channel governing sympathetic function and masculine principle.
- The three primary nadis correspond to the Governing Vessel, Conception Vessel, and their Yin-Yang axes.

## Seven Chakras

- Muladhara root chakra at the pelvic floor governs survival, grounding, and earth element — Kidney correspondence.
- Svadhisthana sacral chakra governs creativity, sexuality, and water element — Kidney-Liver correspondence.
- Manipura solar plexus chakra governs will, power, and fire element — Spleen-Liver correspondence.
- Anahata heart chakra governs love, compassion, and air element — Heart-Pericardium correspondence.
- Vishuddha throat chakra governs expression and space element — Lung correspondence.
- Ajna third eye chakra governs intuition and transcendent mind — Shen-Governing Vessel correspondence.
- Sahasrara crown chakra governs pure consciousness and liberation — Upper Dan Tian correspondence.

## Prana Vayus

- Prana vayu moves upward and inward governing intake — breath, food, sensory experience.
- Apana vayu moves downward and outward governing elimination — breath, waste, reproductive function.
- Samana vayu circulates in the abdomen governing digestion and assimilation — Spleen correspondence.
- Vyana vayu pervades the entire body governing circulation and distribution — Wei Qi correspondence.
- Udana vayu moves upward in the throat governing expression, growth, and the upward movement of spirit.

## TCM Correspondence

- Chong Mai as the central axis channel corresponds precisely to Sushumna as the primary kundalini pathway.
- The Kidney-governing base chakra Muladhara corresponds to the Kidney as the constitutional root.
- The five prana vayus correspond to the five directional qualities of Zong, Wei, Ying, and Yuan Qi.
- Kundalini ascent through chakras corresponds to the TCM cultivation sequence of Jing refining to Qi to Shen.
