# Gait and Locomotion

## Abstract

Bipedal walking is the fundamental human locomotor pattern requiring the coordinated control of dozens of muscles to maintain forward progression while preventing falls. The spinal central pattern generator produces the rhythmic alternating muscle activation underlying the gait cycle while supraspinal centers provide adaptive modulation. Ground reaction forces, step width, cadence, and stride length are key gait parameters measurable with clinical tools. Gait variability — the stride-to-stride fluctuation in timing and length — is an emerging biomarker of neurological health with fractal scaling properties. Tai Chi walking with its emphasis on weight shifting, single-leg stance, and deliberate step placement directly trains the neural and biomechanical foundations of safe efficient gait.

## Gait Cycle

- The gait cycle runs from heel strike of one foot to the next heel strike of the same foot.
- Stance phase occupies 60 percent of the cycle with double support at initial and terminal stance.
- Swing phase occupies 40 percent of the cycle requiring hip flexion, knee flexion, and ankle dorsiflexion.
- Walking speed equals stride length multiplied by cadence — the primary determinant of gait efficiency.

## Central Pattern Generator

- The spinal CPG produces rhythmic alternating flexor-extensor activation without supraspinal input.
- Supraspinal centers including the mesencephalic locomotor region initiate and modulate CPG output.
- The cerebellum coordinates timing and adapts the gait pattern to terrain and task demands.
- Basal ganglia dysfunction in Parkinson disease disrupts gait initiation and produces festinating shuffling gait.

## Gait Variability

- Healthy gait exhibits fractal long-range correlations in stride timing reflecting integrated neural control.
- Loss of fractal scaling toward random variability indicates neurological aging and fall risk.
- Tai Chi practice restores fractal gait variability in older adults corresponding to improved neural integration.
- Stride-to-stride variability measured by coefficient of variation is a clinical fall risk predictor.
