# TCM and Computational Psychiatry

## Abstract

Traditional Chinese Medicine contains the most sophisticated pre-modern system of psycho-spiritual regulation in world medicine. Its Five Shen framework — the five spirit-essences distributed across the organ systems — constitutes a distributed model of mind that maps with remarkable precision onto contemporary computational psychiatry concepts. The Shen of the Heart is the master integrator: the consciousness that receives and arbitrates all experience, corresponding precisely to the Bayesian brain as the seat of prior formation and prediction. The Hun of the Liver is the ethereal, dreaming, planning aspect of mind — the model-based system that simulates possible futures, corresponding to the prefrontal model-based reasoning system disrupted in depression and OCD. The Po of the Lung is the corporeal, instinctual, somatic soul — the body-based reflexive processing of the amygdala and interoceptive system. The Yi of the Spleen is intention and cognitive clarity — the attentional precision-weighting system. The Zhi of the Kidney is willpower, constitutional drive, and long-horizon motivation — the temporal discounting parameter in reinforcement learning. Together, the Five Shen constitute a full computational architecture for mind: Bayesian integrator, model-based planner, interoceptive threat detector, attentional allocator, and motivational reserve. The Five Element emotional correspondences — joy, anger, worry, grief, fear — map cleanly onto computational psychiatry categories when understood as dysregulations of these five systems. Guardian Interface, already integrating TCM channel knowledge throughout the atlas, here synthesizes the psycho-spiritual layer with the computational layer — the deepest integration point in the entire project.
