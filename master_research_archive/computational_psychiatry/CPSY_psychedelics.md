# Psychedelics and Computational Psychiatry

## Abstract

The renaissance of psychedelic research has converged with computational psychiatry to produce some of the most elegant mechanistic theories in contemporary neuroscience. Robin Carhart-Harris and Karl Friston's REBUS model — Relaxed Beliefs Under Psychedelics — proposes that classic psychedelics (psilocybin, LSD, DMT, mescaline) act via 5-HT2A receptor agonism to globally reduce the precision weighting of top-down predictions throughout the cortical hierarchy. The result is a temporary but profound relaxation of the brain's high-level priors — the rigid belief structures, habit patterns, self-narratives, and defense mechanisms that normally constrain perception and cognition. With top-down precision reduced, bottom-up sensory and emotional signals gain disproportionate influence: the world becomes vivid, novel, and emotionally resonant. Deeply held beliefs — including the pathological priors of depression, addiction, PTSD, and OCD — are temporarily made malleable, offering a therapeutic window for new learning and perspective. The entropic brain hypothesis extends this further: psychedelics increase neural entropy — the complexity and unpredictability of brain activity — moving the brain from a low-entropy, highly constrained state into a higher-entropy, more flexible state. Mystical experiences — the core mediator of therapeutic outcomes in psilocybin trials — represent the extreme end of this entropy increase: the dissolution of the Markov blanket separating self from world. ## REBUS Model: Relaxed Beliefs Under Psychedelics

- Core mechanism: 5-HT2A agonism at apical dendrites of pyramidal neurons in deep cortical layers
- Effect: reduces gain on top-down prediction signals - priors become less precise
- Hierarchy: high-level priors (self-model, world-model) most affected - most precision to lose
- Bottom-up release: sensory prediction errors now dominate - experience becomes novel
- Prior relaxation: pathological priors (depressive, addictive, traumatic) temporarily loosened
- Therapeutic window: relaxed priors allow new information to update previously rigid beliefs
- Integration: post-session period when relaxed priors can be replaced with adaptive ones

## Entropic Brain Hypothesis

- Neural entropy: complexity and unpredictability of neural signal - measured by LZW complexity
- Normal waking: intermediate entropy - structured but flexible
- Depression and addiction: low entropy - brain stuck in rigid attractor states
- Psychedelics: high entropy - brain escapes attractors - expanded state space
- Sleep and anesthesia: low entropy - different mechanism - synchrony and downscaling
- Dose-response: entropy increases linearly with psychedelic dose
- Therapeutic entropy: depression treated by temporarily increasing entropy - loosening attractors

## Serotonin 2A Receptor Mechanism

- Location: high density in layer V pyramidal neurons - top-down output neurons
- Agonism: psychedelics bind 5-HT2A - increase glutamate release - excitatory
- Paradox: excitation reduces precision of top-down signal - noise injection into predictions
- Thalamic gating: 5-HT2A on thalamic reticular nucleus - relaxes sensory gating
- Default mode: 5-HT2A density highest in DMN - ego-network most affected
- Ketamine: NMDA antagonist - different mechanism - also reduces top-down precision
- MDMA: 5-HT release - reduces amygdala threat response - different precision effect
