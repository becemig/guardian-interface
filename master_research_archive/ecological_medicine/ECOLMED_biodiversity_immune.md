# Biodiversity and Immune Function

## Abstract

Biodiversity and immune function — the emerging science demonstrating that human immune system calibration depends on ongoing contact with the diverse microbial and macrobial biodiversity of natural environments, with the old friends hypothesis proposing that the immune system evolved to expect and require the presence of specific organisms including helminths, mycobacteria, and diverse environmental bacteria to calibrate its regulatory circuits, and with the collapse of environmental biodiversity in urbanized industrialized environments producing epidemic rates of inflammatory and allergic diseases through the removal of the microbial diversity that the immune system requires for proper regulatory T cell development and inflammatory tolerance. Old friends hypothesis — Graham Rook's refinement of the hygiene hypothesis proposing that the relevant immunological partners are not pathogenic organisms against which we should maintain resistance but rather the commensal and environmental organisms that co-evolved with the human immune system over millions of years, including helminth parasites, Mycobacterium species from soil and water, lactobacilli from fermented foods, and diverse environmental bacteria encountered through outdoor activity and nature contact, with their absence producing the dysregulated inflammatory responses underlying allergies, autoimmunity, inflammatory bowel disease, and depression. Farm effect allergy prevention — the robust epidemiological finding that children raised on traditional farms with exposure to livestock animals, raw milk, and diverse barn microbiomes have dramatically reduced rates of asthma, hay fever, and eczema compared to children in urban environments, with the protective factor identified as the diversity of the microbial environment including specific Acinetobacter, Lactobacillus, and other species, and with the farm effect replicable by administering farm dust extracts in mouse models of allergic asthma. Mycobacterium vaccae and depression — the discovery that injection of heat-killed Mycobacterium vaccae a soil bacterium produces antidepressant effects in mice through stimulation of serotonergic pathways, with urban gardening and soil contact providing a natural route of Mycobacterium vaccae exposure that may contribute to the mental health benefits of nature contact through this environmental microbiome-brain axis. The Earth Qi Spleen soil microbiome correspondence in TCM describes the classical association of the Earth phase and Spleen organ system with the soil and agricultural fertility that sustains human life — a correspondence with the biodiversity-immune research demonstrating that the soil microbiome is a critical environmental input for proper immune calibration, mediated through the digestive system.

## Old Friends Hypothesis

- Co-evolved partners: helminths Mycobacteria environmental bacteria lactobacilli.
- Regulatory T cells: old friends stimulate Treg development — inflammatory tolerance.
- Absence: removal of old friends removes Treg stimulation — dysregulated inflammation.
- Diseases: allergies autoimmunity IBD depression — all associated with old friend loss.

## Farm Effect

- Protection: farm children 50-70 percent lower asthma and allergy rates.
- Factor: barn microbiome diversity — Acinetobacter Lactobacillus specific species.
- Raw milk: unpasteurized milk microbiome — independent protective factor.
- Mechanism: diverse early microbial exposure calibrates immune regulatory circuits.

## Soil Microbiome Contact

- Mycobacterium vaccae: soil bacterium — antidepressant serotonergic effects.
- Gardening: skin and inhalation exposure to soil bacteria — natural old friend contact.
- Outdoor play: children in biodiverse environments show better immune calibration.
- Biodiversity gradient: higher environmental biodiversity correlates with lower allergy rates.
