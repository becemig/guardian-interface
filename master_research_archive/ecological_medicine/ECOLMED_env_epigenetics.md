# Environmental Epigenetics

## Abstract

Environmental epigenetics — the investigation of how features of the physical and social environment including climate, altitude, seasonal light cycles, nature contact, enriched or impoverished environmental conditions, and toxic exposures produce lasting changes in gene expression through epigenetic mechanisms including DNA methylation, histone modification, and non-coding RNA, revealing that the environment continuously writes its signature into the genome's expression pattern and that many of these environmental epigenetic marks are transmitted across cell generations and in some cases across organismal generations through the germline. Landscape epigenomics — the emerging field investigating how geographic and landscape features including altitude, climate, soil chemistry, and vegetation influence the epigenomes of organisms living in those environments, with human populations living at high altitude showing distinct methylation patterns at genes governing hypoxia response, erythropoiesis, and cardiovascular function compared to lowland populations, and with the epigenome serving as an interface through which the organism's gene expression is tuned to the specific environmental conditions of its habitat. Seasonal epigenetic rhythms — the demonstration by Dopico and colleagues that approximately 23 percent of the human genome shows significant seasonal variation in expression, with immune genes showing winter upregulation and summer downregulation, metabolic genes showing seasonal variation aligned with changing energy demands, and seasonal patterns in DNA methylation at immune and metabolic loci — providing a molecular basis for the seasonal variation in disease susceptibility and physiological function recognized in both modern medicine and traditional systems. Environmental enrichment epigenetics — the evidence that environmental enrichment including social interaction, physical activity, cognitive stimulation, and nature contact produces beneficial epigenetic changes including increased BDNF promoter demethylation in hippocampus enhancing neuroplasticity, increased histone acetylation at memory genes, and reduced methylation at stress response genes, while environmental impoverishment including social isolation, sensory deprivation, and chronic stress produces adverse epigenetic changes that impair neuroplasticity and stress resilience. The Heaven Earth Seasonal Qi Epigenetic Imprinting correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that the seasonal rhythms of Heaven and Earth write their influence into the human body through the seasonal variation of Qi, with different seasons requiring different lifestyle and dietary adaptations to maintain alignment with the seasonal Qi — a traditional framework corresponding to the modern epigenetic evidence that seasonal environmental rhythms produce systematic changes in gene expression across the human genome.

## Landscape Epigenomics

- Altitude: high altitude populations show methylation differences at hypoxia genes.
- Climate: temperature humidity exposure shapes methylation of climate-response genes.
- Soil chemistry: mineral availability influences methylation through metabolic cofactor availability.
- Geographic variation: distinct epigenomic signatures in geographically isolated populations.

## Seasonal Epigenetics

- 23 percent of genome: shows significant seasonal expression variation.
- Winter immune: inflammatory genes upregulated in winter — infection defense.
- Summer metabolic: energy metabolism genes shift seasonally.
- Methylation: seasonal methylation rhythms at immune and metabolic loci.

## Environmental Enrichment

- BDNF: enriched environment demethylates BDNF promoter — increased expression.
- Histone acetylation: enrichment increases H3K9ac at memory genes — enhanced plasticity.
- Stress genes: enrichment reduces methylation at CRF and GR genes — stress resilience.
- Nature contact: outdoor exposure produces beneficial epigenetic changes in stress response genes.
