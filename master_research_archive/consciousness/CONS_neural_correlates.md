# Neural Correlates of Consciousness

## Abstract

Consciousness — the subjective experience of being aware — remains the deepest unsolved problem in science. The neural correlates of consciousness (NCC) are the minimal neural mechanisms sufficient for any given conscious experience. Four major theoretical frameworks compete and partially overlap: Global Workspace Theory proposes consciousness arises from ignition and broadcast of information across a thalamocortical global workspace; Integrated Information Theory proposes consciousness is identical to integrated information (phi) and is substrate-independent; Predictive Processing frames consciousness as the brain's best guess about the causes of sensory input, with attention as precision weighting; Higher-Order Theories require a meta-representational state for conscious experience. The binding problem asks how distributed neural activity across specialized cortical areas is unified into a single coherent experience. The hard problem asks why any physical process gives rise to subjective experience at all — a question no current theory fully resolves.

## Global Workspace Theory (Baars, Dehaene)

- Core claim: consciousness arises from ignition and broadcast across a global neuronal workspace
- Architecture: prefrontal and parietal cortices serve as the workspace hub
- Ignition: nonlinear all-or-none transition - stimulus crosses threshold, activates whole network
- Broadcast: conscious content becomes available to all cognitive systems simultaneously
- Unconscious: local processing without ignition - fast, efficient, not globally available
- EEG signature: P300 wave and late gamma burst mark conscious access
- Clinical: vegetative state patients show no ignition response to stimuli

## Integrated Information Theory (Tononi)

- Core claim: consciousness is identical to integrated information - phi
- Phi: measure of information generated by the whole above and beyond its parts
- Substrate independence: any system with high phi is conscious - includes non-biological
- Cerebellum paradox: cerebellum has 70 percent of neurons but low phi - low consciousness
- Cortex: highly integrated recurrent connectivity - high phi
- Criticism: panpsychism implications, phi computationally intractable at scale

## Predictive Processing and Free Energy (Friston)

- Core claim: brain is a prediction machine - minimizes prediction error (free energy)
- Generative model: brain maintains hierarchical model of world - generates predictions
- Perception: resolving discrepancy between prediction and sensory input
- Attention: precision weighting - amplifies reliable prediction errors
- Consciousness: high-level predictions that persist despite error - the content of awareness
- Psychedelics: REBUS model - relaxed beliefs under psychedelics - flattens predictive hierarchy

## Binding Problem

- Problem: how distributed neural activity is unified into single coherent experience
- Gamma synchrony: 40 Hz oscillations proposed to bind distributed representations
- Thalamocortical loops: thalamus as relay and binding hub
- Temporal binding: synchronous firing within 30 ms window binds features
- Critique: gamma synchrony necessary but not sufficient for binding

## Hard Problem (Chalmers)

- Easy problems: explaining cognitive functions - attention, memory, report - tractable
- Hard problem: why any physical process gives rise to subjective experience at all
- Explanatory gap: no mechanistic account bridges neural firing and qualia
- Illusionism: Frankish - qualia are introspective illusions - hard problem dissolves
- TCM parallel: Shen as the animating principle of consciousness - beyond mechanistic reduction
