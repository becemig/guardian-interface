# Functions of Sleep

## Abstract

Sleep serves multiple essential biological functions that cannot be adequately replicated during waking. The discovery of the glymphatic system revealed that slow-wave sleep drives cerebrospinal fluid flushing of metabolic waste including amyloid-beta and tau from the brain interstitium. Memory consolidation during sleep involves hippocampal sharp-wave ripple replay during NREM and REM-dependent associative integration. The synaptic homeostasis hypothesis proposes that SWS downscales synaptic strength following waking potentiation, preventing saturation. Growth hormone secretion is tightly coupled to the first SWS episode. REM sleep serves as emotional memory therapy, replaying distressing memories without noradrenergic stress neurochemistry to achieve emotional resolution. Sleep deprivation rapidly degrades all these functions, with immune, metabolic, cognitive, and emotional consequences accumulating within days.

## Glymphatic Clearance

- Discovery: Maiken Nedergaard 2013 - sleep-dependent brain waste clearance system
- Mechanism: astrocytic aquaporin-4 channels drive CSF through perivascular spaces
- SWS dependence: glymphatic flow increases 60 percent during slow-wave sleep
- Clearance targets: amyloid-beta, tau, alpha-synuclein, lactate, metabolic byproducts
- Alzheimer connection: chronic sleep deprivation accelerates amyloid accumulation
- Body position: lateral sleep position maximizes glymphatic efficiency
- TCM parallel: Kidney governing brain-marrow - sleep restores Jing to nourish brain

## Memory Consolidation

- Declarative memory: hippocampal sharp-wave ripples during N2 and SWS replay daytime experiences
- Sleep spindle coupling: spindles coordinate hippocampal-neocortical transfer during SWS
- Procedural memory: motor sequence learning consolidated during N2 spindles
- REM integration: REM binds new memories to existing knowledge networks - insight and creativity
- Targeted memory reactivation: cues during sleep can bias which memories are consolidated
- Sleep before learning: prepares hippocampus to receive new information
- Sleep after learning: consolidates and protects new memories from interference

## Synaptic Homeostasis Hypothesis (SHY)

- Tononi and Cirelli model: waking potentiates synapses via LTP - sleep downscales via SWS
- Problem solved: without downscaling, synapses would saturate - no room for new learning
- Slow oscillations: SWS slow waves drive net synaptic downscaling across cortex
- Evidence: synaptic markers (GluA1, spine size) increase during waking, decrease after sleep
- Restoration: SHY explains why sleep restores mental clarity and learning capacity

## Hormonal Secretion

- Growth hormone: 70-80 percent of daily GH secreted during first SWS episode
- Prolactin: peaks during sleep - immune modulation and tissue repair
- Testosterone: nocturnal rise during REM sleep - peak at waking
- Cortisol: suppressed during early sleep - rises in final sleep hours preparing for waking
- Leptin: rises during sleep suppressing hunger - sleep deprivation reduces leptin
- Ghrelin: hunger hormone rises with sleep deprivation - drives overeating

## Immune Restoration

- Cytokine production: IL-1, IL-6, TNF-alpha peak during SWS - drive immune activity
- T-cell trafficking: sleep promotes T-cell homing to lymph nodes via integrin expression
- NK cell activity: natural killer cell cytotoxicity peaks during sleep
- Vaccine response: sleep after vaccination doubles antibody response
- Sleep deprivation: one night of 4-hour sleep reduces NK cell activity by 70 percent

## Emotional Processing - REM as Therapy

- Walker model: REM replays emotional memories in absence of norepinephrine stress chemistry
- LC silent during REM: locus coeruleus NE neurons completely off - calm neurochemical tone
- Result: emotional memory content is replayed and reprocessed without re-traumatization
- PTSD disruption: NE hyperactivity during REM prevents emotional processing - nightmares persist
- Prazosin: alpha-1 blocker reduces NE during REM - reduces PTSD nightmares
- Morning feeling: emotional tone of previous day feels less acute after REM processing
