# Sleep Disruption and Pathology

## Abstract

Sleep disorders represent a major and growing public health burden, with insomnia affecting 10-15 percent of the adult population and obstructive sleep apnea affecting 15-30 percent. Beyond clinical disorders, chronic voluntary sleep restriction to 6 hours or less produces cumulative cognitive and physiological deficits that subjects systematically underestimate. A single night of 4-hour sleep reduces NK cell activity by 70 percent, and two weeks of 6-hour sleep produces cognitive deficits equivalent to 24 hours of total sleep deprivation. The allostatic load from chronic sleep disruption accelerates cardiovascular disease, metabolic syndrome, neurodegeneration, and immune dysfunction. TCM frameworks map sleep pathology to Heart Shen disturbance, Blood deficiency, Kidney Yin deficiency, and Yinqiao-Yangqiao imbalance.

## Insomnia

- Definition: difficulty initiating or maintaining sleep with daytime functional impairment
- Prevalence: 10-15 percent chronic insomnia, 30 percent transient insomnia
- Hyperarousal model: insomnia is a disorder of hyperarousal not hypoarousal
- Markers: elevated nocturnal cortisol, high-frequency EEG activity, elevated metabolic rate
- Cognitive model: sleep-related worry and safety behaviors perpetuate insomnia
- CBT-I: first-line treatment - sleep restriction, stimulus control, cognitive restructuring
- Sleep restriction therapy: temporarily restrict time in bed to consolidate and deepen sleep

## Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA)

- Mechanism: upper airway collapse during sleep causes repeated apneas and hypopneas
- Consequence: repeated arousal from sleep, oxygen desaturation, sympathetic activation
- SWS suppression: OSA fragments and suppresses slow-wave sleep
- Cardiovascular: OSA is independent risk factor for hypertension, arrhythmia, stroke
- Treatment: CPAP restores sleep architecture and reverses most cardiovascular risk
- TCM: OSA maps to Phlegm-Dampness obstructing the throat and Lung Qi

## REM Sleep Behavior Disorder (RBD)

- Mechanism: REM atonia fails - patients physically act out dreams
- Brainstem: sublaterodorsal nucleus atonia circuit dysfunction
- Prodrome: 80-90 percent of RBD patients develop Parkinson or Lewy body dementia within 15 years
- Significance: RBD is the strongest known prodromal biomarker for synucleinopathies
- Treatment: melatonin and clonazepam reduce movement without restoring atonia

## Narcolepsy

- Mechanism: autoimmune destruction of orexin-producing neurons in lateral hypothalamus
- Cataplexy: sudden loss of muscle tone triggered by strong emotion - pathognomonic
- Sleep paralysis and hallucinations: REM intrusions into waking
- Flip-flop switch failure: without orexin stabilization, switch flips inappropriately
- Treatment: modafinil for EDS, sodium oxybate for cataplexy and nocturnal sleep

## Chronic Sleep Restriction Consequences

- Cognitive: 6 hours per night for 2 weeks produces deficits equal to 24-hour total deprivation
- Subjective underestimation: subjects report feeling fine despite objective impairment
- Immune: NK cell activity drops 70 percent after one 4-hour night
- Metabolic: insulin sensitivity drops 40 percent after one week of 6-hour sleep
- Cardiovascular: sleeping less than 6 hours increases heart attack risk by 200 percent
- Cancer: WHO classifies night shift work as probable carcinogen due to circadian disruption
- Neurodegeneration: chronic restriction accelerates amyloid-beta accumulation
