# Sub-Riemannian Body Mechanics

## Abstract

The human body is a non-holonomic mechanical system. Its joints do not permit free motion in all directions simultaneously — each joint imposes constraints on the velocity of adjacent segments, and these constraints are not integrable: they cannot be expressed as restrictions on the positions alone, only on the velocities. This is the defining characteristic of a non-holonomic system, and it is precisely the structure that sub-Riemannian geometry is designed to model. The joint constraint at each configuration point defines a horizontal distribution D — the set of instantaneously permitted velocity directions — and the sub-Riemannian metric on D encodes the energetic cost of motion in each permitted direction. The kinematic chain of the body — spine, pelvis, shoulder girdle, limbs — is a cascade of such distributions, each locally constraining and globally bracket-generating so that the full range of human movement is accessible via sequences of constrained motions. The most important consequence for the Guardian Interface project is the geometric explanation of Chan Si Gong — silk-reeling energy in Chen-style Tai Chi. Silk-reeling is not merely a training convention: it is the natural geodesic motion of a body whose joint constraints generate a Heisenberg-type distribution. The spiral, helical, winding quality of Chan Si movement is the sub-Riemannian geodesic of the constrained human body — the path of minimum energetic cost given the actual geometric structure of joints and fascia. The body spirals because spiral is the straight line in the geometry it actually inhabits.

## Joint Constraints as Distributions

- Holonomic constraint: restricts positions — integrable — reduces effective dimension of M
- Non-holonomic constraint: restricts velocities only — not integrable — preserves dimension
- Body joints: most joint constraints are non-holonomic — velocity-level restrictions
- Rolling contact: classic non-holonomic constraint — sphere rolling on plane — no slip
- Facet joints: spinal facet orientation defines permitted vertebral velocity directions
- Hip socket: acetabular geometry constrains femoral velocity within permitted arc
- Distribution D_joint: at each body configuration p, D_p = set of mechanically permitted velocities
- Bracket-generating: iterated brackets of permitted directions span full tangent space
