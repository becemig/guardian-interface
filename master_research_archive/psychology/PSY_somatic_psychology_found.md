# Somatic Psychology Foundations

## Abstract

Somatic psychology as a distinct field emerged from Wilhelm Reich's revolutionary insight that psychological character structure is simultaneously expressed as chronic muscular tension patterns — body armor — that protect against anxiety and unwanted emotional experience. Reich developed character analysis as a method for systematically working with character structure through body posture, muscular tension, and the restriction of organismic energy flow. Reich identified seven body segments where armor tends to organize: the ocular segment eyes and forehead, oral segment mouth and jaw, cervical segment throat and neck, thoracic segment chest, diaphragmatic segment, abdominal segment, and pelvic segment. Therapeutic work releases armor segment by segment from top to bottom allowing the free flow of biological energy through the body. Reich's orgone energy theory proposed that a primordial biological energy pervades living organisms and the cosmos — armoring restricts its flow producing neurosis and physical disease. Alexander Lowen developed Reich's work into bioenergetic analysis refining the character structure analysis and developing the grounding concept as its centerpiece. Stanley Keleman's formative psychology extended somatic character analysis into a developmental somatic framework. The somatic psychology tradition as a whole established the body as a primary therapeutic medium — not merely the container of the mind but an expressive, communicative, and transformative field in its own right. Jing-Shen embodiment in TCM — the progressive incarnation of the spiritual Shen into physical Jing through the developmental process — corresponds to the somatic psychology understanding of character structure as the embodied pattern of psycho-spiritual development.

## Body Armor

- Body armor is the sum total of chronic muscular tensions that protect against anxiety and unwanted emotion.
- It develops through the repeated inhibition of emotional impulses that were unsafe to express in childhood.
- Armor is functionally equivalent to psychological character defense — the same protection in body and psyche.
- Releasing armor through breath, movement, and expression allows the underlying emotion and need to emerge.

## Seven Segments

- Ocular segment: chronic tension around eyes and forehead restricting full visual and emotional contact.
- Oral segment: tension around mouth and jaw restricting expression, crying, and biting impulses.
- Cervical segment: tension in throat and neck restricting the cry, scream, and swallowing of emotion.
- Thoracic segment: held chest restricting breath, sadness, longing, and cardiac emotion.
- Diaphragmatic segment: chronic diaphragm tension restricting full breath and visceral emotion.
- Abdominal segment: tension restricting anger, fear, and digestive emotion.
- Pelvic segment: tension restricting sexual energy, pleasure, and the ground of biological vitality.

## Character Analysis

- Character analysis reads the body posture, muscular pattern, and behavioral style as an integrated whole.
- The characteristic posture reveals the primary defense and the underlying developmental wound.
- Therapeutic work begins with the most superficial armor layer and works progressively deeper.
- Releasing each layer reveals the next — armor removal is a sequential developmental process.
