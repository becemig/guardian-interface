# ACT Advanced

## Abstract

Acceptance and Commitment Therapy ACT advanced practice develops the six core processes of the hexaflex into a sophisticated clinical framework for building psychological flexibility — the capacity to contact the present moment fully and without unnecessary defense, and to change or persist in behavior in service of chosen values. The hexaflex comprises six interdependent processes organized around psychological flexibility: acceptance of difficult internal experiences without struggle, defusion from the literal content of thoughts observing them as mental events, present-moment contact with ongoing experience, self-as-context the stable observer perspective that transcends content, values clarification of what truly matters, and committed action in service of those values. Acceptance does not mean resignation or approval — it means making full contact with experience without adding the layer of struggle against it. Defusion reduces the behavioral impact of difficult thoughts by changing one's relationship to them rather than changing their content — the thought is seen as a thought rather than a fact. Self-as-context is the transcendent observer position — the I that notices thoughts, feelings, and sensations without being identical to any of them. Values in ACT are chosen directions of living rather than goals — they provide ongoing motivation independent of whether specific outcomes are achieved. The psychological flexibility model has been validated across hundreds of conditions and interventions — impaired psychological flexibility is a transdiagnostic factor underlying most psychological disorders. Liver Qi free flow in TCM — the Liver's governing of smooth, unobstructed movement of Qi through all channels enabling adaptive, responsive engagement with life — corresponds to psychological flexibility as its functional equivalent in Western psychological terms.

## Hexaflex Processes

- Acceptance makes full contact with difficult experience without adding struggle against it.
- Defusion observes thoughts as mental events rather than literal facts reducing their behavioral impact.
- Present-moment contact provides the ground for all other processes through ongoing sensory awareness.
- Self-as-context is the stable observer that notices experience without being defined by it.
- Values clarification identifies chosen directions of living that provide intrinsic ongoing motivation.
- Committed action moves in the direction of values even in the presence of difficult internal experience.

## Defusion

- Defusion techniques distance the person from the literal content of thoughts without disputing them.
- Thanking the mind for the thought, naming it as a thought, and singing it reduce its literal impact.
- Defusion works by changing the function of the thought rather than its form or frequency.
- Defused thoughts lose their capacity to control behavior even when they remain present.

## Values and Committed Action

- Values are chosen directions of living — quality of ongoing action rather than specific outcomes to achieve.
- They differ from goals in being process-oriented and never fully achievable — always available as direction.
- Committed action moves in the direction of values even when difficult emotions and thoughts are present.
- The willingness to experience difficulty in service of values is the measure of psychological flexibility.
