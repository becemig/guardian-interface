# Cognitive Biases and Heuristics

## Abstract

Cognitive biases are systematic patterns of deviation from rationality in judgment arising from the heuristics — mental shortcuts — that the cognitive system uses to reduce computational load. Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky's heuristics and biases research documented the systematic errors produced by availability, representativeness, and anchoring heuristics. Kahneman's dual process theory proposes that System 1 — fast, automatic, intuitive — and System 2 — slow, deliberate, analytical — interact to produce judgment with System 1 dominating most decisions. Confirmation bias — the tendency to search for and favor information confirming existing beliefs — is the most pervasive and consequential cognitive bias. Motivated reasoning describes the use of cognitive processes to reach preferred conclusions while maintaining the appearance of rational analysis. Yi — the Spleen's applied intelligence and concentrated thought — when disturbed by worry or emotional need produces the biased cognition of motivated reasoning and confirmation bias.

## Dual Process Theory

- System 1 operates automatically and quickly with little effort and no sense of voluntary control.
- System 2 allocates attention to effortful mental activities including complex computation and deliberate analysis.
- System 1 generates intuitions and impressions that System 2 can endorse or override with effort.
- Cognitive load, time pressure, and emotional arousal increase System 1 dominance reducing analytical checking.

## Availability Heuristic

- The availability heuristic estimates frequency or probability by the ease with which examples come to mind.
- Recent, vivid, and emotionally salient events are more available producing systematic overestimation of their frequency.
- Media coverage of rare dramatic events produces availability bias overestimating risk of plane crashes and shark attacks.
- Practitioners must account for availability bias when assessing pattern prevalence in clinical populations.

## Confirmation Bias

- Confirmation bias involves searching for, favoring, and recalling information consistent with existing beliefs.
- It operates at all stages — information search, interpretation, and memory retrieval — systematically distorting judgment.
- Strong prior beliefs increase confirmation bias making disconfirming evidence harder to process fairly.
- Structured hypothesis testing with genuine disconfirmation criteria reduces confirmation bias in reasoning.
