# Acceptance and Commitment Therapy

## Abstract

Acceptance and Commitment Therapy ACT developed by Steven Hayes is a third-wave cognitive behavioral therapy grounded in relational frame theory and functional contextualism that aims to increase psychological flexibility — the capacity to contact the present moment fully as a conscious human being and to change or persist in behavior when doing so serves valued ends. The ACT hexaflex model identifies six core processes: acceptance of difficult internal experiences rather than avoidance, cognitive defusion creating distance from the literal content of thoughts, contact with the present moment, self as context — the observing self distinct from the content of experience, values clarification identifying what truly matters, and committed action taking effective steps toward values. ACT is based on the recognition that psychological suffering arises not from the content of thoughts and feelings but from the individual's relationship to that content — particularly experiential avoidance and cognitive fusion. ACT is as effective as CBT for depression and superior for anxiety, chronic pain, and stigmatized conditions. Zhi commitment in TCM — the Kidney spirit's capacity for unwavering commitment to meaningful purpose sustaining effort through difficulty — corresponds to the ACT processes of values clarification and committed action.

## Psychological Flexibility

- Psychological flexibility is the capacity to contact the present moment and act in accordance with values.
- Psychological inflexibility — fusion, avoidance, dominance of the conceptualized past or future — produces suffering.
- The six hexaflex processes are the therapeutic levers for increasing psychological flexibility.
- ACT aims to build a life worth living rather than primarily to reduce symptom severity.

## Defusion

- Cognitive fusion is the tendency to take thoughts literally and treat them as facts about reality.
- Defusion creates distance from thoughts by observing them as mental events rather than truths.
- Defusion techniques include naming thoughts, saying thoughts in a silly voice, and observing the thought process.
- Defusion reduces the behavioral influence of unhelpful thoughts without requiring their content to change.

## Values and Committed Action

- Values are chosen directions for living that give meaning and purpose — not goals but ongoing orientations.
- Values clarification identifies what truly matters to the person beneath the noise of avoidance and fusion.
- Committed action involves taking effective steps toward values even in the presence of difficult internal experience.
- Behavioral commitments toward values provide the motivational engine of ACT treatment.
