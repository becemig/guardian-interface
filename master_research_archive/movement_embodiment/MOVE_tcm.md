# TCM Movement Theory: Sinew Channels and Jing Jin

## Abstract

Traditional Chinese Medicine has a dedicated system for understanding musculoskeletal function and movement disorders through the twelve sinew channels (Jing Jin), which map the myofascial pathways of the twelve primary meridians across the body surface. The sinew channels govern the tendons, ligaments, fascia, and muscles associated with each meridian system, providing a diagnostic and therapeutic framework for pain, weakness, spasm, and movement dysfunction. Unlike the primary meridians which transport Qi and Blood through the interior, sinew channels operate at the body surface and are primarily involved with movement, defense, and structural integrity. The correspondence between Jing Jin pathways and Myers anatomy trains myofascial lines is striking and provides a bridge between ancient Chinese somatic mapping and contemporary fascial anatomy. Wei syndrome and Bi syndrome represent the two primary TCM movement pathology categories.

## Twelve Sinew Channels - Jing Jin

- Definition: myofascial pathways of the twelve primary meridians - surface not interior
- Function: govern tendons, ligaments, fascia, muscles - movement and structural support
- Direction: all sinew channels flow from distal to proximal - periphery to center
- Grouping: three Yang of foot, three Yin of foot, three Yang of hand, three Yin of hand
- Binding points: sinew channels bind at joints - pathology concentrates at binding sites
- Treatment: Ashi points along sinew channel pathway - local needling or acupressure

## Anatomy Trains TCM Correspondence

- Superficial back line corresponds to Bladder sinew channel
  plantar fascia, gastroc, hamstrings, erectors, occipital - BL pathway
- Superficial front line corresponds to Stomach sinew channel
  tibialis anterior, quadriceps, rectus abdominis, SCM - ST pathway
- Lateral line corresponds to Gallbladder sinew channel
  peroneals, IT band, lateral abdominals, SCM - GB pathway
- Spiral line corresponds to combined GB and BL sinew channels
- Deep front line corresponds to Kidney and Liver sinew channels
  psoas, diaphragm, pericardium - KD and LV deep pathways
- Arm lines correspond to six arm sinew channels - LU LI PC TH HT SI

## Bi Syndrome - Painful Obstruction

- Pathology: external pathogenic factors invade sinew channels - obstruct Qi and Blood flow
- Wind Bi: moving pain - Wind pathogen - GB and BL sinew channels often affected
- Cold Bi: fixed severe pain - Cold pathogen - local vascular spasm - warming treatment
- Damp Bi: heavy swollen fixed pain - Damp pathogen - ST and SP sinew channels
- Heat Bi: hot red swollen - Heat pathogen or Bi transforming to Heat over time
- Treatment strategy: expel pathogenic factor, restore Qi-Blood flow, warm or cool as indicated
- Key points: GB 34 (Yanglingquan) influential point for sinews - all Bi syndrome

## Wei Syndrome - Flaccid Weakness

- Definition: weakness, atrophy, and loss of function of limbs without pain
- Mechanism: Lung Heat scorches fluids, fails to moisten sinews - or Liver-Kidney deficiency
- Lung Wei: acute onset after febrile disease - Lung Heat burns fluid nourishment to sinews
- Liver-Kidney Wei: gradual onset - deficiency of essence and Blood fails to nourish sinews
- Treatment: tonify Lung, Liver, Kidney - ST 36, GB 34, BL 23, LV 8, KD 3
- Classical rule: treat Wei syndrome only on Yangming meridians - ST and LI
- Modern parallel: Wei maps to motor neuron disease, MS, and post-viral weakness syndromes

## Tendino-Muscular Treatment Principles

- Local treatment: Ashi points at site of pain along sinew channel pathway
- Distal treatment: command points of associated primary meridian
- GB 34 Yanglingquan: influential point for all sinews - universal sinew channel point
- BL 40 Weizhong: command point for back and lumbar sinew channels
- ST 36 Zusanli: tonifies Qi and Blood nourishment to all sinew channels
- LV 3 Taichong: spreads Liver Qi - releases sinew tension and spasm
- GB 20 Fengchi: releases Wind from sinew channels of head and neck

## Acupressure for Movement Disorders

- Neck and shoulder tension: GB 20, GB 21, BL 10, TH 3
- Low back pain: BL 23, BL 40, GV 4, KD 3
- Knee pain: ST 35, SP 9, BL 40, GB 34
- Hip pain: GB 29, GB 30, BL 54
- Ankle sprain: BL 62, KD 6, SP 5, ST 41
- Wrist and forearm: PC 6, TH 5, LI 4, LU 7
- Plantar fasciitis: KD 1, BL 60, SP 4
