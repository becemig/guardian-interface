# Fascial Biomechanics

## Abstract

Fascia is the connective tissue matrix enveloping every structure in the body in a single continuous three-dimensional web. Donald Ingber tensegrity model extended to biological systems as biotensegrity provides the architectural framework: the body is not a compressive column but a tensegrity structure in which bones are compression struts floating in a continuous tensile network of fascia and muscle. Tom Myers Anatomy Trains maps seven primary myofascial continuity lines through which force transmits across the full body. These lines are the primary generators of the sub-Riemannian distribution D in the MCF framework — the fascial network is the geometric substrate of the body manifold.

## Tensegrity and Biotensegrity

- Tensegrity: Buckminster Fuller — tension integrity — stable structure from continuous tension
- Biotensegrity: Levin — bones as compression struts — fascia and muscle as tensile network
- Global distribution: force applied anywhere distributes across entire network instantly
- No local loading: no joint bears load in isolation — all connected through fascial web
- Implication: injury anywhere changes tension throughout — explains distant pain referral
- MCF: biotensegrity provides off-diagonal metric coupling — no segment is independent

## Viscoelasticity

- Viscoelastic: fascia has both viscous fluid and elastic solid mechanical properties
- Creep: slow deformation under sustained load — why prolonged sitting changes posture
- Stress relaxation: force decreases over time under constant deformation — manual therapy
- Hysteresis: loading and unloading curves differ — energy dissipated as heat
- Thixotropy: fascia becomes less viscous with movement and warming — warm-up effect

## Piezoelectricity and Mechanotransduction

- Piezoelectricity: collagen generates electrical charge when mechanically deformed
- Fibroblast sensing: fibroblasts detect mechanical strain via integrin receptors
- Remodeling: habitual movement patterns drive collagen reorientation — fascial adaptation
- Acupoint: Langevin — acupuncture needle rotation winds collagen — mechanical signal
- Guardian: MCF attunement as measure of fascial mechanical coherence

## Anatomy Trains Myofascial Lines

- Superficial back line: plantar fascia calves hamstrings erector spinae scalp fascia
- Superficial front line: tibialis anterior quadriceps rectus abdominis SCM
- Lateral line: peroneus IT band TFL QL intercostals SCM
- Spiral line: wraps body in double helix — rhomboids serratus obliques IT band
- Deep front line: deepest core — psoas iliacus pericardium mediastinum hyoids
- Functional lines: cross-body — posterior right lat to left glute
- Sub-Riemannian: each line defines a horizontal vector field — together they generate D

## Myofascial Force Transmission

- Huijing: 30-40 percent of muscle force transmitted via fascia not tendon
- Chain: fascial force transmission creates kinematic chains — movement is never isolated
- Posterior oblique sling: lat dorsi to TLF to contralateral glute — primary gait driver

## Fascial Hydration and Gliding

- Hyaluronic acid: loose connective tissue between fascial layers — lubricant
- Densification: Stecco — hyaluronic acid polymerizes in low-movement areas — adhesion
- Movement: movement is the primary fascial hydration mechanism — sedentary equals dry fascia
- Tai Chi: slow sustained movement in all planes — optimal fascial hydration protocol
