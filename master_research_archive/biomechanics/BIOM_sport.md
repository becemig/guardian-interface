# Sport and Performance Biomechanics

## Abstract

Athletic excellence is not merely the product of strength and conditioning — it is the result of movement patterns that optimally exploit the body mechanics: elastic energy storage in tendons and fascia, momentum transfer through kinetic chains, stretch-shortening cycle amplification of muscle force, and the precise sequencing of segment accelerations that produces whole-body power from proximal to distal. The greatest athletes are not those who generate the most force but those whose movement patterns most efficiently channel force through the body natural mechanical architecture. This insight bridges sport biomechanics directly to the MCF framework: athletic excellence is geodesic motion in the sub-Riemannian body manifold — movement that follows the natural curvature of the fascial and channel geometry, minimizing energetic waste and maximizing mechanical output.

## Stretch-Shortening Cycle

- SSC: rapid eccentric pre-stretch immediately before concentric contraction
- Elastic contribution: tendon stores energy during stretch — returns it during shortening
- Neural contribution: stretch reflex potentiates motor neuron pool during SSC
- Force enhancement: SSC produces 30-50 percent more force than isolated concentric
- Fast SSC: contact time less than 250ms — plyometric jumping sprinting
- Slow SSC: contact time greater than 250ms — weightlifting countermovement
- Reactive strength index: jump height divided by contact time — SSC quality metric
- Training: plyometrics develop SSC — progressive loading of tendon elastic capacity
- MCF: SSC as rapid geodesic reversal — eccentric loads manifold spring — concentric releases

## Kinetic Chain and Power Transfer

- Kinetic chain: sequential segmental motion proximal to distal — hip shoulder elbow wrist
- Proximal to distal: largest slowest segments first — momentum transferred to smaller faster
- Summation: each segment adds velocity to previous — cumulative acceleration
- Timing: each segment peaks velocity as proximal segment decelerates — precise sequencing
- Ground reaction: all power originates at ground contact — hip drive is primary
- Core: spine and core transfer momentum between lower and upper body — no leakage
- Breakdown: weak link in chain causes compensation proximal or distal — injury risk
- MCF: kinetic chain as sequential geodesic activation through body manifold segments

## Force-Velocity Relationship

- Hill curve: force and velocity inversely related in muscle — cannot maximize both
- Maximum force: isometric contraction — zero velocity
- Maximum velocity: unloaded contraction — zero external force
- Power peak: maximum power at intermediate force and velocity — 30 percent of max force
- Eccentric: force exceeds isometric maximum — velocity negative — SSC pre-load
- Rate of force development: how quickly force rises from onset — explosive strength
- Training: heavy loads shift curve right — higher force, plyometrics shift left — higher velocity
- Sport specificity: match training to sport position on force-velocity curve

## Elastic Energy and Tendons

- Tendon elasticity: tendons store and return elastic strain energy — biological spring
- Achilles: stores 35-50 percent of running energy — primary locomotor spring
- Patellar tendon: stores energy in jumping — returns at toe-off
- Plantar fascia: windlass mechanism — stores energy in late stance — releases at push-off
- Stiffness training: heavy slow resistance training increases tendon stiffness and CSA
- Compliance: compliant tendons absorb shock — stiff tendons return energy — tradeoff
- Optimal stiffness: intermediate — enough compliance for SSC — enough stiffness for return
- MCF: tendon as elastic sub-manifold — stores geodesic potential during eccentric loading

## Injury Prevention Biomechanics

- ACL injury: non-contact — valgus collapse at landing — hip abductor weakness
- Mechanism: knee caves medially on landing — ACL tensioned beyond failure load
- Prevention: hip strengthening neuromuscular training landing mechanics coaching
- Hamstring strain: excessive anterior pelvic tilt — reduced hamstring length under load
- Nordic curl: eccentric hamstring training — best evidence for hamstring strain prevention
- Overuse: repetitive loading without recovery — stress fracture tendinopathy
- Load monitoring: training load spike ratio — weekly load should not increase more than 10 percent
- MCF: elevated A(t) in specific joint during sport movement — injury risk marker

## Functional Movement Screening

- FMS: seven movement patterns assessing mobility stability and neuromuscular control
- Deep squat: bilateral ankle hip thoracic mobility — overhead stability
- Hurdle step: single leg stance hip flexion — step-over stability
- Inline lunge: hip mobility ankle stability — deceleration pattern
- Shoulder mobility: bilateral shoulder reaching — thoracic mobility
- Active straight leg raise: hamstring flexibility — core stability
- Trunk stability pushup: spinal stability under anterior load
- Rotary stability: quadruped diagonal — spinal stability under rotational load
- MCF: FMS tests are distribution horizontality checks — each screen tests a D subspace
