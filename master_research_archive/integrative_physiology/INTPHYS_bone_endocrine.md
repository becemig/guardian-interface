# Bone as Endocrine Organ

## Abstract

Bone — long understood exclusively as a structural and mineral storage tissue — is now recognized as a major endocrine organ that regulates energy metabolism, brain function, male fertility, and muscle performance through the secretion of osteocalcin and other bone-derived hormones, revealing an unexpected inter-organ communication network that places bone at the center of metabolic integration and corresponding to the TCM understanding of Kidney governing bone and Jing as the constitutional essence governing the deepest levels of organismal function. Gerard Karsenty's discovery that osteocalcin — an osteoblast-secreted protein previously thought to be a structural component of bone matrix — functions as a hormone that regulates insulin secretion, insulin sensitivity, energy expenditure, exercise capacity, and brain function has transformed the understanding of bone physiology and established bone as an endocrine organ with systemic metabolic significance. Osteocalcin-metabolism-brain axis: osteocalcin promotes pancreatic beta cell proliferation and insulin secretion, enhances peripheral insulin sensitivity in muscle and adipose tissue, stimulates energy expenditure through brown adipose thermogenesis, crosses the blood-brain barrier to promote hippocampal neurogenesis and spatial memory, and is required for optimal muscle energy production during exercise — providing a comprehensive inter-organ communication network from bone to pancreas, adipose, brain, and muscle. FGF23 — fibroblast growth factor 23 produced by osteocytes in response to elevated phosphate and vitamin D — is the primary regulator of phosphate homeostasis through its actions on the kidney to reduce phosphate reabsorption and suppress vitamin D activation, with elevated FGF23 in chronic kidney disease producing the phosphate retention and vitamin D deficiency that drive cardiovascular calcification and mortality. Sclerostin — the Wnt signaling inhibitor produced by osteocytes in response to mechanical unloading — suppresses bone formation by inhibiting the Wnt pathway in osteoblasts, with sclerostin as the primary mediator of the bone loss response to immobilization and the target of the new anti-sclerostin antibody therapeutics for osteoporosis. The Kidney governing bones and Jing correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding that the Kidney stores the constitutional essence Jing that generates the bone marrow and governs the health and density of bone — a correspondence supported by the modern evidence that constitutional genetics heavily determines peak bone mass, that Kidney Jing deficiency corresponds to the low osteocalcin and reduced bone endocrine function of aging.

## Osteocalcin System

- Osteoblast-secreted protein that functions as systemic hormone.
- Promotes pancreatic beta cell proliferation and insulin secretion.
- Enhances muscle glucose and fatty acid uptake during exercise.
- Crosses BBB: promotes hippocampal neurogenesis and spatial memory consolidation.

## FGF23 Phosphate Regulation

- Osteocyte-produced in response to elevated phosphate and 1,25-vitamin D.
- Acts on kidney to reduce phosphate reabsorption and suppress vitamin D activation.
- Elevated in CKD: drives phosphate retention cardiovascular calcification excess mortality.
- Klotho co-receptor: required for FGF23 signaling — Klotho as aging suppressor protein.

## Bone-Muscle Crosstalk

- Osteocalcin required for optimal muscle energy production during sustained exercise.
- Muscle-derived IL-6 stimulates osteoblast activity and bone formation.
- Mechanical loading of bone by muscle contraction maintains bone density through sclerostin suppression.
- Exercise increases circulating osteocalcin — contributes to exercise cognitive enhancement.
