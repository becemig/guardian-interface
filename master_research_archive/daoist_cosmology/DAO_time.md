# Daoist Cosmological Time

## Abstract

Daoist and Chinese cosmological thought conceives of time as fundamentally cyclical rather than linear, organized by nested rhythmic cycles of varying duration that express the underlying patterns of yin-yang and five phase transformation. The primary framework is the stem-branch (gan-zhi) system, combining ten heavenly stems and twelve earthly branches into a sixty-unit cycle that organizes years, months, days, and hours into a fractal temporal map. The I Ching (Book of Changes) provides 64 hexagrams encoding all possible states and transitions within cyclical change. The Wu Yun Liu Qi (Five Luck Periods and Six Qi) system from the Su Wen maps cosmological cycles onto climate, disease patterns, and therapeutic timing. The twenty-four solar terms (jie qi) divide the year into precise seasonal segments guiding agricultural, medical, and cultivation practice. The twelve-branch organ clock maps two-hour periods to organ system activity peaks. Together these systems constitute a comprehensive chronobiological framework developed millennia before modern chronobiology.

## Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches

- Ten heavenly stems (tian gan): Jia Yi Bing Ding Wu Ji Geng Xin Ren Gui
- Five phase pairs: each pair of stems corresponds to one of the five phases
- Twelve earthly branches (di zhi): Zi Chou Yin Mao Chen Si Wu Wei Shen You Xu Hai
- Animal signs: twelve branches correspond to twelve animals of Chinese zodiac
- Sixty-year cycle: stems and branches combine into sixty unique pairs - jiazi cycle
- Fractal: same sixty-unit cycle applies to years, months, days, and hours
- Medical timing: birth year stem-branch determines constitutional tendencies in TCM

## I Ching Hexagram System

- Eight trigrams: Ba Gua - combinations of three yin and yang lines - eight primary states
- Sixty-four hexagrams: all combinations of two trigrams - complete map of change
- Each hexagram: describes a situation, its inherent tendency, and its natural transformation
- Changing lines: individual lines in transition encode the direction of movement
- Nei Dan use: hexagram sequence used to time firing process in inner alchemy
- Monthly hexagrams: twelve hexagrams map the annual yin-yang cycle
- Fu Xi sequence: original arrangement - before heaven - maps primordial order
- King Wen sequence: received sequence - after heaven - maps manifest order

## Twenty-Four Solar Terms

- Jie Qi: twenty-four divisions of the solar year at 15-degree intervals
- Solstices and equinoxes: four primary divisions - longest and shortest days
- Seasonal transitions: Li Chun (start of spring), Li Xia, Li Qiu, Li Dong
- Medical significance: disease patterns shift at solar term transitions
- Cultivation: practices adapted to each solar term - what to eat, how to sleep, what to cultivate
- Dong Zhi: winter solstice - maximum yin - one yang returns - most important for cultivation
- Chronobiology parallel: circannual rhythms - immune function, mood, and hormone levels vary by season

## Twelve Branch Organ Clock

- 24-hour cycle divided into twelve two-hour periods each governed by an organ system
- 3-5 AM Lung: Wei Qi emerges - cortisol begins rising - respiratory function primes
- 5-7 AM Large Intestine: elimination - bowel movement optimal - letting go
- 7-9 AM Stomach: peak digestive function - largest meal optimal - insulin sensitivity high
- 9-11 AM Spleen: transformation and transportation - cognitive peak - study and planning
- 11 AM-1 PM Heart: peak cardiovascular - connection and communication
- 1-3 PM Small Intestine: sorting pure from impure - integration and discernment
- 3-5 PM Bladder: memory storage - physical performance peak
- 5-7 PM Kidney: transition to yin - rest and conservation begins
- 7-9 PM Pericardium: Heart protection - relationship and emotional processing
- 9-11 PM Triple Warmer: metabolic regulation - body temperature drops
- 11 PM-1 AM Gallbladder: decision and courage - GH pulse - anabolic restoration
- 1-3 AM Liver: blood storage and detoxification - Hun most active - dreaming

## Wu Yun Liu Qi

- Five Luck Periods (Wu Yun): five phase dominance cycles across the year
- Six Qi (Liu Qi): six climatic Qi cycles - Wind Cold Summer-Heat Damp Dryness Fire
- System: stem-branch of year determines which phase and climate Qi dominate
- Disease prediction: system predicts which diseases will be prevalent in a given year
- Treatment timing: optimal times to treat specific conditions based on cosmological cycle
- Seasonal medicine: adapt treatment strategy to cosmological season not just calendar season
- Modern parallel: epidemiological patterns of disease do show seasonal and cyclical variation
