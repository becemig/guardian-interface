# Seasonal Biology and Affective Disorders

## Abstract

Seasonal biology and affective disorders — the systematic variation in physiology, metabolism, immune function, mood, and behavior across the annual photoperiodic cycle, driven by the seasonal variation in day length encoding melatonin signal duration that governs seasonal biological programs in humans and other mammals — represents an underappreciated dimension of human biology with major implications for mental health, metabolic disease, immune function, and the optimal timing of health interventions, corresponding to the TCM five-season model of Qi transformation that governs the appropriate adaptation of physiology, lifestyle, and therapeutic strategy to each season. Photoperiodism mechanisms — the encoding of day length information by the pineal melatonin signal whose duration mirrors the length of the night, with short summer nights producing brief melatonin pulses signaling summer and long winter nights producing extended melatonin pulses signaling winter, and with seasonal breeding mammals using this melatonin duration signal to gate gonadal function, metabolism, and pelage changes through melatonin receptor signaling in the pars tuberalis of the pituitary. Seasonal affective disorder neurobiology — the recurrent winter depression affecting 2-8 percent of northern hemisphere populations, characterized by hypersomnia, carbohydrate craving, weight gain, fatigue, and mood depression beginning in autumn and remitting spontaneously in spring, with the primary pathophysiology involving serotonin transporter upregulation in winter reducing synaptic serotonin, diminished BDNF expression, and circadian phase delay of the SCN relative to the sleep-wake cycle, all effectively treated by morning bright light therapy that advances circadian phase and suppresses excess melatonin. Widespread seasonal gene expression — Xiaofang Dopico's 2015 genomic study demonstrating that approximately 23 percent of the human genome shows significant seasonal variation in expression in blood cells, with immune genes peaking in winter and metabolic and circadian genes varying seasonally in patterns consistent with an ancient mammalian photoperiodic program, establishing seasonal variation in human physiology as far more extensive than previously recognized. The five-season Qi transformation correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding of the five seasons — spring summer late summer autumn winter — each governed by a distinct Five Element Qi with specific physiological, emotional, and therapeutic implications, with the physician's seasonal prescription of lifestyle, diet, and treatment reflecting the deep integration of seasonal biology into TCM clinical practice.

- Serotonin transporter: upregulated in winter — reduced synaptic serotonin availability.

- Phase delay: SCN circadian phase delayed in SAD — light therapy advances phase.
- Light therapy: 10000 lux morning light — advances phase suppresses melatonin normalizes serotonin.
