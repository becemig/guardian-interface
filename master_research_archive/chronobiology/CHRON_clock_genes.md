# Clock Gene Molecular Machinery

## Abstract

Clock gene molecular machinery — the interlocking transcription-translation feedback loops of the circadian clock that generate autonomous approximately 24-hour oscillations in gene expression in virtually every cell of the body — provides the molecular basis for the circadian timing system that coordinates the temporal organization of physiology, metabolism, and behavior with the 24-hour light-dark cycle, corresponding to the TCM Zi Wu Liu Zhu system of circadian Qi flow through the organ meridian system that describes the temporal organization of physiological function through a traditional clinical framework. The primary CLOCK-BMAL1 transcription loop — the positive limb of the mammalian circadian clock in which the bHLH-PAS transcription factors CLOCK and BMAL1 heterodimerize to activate transcription of the Period genes Per1 and Per2 and the Cryptochrome genes Cry1 and Cry2 through E-box regulatory elements in their promoters — drives the rhythmic expression of thousands of clock-controlled genes that govern the circadian regulation of metabolism, immune function, cell cycle, and tissue-specific physiology. The PER-CRY negative feedback loop — the negative limb in which the translated PER and CRY proteins accumulate in the cytoplasm, form complexes, translocate to the nucleus, and inhibit CLOCK-BMAL1 transcriptional activity, thereby suppressing their own transcription and creating the autoregulatory feedback delay of approximately 24 hours that constitutes the molecular clock — is stabilized and tuned by post-translational modifications including CKI-epsilon-mediated phosphorylation of PER proteins that governs their nuclear entry timing and proteasomal degradation rate. Post-translational clock regulation — the layer of kinase-mediated phosphorylation, ubiquitination, and SUMOylation that modulates the stability, nuclear localization, and activity of clock proteins to fine-tune period length and amplitude, with casein kinase 1 epsilon and delta as primary PER kinases, FBXL3 and beta-TrCP as CRY and PER E3 ubiquitin ligases, and SIRT1 as a NAD-dependent deacetylase linking clock function to metabolic state. The Zi Wu Liu Zhu circadian Qi flow correspondence in TCM describes the classical Chinese medical system of circadian medicine in which each of the twelve organ meridian systems has a two-hour peak activity period in the 24-hour cycle, with the Lung active from 3-5 AM, Heart from 11 AM-1 PM, and Kidney from 5-7 PM — a clinical framework for temporal medicine that corresponds to the modern understanding of circadian gene expression rhythms governing organ-specific physiological peaks.

## CLOCK BMAL1 Positive Loop

- CLOCK BMAL1 heterodimer: binds E-box elements in Per and Cry gene promoters.
- Activates transcription: Per1 Per2 Cry1 Cry2 and hundreds of clock-controlled genes.
- Peak activation: late day early evening in most peripheral tissues.
- BMAL1 rhythmic: itself clock-controlled — additional feedback layer.

## PER CRY Negative Loop

- PER1 PER2: accumulate in cytoplasm — form complexes with CRY1 CRY2.
- Nuclear translocation: PER-CRY complex enters nucleus — inhibits CLOCK-BMAL1.
- Delay mechanism: accumulation and translocation delay creates approximately 24-hour period.
- Degradation: phosphorylated PER CRY targeted for proteasomal degradation — resets loop.

## Post-Translational Regulation

- CKI-epsilon delta: phosphorylates PER — governs stability and nuclear entry timing.
- FBXL3: E3 ligase targeting CRY for degradation — period length determinant.
- SIRT1: deacetylates BMAL1 and PER2 — links metabolic NAD+ state to clock.
- CK2: phosphorylates BMAL1 — additional period and amplitude tuning.
