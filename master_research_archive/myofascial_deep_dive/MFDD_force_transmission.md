# Myofascial Force Transmission

## Abstract

Myofascial force transmission — the transmission of muscular force through fascial connections between adjacent muscles, between muscles and bones, and along whole-body fascial chains that connect distant body segments — establishes that muscular force is not confined to the muscle's own tendinous attachments but is distributed through the surrounding fascial matrix to adjacent structures, with significant implications for understanding movement biomechanics, injury patterns, rehabilitation, and the anatomical basis of the TCM Jin sinew channel force transmission pathways. Epimuscular force transmission — Peter Huijing's research demonstrating that force is transmitted between synergistic and antagonistic muscles through their shared fascial envelopes and intermuscular septa, with up to 40 percent of a muscle's force potentially transmitted epimuscularly rather than through its own tendon — challenges the classical isolated muscle model and explains why injury or surgery affecting one muscle produces force production deficits in adjacent muscles through disruption of epimuscular transmission. Myofascial slings — the functional force transmission chains identified through biomechanical analysis including the posterior oblique sling connecting the contralateral latissimus dorsi and gluteus maximus through the thoracolumbar fascia, the anterior oblique sling connecting the contralateral external oblique and adductors through the anterior abdominal fascia, and the longitudinal slings connecting the peroneus longus through the sacrotuberous ligament to the biceps femoris — govern the force transfer between upper and lower limbs through the trunk during walking, running, and throwing, with dysfunction in any component disrupting the entire sling's force transmission efficiency. Closed kinetic chain function — the biomechanical principle that in weight-bearing movements where the distal segment is fixed, forces are transmitted through continuous kinetic chains from ground contact through the entire kinematic linkage, with fascial force transmission enabling efficient proximal force generation from distal ground reaction forces — explains why weight-bearing rehabilitation is more functionally effective than isolated open-chain exercises and why ground reaction force optimization is central to athletic performance. The Jin channel force flow correspondence in TCM describes the classical understanding of the sinew channels as the pathways through which the force and power of movement flows through the body — a functional description that corresponds precisely to the myofascial force transmission chains of modern biomechanics.

## Epimuscular Transmission

- Force transmitted through intermuscular septa and fascial envelopes between adjacent muscles.
- Up to 40 percent of muscular force transmitted epimuscularly in some studies.
- Synergist transmission: force sharing between agonists through shared fascia.
- Antagonist transmission: passive force transmission to antagonist through intermuscular septum.

## Posterior Oblique Sling

- Contralateral latissimus dorsi and gluteus maximus connected through thoracolumbar fascia.
- Active during gait: arm swing force transmitted through TLF to contralateral hip.
- Sacroiliac joint stabilization: sling tension stabilizes SIJ during single-leg loading.
- Dysfunction: contralateral latissimus-gluteus inhibition — SIJ instability and back pain.

## Anterior Oblique Sling

- Contralateral external oblique internal oblique adductors through anterior abdominal fascia.
- Active during rotation: transmits rotational forces between trunk and opposite leg.
- Throwing athletes: anterior sling transmits shoulder force to contralateral hip.
- Dysfunction: groin pain in athletes — anterior sling force transmission deficit.
