# HPT Axis

## Abstract

The hypothalamic-pituitary-thyroid axis regulates metabolic rate, thermogenesis, cardiac function, neurodevelopment, and mood through the thyroid hormones T3 (triiodothyronine) and T4 (thyroxine). The cascade proceeds from TRH in the hypothalamus to TSH in the anterior pituitary to thyroid hormone synthesis and secretion from the thyroid gland. T4 is the primary secreted form but is largely a prohormone — peripheral conversion by deiodinase enzymes generates the active T3 in target tissues. Thyroid hormones act on virtually every cell in the body, regulating gene expression, mitochondrial biogenesis, and protein synthesis. Hypothyroidism produces fatigue, weight gain, depression, cognitive slowing, and cold intolerance. Hyperthyroidism produces anxiety, weight loss, palpitations, heat intolerance, and insomnia. In TCM, thyroid function maps primarily to Kidney Yang (metabolic fire), Liver Qi stagnation (autoimmune thyroid), and Phlegm-Qi stagnation (goiter).

## HPT Cascade

- Hypothalamus: TRH (thyrotropin-releasing hormone) released from paraventricular nucleus
- TRH: travels via hypophyseal portal to anterior pituitary thyrotrophs
- TSH: thyroid-stimulating hormone released from pituitary - binds TSH receptor on thyroid
- Thyroid gland: TSH stimulates synthesis and release of T4 (80 percent) and T3 (20 percent)
- Synthesis: iodination of tyrosine residues on thyroglobulin - requires adequate iodine and selenium
- Negative feedback: T3 and T4 inhibit TRH and TSH - maintains setpoint

## T4 to T3 Peripheral Conversion

- T4: relatively inactive prohormone - long half-life (7 days)
- T3: active form - 3-4 times more potent than T4 - short half-life (1 day)
- Deiodinase type 1: liver and kidney - systemic T3 production
- Deiodinase type 2: brain, pituitary, brown adipose - local T3 for CNS
- Deiodinase type 3: converts T4 to inactive reverse T3 (rT3) - blocks T3 action
- Stress and illness: elevated cortisol and cytokines shift conversion toward rT3 - sick euthyroid syndrome
- Selenium: required cofactor for deiodinase enzymes - deficiency impairs T4-T3 conversion

## Thyroid Hormone Actions

- Metabolic rate: T3 increases basal metabolic rate via mitochondrial uncoupling
- Thermogenesis: T3 upregulates UCP1 in brown adipose tissue
- Cardiac: T3 increases heart rate, contractility, and cardiac output
- CNS development: critical for fetal brain myelination and neuronal migration
- Adult cognition: T3 regulates BDNF, serotonin synthesis, and synaptic plasticity
- Bone: T3 promotes osteoblast activity and bone turnover
- GI motility: T3 regulates gut transit speed - hypothyroidism causes constipation

## Hypothyroidism

- Primary: thyroid gland failure - elevated TSH, low T4
- Hashimoto thyroiditis: autoimmune destruction - most common cause in developed world
- Subclinical: elevated TSH with normal T4 - often symptomatic despite normal range
- Symptoms: fatigue, weight gain, cold intolerance, constipation, dry skin, hair loss, depression, cognitive slowing
- TCM: Kidney Yang deficiency - cold, fatigue, fluid retention, low libido

## Hyperthyroidism

- Primary: thyroid overproduction - low TSH, elevated T4/T3
- Graves disease: autoimmune TSH receptor stimulation - most common cause
- Symptoms: weight loss, anxiety, palpitations, heat intolerance, tremor, insomnia, diarrhea
- TCM: Liver Fire and Phlegm-Fire agitating Heart - anxiety, palpitations, goiter

## Thyroid and Mood

- Depression: 10-15 percent of depression patients have subclinical hypothyroidism
- T3 augmentation: adding T3 to antidepressants improves response in refractory depression
- Bipolar: thyroid dysfunction common - lithium causes hypothyroidism in 20-40 percent
- Anxiety: hyperthyroidism commonly misdiagnosed as anxiety disorder
