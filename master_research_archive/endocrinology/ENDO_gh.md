# Growth Hormone and IGF-1

## Abstract

Growth hormone (GH), secreted by somatotroph cells of the anterior pituitary, is the master regulator of somatic growth, body composition, metabolic homeostasis, and tissue repair throughout the lifespan. The somatotropic axis operates through GHRH stimulation and somatostatin inhibition from the hypothalamus, with ghrelin providing an additional orexigenic drive to GH release. GH acts directly on tissues and indirectly via IGF-1 produced primarily in the liver. The largest daily GH pulse occurs during the first slow-wave sleep episode — making sleep quality a direct determinant of anabolic hormone output. GH declines progressively with aging (somatopause), contributing to sarcopenia, increased adiposity, reduced bone density, and impaired tissue repair. IGF-1 is neuroprotective, promotes neurogenesis, and mediates many of the cognitive benefits of exercise. In TCM, the somatotropic axis maps to Kidney Yang providing growth and transformation energy, with Jing as the constitutional substrate for GH-dependent development.

## Somatotropic Axis

- GHRH: growth hormone-releasing hormone from hypothalamic arcuate nucleus - stimulates GH
- Somatostatin: from periventricular nucleus - inhibits GH release
- Ghrelin: from stomach - stimulates GHRH and directly stimulates pituitary GH release
- GH: released in pulses - largest pulse during first SWS episode
- IGF-1: liver produces IGF-1 in response to GH - mediates most growth-promoting effects
- Negative feedback: IGF-1 inhibits GHRH and stimulates somatostatin - closes loop

## GH Pulsatility

- Pulsatile: GH secreted in discrete pulses - trough levels near zero
- Frequency: 6-12 pulses per day - amplitude highest during sleep
- Sleep dependence: 70-80 percent of daily GH secreted during first SWS episode
- Sleep deprivation: reducing SWS by 90 minutes reduces GH output by 23 percent
- Age effect: pulse amplitude declines with age - somatopause - frequency relatively preserved
- Adiposity: visceral fat suppresses GH pulsatility - reduces amplitude
- Exercise: resistance and high-intensity exercise stimulate GH pulses

## GH Direct Effects

- Lipolysis: GH mobilizes free fatty acids from adipose - shifts fuel toward fat oxidation
- Anti-insulin: GH is insulin-antagonistic - raises blood glucose
- Protein synthesis: GH promotes amino acid uptake and protein synthesis in muscle
- Immune: GH has immunomodulatory effects - promotes thymic function
- Cartilage: GH directly stimulates chondrocyte proliferation independent of IGF-1

## IGF-1 Effects

- Linear growth: IGF-1 drives long bone growth via epiphyseal plate chondrocyte proliferation
- Muscle: IGF-1 activates satellite cells - muscle hypertrophy and repair
- Bone: IGF-1 stimulates osteoblast proliferation and bone matrix synthesis
- Brain: IGF-1 crosses BBB - neuroprotective, promotes BDNF, hippocampal neurogenesis
- Exercise IGF-1: aerobic exercise raises serum and brain IGF-1 - mediates cognitive benefits
- Cancer risk: chronically elevated IGF-1 associated with increased cancer risk

## Somatopause and Aging

- Somatopause: progressive decline in GH and IGF-1 from third decade
- Sarcopenia: loss of muscle mass and strength - GH decline is contributory
- Adiposity: visceral fat increases as GH declines
- Bone density: GH-IGF1 decline contributes to osteopenia
- Cognitive decline: IGF-1 decline reduces neuroprotection
- Sleep-GH cycle: SWS declines with age - GH output declines in parallel - bidirectional
- TCM: Kidney Jing depletion across seven and eight year cycles - progressive somatopause

## GH Deficiency and Excess

- Adult GHD: fatigue, reduced muscle mass, increased adiposity, poor quality of life
- Childhood GHD: short stature, delayed puberty, metabolic abnormalities
- Acromegaly: GH-secreting pituitary adenoma - enlarged hands, feet, jaw, cardiomegaly
- GH doping: athletes use GH for muscle mass and recovery - measurable on anti-doping tests
