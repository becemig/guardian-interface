# Korean Mu-Ism

## Abstract

Korean Mu-Ism — the indigenous shamanic tradition of Korea known as Musok or Muism — is one of the world's oldest living shamanic traditions, with archaeological evidence suggesting continuous practice for at least five thousand years, and is unique among East Asian shamanic traditions for its continued vitality and cultural centrality in contemporary Korean society. The Mudang — the Korean shaman, predominantly female — serves as the intermediary between the human community and the spirit world, performing healing ceremonies Gut that invite specific spirits to possess the Mudang's body and speak directly to the community, resolving conflicts between the living and the dead, identifying the spiritual causes of illness, and creating the conditions for healing and good fortune. Sinbyeong — spirit sickness — is the illness that marks the calling to become a Mudang: a prolonged period of physical illness, psychological disturbance, visionary experiences, and disruption that can only be resolved through acceptance of the shamanic vocation and initiation under an established Mudang. The Gut ceremony is a multi-day healing performance combining music, dance, costume, and spirit possession in which the Mudang embodies a succession of spirits — gods, ancestors, ghosts — each speaking their concerns, receiving their offerings, and providing guidance or healing to the community. Han is the distinctly Korean concept of accumulated grief, resentment, and sorrow — a collective emotional wound carried by the Korean people through centuries of invasion, colonization, and suffering — that manifests as both individual psychological suffering and collective cultural expression in art, music, and literature. Healing Han through Gut ceremony — the communal processing of ancestral grief through ecstatic performance — corresponds to the collective trauma healing practices identified in modern trauma research as essential for intergenerational healing.

- Sinbyeong spirit sickness marks the shamanic calling — illness that resolves only through vocation acceptance.
- The Mudang is predominantly female — Korean shamanism is a female-centered healing tradition.
- Training under an established Mudang transmits the specific spirit relationships and ceremonial knowledge.
- This corresponds to the TCM understanding that certain practitioners are constitutionally called to healing work.

## Gut Ceremony

- Multi-day ceremony combining music, dance, costume change, and spirit possession.
- The Mudang embodies successive spirits — gods, ancestors, nature spirits, ghosts — each in turn.
- Each spirit speaks, receives offerings, and provides healing guidance or resolution.
- The ceremonial container of music and community enables the safely bounded possession states.

## Han

- Accumulated grief, resentment, and sorrow carried individually and collectively through generations.
- Han is both wound and creative force — Korean art, music, and literature are deeply shaped by Han.
- Gut ceremony processes Han through ecstatic communal performance enabling collective grief release.
- This corresponds to the modern trauma research on collective trauma requiring collective healing modalities.
