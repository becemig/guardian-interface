# Ayurveda

## Abstract

Ayurveda — the science of life — is the traditional medicine of the Indian subcontinent, one of the world's oldest and most comprehensive medical systems, developed over more than three thousand years through the systematic observation of nature, the human body, and the relationship between individual constitution and the forces of the natural world. The foundational framework of Ayurveda is the tridosha theory — the three biological humors Vata, Pitta, and Kapha that govern all physiological and psychological processes in the body. The five great elements Panchamahabhutas — earth, water, fire, air, and ether — combine in different proportions to form the three doshas: Vata is composed of air and ether producing the qualities of movement, lightness, dryness, and irregularity; Pitta is composed of fire and water producing the qualities of transformation, heat, sharpness, and intensity; Kapha is composed of earth and water producing the qualities of stability, heaviness, coldness, and cohesion. Prakriti is the individual's constitutional nature — the unique proportion of doshas determined at conception that establishes the baseline physiological and psychological tendencies of the individual throughout life. Vikriti is the current state of dosha balance or imbalance — the deviation from Prakriti produced by diet, lifestyle, seasons, emotions, and other factors. Agni — the digestive fire — is the central concept of Ayurvedic physiology, governing not only the digestion and transformation of food but all metabolic transformation at the cellular and tissue level. Adequate Agni produces ojas — the refined essence of all seven dhatu tissues — which corresponds to vitality, immunity, and the luminous health of a well-nourished constitution. The three refined essences — ojas corresponding to immune vitality, tejas corresponding to metabolic intelligence, and prana corresponding to life force — correspond directly to the TCM triad of Jing, Qi, and Shen.

## Tridosha

- Vata governs movement: nerve impulses, circulation, respiration, elimination, creativity, and fear.
- Pitta governs transformation: digestion, metabolism, vision, courage, anger, and discernment.
- Kapha governs cohesion: structure, lubrication, memory, love, attachment, and stability.
- Each dosha has its primary seat: Vata in the colon, Pitta in the small intestine, Kapha in the stomach.

## Prakriti Assessment

- Prakriti is assessed through pulse diagnosis, physical examination, and detailed constitutional questioning.
- Single-dosha Prakriti: one dosha clearly predominates — the most specific constitutional type.
- Dual-dosha Prakriti: two doshas are roughly equal — more common and requires balancing both.
- Tridoshic Prakriti: all three doshas roughly equal — the rarest and most balanced constitution.

## Agni and Ojas

- Sama Agni balanced digestive fire: optimal digestion, metabolism, and tissue nourishment.
- Vishama Agni irregular Vata-type: variable digestion, gas, bloating, constipation alternating with looseness.
- Tikshna Agni sharp Pitta-type: hypermetabolism, inflammation, acid reflux, loose stools.
- Manda Agni slow Kapha-type: sluggish digestion, weight gain, mucus accumulation, congestion.

## TCM Correspondence

- Vata wind-movement corresponds to TCM Liver Qi and Wind as the moving transforming force.
- Pitta fire-transformation corresponds to TCM ministerial fire and the transforming function of Kidney Yang.
- Kapha earth-water cohesion corresponds to TCM Spleen-Kidney constitutional foundation and Phlegm-Damp pathology.
- Ojas-Tejas-Prana triad corresponds directly to TCM Jing-Qi-Shen as the three treasures of life.
