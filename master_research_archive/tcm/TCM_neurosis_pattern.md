# Neurosis in TCM

## Abstract

Neurosis in TCM encompasses the spectrum of anxiety disorders, somatic symptom disorders, and mild to moderate mood disorders that involve significant psychosomatic distress without the complete loss of reality contact of psychosis. TCM provides a rich pattern vocabulary for neurosis that maps precisely onto the phenomenology of anxiety, depression, somatization, and dissociation through its organ-spirit framework. Liver Qi stagnation is the foundational pattern underlying most neurosis — the frustrated movement of the creative Hun spirit producing the characteristic neurotic tension between impulse and inhibition, between the drive to move forward and the fear of doing so. Heart-Kidney disharmony is the second major neurosis pattern — when the Heart Fire above and the Kidney Water below fail to communicate and nourish each other, the Heart Shen floats anxiously above without the grounding of Kidney Zhi, producing anxiety, insomnia, palpitations, and the restless agitated quality of floating disconnected consciousness. Running Piglet Qi Ben Tun is one of the most dramatic TCM neurosis presentations — a paroxysmal sensation of Qi rushing upward from the lower abdomen to the chest and throat producing acute panic, choking sensation, and terror. This corresponds directly to panic disorder and may involve sudden sympathetic activation with upward-rushing catecholamine surges. Globus hystericus — the sensation of a lump in the throat — is classically explained as Plum Pit Qi Mei He Qi in TCM, caused by Liver Qi stagnation creating constraint in the throat region where the Liver channel traverses. Phlegm obstruction of the mind produces the numbing, clouding, and dissociation of certain neurotic states where the person feels separated from their experience by a fog of Phlegm.

## Liver Qi Stagnation Neurosis

- Liver Qi stagnation produces the characteristic neurotic tension between impulse and inhibition.
- Symptoms: sighing, hypochondriac distension, mood variability, frustration, throat constriction.
- Plum Pit Qi globus hystericus is Liver Qi stagnation constricting the throat.
- Treatment: Chai Hu, Bai Shao, Xiang Fu moving Liver Qi and nourishing Liver Blood.

## Running Piglet Qi

- Ben Tun presents as paroxysmal Qi rushing from lower abdomen to chest and throat.
- Corresponds to panic disorder with acute sympathetic surges and choking sensation.
- Caused by Kidney Qi failing to anchor and Liver Qi rebelling upward.
- Classical formula: Ben Tun Tang containing Li Gen Bai Pi, Gan Cao, Chuan Xiong, Dang Gui.

## Heart-Kidney Disharmony

- Heart Fire floats upward without Kidney Water to anchor — produces anxious agitation.
- Symptoms: insomnia, palpitations, anxiety, low back weakness, night sweats, restless sleep.
- Treatment: Huang Lian E Jiao Tang connecting Heart and Kidney, clearing Heart Fire, nourishing Kidney Yin.
- Corresponds to dysautonomia with elevated sympathetic tone and insufficient parasympathetic grounding.

## TCM Correspondence

- Liver Qi stagnation neurosis corresponds to amygdala-prefrontal miscommunication in anxiety disorders.
- Running Piglet Qi corresponds to panic disorder with acute sympathetic surges and interoceptive catastrophizing.
- Heart-Kidney disharmony corresponds to autonomic dysregulation with insufficient parasympathetic tone.
- Phlegm obstruction neurosis corresponds to dissociative disorders with emotional numbing and derealization.
