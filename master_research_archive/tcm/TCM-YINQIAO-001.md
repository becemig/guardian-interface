# Yinqiao Mai - Yin Motility Vessel

## Abstract

Yinqiao Mai the Yin Motility Vessel - functioning as the yin aspect of the Bridge Channel pair together with Yangqiao Mai - governs the balance of yin energy consumed by each meridian in daily activity, regulates the kidney energy reserves constituting the body constitutional yin resource, controls yin fluid distribution throughout the body, connects the energetic relationship between the head-brain and the heart-emotional center, and governs sleep through regulation of the yin-yang cycle of rest and activity. The channel runs along the medial aspect of the body from the heel through the medial ankle and leg, through the genitalia, up the abdomen and chest to the inner canthus of the eye, connecting the root of yin energy at the kidney-heel axis with sensory and conscious activity at the eye-brain level. Psychologically governs the courage-depression axis, with Yinqiao deficiency manifesting as depression, excessive passivity, lack of courage, and fatigue.

## Channel Pathway

- Begins at K 6 (Zhao Hai / Joyful Sleep) below the medial malleolus
- Ascends medial ankle and leg through Kidney meridian territory
- Passes through medial thigh and genitalia
- Ascends abdomen and chest along interior medial line
- Continues to throat connecting with Lung meridian at Lu 1 region
- Ascends to inner canthus of eye (B 1 / Jing Ming) - terminal convergence with Yangqiao
- Head-heart connection: medial interior pathway links brain and heart centers

## Master Point and Release Pairs

- K 6 (Zhao Hai / Joyful Sleep): directly below the medial malleolus in the groove
- MASTER POINT of the Great Bridge Channel
- Primary quick release pair: Lu7 + K6 (activates Yinqiao and Ren Mai simultaneously)
- Secondary pair: SI3 + B62 (activates Yangqiao and Du Mai for full Bridge balance)

## Clinical Indications

- Sleep: insomnia, disturbed sleep, difficulty settling into rest
- Neurological: epileptic seizures, convulsions (primary extraordinary vessel indication)
- Pain: backaches, cramping, migraines
- Psychological: nightmares, depression, lack of courage, excessive fatigue
- Behavioral: hyperactivity (excess Yinqiao pattern)
- Urogenital: urinary and reproductive dysfunction
- Fluid regulation: edema, fluid imbalance, yin fluid deficiency
- Ocular: eye disorders, inner canthus conditions

## TCM Theory Connections

- Kidney energy reserve governance: Yinqiao directly connects to and regulates kidney yin treasury
- Head-heart connection: channel pathway physically connects brain (eye level) with heart center
- Sleep cycle: Yinqiao governs the yin rest phase - Yangqiao governs the yang waking phase
- Courage-depression axis: Yinqiao yin deficiency manifests as depression and lack of bravery
- Fluid governance: controls yin fluid distribution - excess manifests as edema, deficiency as dryness
- Eye-kidney connection: channel terminus at inner canthus explains kidney deficiency affecting eyesight
