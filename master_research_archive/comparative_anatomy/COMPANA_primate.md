# Primate Comparative Anatomy

## Abstract

Primate comparative anatomy — the study of the morphological, neurological, and behavioral similarities and differences among the approximately 500 living primate species including prosimians, New World monkeys, Old World monkeys, and apes, with humans as the most derived member of the superfamily Hominoidea sharing 98.7 percent DNA sequence identity with chimpanzees — illuminates the evolutionary origins of distinctively human anatomical and behavioral features by identifying the primate precursors from which they evolved, the selective pressures that drove their elaboration in the human lineage, and the degree to which our closest living relatives share our anatomical organization and cognitive capabilities. Great ape morphology — the shared derived features of the superfamily Hominoidea including the loss of an external tail, the broader and flatter thorax with dorsally positioned scapulae enabling the wide range of shoulder motion used in brachiation and overhead reaching, the longer arms relative to legs reflecting arborealist ancestry, the absence of a bony tail reflecting the reduction of the caudal vertebrae to the coccyx, and the Y-5 cusp pattern of the lower molars shared by all hominoids as a diagnostic morphological feature. Primate social brain hypothesis — Robin Dunbar's proposal that the neocortex expanded in primates not primarily in response to ecological challenges but in response to the computational demands of managing complex social relationships within large stable social groups, with neocortex ratio predicting social group size across primate species and the human neocortex scaled to support a natural social group of approximately 150 individuals — Dunbar's number — consistent with the typical size of hunter-gatherer bands, village communities, and functional military units. Primate trichromacy — the independent evolution of trichromatic color vision in Old World primates including humans through duplication of the ancestral long-wavelength opsin gene producing separate medium and long wavelength opsins, enabling discrimination of red and orange fruit against green foliage backgrounds and providing a foraging advantage, while most mammals retain only dichromacy and many New World monkeys have polymorphic trichromacy with trichromatic females and dichromatic males. The Ren Mai primate kinship lineage correspondence in TCM describes the classical Conception Vessel as governing the fundamental life force and reproductive capacity passed through the maternal lineage — a correspondence with the primate kinship structure in which matrilineal bonds form the stable core of primate social groups and mitochondrial DNA traces the maternal lineage of all primates including humans.

## Great Ape Shared Features

- No external tail: coccyx only — shared hominoid derived feature.
- Broad thorax: dorsal scapulae enable overhead reach — arboreal heritage.
- Y-5 molars: five cusps Y-shaped groove — diagnostic hominoid feature.
- Longer arms: brachiation heritage — arms longer than legs in great apes except humans.

## Social Brain

- Dunbar's number: 150 — predicted human social group from neocortex ratio.
- Neocortex ratio: predicts group size across primate species — social brain hypothesis.
- Grooming: social bonding mechanism — scales with group size.
- Language: replaced grooming for social bonding in humans — vocal grooming.

## Primate Color Vision

- Ancestral mammals: dichromacy — two cone types short and long wavelength.
- Old World primates: trichromacy — opsin gene duplication producing M and L opsins.
- Advantage: red orange detection against green foliage — fruit foraging.
- New World: polymorphic — heterozygous females trichromatic males dichromatic.
