# Quantum Coherence in Photosynthesis

## Abstract

The discovery of quantum coherence in biological photosynthesis — published by Graham Fleming's group at UC Berkeley in a landmark 2007 Nature paper — overturned decades of assumption that quantum mechanical effects play no role in warm, wet, noisy biological systems and opened the field of quantum biology as a serious scientific enterprise. Photosynthesis achieves near-unity quantum efficiency in transferring absorbed light energy from the antenna chlorophyll network to the reaction center where it drives charge separation — nearly every photon absorbed results in a successful energy transfer event, an efficiency that classical energy hopping models cannot fully explain. Two-dimensional electronic spectroscopy — a technique that resolves energy transfer dynamics with femtosecond temporal resolution — revealed long-lived quantum coherent oscillations in the Fenna-Matthews-Olson FMO complex, a chlorophyll protein found in green sulfur bacteria, demonstrating that energy moves through the chlorophyll network as a quantum wave exploring multiple pathways simultaneously rather than as a classical random walk hopping from one chlorophyll to the next. The quantum coherence — the maintenance of phase relationships between quantum states — allows the excitation energy to sample all pathways through the chlorophyll network simultaneously and find the most efficient route to the reaction center through quantum interference, explaining the near-unity efficiency. The persistence of quantum coherence in the warm, wet, noisy environment of the photosynthetic protein — which would be expected to destroy quantum states through decoherence in femtoseconds — has been proposed to result from the coupling between the quantum electronic states and the vibrational modes of the protein scaffold, a phenomenon termed vibronic coupling that may actively protect coherence. The correspondence between photosynthetic quantum coherence and the TCM concept of Qi as a coherent field of biological energy — flowing through networks of channels with wavelike properties that find optimal pathways through the body — provides a physically grounded metaphor for the Qi circulation model.

## Exciton Transport

- Absorbed photon creates an exciton — a bound electron-hole pair that carries the excitation energy.
- Exciton moves through the chlorophyll network toward the reaction center.
- Classical hopping model predicts random walk — too slow and inefficient to explain observed transfer rates.
- Quantum coherent transport allows simultaneous exploration of all pathways — explains near-unity efficiency.

## FMO Complex Evidence

- Fenna-Matthews-Olson complex: 8 chlorophyll molecules in a protein scaffold found in green sulfur bacteria.
- 2D electronic spectroscopy reveals quantum beating oscillations lasting hundreds of femtoseconds.
- Oscillations indicate maintained phase coherence between chlorophyll quantum states during energy transfer.
- This is the clearest evidence of quantum coherence playing a functional role in biological energy transfer.

## Vibronic Coupling
